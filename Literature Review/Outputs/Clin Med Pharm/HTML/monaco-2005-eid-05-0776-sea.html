One-page synthesis

A previously healthy 37-year-old woman in Rome presented with severe multilobar pneumonia. Admission findings included fever 39 C, tachycardia, tachypnea, mild hypoxemia, leukocytosis, and bilateral infiltrates. She did not improve on empiric clarithromycin plus ceftriaxone. Admission sputum grew MRSA, and CT showed multiple cavitary lesions consistent with necrotizing pneumonia. On day 3, therapy was changed to linezolid 600 mg three times daily. Fever resolved and her condition improved. She was discharged after 14 days of linezolid treatment.

The isolate was susceptible to the tested non-beta-lactam drugs listed in the letter. Molecular testing identified SCCmec type IV, PVL genes lukS and lukF, hlg, sequence type ST30, and a novel spa type 755. The authors interpreted the spa profile as a descendant or local variant of the southwest Pacific clone. Their main epidemiologic claims were that this was the first Italian report of PVL-positive CA-MRSA necrotizing pneumonia and the first report of a southwest Pacific clone isolate in southern Europe. Those first-report statements are author claims from 2005, not independently verified here.

Source condensation

Case presentation and initial management

The patient had fever, cough, and headache, no notable medical history, no recent travel, and low-level cigarette use. Her daughter had influenza-like symptoms the preceding week. At admission, vital signs were 39 C, heart rate 108 beats/min, respiratory rate 32 breaths/min, and blood pressure 105/70 mm Hg. Room-air PaO2 was 73 mm Hg and PaCO2 34 mm Hg. Leukocyte count was 24,360 cells/uL with 81% polymorphonuclear cells; platelets were 506,000/uL. Radiography showed right upper and lower lobe and left lower lobe infiltrates.

Empiric clarithromycin plus ceftriaxone did not produce clinical improvement. Sputum collected at admission grew MRSA. CT then showed multiple cavitary lung lesions, which the authors interpreted as necrotizing pneumonia.

Therapy and clinical course

On hospital day 3, antimicrobial therapy was changed to linezolid 600 mg three times daily. The letter reports subsequent fever resolution and rapid clinical improvement. This is a temporal case observation, not evidence that linezolid was superior to another regimen.

After 14 days of linezolid treatment, the patient was discharged. Leukocyte count had fallen to 6,040 cells/uL with 58% polymorphonuclear cells, and PaO2 was 88 mm Hg.

Microbiology and molecular typing

The MRSA isolate was susceptible to all tested non-beta-lactam agents listed in the report, including erythromycin, clindamycin, ciprofloxacin, tetracycline, kanamycin, and fusidic acid. Molecular testing detected SCCmec IV, the PVL genes lukS and lukF, and the gamma-hemolysin gene hlg.

MLST classified the isolate as ST30. Spa typing yielded novel type 755. The spa profile contained seven repeats, XJ4AKAOM. The authors considered the isolate a descendant or local variant of the southwest Pacific clone.

Interpretation and uncertainty

The authors state that this was the first reported Italian case of PVL-positive CA-MRSA necrotizing pneumonia and the first southwest Pacific clone isolate reported in southern Europe. They also argue that fusidic-acid resistance could not be used as a reliable marker for PVL-producing CA-MRSA in Italy because this isolate remained susceptible.

The report could not determine whether the strain was circulating locally or intermittently imported, and it could not establish how the patient acquired it. Those unresolved transmission questions are central limits of the epidemiologic interpretation.

Implementation and practice takeaways

  • The report links a severe cavitary pneumonia phenotype with a PVL-positive ST30 MRSA isolate in one previously healthy adult.
  • The molecular data are stronger than the therapeutic inference. Culture, SCCmec, PVL, MLST, and spa results directly characterize the isolate; the linezolid response remains uncontrolled.
  • The reported linezolid regimen should be preserved as historical source content only. Any present-day dosing or treatment decision requires current guidance and patient-specific review.
  • The fusidic-acid screening comment came from one isolate and should not be generalized into a modern diagnostic rule without current surveillance evidence.
