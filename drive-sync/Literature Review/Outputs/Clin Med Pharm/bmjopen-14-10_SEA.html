One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first for stewardship context; do not use as a stand-alone proof of equivalence or harm. The review directly addresses a common ICU stewardship question and consistently finds no statistically significant pooled benefit from antifungal treatment of respiratory Candida colonisation. The evidence base, however, is dominated by retrospective observational studies, several pooled outcomes show substantial heterogeneity, and confidence intervals are often wide.
Core thesisAcross nine included studies, antifungal therapy did not demonstrate improved mortality, ICU length of stay, mechanical-ventilation duration, or ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP).
Best interpretationThe data support absence of demonstrated clinical benefit in the studied population. They do not establish that benefit is impossible, that all antifungal regimens are equivalent, or that antifungal exposure itself causes worse outcomes.
Most useful applicationA stewardship argument against initiating antifungals solely because Candida is recovered from respiratory secretions in an immunocompetent mechanically ventilated adult when invasive candidiasis has been excluded.
Main uncertaintySeven of nine studies were retrospective cohorts; one was case-control and one randomized trial. Treatment regimens were heterogeneous and incompletely described, and several meta-analyses had I² >70%.

Methods

Information sources and study selection

The authors report following PRISMA criteria. Searches covered PubMed, Web of Science, Medline, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Data, and VIP Databases from inception through 25 July 2023, with no language restrictions. The search combined mechanical-ventilation/VAP terms with Candida/airway-colonisation terms, and reference lists of relevant studies were also reviewed.

Two researchers independently screened titles/abstracts and full text, with disagreements resolved with a third researcher. Eligible designs were randomized trials, cohort studies, and case-control studies. Case reports were excluded.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two reviewers independently extracted study characteristics, interventions, and clinical outcomes. For quality assessment, the authors used the Cochrane risk-assessment tool for randomized controlled trials and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for cohort and case-control studies. The paper states that studies scoring at least six points were considered high quality and included.

The nine included studies comprised seven retrospective cohorts, one case-control study, and one randomized clinical trial. Two were conducted in North America, five in Europe, and two in Asia. Detailed study characteristics and quality scores are located in online supplemental tables that are not present in the uploaded PDF.

Statistical analysis

Dichotomous outcomes were pooled as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals; ICU length of stay and mechanical-ventilation duration were pooled as standardized mean differences. The authors used inverse-variance meta-analysis, selecting a fixed-effect model when heterogeneity was not statistically significant at p>0.1 and a random-effects model when p<0.1. Heterogeneity was quantified using I².

Leave-one-study-out sensitivity analysis and treatment-type subgroup analyses were reported. Publication-bias testing was not performed because fewer than ten studies were included. The main manuscript does not report GRADE certainty ratings.

Method detail unavailable from the uploaded article. Supplemental Table 1 contains study-level sample sizes, population details, outcomes, colonisation definitions, and antifungal treatment details; Supplemental Table 2 contains quality-assessment scoring. The full PubMed search strategy is also in supplementary material. Those supplements were not included with the evaluated PDF, so aggregate sample size and several reproducibility checks cannot be independently reconstructed here.

Results

Study flow

The search identified 2,947 records. After 1,246 duplicates were removed, 1,701 records were screened; 1,670 were excluded at title/abstract review. Thirty-one reports underwent eligibility assessment and nine studies were included. Twenty-two were excluded for reasons including no antifungal treatment, special populations, lack of supporting experimental data, or lack of pulmonary secretion detection.

Mortality

No mortality endpoint showed a statistically significant pooled difference between antifungal-treated and untreated groups: ICU mortality OR 1.37 (95% CI 0.84–2.22), hospital mortality OR 1.17 (0.57–2.38), 28-day mortality OR 0.71 (0.45–1.14), and 90-day mortality OR 0.76 (0.35–1.63).

Interpretability differs by endpoint. Twenty-eight-day mortality had no observed heterogeneity (I² 0%), while hospital mortality had substantial heterogeneity (I² 74.4%) and 90-day mortality moderate heterogeneity (I² 54.8%). Study-level estimates in the forest plots sometimes point in opposite directions, emphasizing that the pooled null result does not imply consistent study-level effects.

ICU stay, ventilation duration, and VAP

ICU length of stay was not significantly different (SMD −0.15, 95% CI −0.88 to 0.59), but heterogeneity was extreme (I² 92.8%). Mechanical-ventilation duration was similarly null (SMD 0.11, −0.88 to 1.10) with I² 93.6%. VAP was not reduced by antifungal treatment (OR 1.54, 0.56–4.20), with I² 85.0%.

These high I² values materially weaken the usefulness of a single pooled mean effect for the secondary outcomes. The forest plots show sharply discordant study estimates—for example, VAP estimates range from OR 0.26 in Zhang 2017 to OR 23.01 in Ong 2013—suggesting substantial clinical, design, treatment, or population heterogeneity.

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

The abstract reports treatment-type subgroup estimates for VAP of OR 2.32 (95% CI 0.30–18.09) for inhaled therapy and OR 0.65 (0.13–3.34) for intravenous therapy. Both intervals are extremely wide and cross 1, so the subgroup analysis is not informative enough to establish meaningful route-specific differences.

The Results section reports that leave-one-study-out sensitivity analysis did not materially alter the pooled result, with pooled ORs ranging from 1.00 (0.51–1.96) to 2.07 (0.74–5.78). Because the corresponding supplemental figure is unavailable and the sentence does not explicitly restate the target outcome, this sensitivity result should be interpreted in the immediate context of the VAP/subgroup analysis rather than generalized to every endpoint.

Discussion & conclusion

Authors' interpretation

The authors conclude that antifungal therapy in mechanically ventilated patients with respiratory Candida colonisation does not improve clinical outcomes. They discuss respiratory Candida as a potential marker of disease severity or inflammation rather than a treatment target and note that prior studies have reported conflicting associations.

They further argue that antifungal exposure may have more harm than benefit through toxicity, resistance selection, or inadequate dosing, but the meta-analysis itself does not provide a pooled adverse-event analysis establishing net harm. This portion of the Discussion is mechanistic and interpretive rather than a direct quantitative result of the review.

Authors' limitations

The paper acknowledges that most included studies were retrospective, the number of studies was small for individual outcomes, antifungal drug/dose/administration details were inconsistent, and cohort studies dominated over randomized trials. The authors call for prospective randomized controlled evaluation.

Defensible bottom line from the source: In the defined population, antifungal treatment of respiratory Candida colonisation has not been shown to improve the measured clinical outcomes. The data are more suitable for discouraging routine treatment of colonisation alone than for proving that antifungal treatment can never help any selected patient.

Validity appraisal

DomainAppraisal
Question and eligibilityClinically focused. The review separates colonisation from candidemia/invasive candidiasis and excludes immunocompromised patients, which improves interpretability but narrows generalizability.
Search coverageBroad database coverage. Seven databases, no language restriction, and reference-list review are strengths. The full reproducible search strategy is not available in the uploaded main article.
Screening / extractionReasonable safeguards. Independent review with third-person resolution is reported.
Risk-of-bias assessmentPresent but incompletely inspectable. Cochrane and Newcastle–Ottawa tools were used, but detailed ratings are in an unavailable supplement. Labeling all included studies “high quality” does not eliminate confounding inherent to retrospective designs.
Evidence designMajor limitation. Seven retrospective cohorts, one case-control study, and one RCT were synthesized. Randomized and nonrandomized evidence therefore contribute to the same clinical conclusion despite very different susceptibility to bias.
Confounding by indicationHigh concern. Clinicians may preferentially treat more severely ill patients. The review itself cites an included study in which antifungal use was associated with hospital mortality after multivariable adjustment; residual confounding remains plausible in either direction.
Intervention heterogeneityHigh concern. Drug, route, dose, and administration method were not consistently reported, limiting treatment-specific inference and making “antifungal therapy” a broad exposure category.
Outcome heterogeneitySubstantial for several endpoints. I² was 74.4% for hospital mortality, 92.8% for ICU stay, 93.6% for ventilation duration, and 85.0% for VAP.
PrecisionLimited. Several confidence intervals are wide; route subgroups are especially imprecise (inhaled OR 2.32, 95% CI 0.30–18.09; IV OR 0.65, 0.13–3.34).
Publication biasNot assessed. The authors appropriately state that formal detection methods have low utility with fewer than ten studies; this still leaves publication bias unresolved.
Certainty frameworkNot reported in the main manuscript. No GRADE certainty-of-evidence assessment is presented.
Effect-estimate provenanceUnclear from the main text. The manuscript does not clearly state whether pooled study-level ORs were adjusted estimates, unadjusted estimates, or calculated from raw event counts for each observational study.
External validityMixed. Studies span North America, Europe, and Asia, but the population is restricted to immunocompetent mechanically ventilated adults with airway colonisation.

Practice translation

Use nowAs supportive evidence for an antifungal-stewardship rule that a positive respiratory Candida culture, by itself, is not evidence that antifungal therapy improves outcomes in immunocompetent mechanically ventilated adults.
Do not use forSuspected/proven invasive candidiasis, candidemia, immunocompromised patients, pregnancy, or situations in which antifungal therapy is indicated for another source.
Decision-support implicationIf converted into CDS, the source supports a “colonisation ≠ treatment indication” warning only after explicit exclusion checks for invasive disease and host immunocompromise. It does not support automatic antifungal discontinuation without clinical review.
Stewardship framingThe strongest statement is “no clinical benefit has been demonstrated,” rather than “antifungals are harmful” or “treatment is never indicated.”

What this review should change

  • Increase skepticism toward antifungal initiation based solely on respiratory Candida isolation in the defined population.
  • Direct attention toward confirming whether the patient has colonisation versus invasive candidiasis before treating.
  • Encourage documentation of the actual antifungal indication when respiratory cultures are positive.

What it should not change by itself

  • Management of candidemia or tissue-invasive candidiasis.
  • Decisions in immunocompromised hosts.
  • Choice, dose, route, or duration of antifungal therapy when a true treatment indication exists.
  • Local guideline or stewardship policy without checking current recommendations and local epidemiology.
