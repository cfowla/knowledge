Scope warning: This study characterizes cefazolin pharmacokinetics and modeled target attainment in a very small, noninfected SDHD cohort. It does not establish clinical efficacy, steady-state exposure, or outcomes in patients with serious infection.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In 10 noninfected adults receiving 2-hour short daily hemodialysis (6 days/week) with high-flux F80A dialyzers, cefazolin was substantially removed during dialysis but accumulated sufficiently between daily treatments that 1 g IV after SDHD—or modeled during the last 20 minutes of dialysis—maintained total plasma concentrations above the study's 40 mg/L pharmacodynamic target for more than 90% of the dosing interval after repeated dosing.

Load-bearing results. Median intradialysis clearance was 1.95 L/h (IQR 1.66-2.45) versus interdialysis clearance 0.16 L/h (0.11-0.21). Median intradialysis half-life was 2.3 h (1.7-2.7), while Table 2 reports interdialysis half-life 28.1 h (23.5-59.3); the abstract reports 28.2 h with the same IQR. Median dialysis removal was 41% (35%-53%). After one dose, 9/10 participants remained above 40 mg/L before the next SDHD treatment; after a second dose, all participants remained above target during the off-dialysis interval. No cefazolin-related adverse effects were reported.

Why it matters. The paper provides rare direct PK data for cefazolin in SDHD and a practical dosing model. Its major constraint is that the dosing conclusion rests on total-concentration surrogate target attainment in 10 noninfected participants, with no steady-state sampling, only five analyzable dialysate sample sets, and substantial RRF/BMI heterogeneity.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame SDHD as a growing dialysis modality typically delivered for about 2 hours on 6 days each week. Infection remains a major problem in dialysis populations, often involving Gram-positive organisms, while cefazolin dosing in conventional hemodialysis was better described than in SDHD. The study therefore targeted the gap in cefazolin disposition and dosing under the more frequent SDHD schedule.

The stated primary objective was cefazolin clearance during SDHD. Secondary objectives were broader PK characterization and assessment of whether current dosing recommendations would maintain serum concentrations thought adequate for efficacy while avoiding excessive exposure.

Methods

This prospective, open-label PK study enrolled adults receiving regular SDHD who were noninfected. SDHD used high-flux F80A dialyzers, blood flow 350 mL/min, and dialysate flow 800 mL/min. Participants received cefazolin 1 g IV over 20 minutes immediately after SDHD on two consecutive study days. Six blood samples and two dialysate samples were scheduled around the second SDHD session; residual renal function was separately estimated in participants with urine output.

Samples were analyzed by HPLC (reported assay variance 4%). A one-compartment IV-infusion model with first-order elimination was used. Separate intra- and interdialysis elimination rate constants, half-lives, and clearances were estimated. An "in-practice" model simulated giving cefazolin during the final 20 minutes of dialysis. The authors used an 8 mg/L free-drug breakpoint and assumed 80% protein binding to define a 40 mg/L total-concentration target. Safety events were followed through 2 weeks after the final dose.

Results

Ten participants completed the study. Median age was 69 years (IQR 61-74), weight 75 kg (63-86), and BMI 27 kg/m² (23-30). Median intradialysis clearance was 1.95 L/h (1.66-2.45), with half-life 2.3 h (1.7-2.7); median interdialysis clearance was 0.16 L/h (0.11-0.21), with half-life 28.1 h (23.5-59.3) in Table 2. Median cefazolin removal during the 2-hour SDHD treatment was 41% (35%-53%). Five of ten dialysate sample sets were lost; in the remaining five, median cefazolin recovery in dialysate was 236 mg (196-375), equivalent to 52% (40%-56%) of the amount estimated removed from serum.

Observed and modeled profiles were similar. After one dose, median concentration fell from 96 mg/L at SDHD initiation to 53 mg/L at treatment end, and 9/10 participants remained above 40 mg/L before the next SDHD session. After the second dose, all remained above 40 mg/L during the off-dialysis interval; observed and modeled concentrations exceeded the 40 mg/L target for more than 90% of the dosing interval. No related adverse effects were reported.

Discussion

The authors interpret intradialysis clearance as comparable to prior high-flux conventional hemodialysis data despite shorter individual sessions. They argue that frequent 1 g dosing after each SDHD session provides the same weekly cumulative cefazolin dose as 2 g after each thrice-weekly conventional session and may be a useful extrapolation strategy. Modeled administration during the last 20 minutes of dialysis produced similar off-dialysis target attainment.

Residual renal function had little apparent effect on intradialysis PK but increased interdialysis clearance and shortened interdialysis half-life. The RRF >1 mL/min subgroup had median interdialysis clearance 0.22 L/h versus 0.13 L/h in the <1 mL/min subgroup, with median half-lives 25.0 versus 49.3 h. The authors also flag uncertainty in obesity and emphasize patient-specific factors such as weight, RRF, and infection type.

Limitations

The authors explicitly identify small sample size, loss of half the dialysate sample sets, lack of steady-state analysis, use of total rather than unbound concentrations, short sampling intervals, and substantial between-patient variation in RRF and BMI. They also note that participants were noninfected, so dose requirements in serious infection may differ.

These limitations are directly load-bearing for practice translation: the study supports a PK plausibility argument rather than clinical effectiveness, does not measure the pharmacologically active unbound exposure used to construct the target, and has limited power to define dosing for extremes of body size or renal function.

Conclusion and relevance

The authors conclude that cefazolin clearance during a 2-hour SDHD session using high-flux dialyzers was similar to clearance during 4-hour conventional hemodialysis. They state that cefazolin 1 g administered during the last 20 minutes of SDHD or immediately after treatment appears adequate to maintain six times the Staphylococcus MIC for more than 90% of the dosing interval.

This conclusion should be read as a PK/PD target-attainment inference within the studied dialysis prescription and cohort, not as evidence of treatment success in infected patients.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High-value niche PK evidence for cefazolin dosing in SDHD, useful for dose rationale and protocol design, but insufficient alone to establish clinical efficacy or universally applicable dosing.

Relevance: 9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses cefazolin dosing under an uncommon but clinically important dialysis schedule.

Evidence basis: Prospective SDHD PK data, drug removal, RRF subgrouping, and target-attainment modeling.

Principal limit: Narrow modality and small cohort.

Would raise/lower: Higher if the decision specifically concerns 2-hour, 6-day/week high-flux SDHD; lower for other dialysis prescriptions.

Novelty: 8/10

Rationale: The study fills a sparse evidence gap by directly estimating cefazolin PK in SDHD.

Evidence basis: Authors report first SDHD intradialysis elimination-rate and half-life estimates for cefazolin.

Principal limit: Single-center, small-scale confirmation only.

Would raise/lower: External replication or modern population-PK work would strengthen novelty and durability.

Method strength: 6/10

Rationale: The sampling scheme and PK calculations are explicit, but the design is small, open-label, and analytically simple.

Evidence basis: Timed blood/dialysate sampling, HPLC assay, separate intra/interdialysis parameters, prespecified modeling.

Principal limit: n=10, five lost dialysate sets, sparse sampling, one-compartment model, no population-PK model.

Would raise/lower: Denser sampling, full dialysate recovery, population-PK analysis, and prospective testing of the in-practice regimen would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 6/10

Rationale: Results support PK target attainment but not patient-centered efficacy.

Evidence basis: Measured clearance/removal and concentration profiles; 9/10 target attainment after one dose and 10/10 off-dialysis after two doses.

Principal limit: Surrogate total-concentration target, no infected cohort, no steady-state clinical outcomes.

Would raise/lower: Measured unbound exposure, steady-state data, infected patients, and clinical/microbiologic outcomes would raise the score.

External validity: 4/10

Rationale: Generalizability is limited by the cohort and dialysis prescription.

Evidence basis: Adults at one Toronto center, high-flux F80A, 2-hour SDHD, blood flow 350 mL/min, dialysate flow 800 mL/min.

Principal limit: Small noninfected cohort with RRF and BMI heterogeneity; no other dialyzer schedules or severe infection.

Would raise/lower: Multicenter validation across dialyzers, schedules, body sizes, RRF strata, and infection severity would raise the score.

Implementation value: 7/10

Rationale: The paper offers a concrete, operationally simple regimen and timing model.

Evidence basis: 1 g IV after SDHD or modeled during final 20 minutes, with target-attainment context.

Principal limit: The regimen remains a PK extrapolation rather than outcome-validated dosing guidance.

Would raise/lower: Prospective validation of clinical efficacy/safety and steady-state exposure would justify stronger implementation confidence.

Main limitations: small sample; noninfected participants; five lost dialysate sample sets; no steady-state analysis; total rather than unbound concentrations; short sampling window; simple one-compartment modeling; substantial RRF/BMI variation; modeled rather than directly tested intradialysis administration.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Potential use

Support a pharmacokinetic rationale for cefazolin 1 g IV after each 2-hour SDHD treatment, or during the final 20 minutes, when the dialysis prescription resembles the study.

Do not overclaim

Do not treat the study as proof of clinical cure, as direct evidence in serious infection, or as validation across all SDHD prescriptions, dialyzers, body sizes, or residual renal function.

Monitoring implications

Weight, residual renal function, dialysis prescription, infection severity, and the distinction between total and free cefazolin exposure are the main variables the source itself identifies as limiting or potentially dose-modifying.

Source-based bottom line: Use as moderate-strength PK support for a 1 g post-SDHD strategy in a closely matched setting; require additional clinical judgment and evidence before extending the regimen to severe infection, extreme body size, materially different dialysis conditions, or decisions requiring outcome-validated dosing.
