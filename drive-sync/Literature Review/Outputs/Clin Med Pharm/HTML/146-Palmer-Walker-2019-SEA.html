One-page synthesis

Core finding: During 2-hour high-flux SDHD, median cefazolin intradialysis clearance was 1.95 L/h (IQR 1.66-2.45) and median drug removal was 41% (IQR 35%-53%). A 1-g IV dose after each SDHD session, and a model simulating infusion during the final 20 minutes of dialysis, generally maintained total plasma cefazolin above the study's 40 mg/L pharmacodynamic threshold outside dialysis and for more than 90% of the dosing interval after repeat dosing.

The study fills a narrow but clinically relevant evidence gap: cefazolin PK had been described in conventional and nocturnal hemodialysis but not in short daily hemodialysis. Ten adults on stable SDHD received two cefazolin doses. Concentration-time data were fit with a one-compartment, first-order model, and the investigators separately estimated intradialysis and interdialysis elimination, clearance, volume of distribution, dialysis removal, and an in-practice regimen with dosing during the last 20 minutes of SDHD.

The signal is internally coherent but limited. Median interdialysis clearance was 0.16 L/h (IQR 0.11-0.21), interdialysis half-life 28.1 hours (IQR 23.5-59.3), and volume of distribution 7.00 L (IQR 6.49-7.84). Nine of 10 participants were above 40 mg/L before the next SDHD session after one dose, and all were above the threshold during the off-dialysis interval after the second dose. Residual renal function increased interdialysis clearance and shortened interdialysis half-life, while intradialysis clearance was similar across RRF groups. Five dialysate sample sets were lost, participants were noninfected, unbound concentrations were not measured, and the regimen was not studied at steady state.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame SDHD as a growing dialysis modality with sparse antibiotic dosing data. Cefazolin is clinically important for Gram-positive infections in hemodialysis patients, but published dosing information largely came from conventional hemodialysis.

The primary objective was cefazolin clearance during SDHD; secondary objectives were broader PK characterization and assessment of whether existing dosing concepts produced concentrations considered pharmacodynamically adequate.

Methods

This prospective open-label study enrolled adults on stable 2-hour SDHD six days/week. Participants were noninfected. Cefazolin 1 g IV was infused over 20 minutes immediately after dialysis on study days 1 and 2. SDHD used F80A high-flux dialyzers with blood flow 350 mL/min and dialysate flow 800 mL/min. Six blood and two dialysate samples were scheduled around day-2 dialysis and dosing, with a final predialysis blood sample on day 3.

HPLC measured cefazolin concentrations. The study used a one-compartment, first-order model to estimate on- and off-dialysis elimination and clearance, calculated dialysis drug removal from plasma and dialysate, and modeled an in-practice regimen administering cefazolin during the final 20 minutes of SDHD. A 40 mg/L total-plasma concentration threshold was derived from an 8 mg/L free-drug breakpoint and assumed 80% protein binding. The methods text contains an apparent sign inconsistency around this threshold, while the results, figures, discussion, and conclusion consistently treat 40 mg/L as the minimum target.

Results

All 10 participants completed the study. Median age was 69 years (IQR 61-74), median weight 75 kg (IQR 63-86), and median RRF 0.6 mL/min (IQR 0-5.8). Median intradialysis clearance was 1.95 L/h (IQR 1.66-2.45), intradialysis half-life 2.3 hours (IQR 1.7-2.7), interdialysis clearance 0.16 L/h (IQR 0.11-0.21), interdialysis half-life 28.1 hours (IQR 23.5-59.3), and volume of distribution 7.00 L (IQR 6.49-7.84).

After one dose, median concentration fell from 96 to 53 mg/L over the SDHD session. Median dialysis removal was 41% and the calculated amount removed from plasma was 387 mg. Five of 10 dialysate sample sets were lost; in the remaining five, median dialysate recovery was 236 mg and represented a median 52% of serum drug removal. Nine of 10 participants remained above 40 mg/L before the next session after one dose; after two doses all participants were above 40 mg/L off dialysis. No cefazolin-related adverse effects were reported.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpreted SDHD cefazolin clearance as similar to prior conventional high-flux dialysis estimates despite the shorter session. Residual renal function mainly affected interdialysis PK: median interdialysis clearance was 0.22 L/h with RRF >1 mL/min versus 0.13 L/h with RRF <1 mL/min, while intradialysis clearance was 1.95 L/h in both groups. The observed and modeled regimens generally preserved the study target between sessions.

The paper proposes cefazolin 1 g IV after each SDHD session, or during the last 20 minutes, as a plausible regimen. This is a PK/PD inference rather than a clinical-outcome finding. The authors explicitly note uncertainty in morbid obesity, patients with serious infection, variable RRF, and other patient-specific factors.

Limitations

Major limitations were n=10, five lost dialysate sets, no steady-state PK sampling, total rather than unbound drug measurement, short sampling intervals, and a simple individual one-compartment modeling approach without population PK modeling. The study included noninfected participants, so the relationship between the surrogate concentration target and clinical success was not tested.

Generalizability is further constrained by a single center, stable SDHD conditions, interparticipant variation in RRF and BMI, and sparse representation of morbid obesity. These issues are especially important if applying the regimen to severe MSSA infection, where pathogen MIC, source control, altered volume of distribution, and critical illness could materially change exposure requirements.

Validity

Internal PK estimation is reasonably transparent: regimen, sampling, assay, equations, and model are described. However, the sample is very small, half of dialysate sets were lost, uncertainty is summarized primarily with IQRs rather than model-based precision, and the model assumes stable distribution volume and first-order one-compartment behavior. The study does not provide an external validation cohort.

Practice translation

The strongest defensible translation is that 1 g after each 2-hour SDHD session is pharmacokinetically plausible under the studied conditions. It should not be interpreted as definitive therapy for severe MSSA infection, morbid obesity, substantially different dialyzer settings, or unusual RRF without patient-specific assessment. The study's total concentration target also depends on assumed protein binding rather than measured free cefazolin.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
Use as PK support when designing or reviewing cefazolin dosing for patients on 2-hour high-flux SDHD with similar flow conditions.
Use conditionally
1 g IV after SDHD or during the last 20 minutes is a reasonable starting hypothesis, with attention to infection severity, body size, RRF, organism MIC, and local clinical context.
Do not overclaim
The study does not establish clinical cure, bacteremia outcomes, mortality benefit, optimal dosing in severe infection, or universally adequate exposure across all SDHD prescriptions.
