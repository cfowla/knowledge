One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. High relevance for direct dapagliflozin-versus-empagliflozin comparison and formulary evidence synthesis, but it should not be used alone to prefer one agent clinically.

Using Korean NHIS and health-screening data, investigators identified new SGLT2-inhibitor users and applied an active-comparator, new-user design with 1:1 nearest-neighbor propensity-score matching. The final cohort included 145,504 people, approximately 42% women, with a broad cardiovascular-risk spectrum; roughly 40% had T2DM as their only cardiovascular risk factor. Baseline characteristics were well balanced after matching, with all absolute standardized differences below 5%.

The primary composite cardiovascular outcome did not differ significantly between drugs (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.855–1.006; p=0.07). Dapagliflozin was associated with lower HF-related events (HR 0.84) and cardiovascular death (HR 0.76), while ischemic stroke and MI were similar. Safety outcomes were similar. The female subgroup had a lower primary-outcome HR with dapagliflozin and a significant sex interaction, whereas low-risk patients showed no differential effect. As-treated sensitivity analyses broadly supported the main pattern. These secondary/subgroup findings remain association signals because the study is nonrandomized.

Section condensation

Background / objective

The paper frames potential within-class heterogeneity in cardiovascular outcomes as unresolved because dapagliflozin and empagliflozin CVOTs enrolled different populations and underrepresented Asians, women, and lower-risk patients. Its objective was a direct real-world comparison between new users of the two drugs in Korea.

Methods

NHIS claims, screening data, and national death records were linked. Adults with T2DM initiating the two drugs from May 2016 through December 2018 were eligible; patients younger than 20, using another SGLT2 inhibitor, with ESRD, experiencing a study event within 28 days of initiation, or with missing variables were excluded. Propensity scores used index year plus 48 clinical covariates, followed by 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching without replacement.

The primary composite combined HF hospitalization/HF death, ischemic stroke, MI, and CV death. Individual components were secondary outcomes; ESRD, DKA, hypoglycemia, and genitourinary infection were safety outcomes. Incidence rates were per 1,000 person-years; Kaplan–Meier/log-rank and Cox models were used. Sensitivity analyses examined subgroups, 28-day-gap as-treated exposure, and non-CV mortality.

Results

Prematch groups contained 118,064 dapagliflozin and 73,110 empagliflozin users. Matching produced 72,752 per group with close balance. During median 2.08-year follow-up, 2,444 primary events occurred (1,172 dapagliflozin; 1,272 empagliflozin).

The primary composite was nonsignificant. HF-related events and CV death favored dapagliflozin, while stroke and MI were neutral. Safety outcomes were neutral. Female users had a lower primary HR with a significant sex interaction; cardiovascular-risk strata had no significant interaction. As-treated analyses preserved the overall neutral primary endpoint with directionally similar HF/CV-death results.

Discussion / conclusion

The authors interpret the results as evidence of broadly similar composite cardiovascular effectiveness but possible lower HF-related and CV-death risk with dapagliflozin. Their mechanistic discussion is hypothesis-generating and should not be treated as demonstrated mediation. They explicitly acknowledge residual confounding, Korean-population generalizability limits, absent dose information, and uncertain adherence.

Evaluation & appraisal

9/10

Relevance

Rationale: Direct head-to-head comparative-effectiveness evidence for dapagliflozin versus empagliflozin.

Evidence basis: Nationwide matched cohort with cardiovascular and safety outcomes.

Limiting factor: Not randomized; should not alone determine formulary preference.

Would raise confidence: A randomized active-comparator trial or concordant multi-country causal evidence.

7/10

Novelty

Rationale: Addresses within-class differences in a large Asian real-world cohort with many lower-risk patients.

Evidence basis: Direct drug-to-drug new-user comparison and underrepresented subgroups.

Limiting factor: Observational head-to-head comparisons already existed.

Would raise confidence: Prospective randomized comparative evidence or mechanistic validation.

7/10

Method strength

Rationale: Strong observational design features reduce, but do not eliminate, confounding.

Evidence basis: Active-comparator new-user design, 48-covariate PS match, balance checks, time-to-event analyses, sensitivity analyses.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding, unmeasured drug use, no dose data, imperfect adherence ascertainment.

Would raise confidence: Target-trial emulation with richer confounder capture or randomization.

6/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: Large sample and internally consistent sensitivity results support association, not causal superiority.

Evidence basis: 145,504 matched participants; outcome-specific HRs and CIs; safety and subgroup analyses.

Limiting factor: Primary composite was nonsignificant and secondary findings may reflect multiplicity/residual confounding.

Would raise confidence: Replication with prespecified multiplicity control and randomized direct comparison.

6/10

External validity

Rationale: Nationwide Korean coverage is broad within that health system.

Evidence basis: Whole-population insurance database, broad CV-risk spectrum, substantial female representation.

Limiting factor: Predominantly Korean population and claims-based phenotype definitions.

Would raise confidence: Replication across diverse countries and healthcare systems.

7/10

Implementation value

Rationale: Useful for formulary evidence synthesis and counseling about likely class similarity, with a hypothesis of HF/CV-death differences.

Evidence basis: Direct comparator estimates plus safety and sensitivity data.

Limiting factor: Cannot establish one agent as clinically superior and lacks dosing/adherence detail.

Would raise confidence: Concordance with randomized, guideline, and pharmacologic evidence.

Validity

Strengths: nationwide data coverage; active comparator and new-user framing; large matched sample; rich measured-covariate adjustment; explicit balance diagnostics; time-to-event methods; multiple sensitivity analyses; broad CV-risk range.

Weaknesses: residual and unmeasured confounding; no aspirin/ARNI data; no SGLT2 dose data; uncertain adherence; claims-based endpoints; predominantly Korean population; no randomized head-to-head assignment. The primary endpoint was not significant, so significant secondary/subgroup signals require more caution than a simple superiority narrative suggests.

Practice translation

This study supports the view that major composite cardiovascular effectiveness is broadly similar between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin in a large real-world T2DM population. The HF-related and CV-death estimates are clinically interesting but should be integrated with randomized class evidence, guideline recommendations, drug-specific indications, renal function, formulary constraints, and patient factors rather than used as proof that dapagliflozin is superior.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Use as direct comparative real-world evidence in an SGLT2 formulary or evidence review.

Do not overclaim

Do not convert the HF/CV-death associations into causal superiority or therapeutic interchange rules by themselves.

Monitor

Look for replication of direct head-to-head outcomes, especially sex-specific and HF-history signals.

Best contextual use

Pair with CVOTs, HF/CKD trials, meta-analyses, guidelines, labeling, and local acquisition/cost considerations.
