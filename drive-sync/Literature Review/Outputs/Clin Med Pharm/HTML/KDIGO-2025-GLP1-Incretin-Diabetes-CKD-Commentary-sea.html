FLOW provides the strongest direct kidney-outcome evidence in T2D and CKD, while SELECT and SMART support the authors’ contention that cardiorenal effects cannot be explained solely by glucose lowering because benefits were observed in populations without diabetes. SURPASS-4 is a post hoc kidney analysis, so its inferential status differs from FLOW’s prespecified kidney-outcome trial.

3. Interpretation and proposed direction

The commentary keeps the 2022 KDIGO recommendation intact: long-acting GLP-1 RA therapy with documented cardiovascular benefit remains appropriate for T2D and CKD when individualized glycemic targets are not achieved despite SGLT2 inhibitor therapy, with or without metformin, or when those therapies cannot be used. The authors argue that obesity, albuminuria, and established cardiovascular disease may also identify patients with high absolute benefit.

The article does not convert these ideas into a new formal KDIGO recommendation. Universal incretin treatment for all T2D and CKD is discussed as an alternative future approach but is tempered by potent glucose/weight effects and cost. Use in CKD without T2D remains investigational enough that the authors call for additional trials. People with T1D remain a major evidence gap.

Strengths. Focused update on decisive new incretin trials, explicit separation between the existing guideline recommendation and possible future expansion, and a concise quantitative table. Limitations. It is not a systematic review or guideline update; mechanistic explanations remain uncertain; SURPASS-4 kidney findings are post hoc; and T1D/non-diabetic CKD evidence remains incomplete.
