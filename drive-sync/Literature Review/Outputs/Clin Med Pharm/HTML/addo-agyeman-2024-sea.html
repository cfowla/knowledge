Scope warning: This is a secondary synthesis of dapagliflozin versus placebo/standard therapy. It does not establish direct comparative equivalence between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin, and several internal reporting inconsistencies limit decision-grade use.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The review is useful for quickly locating dapagliflozin evidence on KCCQ, 6MWD, heart-failure hospitalization, mortality, and adverse events, but it should not be a cornerstone source when cleaner primary trials or higher-quality syntheses are available.

The review pooled randomized evidence spanning HFrEF and HFpEF. The strongest displayed clinical result was reduced heart-failure hospitalization (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.68–0.84). All-cause mortality was modestly lower overall (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.83–0.99), driven by the HFrEF subgroup (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.72–0.97); HFpEF mortality was not significant (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.84–1.06). KCCQ score improved by a pooled mean difference of 2.75 points (95% CI 1.95–3.56), while 6MWD did not significantly improve (MD 3.59 m, 95% CI -1.44 to 8.63).

Any adverse events were similar overall (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.93–1.03). The scientific signal is broadly compatible with dapagliflozin's established heart-failure benefits, but the article contains nontrivial reporting defects: continuous outcomes are repeatedly mislabeled as risk ratios in prose although the forest plots use mean differences; the risk-of-bias study count is internally inconsistent; Table 2 contains a Wiviott 2019/DECLARE row not represented among the 10 included-study rows or outcome forests; the abstract adverse-event estimate/CI are inconsistent with the main result; and discussion/conclusion language occasionally overstates nonsignificant subgroup or adverse-event findings.

Methods and development process

Clinical question / PICO

Population: adults with heart failure across reduced and preserved ejection fraction. Intervention: dapagliflozin plus standard therapy. Comparator: placebo or standard therapy. Outcomes: change in 6MWD, KCCQ, all-cause mortality, HF hospitalization, and any adverse event.

Search and selection

PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched through January 2024 using dapagliflozin/Farxiga terms combined with HF/HFrEF/HFpEF terms. Duplicates were removed in EndNote. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts, with a third author resolving conflicts; Rayyan supported screening.

The search is only partially reproducible from the article: it gives a primary Boolean expression but not full database-specific strategies, exact run dates, registry/grey-literature methods, or a reported protocol/PROSPERO registration.

Bias and synthesis

RoB 2 was used for trial-level risk of bias. RevMan 5.3 performed pooling. The methods specify risk ratios for dichotomous outcomes and mean differences for continuous outcomes. Heterogeneity was assessed with chi-square and I²; leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was planned for heterogeneity.

The forest plots use fixed-effect models, but the Methods do not provide a clear rationale for fixed-effect pooling across clinically diverse HF populations, settings, and outcome instruments. The article does not report a GRADE/certainty assessment or a publication-bias/small-study-effects analysis.

Table 1 — Summary of included studies

Table 1 spans 10 study rows: Ibrahim 2020; McMurray 2019; McMurray 2021; McMurray 2023; Nassif 2019; Nassif 2021; Palau 2022; Singh 2020; Solomon 2022; and Borlaug 2023. It details setting, follow-up, eligibility, and each source trial's main findings. The evidence spans acute decompensated HFrEF, chronic HFrEF, HFpEF, and a CKD trial's prespecified HF analysis.

Table 2 — Study population characteristics

Table 2 reports arm-level demographics, comorbidities, hemodynamics, ejection fraction, renal function, NT-proBNP, NYHA class, dapagliflozin regimen, background therapy, and follow-up. Missing baseline fields are common across trials. Most HF intervention rows use dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily.

Table 2 contamination / accounting concern: it additionally includes Wiviott 2019 (DECLARE-TIMI 58) rows, although Wiviott is absent from Table 1, Figure 2, and the outcome forest plots. Those rows contain more than 17,000 randomized participants and therefore cannot be part of the review's reported total N=12,695. This is a material source-consistency defect.

Evaluation and appraisal

8.5/10

Relevance

Rationale: Directly addresses dapagliflozin in HFrEF/HFpEF, including symptoms, functional capacity, hospitalization, mortality, and adverse events.

Evidence basis: Large HF trial evidence is synthesized across clinically important outcomes.

Principal limit: It does not directly compare dapagliflozin with empagliflozin or other SGLT2 inhibitors.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with direct comparative evidence or a clearly specified formulary-equivalence question.

6/10

Novelty

Rationale: Adds an updated synthesis focused on 6MWD and KCCQ alongside major clinical outcomes.

Evidence basis: Includes DETERMINE and several functional-capacity trials beyond the pivotal outcome trials.

Principal limit: Core hospitalization and mortality effects were already established by large RCTs and earlier meta-analyses.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with prespecified novel analyses, robust comparative-effect modification, or new patient-level data.

4/10

Method strength

Rationale: Standard review components are present, but multiple internal reporting inconsistencies and incomplete meta-analytic safeguards materially limit confidence.

Evidence basis: Four databases, dual screening, RoB 2, prespecified outcomes, RevMan, heterogeneity testing, and sensitivity analysis are described.

Principal limit: No protocol/registration, no publication-bias assessment or certainty grading reported, fixed-effect model is not justified, and study/effect-measure reporting is inconsistent.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with a registered protocol, fully reproducible searches, justified model choice, publication-bias/certainty assessment, and corrected study accounting.

5/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: Large pivotal trials support hospitalization and mortality estimates, but symptom/functional outcomes use smaller subsets and the review has reporting defects.

Evidence basis: HF hospitalization RR 0.76 and all-cause mortality RR 0.90 have narrow CIs and low statistical heterogeneity in the displayed fixed-effect analyses.

Principal limit: 6MWD and KCCQ use fewer participants and heterogeneous instruments/domains; several claims in text conflict with forest plots.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with clean effect-measure reporting, sensitivity analyses shown, certainty assessment, and a more rigorous synthesis model.

6.5/10

External validity

Rationale: The included evidence spans HFrEF and HFpEF and multiple clinical settings, but outcome-specific populations differ substantially.

Evidence basis: Trials range from chronic ambulatory HF to decompensated HF and preserved EF populations.

Principal limit: Some evidence comes from preplanned HF subgroups of non-HF parent trials; HFpEF functional analyses are relatively small.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with more large HFpEF functional-outcome trials and clearer population-level synthesis.

5.5/10

Implementation value

Rationale: Useful as a secondary orientation to dapagliflozin symptom and HF-outcome evidence, but not suitable as the sole practice or formulary source.

Evidence basis: Pooled outcomes are easy to map to hospitalization, mortality, KCCQ, 6MWD, and adverse-event questions.

Principal limit: No direct SGLT2-to-SGLT2 comparison, no dosing/implementation synthesis beyond trial regimens, and methodological inconsistencies weaken decision-grade use.

Would raise/lower: Would rise when paired with pivotal RCTs, current guidelines, and direct comparative effectiveness evidence.

Key strengths

  • Clinically relevant outcome set spanning symptoms/function and hard HF outcomes.
  • Dual-reviewer screening and RoB 2 assessment are described.
  • Large pivotal dapagliflozin HF trials contribute to hospitalization and mortality estimates.
  • Forest plots provide transparent study-level weights, denominators, confidence intervals, and subgroup estimates.

Major limitations / red flags

  • Continuous outcomes are repeatedly labeled as risk ratios in the Abstract/Results even though the method and forest plots use mean difference.
  • Study accounting is inconsistent across narrative, Figure 2, and Table 2.
  • The Abstract adverse-event estimate conflicts with the main analysis; discussion claims a significant adverse-event reduction despite a nonsignificant pooled result.
  • Conclusion wording overstates consistency of mortality benefit across HF phenotypes.
  • No reported protocol registration, GRADE/certainty assessment, or publication-bias assessment.
  • Fixed-effect pooling is displayed without a clear methodological justification for clinically diverse trials.
  • KCCQ pooling combines different KCCQ domains/variants; interpretation as one homogeneous continuous outcome is not fully justified.
Practice-use ceiling: Do not use this meta-analysis alone to justify SGLT2 class interchangeability, dapagliflozin-vs-empagliflozin comparative effectiveness, or a safety-equivalence claim.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use it for
Fast orientation to the dapagliflozin HF evidence landscape; locating functional/symptom trials; a secondary pooled estimate for HF hospitalization and mortality.
Do not use it for
Direct dapagliflozin-vs-empagliflozin comparisons, definitive class-effect claims, formulary equivalence, or detailed adverse-event incidence by type.
Read next
The pivotal primary trials behind DAPA-HF and DELIVER, DETERMINE/DEFINE-HF/PRESERVED-HF for functional outcomes, and a higher-quality contemporary systematic review with registered methods and certainty grading.
Clinical bottom line
The pooled direction supports dapagliflozin reducing HF hospitalization and improving patient-reported status, but source-level inconsistencies require verification against the primary trials before decision use.
