One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The 2024 update broadens first-line treatment of active proliferative lupus nephritis from conventional glucocorticoid plus mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide regimens to include belimumab-based and calcineurin-inhibitor-based triple therapy. It pairs this expansion with lower glucocorticoid exposure, explicit maintenance duration, response algorithms, and safety measures.

The guideline is a treatment-focused update of the 2021 lupus nephritis chapter. The evidence base is strongest for short- to intermediate-term kidney response. Voclosporin plus mycophenolate and rapidly tapered glucocorticoids increased complete remission in the supplement with high-certainty evidence. Belimumab improved complete kidney response with moderate-certainty evidence. CNI-included triple therapy also improved complete remission, but the pooled estimate carried more indirectness and trial-population constraints.

Hard outcomes remain less settled. Mortality, kidney failure, and long-term GFR loss are sparse or imprecise for several newer regimens. The guideline itself notes that the proteinuria benefit from calcineurin inhibitors may partly reflect direct podocyte effects and that the effect on long-term kidney survival is unclear. Trials often excluded advanced kidney dysfunction, which narrows confidence in patients with severe CKD.

The operational guidance is unusually usable. Figures specify biopsy triggers, complication prevention, drug selection, doses, steroid tapers, maintenance options, response definitions, rescue steps, and the TMA differential. The tradeoff is that 26 of 29 formal statements are ungraded Practice Points. Many bedside decisions therefore depend on Work Group judgment rather than a graded recommendation tied to a dedicated systematic review.

Clinical condensation

Diagnosis and pathology

Patients with SLE should undergo active kidney surveillance with creatinine and eGFR, urinalysis with sediment, spot protein-creatinine ratio, anti-dsDNA, and complement. The Figure 1 pathway uses 24-hour proteinuria of at least 500 mg/day as a biopsy trigger after abnormal proteinuria is confirmed. The narrative warns that severe active nephritis can still present with less impressive proteinuria, so trends, sediment, serology, and kidney function matter. Biopsy is used to classify LN and separate potentially reversible activity from chronic injury.

General treatment and risk reduction

Hydroxychloroquine is recommended unless contraindicated. The guideline also treats infection prevention, cardiovascular risk, renoprotection, bone health, fertility preservation, contraception, pregnancy planning, ultraviolet exposure, and malignancy risk as part of LN treatment rather than optional add-ons. It specifically warns against overimmunosuppression and calls for vaccination and Pneumocystis prophylaxis to be considered in context.

Class I and II disease

Low-level proteinuria with Class I or II histology generally does not require kidney-specific immunosuppression beyond therapy for extrarenal SLE. Nephrotic syndrome changes the problem. The guideline asks clinicians to evaluate for lupus podocytopathy, use electron microscopy when available, and treat the podocytopathy along minimal-change-disease lines.

Active Class III or IV disease

The central 1B recommendation is glucocorticoids plus a mycophenolic acid analog, low-dose intravenous cyclophosphamide, belimumab plus mycophenolate or low-dose cyclophosphamide, or mycophenolate plus a calcineurin inhibitor. Figure 5 makes the treatment menu concrete. It lists methylprednisolone 0.25 to 0.50 g/day for 1 to 3 days when appropriate, followed by oral prednisone with tapering; MMF 1.0 to 1.5 g twice daily; intravenous cyclophosphamide 500 mg every 2 weeks for 6 doses or 0.5 to 1.0 g/m2 monthly for 6 doses; belimumab 10 mg/kg intravenously every 2 weeks for 3 doses then every 4 weeks; and voclosporin 23.7 mg twice daily in trial-like patients with eGFR above 45 mL/min/1.73 m2.

Choice is individualized. Mycophenolate is favored when fertility toxicity from cyclophosphamide matters. CNI-containing regimens are attractive when nephrotic-range proteinuria suggests major podocyte injury, while belimumab-based triple therapy may be preferred for repeated kidney flares or high progression risk. Reduced-dose glucocorticoid schedules are explicitly supported when disease is responding.

Maintenance

Mycophenolic acid analogs are the 1B preferred maintenance therapy. Early maintenance doses are approximately MMF 750 to 1000 mg twice daily or mycophenolic acid 540 to 720 mg twice daily. Azathioprine is the main alternative when mycophenolate is not tolerated, unavailable, or pregnancy is being considered. Total initial plus maintenance immunosuppression should last at least 36 months. Triple regimens that include belimumab or a CNI can continue during maintenance when that was the successful initial strategy.

Class V disease

Pure Class V LN is managed according to proteinuria burden. Low-level proteinuria is handled with monitoring, blood pressure and renin-angiotensin system treatment, hydroxychloroquine, and therapy for extrarenal lupus. Nephrotic-range proteinuria supports glucocorticoid plus another immunosuppressive agent. The evidence for pure Class V subgroups is thinner than for proliferative disease, including sparse belimumab and voclosporin subgroup data.

Response, relapse, and refractory disease

Figure 11 standardizes trial response definitions. Complete response is PCR below 0.5 g/g with stable or improved kidney function, generally within 6 to 12 months, although response may take longer. Partial response requires at least a 50% proteinuria reduction to below 3 g/g with stable or improved kidney function. For an unsatisfactory response, Figure 12 starts with adherence and drug exposure, then asks whether repeat biopsy is needed before switching therapy or escalating to rituximab, another biologic, extended cyclophosphamide, or a clinical trial. Relapse is treated with the prior effective initial regimen or another recommended regimen.

Special situations

Thrombotic microangiopathy is treated by cause rather than as one syndrome. The Figure 13 pathway separates TTP, antiphospholipid syndrome nephropathy, complement-mediated TMA, and other etiologies using ADAMTS13 testing and antiphospholipid antibodies. Pregnancy should be deferred during active LN and for at least 6 months after inactivity. Hydroxychloroquine is continued in pregnancy, low-dose aspirin should start before 16 weeks, and glucocorticoids, hydroxychloroquine, azathioprine, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine are listed as pregnancy-compatible immunosuppressive options. Pediatric regimens generally follow adult regimens with age-specific dose, growth, fertility, and psychosocial considerations. Transplantation is preferred to long-term dialysis for kidney failure when feasible.

Appraisal

Evidence maturity
Moderate overall. Stronger for renal-response effects of selected modern regimens, weaker for mortality, kidney failure, long-term GFR loss, pure Class V disease, pediatrics, and advanced CKD.
Implementation value
High. The guideline provides concrete algorithms, doses, response definitions, safety measures, and maintenance durations.
Recommendation certainty
Mixed. Only 3 of 29 formal guidance statements are graded recommendations; 26 are Practice Points.
SEA score
Not assigned
The v4 scoring rubric was not directly retrievable.

What the source does well

  • It updates a narrow treatment question instead of rewriting the whole 2021 chapter, which keeps the new evidence visible.
  • The evidence review is explicit. Clinical questions use PICOD, RCTs drive the 2024 treatment update, two reviewers assess study selection and risk of bias, and GRADE is used for certainty.
  • The supplement exposes effect estimates, absolute effects, certainty ratings, and reasons for downgrading. This makes weak evidence visible instead of burying it in prose.
  • Figures convert recommendations into usable bedside decisions. Initial therapy, steroid tapering, maintenance, Class V disease, response failure, and TMA all have a practical workflow.
  • The guideline acknowledges uncertainty around new regimens, including the unresolved effect of CNI therapy on long-term kidney survival.

What limits confidence

  • Most formal guidance is ungraded. The guideline is operationally rich, but many exact choices depend on expert consensus.
  • Newer therapies are supported mainly by response and proteinuria endpoints. Hard kidney outcomes and long-term comparative safety are less certain.
  • Trial eligibility limits generalization to severe CKD. The voclosporin program used eGFR above 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, while other regimens also have restricted severe-kidney-disease evidence.
  • Some tacrolimus multitarget evidence comes mainly from Chinese populations. The source itself flags this when discussing generalizability.
  • Pure Class V LN and pediatric LN remain evidence-thin compared with proliferative adult LN.
  • The evidence search ended in April 2023. This artifact does not add later literature because external verification was not requested.

Clinical reading of the evidence

The update supports a move away from one universal induction regimen. Mycophenolate and low-dose cyclophosphamide remain credible standards, but belimumab and CNI-containing triple therapy create useful options for different risk profiles. The strongest newer short-term efficacy signal in the supplied evidence is the increase in complete remission with voclosporin-based therapy. That should not be read as proof of superior long-term kidney survival. The supplement is explicit that mortality and kidney-failure estimates are much less precise.

Maintenance evidence is more directional. The S24 comparison favors mycophenolate over azathioprine for relapse and doubling of serum creatinine, which fits the 1B recommendation to use a mycophenolic acid analog after initial therapy. Azathioprine remains important because pregnancy planning, tolerability, access, and patient preference can outweigh average comparative efficacy.

Source-integrity findings

eGFR threshold conflict. Recommendation 10.2.3.1.1 says CNI therapy can be used when kidney function is "not severely impaired" but prints the parenthetical as eGFR less than or equal to 45 mL/min/1.73 m2. Figure 5 specifies voclosporin for eGFR greater than 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 and warns about CNI nephrotoxicity in impaired kidney function. The artifact preserves this discrepancy rather than choosing one value.
Search date mismatch. The main methods section gives an April 25, 2023 update date. Supplement Table S1 gives April 23, 2023.
Supplement wording errors. Several plain-text summary cells appear to carry copied outcome labels. Table S10's infection row refers to complete remission. Table S11's infection and malignancy summaries refer to all-cause mortality and kidney failure. The numerical rows and certainty ratings were not silently rewritten.
