One-page synthesis

Verdict: Do not use for practice. Best used as a historical synthesis and as a pointer to the underlying randomized trials. Internal denominator/result conflicts and the July 2020 search cutoff materially limit decision-grade use.

The review pooled placebo-controlled dapagliflozin trials in patients with both heart failure and type 2 diabetes. The stated evidence base was five trials with 5,252 participants. Across all heart-failure patients, hospitalization for heart failure favored dapagliflozin (OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.61-0.88; I²=0%). Cardiovascular death was numerically lower but not statistically significant (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.69-1.03; I²=0%). All-cause mortality favored dapagliflozin, although the source reports inconsistent effect estimates: Results prose gives OR 0.76 (95% CI 0.66-0.94), while Figure 3C gives OR 0.79 with the same interval.

The HFrEF subgroup favored dapagliflozin for HHF, cardiovascular death, and all-cause mortality in the main figures. HFpEF analyses included only cardiovascular death and all-cause mortality from two studies and did not show statistically significant benefit; no HFpEF HHF subgroup analysis was performed. The authors nevertheless conclude that dapagliflozin has “no cardiovascular benefits” in HFpEF, a stronger statement than the sparse, imprecise subgroup data support.

Load-bearing source consistency problems:
  • McMurray 2019 is reported as 2,139 diabetic participants, then 2,319 in the next sentence; Table 1 lists the full 4,744-trial arms.
  • Figure 3C ACM OR 0.79 conflicts with prose/abstract OR 0.76.
  • Abstract HFrEF CVD and ACM heterogeneity/effect values conflict with the main Results/Figure 4.
  • Figure 1 has report-retrieval and eligibility boxes labeled n=0 despite five studies ultimately being included.
  • Discussion says “the other four trials” were small after saying two were large, although there were only five trials total.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame heart failure plus type 2 diabetes as a high-risk clinical intersection and position SGLT2 inhibitors as glucose-lowering drugs with cardiovascular and renal effects. They note published cardiovascular-outcome and heart-failure trials of dapagliflozin and state an intent to synthesize cardiovascular outcomes for clinical guidance.

Methods

The review reports PRISMA-based methods, searches of PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Chinese databases, and OPENGREY through July 2020, and inclusion of English/Chinese randomized placebo-controlled dapagliflozin trials in patients with HF plus T2DM. Two investigators independently selected studies and extracted data. Primary outcomes were HHF and CVD; ACM was secondary. GRADE and RoB 2 were used for evidence-quality/risk-of-bias assessment.

Dichotomous outcomes were summarized as ORs with 95% CIs under random-effects models. Heterogeneity was assessed with I², sensitivity analyses were described, and Begg/Egger tests were used for publication bias. RevMan 5.4, RoB-2, and Stata 12 were used. The paper also performed trial sequential analysis.

Results

Five trials were reported as included, with a pooled total of 5,252. Four contributed HHF, five CVD, and four ACM. All included trials used dapagliflozin 10 mg/day versus placebo. HHF favored dapagliflozin (237/2,136 vs 311/2,151; OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.61-0.88). CVD did not reach statistical significance (205/2,618 vs 241/2,634; OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.69-1.03). ACM favored dapagliflozin, but the printed pooled OR conflicts between prose (0.76) and Figure 3C (0.79), with the same 95% CI 0.66-0.94.

In HFrEF, Figure 4 reports HHF OR 0.74 (0.60-0.91), CVD OR 0.72 (0.58-0.91), and ACM OR 0.69 (0.53-0.89). In HFpEF, Figure 5 reports CVD OR 1.45 (0.95-2.22) and ACM OR 1.04 (0.76-1.43); no HFpEF HHF analysis was performed. Trial sequential analysis deemed the accrued HHF and ACM sample sizes above stated required information sizes, but not CVD.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the results as supporting dapagliflozin in HFrEF with diabetes and discuss hemodynamic, natriuretic, renal, ion-exchange, adiposity, metabolic, vascular, and anti-inflammatory mechanisms. These mechanistic passages are background interpretation rather than evidence generated by the meta-analysis.

Explicit limitations include rough baseline-characteristic reporting, inclusion of small RCTs, inability to rule out publication bias, restriction to the 10-mg/day dose, few total RCTs, and dominance by two large trials. The source also contains several internal numerical and flow-diagram inconsistencies that are not acknowledged in its limitations section.

Conclusion

The authors conclude that dapagliflozin reduces HHF, CVD, and ACM in HFrEF with diabetes, and state that it has no cardiovascular benefit in HFpEF. The HFrEF conclusion tracks the main subgroup forest plots; the HFpEF statement is overconfident relative to only two mortality analyses, wide confidence intervals, and absence of an HFpEF hospitalization analysis.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance — 7/10

Directly relevant to dapagliflozin, heart failure, and diabetes, but the evidence set is narrow and historical.

Evidence basis: Five dapagliflozin/placebo RCTs in HF + T2DM; HFrEF/HFpEF subgroup analyses.

Limiting factor: A 2021 search cutoff limits use for current formulary decisions.

Would raise score: A current, comprehensive evidence update with direct comparative or contemporary class-effect evidence would raise the score.

Novelty — 5/10

The paper offered a dapagliflozin-specific synthesis with EF-stratified analyses, but largely recombined established trial data.

Evidence basis: Five existing RCTs, including DAPA-HF and DECLARE-derived subgroups.

Limiting factor: No new primary data; only two HFpEF studies informed mortality analyses.

Would raise score: A prospectively registered review with a broader and more complete trial base would raise the score.

Method strength — 4/10

The review used PRISMA, duplicate screening/extraction, GRADE, RoB 2, random-effects pooling, and publication-bias tests, but internal reporting errors undermine reproducibility.

Evidence basis: Methods section plus Figures 1-5 and Tables 1-2.

Limiting factor: Multiple denominator/result conflicts and a malformed PRISMA flow; no supplied supplement/protocol.

Would raise score: A reproducible protocol, corrected denominators, transparent subgroup extraction, and consistent figures/text would materially raise the score.

Evidence strength — 4/10

Pooled estimates derive from randomized trials, but much of the analysis relies on selected diabetic/HF subgroups and a small number of studies, with several conflicting reported estimates.

Evidence basis: HHF/CVD/ACM pooled estimates and EF subgroup forest plots.

Limiting factor: Five total trials, dominance by two large RCTs, conflicting ACM and HFrEF values, and no HFpEF HHF analysis.

Would raise score: Consistent endpoint datasets and larger dedicated HF+T2DM trials would raise the score.

External validity — 4/10

Applicability is restricted to patients with both heart failure and type 2 diabetes, and the HFpEF evidence is especially sparse.

Evidence basis: Eligibility criteria required HF + T2DM; only two studies contributed HFpEF mortality results.

Limiting factor: Small trials and selected subgroups limit representativeness.

Would raise score: Broader dedicated HF populations with prespecified EF strata and complete outcome reporting would raise the score.

Implementation value — 3/10

Useful mainly as a historical synthesis and a map to primary trials; it is insufficient as a stand-alone basis for current treatment or formulary policy.

Evidence basis: Clear trial/outcome inventory and pooled effects.

Limiting factor: Search through July 2020, internal inconsistencies, and sparse HFpEF evidence.

Would raise score: A current evidence synthesis with verified data and explicit practice translation would raise the score.

Main strengths

  • Randomized-trial source base.
  • Duplicate selection/extraction was reported.
  • Multiple databases plus gray literature were searched.
  • Forest plots provide arm-level event counts and subgroup estimates.
  • The authors attempted RoB 2, GRADE, publication-bias assessment, sensitivity analysis, and trial sequential analysis.

Main limitations

  • Only five trials; several analyses rely on selected subgroups rather than dedicated HF+T2DM trial populations.
  • Multiple contradictions in denominators, pooled estimates, heterogeneity values, and study counts.
  • Figure 1 contains structurally implausible report-retrieval counts.
  • HFpEF evidence is especially sparse and lacks HHF subgroup analysis.
  • No separate supplement was supplied in the selected source packet.
  • Search cutoff July 2020 makes this a historical evidence snapshot.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate-high. The full PDF, all five figures, both tables, and the bibliography were inspected. Confidence is limited by the source's own inconsistent reporting, not by incomplete access to the main article.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Best use
Historical synthesis of dapagliflozin RCT-derived HF/T2DM outcomes and a map to underlying primary trials.
Do not use for
Current stand-alone formulary selection, drug-to-drug superiority, or a definitive HFpEF efficacy conclusion.
Read next
Use the paper's included primary trials directly when trial-level evidence atoms or decision-grade outcome estimates are needed.
