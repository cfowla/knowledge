1. Synthesis

Core thesis. Diabetes and CKD care should be layered rather than glucose-centric: lifestyle and risk-factor management form the base; RAS blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin, nonsteroidal MRA therapy, GLP-1 receptor agonists, individualized glycemic monitoring/targets, and multidisciplinary care are added according to phenotype, kidney function, residual risk, and tolerability.

The practical center of gravity is organ protection. The guideline gives a Level 1A recommendation for SGLT2 inhibitor therapy in T2D with CKD and eGFR ≥20 ml/min per 1.73 m², continues RAS blockade as foundational therapy for hypertension with albuminuria, adds finerenone-class nonsteroidal MRA therapy for persistent albuminuria in eligible T2D, and places long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonists after metformin/SGLT2i when individualized glycemic targets remain unmet or those drugs cannot be used.

The guideline also makes explicit safety/implementation rules that are easy to lose in summary: monitor creatinine and potassium after RASi initiation or titration; generally tolerate the initial hemodynamic eGFR fall with SGLT2i; withhold SGLT2i during prolonged fasting, surgery, or critical illness; monitor potassium with nonsteroidal MRA treatment; and individualize HbA1c because HbA1c becomes less reliable in advanced CKD and dialysis.

2. Development and evidence architecture

The Work Group used the KDIGO recommendation framework: Level 1 (“we recommend”) and Level 2 (“we suggest”) recommendations are graded A–D for certainty, while Practice Points are ungraded expert guidance. Screening, extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment were independently checked by a second Evidence Review Team member. The 2022 search update screened 1,078 citations and added 102 RCTs; overall the evidence review comprised 346 RCTs, 31 observational studies, and 50 systematic reviews.

Appraisal boundary. The guideline is a secondary evidence synthesis. Trial results summarized in its text or supplement remain secondary reports; primary-study LiteratureAtoms require extraction from the cited primary publications.

4. Evidence supplement

The 221-page supplement contains search strategies, Institute of Medicine reporting checklists, and Summary-of-Findings tables spanning RAS blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors, MRAs, glycemic targets/monitoring, diet and physical activity, glucose-lowering drugs, self-management, and models of care. The evidence tables reinforce the guideline’s heterogeneous certainty: large contemporary organ-outcome evidence is strongest for SGLT2 inhibition, while several lifestyle, glycemic-target, and implementation questions rely on lower-certainty or indirect evidence.

Selected source-reported safety/effect signals in the supplement include SGLT2 inhibitor diabetic ketoacidosis HR 2.22 (95% CI 1.18–4.16), genital infection HR 2.60 (95% CI 2.02–3.34), and HbA1c mean difference −0.31 percentage points (95% CI −0.37 to −0.24) versus placebo/standard care in the relevant pooled analyses. These are evidence summaries within the guideline supplement, not newly generated data.

Strengths. Explicit GRADE framework, structured ERT review, extensive supplement, clinically operational thresholds, and tight integration of kidney/cardiovascular outcomes. Limitations. The update is RCT-focused, not every implementation statement is supported by a direct systematic review, and the source is from 2022. The guideline itself notes that Practice Points may rest on expert judgment rather than a dedicated systematic review.

7. Practice translation

For CDS or protocol use, preserve the eligibility conditions and safety boundaries rather than extracting drug names alone. High-value computable elements include eGFR thresholds, albuminuria criteria, potassium prerequisites, RASi monitoring intervals, SGLT2i sick-day/perioperative holds, metformin dose adjustment thresholds, HbA1c reliability caveats, and the distinction between graded recommendations and Practice Points.
