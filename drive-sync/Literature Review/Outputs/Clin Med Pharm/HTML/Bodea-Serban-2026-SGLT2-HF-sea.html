One-page synthesis

Bottom line. The review assembles a useful four-domain picture of SGLT2-inhibitor effects in heart failure. The strongest evidence is the reduction in cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization. Patient-reported health status also improves. Functional-capacity estimates are less precise, and remodeling findings come mainly from small mechanistic HFrEF trials. The paper is useful as a synthesis, but several internal numerical and methods-description inconsistencies reduce confidence in treating its pooled denominators and analytic description as error-free.

The source reports 11 randomized trials and 23,812 participants. Its primary clinical synthesis gives HR 0.77 for cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization, with a 95% CI of 0.72 to 0.82 and NNT 24 over a median 18 months. All-cause mortality, cardiovascular death, and recurrent HF hospitalization also favor SGLT2 inhibition. Effects are described as consistent across ejection fraction and diabetes status.

Across patient-centered domains, KCCQ-CSS improved by 4.6 points and KCCQ-TSS by 5.1 points. The odds of at least a 5-point KCCQ-CSS improvement were 1.49, with source-reported NNT 12. Six-minute walk distance improved by 21.8 m, but heterogeneity was higher. CMR remodeling effects largely come from EMPA-TROPISM, while EMPA-VISION did not show a significant PCr/ATP change. The source reports no excess ketoacidosis, severe hypoglycemia, acute kidney injury, or intervention-requiring volume depletion, while genital mycotic infection risk was higher.

Methods

Review design and selection

The protocol was prospectively registered in PROSPERO and the authors report PRISMA 2020 methods. Searches covered PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Web of Science from inception through 1 February 2025. Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data, with third-reviewer adjudication when needed. The review included adult randomized HF trials across HFrEF, HFmrEF, and HFpEF if they reported at least one clinical, health-status, functional, or remodeling outcome.

Analysis and appraisal framework

The authors used random-effects models, Hartung-Knapp adjustment for smaller-study domains, heterogeneity statistics, subgroup analyses, leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, RoB 2, and GRADE. The paper reports high certainty for clinical outcomes, moderate certainty for KCCQ and functional capacity, and low certainty for myocardial energetics. A methods-description inconsistency remains: section 2.7.2 calls the model DerSimonian-Laird while also saying tau-squared was estimated by Paule-Mandel.

Functional capacity

Four source-described trials with 1,203 participants contributed 6MWD data. The pooled mean difference was +21.8 m, 95% CI 9.4 to 34.2, with I² 61%. EMPA-TROPISM separately reported peak VO2 +1.9 mL/kg/min and VE/VCO2 slope -3.1.

Remodeling and energetics

EMPA-TROPISM reported reductions in LVEDV, LVESV, LV mass, global longitudinal strain, and extracellular volume, plus a 6.1-percentage-point increase in LVEF at six months. EMPA-VISION did not show statistically significant changes in PCr/ATP or its listed CMR measures. The source itself emphasizes that the structural evidence is mainly small, short-duration, HFrEF mechanistic evidence.

Safety

The review reports no excess diabetic ketoacidosis, severe hypoglycemia, acute kidney injury, or volume depletion requiring intervention. Genital mycotic infections were more common, RR 3.42, 95% CI 2.11 to 5.54, and were described as usually mild with treatment discontinuation under 1%.

Limitations and source integrity

The authors explicitly note heterogeneous symptom and functional endpoints, variation in KCCQ timing, underpowered HFmrEF/HFpEF subgroup analyses, no individual participant data, possible residual publication bias, heterogeneous time points, small mechanistic trials, no network meta-analysis, English-only included full texts, limited HFpEF imaging, regulatory differences across countries, and sparse KCCQ measurement within the first 30 days.

Source-internal numerical inconsistencies.
1. The paper reports total n=23,812, while the Table 1 trial Ns sum to 23,999.
2. Section 3.3 reports n=22,927 for six cited outcome trials, while the same six Table 1 Ns sum to 22,477.
These values are retained as source-reported facts where relevant. This appraisal does not invent a replacement denominator.
Other internal inconsistencies. Eligibility wording lists dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and canagliflozin as the approved SGLT2 inhibitors, but SOLOIST-WHF with sotagliflozin is included and later handled in sensitivity analysis. The random-effects methods also mix the labels DerSimonian-Laird and Paule-Mandel in the same model description.

The source-linked supplement was not present in the Drive materials folder. Supplement-only RoB, GRADE, mortality, leave-one-out, and complete search-strategy content could therefore not be independently inspected.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best use is as a broad HF SGLT2 evidence map and quantitative refresher. Do not use it as the sole source for trial-level extraction, exact pooled denominators, current prescribing guidance, or detailed safety incidence.
Relevance: 9/10
Directly addresses a major HF drug class across outcomes clinicians care about.
Basis: Large HF outcome trials plus patient-reported and mechanistic domains.
Limit: Much of the clinical conclusion is already established by prior trials and guidelines.
Would rise with: A clearly practice-changing new comparison or implementation question.
Novelty: 7/10
The four-domain integration is useful even though the underlying trial evidence is familiar.
Basis: Clinical events, KCCQ, exercise capacity, imaging, and energetics are presented together.
Limit: No new primary data or participant-level meta-analysis.
Would rise with: IPD synthesis or a validated cross-domain analytic framework.
Method strength: 7/10
Registered, multi-database, dual-review methods use standard bias and meta-analysis tools.
Basis: PROSPERO, PRISMA, RoB 2, GRADE, sensitivity analyses, Hartung-Knapp.
Limit: Internal denominator and estimator-description inconsistencies, plus unavailable supplement in the supplied packet.
Would rise with: Reconciled denominators, fully transparent code/data, and supplement-level verification.
Evidence strength: 8/10
Hard clinical outcomes are supported by large randomized trials with narrow intervals.
Basis: Primary HR 0.77 with low heterogeneity; mortality and hospitalization effects are consistent.
Limit: Functional and mechanistic domains are smaller and more heterogeneous.
Would rise with: More large randomized functional and phenotype-specific imaging trials.
External validity: 8/10
The clinical evidence spans EF categories, diabetes status, and acute/chronic settings.
Basis: Broad trial programs and source-reported subgroup consistency.
Limit: Imaging evidence is mainly HFrEF and drug indications differ by country.
Would rise with: More diverse structural studies and explicit country-level applicability analysis.
Implementation value: 7/10
The review is useful for evidence briefing and class-level formulary discussion.
Basis: It condenses event, symptom, function, remodeling, and safety results.
Limit: It is not a dosing, monitoring, sequencing, or guideline document.
Would rise with: Direct implementation comparisons and current guideline/label crosswalks.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate-high for the main clinical effect direction and magnitude, moderate for the paper's exact pooled denominators and methods description because of the internal inconsistencies above.

Practice and implementation takeaways

What the source supportsClass-level reductions in HF clinical events plus measurable health-status benefit across randomized HF programs.
What needs cautionFunctional and remodeling estimates are less mature, with higher heterogeneity and small mechanistic samples.
What not to overclaimThis review alone does not establish dosing, sequencing, contraindications, renal monitoring, regulatory status, or a current guideline recommendation.
What to read nextUse the cited primary outcome trials when trial-specific atoms are needed and pair this review with current guideline and label sources for practice decisions.
