One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. High-value historical phase III evidence defining the glycemic, weight, blood-pressure, and short-term safety profile of empagliflozin added to metformin plus sulfonylurea.

The randomized full analysis set contained 666 adults with inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes. At 24 weeks, empagliflozin 10 and 25 mg reduced HbA1c by 0.64 and 0.59 percentage points more than placebo, respectively, with both confidence intervals excluding no effect. Both doses also reduced body weight by about 1.8–2.0 kg more than placebo, lowered fasting and mean daily glucose, and modestly lowered systolic blood pressure. Diastolic blood-pressure effects were not significant.

Short-term overall adverse-event frequencies were similar across groups, but confirmed hypoglycemia was more frequent when empagliflozin was layered onto sulfonylurea; none required assistance. Genital-infection events were uncommon but more frequent with empagliflozin, while UTI-event frequencies were similar to slightly higher. The trial’s internal validity is strengthened by randomization, blinding, prespecified endpoints and multiplicity control, but interpretation is limited by 24-week follow-up, LOCF with substantial MDG missingness, a small moderate-renal-impairment subgroup, sponsor involvement, and the nonrandomized open-label HbA1c >10% cohort.

Methods and design

Population and treatment

Adults aged ≥18 years with BMI ≤45 kg/m² and HbA1c 7–10% despite stable metformin plus sulfonylurea were randomized after a 2-week placebo run-in to empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or placebo once daily for 24 weeks. Background metformin was ≥1,500 mg/day or the maximum tolerated/local-label dose; sulfonylurea was at least half the maximum recommended dose or the maximum tolerated/local-label dose. Patients with HbA1c >10% could enter a separate open-label empagliflozin 25-mg arm.

Randomization was stratified by HbA1c, eGFR and region. The primary endpoint was week-24 HbA1c change; key secondary endpoints were body weight and mean daily glucose. ANCOVA supported the primary analysis with hierarchical 2.5% testing per dose. Continuous post-rescue values were treated as missing and generally handled with LOCF; open-label efficacy used observed cases. The supplement reports a planned 205 participants per randomized arm to provide ≥90% overall power for a 0.5-point HbA1c treatment difference.

Results

Glycemic efficacy

HbA1c changed by −0.17% with placebo, −0.82% with 10 mg and −0.77% with 25 mg. Adjusted differences versus placebo were −0.64% (95% CI −0.77 to −0.51) and −0.59% (−0.73 to −0.46), p<0.001 for both. HbA1c <7% was achieved by 9.3%, 26.3% and 32.2% among those with baseline HbA1c ≥7%. FPG fell 1.60 mmol/L more than placebo with each dose; 2-h PPG fell about 1.9 mmol/L more in the meal-test subset.

MDG results favored both doses. The LOCF differences were −0.56 and −0.72 mmol/L, but the article notes 46.9% missing/invalid week-24 MDG values requiring baseline carry-forward; observed-case differences were larger (−1.08 and −1.17 mmol/L). HbA1c benefit was statistically significant across reported eGFR strata, although the eGFR 30–<60 subgroup was small.

Weight, blood pressure, and rescue therapy

Body-weight differences versus placebo were −1.76 kg with 10 mg and −1.99 kg with 25 mg. More than 5% weight loss occurred in 27.6% and 23.6% versus 5.8% with placebo. Systolic blood pressure fell 2.7 and 2.1 mmHg more than placebo; diastolic differences were not significant. Rescue therapy was required by 11.6% with placebo, 2.2% with 10 mg and 0.9% with 25 mg.

Safety

Any adverse event occurred in 62.7%, 67.9% and 64.1% of placebo, 10-mg and 25-mg groups. Confirmed hypoglycemia was 8.4%, 16.1% and 11.5%; no event required assistance. UTI-consistent events occurred in 8.0%, 10.3% and 8.3%, and genital-infection-consistent events in 0.9%, 2.7% and 2.3%. One acute-myocardial-infarction death occurred in the 10-mg group and was judged unrelated by the investigator.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 9/10

Directly informs efficacy/safety of empagliflozin as third-line therapy with metformin plus sulfonylurea.

Evidence basis: Randomized phase III add-on design with clinically familiar endpoints.

Limiting factor: Older glycemic-efficacy context predates contemporary outcome-driven positioning.

Would raise/lower: Contemporary comparative outcome or implementation evidence in the same regimen.

Novelty: 7/10

At publication, this added phase III evidence for SGLT2 inhibition as third-line oral therapy.

Evidence basis: Placebo-controlled evaluation of two empagliflozin doses plus detailed metabolic and safety outcomes.

Limiting factor: The mechanism/class is now established and the study is historical.

Would raise/lower: Evidence identifying durable dose-response or clinically differentiated effects.

Method strength: 8/10

Randomization, double blinding, prespecified endpoints, multiplicity control, and stratification support internal validity.

Evidence basis: 666-person FAS; ANCOVA; hierarchical testing; detailed supplement.

Limiting factor: LOCF and substantial MDG missingness; short follow-up; open-label ancillary cohort.

Would raise/lower: Longer follow-up with modern missing-data estimands and stronger safety power.

Evidence strength: 8/10

Primary glycemic and key secondary effects were consistent, quantitatively precise, and supported by randomized comparisons.

Evidence basis: HbA1c, weight, MDG, FPG and SBP effects with confidence intervals.

Limiting factor: Exploratory outcomes were not multiplicity-adjusted; safety event counts are modest.

Would raise/lower: Larger event-powered trial with longer safety follow-up.

External validity: 7/10

Multinational enrollment improves transportability to adults resembling the study population.

Evidence basis: 148 centers in 12 countries with broad geographic representation.

Limiting factor: eGFR <30 excluded; moderate renal subgroup small; 24-week treatment; background therapy requirements narrow the population.

Would raise/lower: Broader kidney-function range and longer real-world follow-up.

Implementation value: 8/10

Provides specific dose, background therapy, efficacy magnitude, and adverse-event frequencies.

Evidence basis: Practical 10/25-mg regimens and detailed safety patterns.

Limiting factor: Current use should be interpreted within newer SGLT2 outcome evidence and guidelines, which this 2013 paper cannot address.

Would raise/lower: Integration with current guideline/outcome evidence.

Strengths

Randomized double-blind design, multinational recruitment, prespecified endpoints, detailed supplement, quantitative confidence intervals, and multiplicity control for the primary/key secondary hierarchy.

Limitations and bias considerations

The 24-week duration constrains long-term safety and durability. LOCF is vulnerable to bias under informative missingness, and MDG had particularly high missing/invalid week-24 data. Exploratory endpoints do not carry the same multiplicity protection as the primary hierarchy. The eGFR 30–<60 subgroup is small, eGFR <30 was excluded, and the HbA1c >10% arm is open-label and uncontrolled. Antihypertensive changes were permitted. The sponsor funded the trial, several authors were sponsor employees, and sponsor-funded medical writing was disclosed.

Source-level discrepancy: Supplementary Table 1 says the valid meal-tolerance-test subset contained 125 randomized patients, but its listed arm counts are 35 + 43 + 46 = 124; the main article also describes 124. This report does not silently repair the discrepancy.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this source for
Magnitude of short-term HbA1c, weight, glucose, and SBP effects when empagliflozin is added to metformin + sulfonylurea; short-term hypoglycemia/urogenital-event patterns.
Do not use it alone for
Current guideline positioning, cardiovascular/kidney outcome claims, long-term safety, or patients with eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m².
Clinical signal
When an SGLT2 inhibitor is added to sulfonylurea, hypoglycemia frequency can rise even though severe assistance-requiring events were absent in this trial.
Evidence handling
Treat the open-label severe-hyperglycemia arm and renal subgroups as supportive/descriptive rather than equivalent to the primary randomized comparison.
