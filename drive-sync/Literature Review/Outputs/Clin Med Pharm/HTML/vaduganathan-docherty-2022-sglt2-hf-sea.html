One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. High-value class-effect evidence for SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure, especially the consistency of hospitalization benefit across LVEF and clinical subgroups.

The prespecified synthesis of DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved (12,251 participants) found a 20% relative reduction in cardiovascular death or first heart-failure hospitalization (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.73–0.87), driven chiefly by first HF hospitalization (HR 0.74, 0.67–0.83); cardiovascular death alone was directionally favorable but borderline (HR 0.88, 0.77–1.00; p=0.052). The supportive five-trial meta-analysis (21,947 participants) produced HR 0.77 (0.72–0.82) for cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization, HR 0.72 (0.67–0.78) for first HF hospitalization, HR 0.87 (0.79–0.95) for cardiovascular death, and HR 0.92 (0.86–0.99) for all-cause death.

The strongest practical signal is prevention of HF hospitalization: reported NNT 28 (95% CI 24–35) over roughly 23 months. Benefits on the primary composite were broadly consistent across 14 subgroup families, including LVEF ≥60%; the notable nominal interaction was NYHA class (II HR 0.72 vs III/IV HR 0.86; interaction p=0.015). KCCQ responder analyses also favored SGLT2 inhibition. Safety data from DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved were reassuring descriptively, but the authors correctly did not directly compare or meta-analyze safety because definitions and ascertainment windows differed.

Interpretive boundary. The two-trial HFmrEF/HFpEF analysis is prespecified and preregistered; the five-trial extension is post hoc with no assigned alpha. Do not collapse these into one inferential tier.

Methods

The investigators searched PubMed/MEDLINE for randomized placebo-controlled SGLT2-inhibitor cardiovascular or kidney outcome trials published from Jan 1, 2015 through July 1, 2022, requiring at least 1,000 participants with HF. No additional eligible trials were found. The prespecified DELIVER + EMPEROR-Preserved protocol was registered as PROSPERO CRD42022327527 before DELIVER unmasking; the five-trial extension was post hoc (CRD42022347574).

The primary endpoint was time to cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization. Secondary endpoints included cardiovascular death, first HF hospitalization, cardiovascular death or any worsening-HF event, and all-cause death. Fixed-effects inverse-variance pooling was used with Cox HRs and 95% CIs; Cochran Q assessed heterogeneity. KCCQ clinically meaningful improvement/deterioration was defined by ±5 points from baseline to 8 months. The supplement documents harmonization of DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved endpoint definitions and prespecified sensitivity analyses.

Safety and health status

In DELIVER, serious adverse events occurred in 43.5% with dapagliflozin vs 45.5% with placebo; in EMPEROR-Preserved, 47.9% with empagliflozin vs 51.6% with placebo. Amputation, ketoacidosis, hypoglycaemia, and renal-event counts were low or broadly balanced within each trial. These are descriptive data only: the source explicitly avoided cross-trial safety meta-analysis because definitions, ascertainment, and reporting windows differed.

At 8 months, SGLT2-inhibitor participants were reported to be roughly 10–20% more likely to achieve clinically meaningful health-status improvement and 10–20% less likely to experience clinically important deterioration. The direction was consistent across the three KCCQ summary scores and across the two HFmrEF/HFpEF trials.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance — 9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses class-level SGLT2 inhibitor effects across the full HF ejection-fraction spectrum.

Evidence basis: Five large outcome trials and clinically central HF endpoints.

Limiting factor: The source is from the 2022 evidence era.

Would raise/lower: Current guideline/regulatory verification would determine present-day practice currency.

Novelty — 8/10

Rationale: At publication, the prespecified DELIVER + EMPEROR-Preserved synthesis and five-trial context materially clarified class effects across LVEF.

Evidence basis: New DELIVER data integrated with EMPEROR and HFrEF/worsening-HF trials.

Limiting factor: Much of the class-effect conclusion is now incorporated into later practice guidance.

Would raise/lower: Later patient-level or head-to-head comparative data could add novelty.

Method strength — 8/10

Rationale: The prespecified two-trial analysis used harmonised definitions, fixed-effects pooling, RoB assessment, and preregistration.

Evidence basis: Protocol preregistration, prespecified primary analysis, ITT data, 95% CIs, heterogeneity testing.

Limiting factor: The five-trial extension was post hoc; some subgroup data were aggregate/public-domain rather than uniform IPD.

Would raise/lower: A fully prespecified IPD meta-analysis with harmonised safety capture would raise this score.

Evidence strength — 8/10

Rationale: Large randomized-trial evidence strongly supports reduced HF hospitalization, with smaller mortality effects and consistent sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: 21,947 participants; primary HR 0.77 (0.72–0.82); HF hospitalization HR 0.72 (0.67–0.78).

Limiting factor: CV-death effect in HFmrEF/HFpEF alone was borderline, and five-trial inference is supportive/post hoc.

Would raise/lower: Prospectively specified pooled mortality testing or IPD would strengthen inference.

External validity — 8/10

Rationale: Trials span HFrEF through HFpEF and ambulatory/recently hospitalized settings with broad clinical subgroups.

Evidence basis: Five complementary trial populations and 14 subgroup families.

Limiting factor: Black representation was <5–10% in each trial; severe kidney dysfunction/dialysis were not represented.

Would raise/lower: More diverse enrollment and data in eGFR <20/dialysis populations would raise this score.

Implementation value — 9/10

Rationale: The effect sizes, NNTs, consistency across LVEF, and practical safety context are directly interpretable for HF therapeutics.

Evidence basis: NNT 25 for primary composite; NNT 28 for first HF hospitalization; broad subgroup consistency.

Limiting factor: The article itself is not current practice guidance and does not establish head-to-head equivalence among agents.

Would raise/lower: Current guideline/label alignment and direct comparative evidence would improve actionability.

Bottom line: Strong class-effect evidence for reduced HF hospitalization across the LVEF spectrum. Mortality effects are smaller; the five-trial mortality inference is supportive rather than the prespecified primary inferential layer. Confidence in appraisal: high for source fidelity, moderate-high for practice translation because the source is from 2022.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Use

Use this source to support a class-level SGLT2 inhibitor benefit in HF, particularly prevention of HF hospitalization across reduced, mildly reduced, and preserved LVEF.

Do not overclaim

Do not interpret pooled class consistency as proof of head-to-head equivalence among dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and sotagliflozin.

Safety

Use safety data descriptively only. Cross-trial adverse-event definitions and capture windows were not harmonized enough for formal comparative inference.

Subgroups

Broad consistency is more defensible than subgroup-specific treatment selection. The NYHA interaction is nominal and uncorrected for multiplicity.

Limitations and uncertainty

  • Five-trial extension post hoc; no assigned alpha.
  • No uniform IPD for EMPEROR or SOLOIST-WHF; aggregate/public data used for parts of the synthesis.
  • Subgroup interaction tests may be underpowered; no multiplicity correction.
  • SOLOIST-WHF subgroup data for the primary outcome unavailable.
  • Each trial enrolled fewer than 5–10% Black participants.
  • No patients with eGFR <20 mL/min/1.73 m² or dialysis; no inference for those populations.
  • Safety definitions and ascertainment windows differed; renal outcomes were not comparable across trials.
  • Printed bibliography skips reference number 18; preserved as a source-format inconsistency.
