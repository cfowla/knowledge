2. One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Replacing TBW with AdjBW for initial protocolized IV UFH dosing in adults aged at least 65 years and/or weighing at least 100 kg was associated with a shorter median time to first therapeutic anti-Xa level. The overall signal was driven principally by the age-only subgroup and by a post-hoc subgroup that received an initial bolus; the weight-only subgroup did not show a statistically significant difference.
Primary resultMedian time to first therapeutic anti-Xa: 20.9 h with TBW vs 13.6 h with AdjBW; Hodges-Lehmann point estimate 5.3 h, 95% CI 0.2-9.9; p=0.02.
Older-adult signalAge-only subgroup, n=27 per cohort: 21.9 h vs 13.6 h; point estimate 7.0 h, 95% CI 0.2-12.9; p=0.026.
Higher-weight signalWeight-only subgroup, n=11 per cohort: 13.9 h vs 11.2 h; point estimate 2.7 h, 95% CI -5.0 to 12.4; p=0.47.
What the study adds. It provides a direct TBW-versus-AdjBW comparison using anti-Xa monitoring in an older/high-weight VTE population, with a clinically relevant protocol question and a measurable operational endpoint.
Main restraint on interpretation. The evidence is observational, single-center, temporally separated, small, and focused on a surrogate laboratory endpoint. Safety events were sparse, the obesity-specific subgroup was underpowered, and the report contains an internal discrepancy for the 24-hour therapeutic anti-Xa percentage.

4. Section-by-section condensation

Abstract / graphical abstract / highlights

The article frames a practical UFH dosing problem: standard weight-based dosing may overestimate requirements in older or higher-weight adults. In 80 analyzed patients, AdjBW dosing was associated with faster attainment of therapeutic anti-Xa than TBW dosing (13.6 vs 20.9 h). The graphical abstract emphasizes that the overall difference was 5.3 h and that the age subgroup, but not the weight-only subgroup, showed a significant difference.

The highlights appropriately acknowledge that larger prospective studies are needed to determine safety and clinical efficacy. The graphical abstract's broad concluding language is stronger than the subgroup evidence for higher-weight adults younger than 65 years.

Background

The rationale is pharmacokinetic and safety-oriented: increasing body weight may reduce UFH dose requirements when expressed per kg, and older adults may have physiologic changes that increase risk of excessive anticoagulation. The authors cite prior adjusted-weight and obesity literature, but note limited direct TBW-versus-AdjBW evidence using anti-Xa and little direct study of older adults.

The institution changed its protocol in 2019 to use AdjBW in adults aged >=65 years or weighing >=100 kg, creating the natural pre-post comparison. The hypothesis was that AdjBW would shorten time to first therapeutic anti-Xa.

Methods - design and patients

Patients treated July-September 2019 formed the TBW cohort and those treated July-September 2021 formed the AdjBW cohort. UFH started at 18 units/kg/h; a bolus was discretionary. Anti-Xa was sampled 6 h after initiation and after dose changes, with a 0.3-0.7 units/mL target; after two consecutive therapeutic values, monitoring changed to every 24 h.

Important exclusions included significant protocol deviations, pregnancy, incarceration, ECMO, exposure to an oral factor Xa inhibitor during or immediately before the admission, missing height or height <=152.4 cm, and other deviations affecting interpretable anti-Xa monitoring. The requirement for at least 24 h of UFH and exclusion of early non-protocol discontinuations select a narrower population than all patients started on UFH for VTE.

Methods - endpoints and statistics

The primary endpoint was time to first therapeutic anti-Xa. Secondary laboratory endpoints captured early attainment and overall first-24-hour therapeutic measurements; safety endpoints were mortality and ISTH major bleeding. The primary endpoint was tested with Mann-Whitney U and summarized using a Hodges-Lehmann estimate of the median difference. Categorical outcomes used chi-square or Fisher exact testing.

Prespecified subgroups included age-only, weight-only, and patients meeting both age and weight criteria; the both-criteria group was not analyzed because only two patients per cohort met both criteria. A post-hoc bolus-versus-no-bolus subgroup analysis was added. No sample-size calculation or multiplicity correction is reported in the article.

Results

Of 571 screened patients, 90 met eligibility criteria and 10 were randomly excluded to force equal age/weight stratum representation, leaving 80 patients. Each cohort contained 27 age-only patients, 11 weight-only patients, and 2 patients meeting both criteria. Thirteen patients in each cohort received an initial 80 units/kg bolus. Most baseline characteristics were similar, but concomitant antithrombotic use was higher in the AdjBW cohort (85% vs 62.5%, p=0.02).

The primary endpoint favored AdjBW: 13.6 vs 20.9 h (point estimate 5.3 h, 95% CI 0.2-9.9, p=0.02). The age-only subgroup favored AdjBW (p=0.026), the weight-only subgroup did not (p=0.47), and the post-hoc bolus subgroup favored AdjBW (p=0.02). First-measured therapeutic anti-Xa did not differ significantly (43% vs 30%, p=0.24). Major bleeding (5% vs 10%, p=0.68) and mortality (10% vs 7.5%, p=1) did not significantly differ.

Discussion / conclusion

The authors interpret the primary result as support for AdjBW dosing, particularly in older adults. They regard the weight-only subgroup as likely underpowered and extend the argument for AdjBW in higher-weight adults using prior literature. They also interpret the bolus subgroup as supporting AdjBW for weight-based boluses, while acknowledging the need for larger prospective work.

The authors identify retrospective single-center design, institution-specific protocol, delayed/hemolyzed anti-Xa samples, surrogate laboratory outcomes, exclusion of oral factor Xa inhibitor users, and limited applicability to COVID-associated coagulopathy as limitations. The conclusion appropriately notes that the significant subgroup signal was primarily in older adults and was not demonstrated in higher-weight adults younger than 65 years.

6. Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High-value anticoagulation/protocol literature with a useful older-adult signal, but not sufficiently strong to establish AdjBW as a superior therapeutic-UFH dosing standard or to justify practice change by itself.

Evidence basis: The entire intervention and outcome structure is clinically operational.

Evidence strength

Rationale: The primary laboratory endpoint shows a statistically significant difference with confidence interval reporting, but clinical outcomes are underpowered and subgroup findings are fragile.

Evidence basis: Primary p=0.02; major bleeding 4 vs 2 and mortality 3 vs 4; weight-only subgroup n=11 per cohort; post-hoc bolus subgroup.

Limiting factor: Surrogate outcome, multiplicity, small subgroups, and reporting inconsistency for a secondary outcome.

Would rise/fall with: Prospective confirmation with VTE recurrence/bleeding endpoints and consistent reporting would raise the score.

4/10

External validity

Rationale: Applicability is constrained by one anti-Xa protocol, one center, selective exclusions, and limited representation of the weight-only group.

Evidence basis: DOAC-exposed patients, protocol deviations, height <=152.4 cm, ECMO, and early UFH discontinuations were excluded.

Limiting factor: Many real-world UFH patients would not meet the analyzed pathway.

Would rise/fall with: Multicenter replication across different anti-Xa/aPTT protocols and more diverse populations would raise the score.

5/10

Implementation value

Rationale: The protocol trigger (age/weight), starting rate, monitoring target, and main effect are actionable, but exact AdjBW calculation and full titration details are not in the article PDF.

Evidence basis: 18 units/kg/h, anti-Xa 0.3-0.7 units/mL, 6-hour monitoring; protocol details deferred to supplement.

Limiting factor: Incomplete intervention specification in the accessible source plus insufficient outcome evidence for protocol adoption.

Would rise/fall with: Complete protocol details, external validation, and demonstrated safety/efficacy would raise the score.

Validity analysis

  • Temporal confounding: TBW patients came from 2019 and AdjBW patients from 2021. Any contemporaneous changes in care, staffing, laboratory turnaround, order sets, or patient mix could contribute to observed differences; the design cannot isolate weight basis as cleanly as concurrent randomization.
  • Selection: 10 otherwise eligible patients were randomly excluded to balance age/weight strata. Random selection limits systematic bias but reduces precision and departs from analysis of the full eligible cohort.
  • Baseline imbalance: concomitant antithrombotic use differed (62.5% vs 85%, p=0.02). This is especially relevant to safety interpretation; no multivariable adjustment is reported.
  • Outcome measurement: anti-Xa sampling was protocolized, but delayed draws and hemolyzed samples could prolong measured time to therapeutic range. These occurred in both cohorts, yet the degree of delay may vary at the patient level.
  • Subgroups: the age-only signal is based on 27 patients/cohort; weight-only on 11/cohort; bolus analysis was post hoc. No multiplicity correction or formal interaction testing is reported.
  • Clinical outcomes: bleeding and mortality counts are too small to establish safety superiority; VTE resolution/recurrence was not a primary clinical endpoint.
  • Reporting quality: the first-24-hour therapeutic anti-Xa result differs between narrative text and Table 2, lowering confidence in that secondary endpoint.

7. Pharmacotherapy and practice translation

Most defensible findingWithin this center's anti-Xa UFH pathway, AdjBW initiation was associated with faster first therapeutic anti-Xa attainment overall, with the clearest subgroup signal among age-only patients >=65 years.
What is not establishedAdjBW superiority for higher-weight adults younger than 65 years, reduced major bleeding, lower mortality, or improved VTE treatment outcomes.
Bolus interpretationThe bolus subgroup favored AdjBW, but it was post hoc and bolus administration was at provider discretion; it should be viewed as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.
Monitoring relevanceThe study is most applicable to institutions using anti-Xa 0.3-0.7 units/mL with roughly 6-hour reassessment and comparable protocolized titration.
Protocol-design useUseful as a trigger for local retrospective analysis or prospective QI: compare age-stratified time-to-range, first supratherapeutic anti-Xa, bleeding, VTE outcomes, and dose-response under the local UFH protocol.
Practice-change thresholdInsufficient as a sole basis for changing therapeutic UFH dosing policy. A local change would require complete protocol review, assessment of the AdjBW formula, validation against local outcomes, and stronger safety/efficacy evidence.
Clinical bottom line: The paper supports further evaluation of age-adjusted/AdjBW UFH initiation, especially in older adults, but it should not be interpreted as proof that AdjBW is the preferred strategy for all patients aged >=65 years or weighing >=100 kg.
