One-page synthesis

The review combines 75 randomized HFrEF trials into a component network meta-analysis to estimate aggregate effects of multidrug therapy. The central result is that ARNi + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2 inhibitor had the lowest modeled hazards among the evaluated combinations: HR 0.39 (95% CI 0.31-0.49) for all-cause mortality, HR 0.36 (0.29-0.46) for CV death or HF hospitalization, and HR 0.33 (0.26-0.43) for CV mortality. Relative to ARNi + beta-blocker + MRA, adding SGLT2 inhibitor was associated with HR 0.88 (0.78-0.99; P=0.037) for all-cause death.

The evidence base is large and predominantly randomized, with low reported overall risk of bias, low-to-moderate heterogeneity for major efficacy outcomes, no detected incoherence, and mostly high/moderate CINeMA confidence. The central limitation is inferential: the exact comprehensive regimens are reconstructed through additive component network assumptions across trials conducted in different eras and on different background therapies. Life-year modeling is useful but more assumption-dependent; the main text reports 5.0 years gained at age 70 versus no treatment, whereas Supplementary Table 10 reports 5.4 years (95% CI 2.9-7.8) for the same named regimen versus placebo/no treatment. That unresolved source-internal discrepancy should be preserved rather than silently reconciled.

Verdict: Read soon. High clinical relevance and a strong evidence-synthesis framework, but use the paper chiefly as a comparative aggregate-efficacy model rather than direct proof of one exact sequencing strategy.

Section condensation

Background and objective

The paper addresses a practical consequence of sequential HFrEF drug development: multiple therapies have proven incremental benefit, but the aggregate effect of contemporary combinations is not directly characterized. The stated objective was to estimate and compare cumulative pharmacologic benefit across treatment combinations.

Methods

MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL were searched for randomized HFrEF trials beginning in 1987. Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data. The analysis used frequentist network meta-analysis and an additive component model, treating background therapy as present when at least 50% of participants received it. Fixed or random effects were selected using heterogeneity, and confidence was appraised with CINeMA across within-study bias, reporting bias, indirectness, imprecision, heterogeneity, and incoherence.

The supplement provides the protocol, detailed searches, 10 tables, and 13 figures. It also records February 20, 2021 amendments adding SUCRA P-scores and CINeMA. The protocol describes HFrEF as LVEF <50% and includes an upper age statement of 70 years, whereas the main article describes adult HFrEF without that upper-age criterion; this is a source-level documentation inconsistency, not a corrected finding.

Results

Seventy-five trials included 95,444 participants and 17,684 deaths. Individual class estimates for all-cause mortality versus placebo included ARNi HR 0.75 (0.66-0.85), MRA 0.76 (0.67-0.85), beta-blocker 0.78 (0.72-0.84), SGLT2 inhibitor 0.88 (0.78-0.99), ACE inhibitor 0.89 (0.82-0.96), and ARB 0.95 (0.88-1.02). Vericiguat 0.94 (0.79-1.11) and omecamtiv mecarbil 1.00 (0.92-1.09) did not show a significant all-cause mortality reduction in the network.

The SGLT2-containing quadruple regimen ranked most favorably across all-cause mortality, composite CV death/HF hospitalization, and CV death. Sensitivity analyses substituting ACE inhibitor for ARNi background, excluding hydralazine/digoxin, and restricting to trials with at least 100 events were directionally similar. Discontinuation differed: ARNi OR 0.77 (0.67-0.93) versus placebo, ARB OR 1.20 (1.07-1.36), but the discontinuation network had I2 60.7% and was explicitly cautioned.

Life-year modeling

In 7,376 BIOSTAT-CHF and ASIAN-HF participants, modeled comprehensive therapy added 4.9 life-years (1.4-8.4) at age 50 and 3.3 years (0.7-5.8) at age 70 compared with actual care. Compared with no treatment, the main text reports 7.9 years (4.7-11.2) at age 50 and 5.0 years (2.5-7.5) at age 70. Supplementary Table 10 instead gives 5.4 years (2.9-7.8) at age 70 for the SGLT2-containing quadruple regimen versus placebo.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the aggregate estimates as support for early comprehensive therapy rather than prolonged sequential titration. However, they could not model vericiguat or omecamtiv on top of SGLT2 inhibitor, recent trials had <50% ARNi background use, dose differences were not modeled, and life-year estimates assumed persistent adherence. Trial-era differences, heterogeneity in discontinuation definitions, and omitted device therapy further limit causal and implementation interpretation.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Directly addresses multidrug HFrEF pharmacotherapy and outcome magnitude.

Evidence basis: 75-trial HFrEF network plus life-year modeling.

Limiting factor: Evidence is from a 2022 synthesis rather than a current-practice update.

Would raise/lower: Current comparative data would increase applicability.

Novelty 8/10

Integrates legacy and contemporary drug classes and estimates aggregate combination effects and life-years.

Evidence basis: Component NMA plus BIOSTAT-CHF/ASIAN-HF projections.

Limiting factor: Many combination effects are modeled indirectly.

Would raise/lower: Direct randomized comparisons of comprehensive regimens would raise novelty and certainty.

Method strength 7/10

Dual screening/extraction, prespecified protocol, CINeMA, heterogeneity/incoherence checks, and sensitivity analyses strengthen the review.

Evidence basis: 75 RCTs; CINeMA; Egger tests; sensitivity analyses.

Limiting factor: Additive component assumptions, background-therapy thresholding, and post-search protocol amendments remain important constraints.

Would raise/lower: Prospectively locked analytic choices and individual-patient data would raise the score.

Evidence strength 7/10

Large randomized evidence base supports class effects, but the key quadruple-regimen estimates are largely indirect network estimates.

Evidence basis: 95,444 participants; low reported risk of bias; mostly high/moderate CINeMA confidence.

Limiting factor: No direct trial of the exact four-drug strategy and source-internal life-year discrepancy.

Would raise/lower: Direct head-to-head regimen trials and reconciled projection data would raise the score.

External validity 7/10

Trials span multiple eras and the life-year analysis used European and Asian cohorts.

Evidence basis: BIOSTAT-CHF and ASIAN-HF provide geographic diversity.

Limiting factor: Women were 23% of trial participants; trial-era and treatment-background differences limit transportability.

Would raise/lower: More representative contemporary cohorts would raise the score.

Implementation value 7/10

Supports comprehensive disease-modifying therapy as a strategy but does not establish sequencing, titration, patient-specific contraindications, or current guideline status.

Evidence basis: Aggregate outcomes and life-year estimates are clinically interpretable.

Limiting factor: Dose differences, adherence, device therapy, and tolerability heterogeneity were not fully modeled.

Would raise/lower: Implementation trials and current guideline integration would raise the score.

Bottom line

The paper provides a high-value quantitative synthesis supporting comprehensive HFrEF pharmacotherapy, especially the ARNi/beta-blocker/MRA/SGLT2 inhibitor combination. Its strongest contribution is comparative aggregate modeling across a large RCT network; its weakest point is that the clinically compelling four-drug estimate is not a direct randomized comparison of a complete regimen and depends on additive, background-therapy, and long-term-adherence assumptions.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Use for
Understanding the modeled cumulative effect size of major HFrEF drug classes and the rationale for comprehensive therapy.
Do not use alone for
Current sequencing, titration, contraindication, monitoring, or patient-specific treatment decisions.
Key caution
Preserve the source's indirectness and the unresolved life-year discrepancy; do not convert the model into a directly observed four-drug trial result.
