Bottom line

Source finding. In 6,522 high-risk patients randomized soon after acute myocardial infarction, empagliflozin reduced first heart failure hospitalization by 23% and total heart failure hospitalizations by 33% relative to placebo. The primary EMPACT-MI composite of first heart failure hospitalization or all-cause death was neutral.

Appraisal. The heart failure signal is coherent across first-event, recurrent-event, broad adverse-event, and sensitivity analyses. It is still not equivalent to a positive primary trial. The authors explicitly state that failure of the primary endpoint makes the HF analyses exploratory in a strict statistical sense. The cleanest interpretation is a credible reduction in HF morbidity in the selected high-risk post-MI population, with no demonstrated mortality benefit in this report.

Clinical question

Does starting empagliflozin 10 mg daily after acute MI reduce subsequent heart failure events in patients without established prior HF who have new LVEF below 45% or treated congestion and at least one additional HF risk factor?

Design and population

The parent EMPACT-MI trial was international, randomized, double blind, placebo controlled, and event driven. It enrolled 6,522 patients at 451 sites in 22 countries from December 2020 through March 2023. Mean age was 63.6 years, 24.9% were women, 74.3% had STEMI, 78.4% had LVEF below 45%, and 57.0% had treated congestion. Type 2 diabetes was present in 31.9%.

Endpoint collection used blinded trained site investigators, structured electronic case-report forms, source verification, automated checks, and medical or data-management review. There was no conventional central event-adjudication committee.

Endpoint hierarchy and analysis status

The trial's primary endpoint was time to first HHF or all-cause death. It was neutral at HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.76 to 1.06, P=0.21. Supplementary Table 1 shows that standalone first HHF and total HHF analyses were prespecified. The primary endpoint failure matters because the article's Discussion states that the HF endpoint analyses should be considered exploratory in a strict statistical sense.

First events used adjusted Cox models. Recurrent events used negative binomial regression, with Wei-Lin-Weissfeld and joint-frailty sensitivity analyses. The joint-frailty analysis accounted for all-cause death as a competing risk and gave a result consistent with the main recurrent-HHF analysis.

About half of first HHF events occurred within 89 days. During that early interval, 56 empagliflozin patients, 1.7%, and 77 placebo patients, 2.4%, had a first HHF. After day 89, the corresponding values were 62, 2.0%, and 76, 2.5%.

Sequential-event analysis supported a recurrent-event effect. The second-HHF HR was 0.53, 95% CI 0.31 to 0.91. The third-HHF estimate was 0.42, 95% CI 0.14 to 1.24, but it was imprecise because only 1 empagliflozin patient and 9 placebo patients reached that event order. The consistency test across event order was P=0.290.

The supplement's joint-frailty sensitivity analysis estimated the total-HHF effect at 0.71, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.91, consistent with the main rate ratio of 0.67.

Subgroups and therapy escalation

Figures 4 and 5 show generally consistent HF hospitalization effects across age, sex, MI type, diabetes, renal function, blood pressure, atrial fibrillation, and background therapies. The one statistically positive interaction for first HHF was race, P=0.027. The paper warns that non-White event counts were small. STEMI and NSTEMI estimates did not show a significant interaction, P=0.397.

Within 6 months after discharge, patients assigned empagliflozin were less likely to start ARNI, ACE inhibitor or ARB or ARNI, or MRA if they were not receiving the relevant therapy at discharge. Beta-blocker initiation and ICD or CRT implantation were numerically lower but not statistically significant.

Source-integrity note. Figure 6A and the Results prose do not present the same diuretic-initiation analysis. The prose reports 138, 12.2%, versus 174, 15.3%, HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.00, P=0.046. Figure 6A begins with larger at-risk populations, 1,788 and 1,873, and reports HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.96, P=0.021 for diuretics excluding MRA. These values were not silently merged.

Strengths

The randomized and blinded design supports causal inference for treatment assignment. The trial enrolled a large international cohort, started therapy early after MI, and used complementary first-event and recurrent-event methods. The central HF signal persisted when definitions were broadened and when recurrent events were modeled with a competing-risk approach. The direction of effect also remained similar across most prespecified subgroups.

Limitations

The failed primary composite is the main statistical constraint. The standalone HF analyses were prespecified, but they do not rescue the trial's formal primary hypothesis. The outpatient and broad adverse-event analyses are more exploratory still.

There was no central endpoint adjudication. HF adverse-event capture also depended on protocol-specific reporting rules. Selected narrow-SMQ cardiac-failure terms were always serious and mandatory to report, while other terms in the same SMQ were not necessarily captured unless another reporting criterion applied.

The trial ran during the COVID-19 pandemic and in regions affected by war. Those conditions could change hospitalization behavior. The streamlined design did not collect serial imaging or mechanistic data, and most concomitant medication data stopped at 6 months. The randomized population was selected for high post-MI HF risk and excluded established prior HF, so these results should not be generalized to every post-MI patient.

Funding came from Boehringer Ingelheim and Eli Lilly. Several authors were Boehringer Ingelheim employees, and sponsor employees performed the statistical analyses under executive-committee oversight. These disclosures do not invalidate the results, but they belong in the appraisal.

Appraisal

Internal validity. Strong for randomized treatment assignment and the prespecified HHF signal, with a meaningful limitation from investigator-based endpoint ascertainment. The recurrent-event sensitivity work strengthens the HF morbidity finding.

Statistical interpretation. The primary composite was negative. That prevents a simple "positive trial" label. The first and total HHF results are better read as consistent secondary evidence of reduced HF morbidity, not proof of a broad post-MI mortality or composite benefit.

External validity. The result applies most directly to patients resembling EMPACT-MI: recent STEMI or NSTEMI, no prior diagnosed HF, and new LV dysfunction or treated congestion plus another enrichment factor.

Implementation value. The paper supports the biological and clinical plausibility of early empagliflozin for HF-event reduction in this selected population. No current guideline or regulatory verification was performed in this run, so this SEA should not be read as a current-practice recommendation.

Verdict. High-value secondary analysis with a coherent reduction in HF morbidity, particularly first and recurrent HHF. The failed primary endpoint, lack of central adjudication, exploratory broad-HF analyses, and selected population keep the conclusion narrower than the article title can suggest at a glance.
