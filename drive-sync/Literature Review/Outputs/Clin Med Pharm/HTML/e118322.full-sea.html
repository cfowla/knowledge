One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The authors propose an indication-stratified systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis to quantify methadone-associated QTc change and the odds of clinically important QT prolongation and major cardiac events, testing dose- and time-dependent effects.

The planned review includes adult and pediatric inpatients and outpatients receiving oral or intravenous methadone for opioid use disorder (OUD), cancer pain, surgical pain, or other indications. The primary outcome is QTc duration in milliseconds. Secondary outcomes are major cardiac events—ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, or sudden cardiac death—and clinically relevant QTc prolongation, defined as QTc >500 ms, >25% increase from baseline, or >60 ms absolute increase from baseline. Comparators include within-person pre-methadone baseline and external non-methadone controls.

The protocol has several strong reproducibility features: prospective PROSPERO registration, dual screening, duplicate data extraction with validation, RoB 2/ROBINS-I assessment, explicit correction-formula separation, prespecified dose/duration strata, REML random-effects models, publication-bias methods, and a SWiM fallback if pooling is inappropriate. The main methodological concern is a prespecified nonstandard scheme that multiplies inverse-variance study weights by 1.0, 0.5, or 0.25 according to overall risk of bias; this converts qualitative risk-of-bias judgments into deterministic quantitative weights without a clear empirical calibration.

Two interpretation constraints. First, the abstract says certainty will be evaluated with GRADE, whereas the Methods section says one investigator will grade quality using Evidence-based Practice Center programme guidelines. Second, the planned timeline states that literature search, study selection, data extraction, and data synthesis had already been completed when this protocol was published; the PROSPERO record is therefore important for establishing which decisions were prospectively locked, but that registry history was not externally verified in this source-only workflow.

Methods — faithful condensation

Protocol and registration

The protocol follows PRISMA and states that a prospective version was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251143441), with modifications to be updated in the registry. The source does not provide registry-version history, so prospective fidelity cannot be independently checked from the PDF alone.

Population, exposure, comparator, outcomes

Population: adult or pediatric inpatients or outpatients. Exposure: oral or intravenous methadone for OUD, cancer pain, surgical pain, or other indications. Comparators: (1) internal pre-methadone baseline; (2) external patients not receiving methadone, including other opioids or placebo.

Primary: QTc interval length (ms). Secondary: major cardiac events (ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, sudden cardiac death) and clinically relevant QT prolongation (QTc >500 ms, >25% increase from baseline, or >60 ms absolute increase).

Eligibility

Eligible designs are RCTs, prospective and retrospective cohort studies, and case-control studies reporting raw outcome data or effect estimates. The protocol excludes preclinical studies, case reports/series, abstracts, reports without full text in English or Spanish, and non-peer-reviewed publications.

Search and study handling

Databases: Ovid MEDLINE, Ovid Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, and ClinicalTrials.gov. The search spans 1 January 1970 through 21 October 2025, limited to human studies and English/Spanish. A medical librarian designed the strategy; duplicates are to be removed manually in EndNote. Covidence is used for screening. Two investigators independently screen titles/abstracts and full texts; disagreements go to a third reviewer; the completed review will present a PRISMA flow diagram.

Two authors extract study, sample, methadone exposure, QTc measurement, and outcome data into a pre-elaborated Excel spreadsheet, with a third author validating extraction.

Risk of bias and certainty

Two investigators independently use RoB 2 for RCTs and ROBINS-I for observational studies. Outcome-measurement domains are specifically intended to capture possible bias from automated QTc measurements. Overall risk is categorized as low, moderate, or high based on the highest domain rating, with third-reviewer resolution of disagreements.

Internal inconsistency: the abstract names GRADE for certainty, while the Methods section states that one investigator will grade evidence quality using Evidence-based Practice Center programme guidelines, with second-investigator review. The source does not reconcile these frameworks.

Statistical plan

Analyses will use R 4.2.3 and be stratified by OUD, cancer pain, surgical pain, and other indications. QTc is analyzed continuously using mean differences for within-group pre/post change and between-group methadone-versus-control comparisons. Random-effects meta-analysis uses restricted maximum likelihood. Bazett- and Fridericia-corrected QTc studies are analyzed separately.

Risk of bias is incorporated by multiplying inverse-variance weights by 1.0, 0.5, or 0.25 for low risk, some concerns, and high risk, respectively. Median/range/IQR data may be transformed using Wan and Luo methods. Prespecified dose strata are <50, 50–100, and >100 mg/day for OUD; ≤0.1, 0.11–0.2, and >0.2 mg/kg for surgical pain. Duration is stratified at <1 vs >1 month for cancer pain and OUD. Secondary dichotomous outcomes use random-effects ORs with 95% CIs.

Heterogeneity is evaluated with I² and Cochran's Q; potentially important heterogeneity is flagged at I² >25% or Q p<0.1. Publication bias/small-study effects are assessed using funnel plots, Egger regression, and trim-and-fill. If meta-analysis is not feasible, the review will use evidence tables consistent with SWiM.

Timeline, ethics, dissemination

The PICOT question was formulated in August 2025. The authors state that literature search, study selection, data extraction, and data synthesis were already completed, with analysis and quality assessment planned over the following two months and manuscript submission by September 2026. Because only previously published data are used, no ethics approval is required. Patients/public were not involved.

Discussion & source-stated limitations

The authors frame the review as an attempt to resolve uncertainty created by different indications, doses, exposure durations, QTc correction formulas, and measurement methods. They explicitly plan to keep Bazett and Fridericia analyses separate, use a sex-neutral QTc >500 ms threshold for specificity and comparability, and add relative/absolute change definitions to capture clinically relevant prolongation.

The source anticipates major heterogeneity in dose, duration, QTc technique, and timing; mixed adult/pediatric and inpatient/outpatient populations; positive publication bias; a literature dominated by uncontrolled prospective or retrospective observational studies with incomplete confounding adjustment; and inability to incorporate CYP2B6/CYP2C19 phenotype, NOS1AP variants, KCNH2 polymorphisms, and inconsistently reported drug interactions/pharmacogenomics. The authors suggest future network meta-analysis for concomitant-medication interactions.

Important provenance boundary. Quantitative values discussed in the Introduction/Discussion come from cited prior studies. They are background evidence reported by this protocol, not results generated by the planned systematic review; they therefore were not represented as protocol-generated quantitative LiteratureAtoms.

Evaluation & appraisal

Verdict: Do not use for practice. Best use: understand the forthcoming evidence synthesis, assess its planned methods, and identify what to scrutinize when the completed meta-analysis is published. The protocol itself does not justify changing ECG monitoring, methadone dosing, or prescribing thresholds.

Principal strengths

  • Prospective PROSPERO registration and explicit plan to update modifications.
  • Broad but clinically meaningful indication stratification instead of indiscriminate pooling.
  • Continuous QTc primary endpoint plus prespecified clinically relevant thresholds and major cardiac events.
  • Separate handling of Bazett and Fridericia correction formulas.
  • Independent screening and risk-of-bias assessment with third-reviewer adjudication.
  • REML random-effects model, prespecified dose/duration subgroups, publication-bias assessment, and SWiM fallback.

Critical methodological concerns

  1. Nonstandard risk-of-bias weighting. Multiplying inverse-variance weights by 1.0/0.5/0.25 gives subjective RoB categories a quantitative precision penalty whose calibration is not justified in the protocol. This can alter pooled estimates and standard errors. Conventional primary analysis plus sensitivity/exclusion analyses would be easier to interpret.
  2. Evidence-certainty framework conflict. GRADE is named in the abstract; EPC programme guidelines are named in Methods. The final review should state which framework governed the analysis and whether this changed after registration.
  3. Protocol timing. At publication, search, selection, extraction, and “data synthesis” were already complete. This does not invalidate the project—PROSPERO was reportedly prospective—but it makes registry/version history important for detecting post hoc analytic changes.
  4. Search reproducibility. Tables 1–2 contain apparent line-number/logic artifacts as printed. Direct replication should use a validated executable strategy rather than copying the table blindly.
  5. Clinical heterogeneity. OUD maintenance, chronic cancer pain, and single-dose perioperative methadone differ substantially in dose, duration, comorbidity, co-medication, and baseline arrhythmia risk. Stratification helps, but sparse strata could still make pooled results unstable.

What would materially change the appraisal?

A completed review showing transparent registry concordance, clean executable searches, conventional risk-of-bias sensitivity analyses, adequate study numbers within indication strata, effect estimates with uncertainty, and a consistent certainty framework would raise method/evidence scores. Conversely, heavy reliance on sparse observational data, unexplained protocol deviations, unstable subgroup findings, or funnel-plot methods applied to too few studies would lower confidence.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use nowUse the protocol to anticipate which methadone-QTc questions the completed review should answer: magnitude of QTc change, dose/duration gradients, correction-formula effects, and occurrence of clinically important QT prolongation/MACE.
Do not use nowDo not infer an ECG monitoring schedule, methadone dose ceiling, QTc stopping threshold beyond what the protocol merely defines as an outcome, or a comparative safety conclusion from this paper.
When the review publishesCheck whether OUD, cancer, and surgical strata remain separate; inspect absolute denominators for rare cardiac events; verify confounder handling; compare analyses with and without the 1.0/0.5/0.25 RoB weights; and confirm the final certainty framework.
Clinical translation boundaryThe final meta-analysis may inform risk estimates, but patient-level decisions will still require indication, dose, duration, co-medications, electrolytes, cardiac history, QTc method, and local monitoring guidance.
