One-page synthesis

Core thesis. This case documents within-patient emergence of daptomycin resistance during treatment of MRSA right-sided endocarditis, despite no prior vancomycin exposure. The central microbiologic signal is an increasing daptomycin MIC during persistent bacteremia, reaching 2 mg/L in isolate S3. The source contains an internal discrepancy at baseline: the case-report narrative states 0.25 mg/L for the initial isolate, whereas Figure 1 displays 0.125 mg/L for S1; both values are preserved here rather than reconciled.

A 55-year-old woman with COPD developed MRSA catheter-related bacteremia and a 1.5-cm tricuspid vegetation. Daptomycin 7 mg/kg was started on hospital day 52. After 8 days, bacteremia persisted and the daptomycin MIC had increased from 0.25 to 0.75 mg/L. Daptomycin was escalated to 16 mg/kg/day and rifampin 1200 mg/day was added. Eight days later, bacteremia still persisted and the isolate was daptomycin-resistant at MIC 2 mg/L; rifampin MIC had increased to >32 mg/L. Linezolid susceptibility remained stable at 0.38 mg/L and vancomycin MIC rose from 0.75 to 1.5 mg/L but remained categorized as susceptible by the source.

Source-integrity note. The narrative baseline daptomycin MIC is 0.25 mg/L, but Figure 1 labels S1 as 0.125 mg/L. The supplied article does not reconcile this difference. Downstream use should retain the locator-specific values.

At day 68, therapy was changed to vancomycin 3 g/day by continuous infusion, gentamicin 4 mg/kg/day, and linezolid 1200 mg/day; blood cultures were subsequently negative. Gentamicin and linezolid were stopped after 5 and 12 days, while vancomycin continued for 6 weeks with reported concentrations of 25-35 mg/L. By day 113, the vegetation measured 1.2 cm. Candidate-gene comparison did not identify new mutations in mprF, rpoB, rpoC, or yycG that explained daptomycin resistance; the source reports one rpoB-region mutation associated with rifampin resistance. The authors therefore left the molecular mechanism of daptomycin resistance unresolved.

Section condensation

Summary

The abstract-level summary states the report's distinctive observation: a daptomycin-resistant S. aureus isolate emerged during daptomycin treatment of right-sided infective endocarditis in a patient who had never received vancomycin.

Introduction

The authors frame MRSA bacteremia/endocarditis as a difficult-to-treat infection and daptomycin as an important bactericidal option. They note that in-vivo daptomycin resistance had been rarely reported and that prior vancomycin exposure had often been associated with such cases. These are background claims supported by cited literature, not new primary findings from this patient.

Case report

The patient received several earlier antimicrobials, including linezolid for MRSA isolated from bronchoalveolar lavage, but no vancomycin before the resistance event. Daptomycin 7 mg/kg began on day 52 for MRSA catheter-related bacteremia. The case-report narrative states that the initial isolate was susceptible to daptomycin (MIC 0.25 mg/L), vancomycin (0.75 mg/L), rifampin (0.006 mg/L), and linezolid (0.38 mg/L); Figure 1 separately displays 0.125 mg/L for S1 daptomycin. A TEE on day 60 showed a 1.5-cm tricuspid vegetation.

After 8 days of daptomycin, cultures remained positive and daptomycin MIC rose to 0.75 mg/L. Daptomycin was increased to 16 mg/kg/day and rifampin 1200 mg/day added. After another 8 days, bacteremia persisted, daptomycin MIC reached 2 mg/L and rifampin MIC exceeded 32 mg/L. Therapy was then changed to vancomycin/gentamicin/linezolid, after which blood cultures remained negative. At day 113, TEE showed a 1.2-cm vegetation.

Discussion

The authors interpret the case as unusual because daptomycin resistance emerged without previous vancomycin treatment. They contrast this with prior literature describing daptomycin MIC increases and known resistance-associated genes. Their comparison of the pretreatment susceptible and resistant isolates did not identify explanatory changes in mprF, rpoB, rpoC, or yycG, apart from an rpoB-region mutation associated with rifampin resistance.

The mechanistic conclusion is appropriately limited: the molecular basis of daptomycin resistance remained undetermined. The authors' novelty claim — that this was the first de novo clinical resistance/failure case without prior vancomycin and without those de novo candidate-gene mutations — is an author claim and is not independently established by the case itself.

Acknowledgements, ethics, and conflicts

Broth-dilution confirmation was acknowledged. Funding was reported from INSERM U914. Local ethics approval and patient informed consent were reported. The authors declared no conflict of interest.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. High-value historical signal for emergent daptomycin resistance during persistent MRSA endocarditis, but evidence is inherently limited by a single case and unresolved mechanism.
Relevance
8/10

Rationale: Directly relevant to MRSA bacteremia/endocarditis, daptomycin failure, serial susceptibility testing, and antimicrobial stewardship. Evidence basis: detailed treatment/MIC timeline. Limit: historical single case. Would rise: replication in larger contemporary cohorts.

Novelty
8/10

Rationale: The authors describe an unusual resistance event without prior vancomycin exposure and without the candidate mutations they examined. Evidence basis: serial isolates and gene comparison. Limit: "first case" is a literature-positioning claim. Would fall: prior equivalent cases.

Method strength
4/10

Rationale: Serial isolates, two MIC methods, and molecular comparison strengthen the observation. Evidence basis: Figure 1 legend and discussion. Limit: uncontrolled case report; incomplete laboratory-method detail. Would rise: prospective protocol, genomic analysis, replicate phenotyping, and controls.

Evidence strength
4/10

Rationale: Strong evidence that this patient's isolate phenotype changed over time; weak evidence for why it changed or which treatment caused clearance. Evidence basis: serial MICs, persistent cultures, treatment sequence. Limit: confounding and no counterfactual. Would rise: mechanistic confirmation and replicated clinical series.

External validity
2/10

Rationale: One ICU patient with a complex antimicrobial course cannot define frequency, risk factors, or expected outcomes. Limit: n=1. Would rise: multicenter longitudinal data.

Implementation value
5/10

Rationale: Useful as a warning that susceptibility can evolve during persistent bacteremia and that repeat microbiology may matter. Limit: does not define a modern management algorithm. Would rise: current guideline linkage and outcome-tested management pathways.

Main strengths

  • Clear temporal sequence linking therapy, persistent bacteremia, TEE findings, and serial isolate MICs.
  • Daptomycin susceptibility was checked with E-test and broth dilution.
  • The authors investigated several candidate resistance genes instead of assuming a known mechanism.

Main limitations

  • The source contains an unresolved baseline daptomycin MIC discrepancy: 0.25 mg/L in narrative text versus 0.125 mg/L for S1 in Figure 1.
  • Single-patient descriptive design with no comparator and no causal inference.
  • Clinical clearance occurred after a three-drug switch, so the contribution of any one agent cannot be isolated.
  • The molecular mechanism of daptomycin resistance remained unresolved.
  • Breakpoints, dosing conventions, and endocarditis management standards are time-sensitive; 2013 interpretations should not be used as current guidance without verification.

Practice translation

Use this source for: recognition that an initially susceptible MRSA isolate can develop higher daptomycin MICs during ongoing therapy, especially in the setting of persistent bacteremia; teaching the value of serial cultures and repeat susceptibility assessment when response is poor.
Do not use this source for: selecting a present-day salvage regimen, setting current daptomycin/vancomycin breakpoints, or attributing culture clearance to vancomycin, gentamicin, or linezolid individually.

Currency boundary: The susceptibility categories and dosing context are reported as interpreted in 2013. This appraisal does not treat those historical breakpoints or regimens as current clinical guidance; contemporary application requires separate current-source verification.

Bottom line: The report is a useful mechanistic and stewardship teaching case, but it is hypothesis-generating rather than practice-defining.
