One-page synthesis

Core thesis: pDDIs were common in this two-center oncology cohort: 529/678 patients (78%) had at least one identified pDDI. Among 1,843 detected interactions, 67.3% were major and 27.5% moderate. In adjusted analysis, >7 total prescribed drugs and ≥3 anticancer drugs independently predicted pDDI presence.

The study's strongest contribution is a concrete description of interaction burden and medication-count risk stratification in a hospital oncology population. The adjusted odds of pDDI presence were 3.5 (95% CI 2.2–5.5) for >7 versus ≤7 total drugs and 3.6 (95% CI 2.1–6.2) for ≥3 versus ≤2 anticancer drugs. Other univariate associations—supportive-care drug count, hospitalization, solid malignancy, and combination treatment—were not significant after adjustment.

The clinical interpretation must remain limited to potential interactions. The investigators explicitly did not determine the extent of patient harm caused by these pDDIs, used one interaction database, sampled only two hospitals, and excluded home-based cancer care. The paper is useful for screening workflow design and interaction awareness, not for estimating causal adverse-event incidence.

Methods

Design and setting

A cross-sectional study was conducted at Hayatabad Medical Complex (public tertiary teaching hospital) and Northwest General Hospital and Research Center (private tertiary hospital) in Peshawar. The ethics section later describes the study as retrospective, which is compatible with a retrospective cross-sectional design based on hospital data.

Eligibility and data collection

Patients of any age or gender with any cancer treated with intravenous and/or oral anticancer agents were included; profiles lacking required information were excluded. Demographics, symptoms, laboratory results, and prescribed medications were collected. Medication profiles were screened with Micromedex Drug-Reax, and interactions were classified by severity and documentation level.

Statistics

Frequencies and percentages summarized quantitative data. Logistic regression assessed associations between pDDI presence and gender, age, medication counts, hospitalization, cancer type, metastasis, treatment type, and treatment intent. Variables significant in univariate analysis were entered into multivariate analysis; p≤0.05 was the significance threshold. SPSS 23 was used.

Discussion and limitations

The authors compare the 78% prevalence with lower rates reported in prior oncology and non-oncology studies and attribute variability to design, populations, settings, prescribing patterns, clinical pharmacy services, drugs involved, and interaction-screening sources. They emphasize the high proportion of major pDDIs and the persistence of total-drug and anticancer-drug counts as adjusted predictors.

Primary limitations reported by the authors: actual patient suffering from the identified interactions was not measured; the study involved only two hospitals; one interaction database was used; and home-based cancer patients were excluded. These limitations directly constrain causal interpretation, generalizability, and the use of the results as estimates of observed adverse-event risk.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best for oncology medication-safety screening, polypharmacy risk stratification, and a historical interaction shortlist. Do not use it alone to infer that a listed pDDI caused harm or to define current interaction-management rules.

Main strengths

  • Moderately large cohort (N=678) from both public and private tertiary hospitals.
  • Explicit interaction-screening method and severity/documentation classification.
  • Adjusted regression estimates with 95% confidence intervals.
  • Practical ranked list of common interaction pairs.

Main concerns

  • Potential interactions are database signals, not adjudicated clinical events.
  • Cross-sectional/retrospective design cannot establish causal harm.
  • Single interaction database can materially shape prevalence and classification.
  • Two-center hospital sample limits transportability, especially to outpatient/home oral anticancer therapy.
  • Supplementary interaction table referenced in the article was unavailable in the retrieved PDF.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Useful now
Use medication count as a trigger for closer interaction review: the source found independent associations at >7 total drugs and ≥3 anticancer drugs.
Do not overclaim
Do not equate “major” pDDI classification with an observed major adverse event in this cohort.
Workflow idea
Prioritize high-burden oncology regimens for pharmacist review, then verify each specific pair with current interaction references and patient-specific factors.
