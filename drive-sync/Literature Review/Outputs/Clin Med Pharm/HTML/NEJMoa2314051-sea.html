Study design and statistical architecture

EMPACT-MI was an international, event-driven, double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial. Patients were randomized within 14 days of acute MI admission to empagliflozin 10 mg once daily or matching placebo, both on top of standard care. Randomization was stratified by type 2 diabetes and geographic region.

Enrollment required a new LVEF below 45% or treated congestion during the index admission, plus at least one enrichment factor. Patients with chronic heart failure before the index MI were excluded. The trial therefore tested early SGLT2 inhibition in a post-MI population at increased risk for heart failure, not treatment of established chronic heart failure.

The primary endpoint was time to first HHF or all-cause death. The final protocol targeted 532 primary events for 85% power at two-sided alpha 0.05. Recruitment and event accrual were slower than expected. Blinded data led to an increase from the original 3312-patient plan to 5000, with an option to reach 6500 without changing the event target. The final TSAP states that there were no changes in the planned analysis.

Endpoint ascertainment matters. EMPACT-MI did not use a conventional central adjudication committee. Trained site investigators, blinded to treatment, categorized events using prespecified definitions. Structured eCRFs, source-document verification, automated checks, and medical/data-management review provided additional verification.

Evaluation and appraisal

What the trial establishes

In high-risk patients randomized early after acute MI, empagliflozin did not produce a statistically significant reduction in the prespecified composite of first HHF or all-cause death. That is the confirmatory result and should remain the headline finding.

What remains suggestive

The first-HHF and total-HHF estimates favor empagliflozin and align directionally with the established heart-failure effect of SGLT2 inhibition described by the authors. Within EMPACT-MI, however, these are exploratory findings. The negative primary endpoint, neutral mortality component, and multiplicity framework limit the strength of inference.

Internal validity

Randomization, double blinding, high vital-status completeness, a prespecified event target, blinded sample-size adaptation, a final SAP with no stated analysis-plan changes, and independent validation of key analyses support internal validity. The main weakness is investigator-based endpoint assessment without conventional central adjudication. The prespecified definitions and verification workflow reduce, but do not eliminate, that concern.

Endpoint fit and event rate

The composite combines HHF, which appears treatment-responsive, with early post-MI all-cause mortality, which was neutral and can arise from mechanisms less likely to respond to SGLT2 inhibition. The authors also identify unexpectedly low event rates, COVID-era changes in hospitalization, regional wars, and exclusion of outpatient worsening-HF events as factors that may have reduced measured HF burden.

External validity

The trial applies most directly to patients without prior chronic heart failure who are clinically stable after acute MI but have new LV dysfunction or treated congestion plus additional risk. Nearly three quarters had STEMI and almost 90% were revascularized. Women made up 24.9%, Black or African American participants 1.4% overall, and Europe contributed 66% of enrollment. These features narrow demographic and geographic generalization even though 22 countries participated.
