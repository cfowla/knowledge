One-page synthesis

Verdict: Do not use for practice as a standalone current source. Best use: historical comparative efficacy/safety landscape and network-meta-analysis methods. It remains informative about tradeoffs among adjuncts to metformin, but its evidence base predates subsequent long-term outcome evidence and current treatment frameworks.

The authors constructed a broad network of randomized trials to compare adjunctive antidiabetic therapies after inadequate response to optimized metformin. Across the network, every evaluated therapy reduced HbA1c versus placebo, with network estimates ranging from −0.43 percentage points for miglitol to −1.29 for glibenclamide. The largest HbA1c effects were seen with glibenclamide, insulin glargine, alogliptin/pioglitazone, empagliflozin/linagliptin, and repaglinide. SGLT2 inhibitors produced moderate HbA1c lowering while consistently reducing weight and systolic blood pressure.

Tradeoffs were clinically important. Sulfonylureas and insulin glargine increased confirmed hypoglycemia; SGLT2 inhibitors had low hypoglycemia risk but increased genital tract infection risk. No therapy showed a statistically significant increase in UTI versus placebo. Weight changes separated classes strongly: SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 analogs generally lowered weight, whereas sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, and basal insulin generally increased it. Safety and long-term interpretation are constrained by short follow-up, sparse infection reporting, indirect comparisons, multiplicity, and inability to assess cardiovascular events, pancreatitis, renal dysfunction, or heart failure.

Methods and validity

Search and selection

MEDLINE and Cochrane CENTRAL were searched from inception through May 2014. The authors supplemented database searching with reference review, ClinicalTrials.gov, regulatory reports, and investigator contact. Eligibility required English-language parallel RCTs in adults with T2D receiving an approved adjunct plus metformin, inadequate response to optimized metformin, 12–52 weeks of follow-up, and HbA1c change reporting.

Two investigators independently screened citations. The S1 Appendix provides the reproducible MEDLINE strategy, while the PRISMA checklist maps reporting locations. The language restriction is a review-level limitation.

Extraction and risk of bias

Two investigators independently extracted data using a standardized tool, resolving disagreements by discussion or a third investigator. Two investigators independently rated seven Cochrane risk-of-bias domains. S2 Fig shows many trials with low-to-unclear risk across domains but meaningful pockets of high/unclear risk.

Synthesis

Pairwise analyses used DerSimonian-Laird random effects. The NMA used netmeta 0.5-0 in R 3.0.2, with random effects and common heterogeneity. Direct and indirect estimates were compared for inconsistency when possible. The authors also defined clinical-superiority thresholds (HbA1c 0.3 percentage points, body weight 2.3 kg, SBP 5 mmHg) and repeated inference using 99% CIs as a multiplicity-focused sensitivity analysis.

Where paired variance was unavailable, variance was reconstructed from reported statistics and a baseline-to-final correlation of 0.5 was assumed. This is transparent but introduces an analytic assumption.

HbA1c: all therapies lowered HbA1c statistically versus placebo. By the authors’ prespecified clinical-superiority rule, colesevelam, nateglinide, and miglitol were the exceptions that did not clear the 0.3-point threshold with 95% certainty.

Body weight: SGLT2 inhibitors showed network weight reductions of roughly 2.1 kg versus placebo; GLP-1 analogs and miglitol also reduced weight. Sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, insulin glargine, and repaglinide increased weight. No agent met the 2.3-kg clinical-superiority criterion versus placebo.

SBP: SGLT2 inhibitors lowered SBP versus placebo, with empagliflozin −5.14 mmHg and dapagliflozin −4.50 mmHg in the network. The authors found no active therapy clinically superior by their 5-mmHg threshold across the full comparison set.

Safety: insulin glargine and several sulfonylureas markedly increased confirmed hypoglycemia versus placebo. UTI risk did not significantly increase versus placebo. Genital infection risk was higher with canagliflozin (RR 8.03, 95% CI 2.44–26.39) and empagliflozin (RR 6.84, 95% CI 1.92–24.37); infection-event evidence was sparse and imprecise.

Robustness

Using 99% CIs, 91/124 HbA1c comparisons remained significant; body-weight significance was also largely retained. SBP and confirmed-hypoglycemia findings were more sensitive to the stricter interval. Eight of 151 direct-versus-indirect comparisons were statistically significant; the authors considered this little evidence of incoherence because approximately 7–8 such findings would be expected by chance at α=0.05, while acknowledging that inconsistency could not be ruled out.

Evaluation and appraisal

7/10
Relevance

Useful for historical comparative add-on efficacy/safety and NMA methods; the evidence base ends in May 2014.

Evidence basis: 62-RCT network spanning 25 active treatments plus placebo.

Principal limit: Current therapeutic decision-making should not rely on a 2014 evidence cutoff.

Would raise score: A contemporary update with current outcome trials and recommendations.

7/10
Novelty

Broad cross-class NMA with then-new SGLT2 inhibitors and multiple metabolic/safety endpoints.

Evidence basis: Six endpoints and a connected multi-class network.

Principal limit: Many comparisons are indirect and the work is now historically dated.

Would raise score: Direct head-to-head or updated network evidence.

7/10
Method strength

Systematic search, duplicate screening/extraction, Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, random-effects NMA, inconsistency and sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: Methods plus S1/S2 appendices and PRISMA checklist.

Principal limit: English-only inclusion, common-heterogeneity assumption, multiplicity, and imputed paired variance in some cases.

Would raise score: Broader-language search, formal certainty grading, modern NMA diagnostics, prespecified protocol.

6/10
Evidence strength

Large aggregate sample and coherent network for surrogate/metabolic outcomes, but safety data are sparse and follow-up is short.

Evidence basis: 62 RCTs, n=32,185; most HbA1c results robust to stricter CIs.

Principal limit: Indirect evidence, sparse infection events, imprecision, and no long-term cardiorenal outcomes.

Would raise score: Longer follow-up and patient-centered outcome evidence.

5/10
External validity

Participants had inadequately controlled T2D on optimized metformin, but trial populations and 12–52-week horizons constrain generalizability.

Evidence basis: Included RCT population and treatment window.

Principal limit: Trial selection and historical treatment landscape.

Would raise score: Contemporary pragmatic and diverse-population evidence.

4/10
Implementation value

Useful to understand tradeoffs but not sufficient as a current standalone treatment-selection source.

Evidence basis: Clear comparative HbA1c/weight/SBP/hypoglycemia/infection patterns.

Principal limit: Outdated evidence cutoff and absence of modern outcome-driven treatment considerations.

Would raise score: Updated comparative synthesis linked to current clinical guidance.

Main strengths

  • Large connected RCT network with duplicate screening/extraction and independent risk-of-bias assessment.
  • Both direct and indirect evidence were synthesized; inconsistency was explicitly tested.
  • Weight, SBP, hypoglycemia, UTI, and GTI make the review more decision-relevant than HbA1c alone.
  • Multiplicity was confronted through 99% CI sensitivity analyses rather than ignored.

Main limitations

  • English-only inclusion and possible publication bias.
  • Short 12–52-week trials and inability to evaluate major long-term outcomes.
  • Sparse UTI/GTI data and very wide CIs for several safety comparisons.
  • Hundreds of comparisons across six endpoints increase type-I error risk; some findings weaken materially under stricter CIs.
  • Network estimates rely on transitivity/common-heterogeneity assumptions and indirect evidence for many comparisons.
  • Industry funding and one author’s disclosed grants warrant awareness but do not, by themselves, establish bias.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use for
Historical class tradeoffs; methods teaching; understanding how NMA can compare many add-on therapies.
Do not use for
Current standalone drug selection, long-term cardiorenal outcome decisions, or contemporary formulary policy without newer evidence.
High-value finding
SGLT2 inhibitors showed a favorable weight/SBP/hypoglycemia profile in this historical network, offset by genital-infection risk.
Interpret cautiously
Safety endpoints with sparse events, active-vs-active indirect comparisons, and findings that lose significance under 99% CIs.
