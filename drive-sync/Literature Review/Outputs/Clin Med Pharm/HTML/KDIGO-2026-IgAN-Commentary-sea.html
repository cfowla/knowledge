Source classification. Six-page KDIGO editorial special report and commentary. It is a stopgap after the 2025 IgAN guideline and explicitly does not make recommendations or Practice Points.

Summary

The commentary updates the treatment map after accelerated US approvals of atrasentan, iptacopan, and sibeprenlimab following the 2025 guideline. It also discusses atacicept, which was under FDA review in the source. The authors place these therapies into a mechanistic framework based on the four-hit hypothesis of IgAN while keeping CKD-directed therapy active in parallel.

The central caution is comparative. Proteinuria and eGFR results come from separate trials with different populations and designs. The authors state that the therapies cannot be ranked reliably without head-to-head comparisons, and Table 1 is not intended for direct cross-trial comparison.

Therapeutic framework

Figure 1 modifies the 2025 KDIGO treatment map. Nefecon, APRIL inhibitors, and dual APRIL/BAFF inhibitors are positioned mainly against the production of pathogenic IgA and IgA-containing immune complexes, corresponding predominantly to hits 1 through 3. Systemic glucocorticoids and complement inhibitors are positioned mainly against immune-complex-mediated kidney injury at hit 4. Lifestyle measures, RAS inhibition or dual endothelin angiotensin receptor antagonism, and SGLT2 inhibition address generic responses to nephron loss.

The figure does not set a ranking or treatment sequence. The authors also avoid emphasizing combinations because clinical data on combining the new agents were not available.

Evaluation

Strength. The commentary is current to April 15, 2026 for its comparative table and gives a concise mechanistic placement of newly available or emerging therapies. It clearly distinguishes evidence already available for eGFR from outcomes still pending.
Evidence boundary. This is expert commentary, not a new systematic review. Its trial results are secondary reports from the cited studies. Primary-study extraction is required before treating those trial estimates as primary LiteratureAtoms.
Clinical boundary. The document deliberately does not issue recommendations or Practice Points. It helps organize treatment choices but cannot substitute for the next formal KDIGO update, product labeling, or patient-specific decision-making.
Uncertainty. Head-to-head comparisons are absent. Combination therapy, treatment duration, switching, maintenance, low-level proteinuria, hematuria, and long-term safety for newer mechanisms remain unresolved.
