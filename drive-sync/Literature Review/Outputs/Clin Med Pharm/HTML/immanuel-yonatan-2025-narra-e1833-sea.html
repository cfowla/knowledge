Scope warning. This is a secondary synthesis. It can inform interpretation of class-level early initiation in AHF, but it does not replace direct reading of the contributing RCTs or establish head-to-head equivalence between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Across nine RCTs, early inpatient dapagliflozin or empagliflozin was associated with lower pooled mortality, less in-hospital worsening HF, and fewer serious adverse events, while rehospitalization, length of stay, diuretic response, serum sodium, and most specific adverse events were not significantly changed.

The strongest source-reported pooled effects were all-cause mortality (RR 0.58, 95% CI 0.36–0.91), in-hospital worsening HF (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.43–0.86), and serious adverse events (RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.59–0.90). The authors rated these outcomes high certainty by GRADE. Several other outcomes were moderate certainty, while discharge serum sodium was low certainty because of heterogeneity and imprecision.

Interpretation requires caution. The mortality endpoint is repeatedly called “in-hospital,” but the GRADE table gives 5–90-day follow-up; the AKI row prints an RR whose direction conflicts with the displayed raw event rates; and there are additional count/label inconsistencies. Follow-up was short, the population was diabetes-heavy, and background HF therapy was incompletely accounted for. These issues lower independent confidence below the article’s most favorable GRADE labels.

Methods

The review followed PRISMA 2020 and reported PROSPERO registration CRD42024585838. The authors searched PubMed, ProQuest, SAGE Journals, EBSCOhost, Wiley Online Library, Google Scholar, and the Cochrane Library on July 22, 2024, restricted to English-language studies from 2019–2024, and manually screened relevant review references. Adults with AHF were eligible when dapagliflozin or empagliflozin was initiated within five hospitalization days; non-RCTs, pediatric studies, other SGLT2 inhibitors, non-AHF populations, later initiation, missing prespecified outcomes, and non-English reports were excluded.

Three investigators independently screened/extracted data and assessed RoB 2; disagreements were resolved with senior authors. Dichotomous outcomes used Mantel-Haenszel RRs and continuous outcomes inverse-variance SMDs, both under random effects. I² >50% or chi-squared p<0.10 indicated substantial heterogeneity. GRADE was applied. A methods tension remains: the paper says high-risk studies were excluded from analysis, yet also describes sensitivity analyses that remove high-risk studies.

Results and quantitative evidence

The review included nine RCTs (2020–2024) with 1,417 participants. The Results narrative reports 28.44% received early dapagliflozin, 21.52% early empagliflozin, 70.94% had ADHF, 29.06% de novo HF, and approximately 49.36% diabetes. Table 1 instead reports 51.1% diabetes. Four studies had “some concerns” on RoB 2; the rest were rated low risk.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High clinical relevance and useful class-level inpatient evidence, but methodological/reporting inconsistencies and limited follow-up mean it should not be the sole basis for formulary interchange or agent equivalence.
Relevance9/10

Directly addresses early inpatient SGLT2 inhibitor initiation in acute heart failure.

Evidence: Nine acute-HF RCTs; clinically meaningful mortality, worsening-HF, rehospitalization and safety outcomes.
Limiting factor: Does not directly test formulary equivalence between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin.
Would raise confidence: Head-to-head randomized or high-quality comparative effectiveness evidence.
Novelty7/10

Focuses specifically on initiation within the first five hospital days and synthesizes multiple inpatient outcomes.

Evidence: Timing-focused inclusion criteria and agent subgroup analysis.
Limiting factor: Builds on prior acute-HF SGLT2 evidence and includes overlapping trial programs.
Would raise confidence: Newer trials with longer follow-up and prespecified timing comparisons.
Method strength6/10

PRISMA, PROSPERO, RoB 2, GRADE and random-effects pooling are strengths, but selection/search and internal-reporting issues constrain confidence.

Evidence: Seven databases, independent screening, RoB 2, GRADE, nine RCTs.
Limiting factor: English/2019–2024 restriction; high-risk-study inclusion/sensitivity logic tension; several internal inconsistencies.
Would raise confidence: A fully reproducible search, protocol-consistent risk-of-bias handling, and reconciliation of source discrepancies.
Evidence strength6/10

Several pooled clinical outcomes favor early SGLT2i, but event counts are limited and some endpoint labels/rows are internally inconsistent.

Evidence: Mortality RR 0.58 and worsening-HF RR 0.61 with I²=0%; authors rate both high-certainty.
Limiting factor: Mortality time-window conflict, AKI-row inconsistency, short follow-up, and imprecise secondary/safety outcomes.
Would raise confidence: Larger event counts, consistent endpoint definitions, and long-term outcome confirmation.
External validity5/10

The included AHF trials are clinically relevant but heavily diabetic and heterogeneous in design, phenotype and background therapy.

Evidence: Nine RCTs spanning ADHF and de novo HF.
Limiting factor: Majority diabetes; short follow-up; concomitant/background therapy incompletely handled.
Would raise confidence: Broader non-diabetic enrollment and clearer renal, blood-pressure and stabilization boundaries.
Implementation value6/10

Supports class-level early initiation as a plausible inpatient strategy but does not establish dapagliflozin-only substitution for empagliflozin.

Evidence: Early initiation within five days showed favorable pooled mortality/worsening-HF and no excess specific adverse events.
Limiting factor: No direct dapagliflozin-vs-empagliflozin efficacy/safety comparison and limited operational thresholds.
Would raise confidence: Direct comparative evidence plus explicit initiation/hold criteria and longer safety follow-up.

Main strengths

  • Focused early-initiation question in hospitalized AHF.
  • RCT-only synthesis with PRISMA, PROSPERO, RoB 2, random-effects pooling, and GRADE.
  • Includes patient-centered outcomes (mortality, worsening HF, rehospitalization) and clinically relevant safety outcomes.
  • Low statistical heterogeneity for several major outcomes.

Main limitations

  • Short and variable follow-up; mortality time window is internally inconsistent.
  • Diabetes-heavy population and incompletely characterized background HF therapy limit generalizability.
  • English-language and 2019–2024 search restrictions may omit evidence.
  • Multiple source-internal count, label, and effect inconsistencies reduce confidence in extraction/reporting fidelity.
  • No direct randomized comparison of dapagliflozin versus empagliflozin; pooled class inference should not be mistaken for therapeutic equivalence.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this can support

Class-level consideration of early SGLT2 inhibitor initiation during AHF hospitalization after appropriate clinical stabilization, with no clear pooled signal of excess AKI, hypotension, hypoglycemia, or UTI in this review.

What it cannot establish

A dapagliflozin-only formulary policy as equivalent to empagliflozin. The source pools both drugs and does not provide a head-to-head randomized comparison with sufficient agent-specific outcomes.

What to verify before practice change

Direct primary RCT evidence, current guideline/label boundaries, renal-function and hemodynamic initiation criteria, ketoacidosis risk/hold rules, and agent-specific comparative effectiveness.

Source-integrity findings

  1. Figure 1 / Study selection: Narrative says 473 database records plus 1 hand-search record = 474; the database-specific counts shown in Figure 1 sum to 474 before adding the hand-search record, so the component counts do not reconcile with the stated total. Preserved the reported totals and recorded the mismatch; no count was silently corrected.
  2. Results vs Table 1: Results narrative reports approximately 49.36% of patients had diabetes, whereas the Table 1 summary reports 51.1%. Both source-reported values were retained and tagged as inconsistent.
  3. Mortality outcome heading/abstract vs Table 2: The paper repeatedly describes the mortality endpoint as in-hospital all-cause mortality, while Table 2 labels follow-up as ranging from 5 to 90 days. The atom uses the Table 2 follow-up range and flags the source labeling conflict.
  4. Table 2 acute kidney injury row: Table 2 reports 20/420 control vs 15/423 early SGLT2i but prints RR 1.20 (95% CI 0.27–5.25), a direction inconsistent with the displayed event rates; it also labels 3 RCTs while listing only references [15,16]. Reported RR, CI, event counts, participant count, and study count were preserved exactly; discrepancy flagged without recalculation.
  5. Methods risk-of-bias inclusion vs sensitivity analysis: Methods state that only low-risk or some-concerns studies were included, yet sensitivity analysis is described as removing high-risk studies. Both statements retained as a methods-logic inconsistency.
  6. Adverse-event narrative vs Figure/Table labels: The Results sentence refers to symptomatic hypertension, while Figure 12 and Table 2 specify symptomatic hypotension. Outcome atom follows the figure/table label (symptomatic hypotension) and the prose typo is flagged.
  7. Serious adverse events narrative vs pooled figure/table: Results prose attributes the significant serious-adverse-event reduction to empagliflozin, while Figure 15 and Table 2 label the pooled intervention as early dapagliflozin and empagliflozin. The atom follows the pooled Table 2/figure label and records the intervention-label discrepancy.

No discrepancy was silently repaired.
