One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. EMPA-KIDNEY is a pivotal kidney-outcomes trial because it demonstrates substantial kidney-protection from empagliflozin in a CKD population substantially broader than earlier SGLT2 trials, including large groups without diabetes, with eGFR below 30, and with lower albuminuria.

The trial randomized 6609 adults with CKD to empagliflozin 10 mg daily or placebo. The primary composite of kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death occurred in 13.1% versus 16.9% (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.64–0.82; P<0.001), equivalent to 42 fewer primary events per 1000 treated over roughly 2 years. Kidney disease progression alone was similarly reduced (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.62–0.81), and recurrent all-cause hospitalization was lower (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78–0.95). Heart-failure hospitalization/CV death and all-cause mortality were directionally favorable but not statistically significant.

The key nuance is low albuminuria. Event-based primary-outcome estimates were imprecise at UACR <30 mg/g (HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.66–1.55) and 30–300 mg/g (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.65–1.26), where fewer events occurred. In contrast, prespecified eGFR-slope analyses showed slower chronic decline even at low albuminuria. The paper therefore supports kidney-function preservation across a broader CKD spectrum, but it should not be summarized as proving an equally precise event reduction in every albuminuria stratum.

Methods and validity

Design and population

EMPA-KIDNEY was conducted at 241 centers in eight countries. Adults were eligible with eGFR 20 to <45 mL/min/1.73 m² regardless of albuminuria, or eGFR 45 to <90 with UACR ≥200 mg/g. RAS inhibition was expected when tolerated and indicated. The final protocol excluded, among others, current SGLT2/SGLT1/2 inhibitor treatment, polycystic kidney disease, maintenance dialysis/functioning transplant/scheduled living-donor transplant, recent ketoacidosis, and type 1 diabetes. A single-blind placebo run-in preceded randomization.

Randomization, blinding, and follow-up

Participants were randomized 1:1 to empagliflozin 10 mg daily or matching placebo using minimization on age, sex, diabetes, eGFR, UACR, and region with a 10% stochastic component. Visits occurred at 2 and 6 months and then every 6 months. Follow-up completeness was high: 99.1% completed final follow-up or died, with vital status missing for 0.3% and consent withdrawal in 0.6%.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary outcome was first kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death. Kidney progression included ESKD, sustained eGFR <10, sustained ≥40% eGFR decline, or renal death. Analyses were intention-to-treat. Cox models adjusted for minimization factors; key secondary outcomes used Hochberg multiplicity control; recurrent hospitalization used a joint frailty model; eGFR slope used shared-parameter models. A formal interim analysis and efficacy-stopping boundary were prespecified. The DMC reported both stopping criteria met on March 7, 2022; final follow-up continued through July 5, 2022.

Methodological strength: prespecified event-driven design, allocation concealment/blinding, near-complete follow-up, centralized/statistically explicit endpoint handling, blinded adjudication of selected clinical events, and a final DAP defined before unblinding.

Kidney-function trajectory

There was the expected early eGFR dip after starting empagliflozin followed by slower decline. The total slope difference was 0.75 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (95% CI 0.54–0.96), and the chronic slope from month 2 was 1.37 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (95% CI 1.16–1.59) in favor of empagliflozin. UACR was 19% lower on average.

Safety

Serious adverse-event rates were broadly similar. Table 2 showed serious AKI HR 0.78 (0.60–1.00), serious hyperkalemia HR 0.83 (0.63–1.09), and serious UTI HR 0.94 (0.64–1.37). Ketoacidosis was uncommon but numerically higher with empagliflozin (6 vs 1; 0.09 vs 0.02/100 patient-years), as were amputations (28 vs 19; HR 1.43, 0.80–2.57), with wide uncertainty.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 10/10
Direct large CKD SGLT2 outcome trial with broad applicability.
Evidence: Randomized CKD population spanning diabetes status, low eGFR, and albuminuria.
Limiting factor: The source predates later follow-up and guideline integration.
Would change: Would remain high; practice impact depends on downstream guideline/label context.
Novelty: 9/10
Substantially broadened CKD evidence beyond earlier diabetic/high-albuminuria SGLT2 trials.
Evidence: 54% without diabetes; 34.5% eGFR <30; 48.3% UACR ≤300.
Limiting factor: Short median follow-up limited long-horizon differentiation.
Would change: Longer-term randomized/post-trial data would improve maturity.
Method strength: 9/10
Large double-blind placebo-controlled event-driven trial with prespecified interim monitoring and near-complete follow-up.
Evidence: 6609 randomized; 99.1% completed final follow-up or died; ITT; prespecified multiplicity and adjudication.
Limiting factor: Early efficacy stopping and missing supplied Supplementary Appendix limit some secondary-method review.
Would change: Direct inspection of the full Supplementary Appendix and independent replication would raise confidence.
Evidence strength: 9/10
Primary kidney/CV composite showed a precise, clinically meaningful reduction with concordant kidney-progression results.
Evidence: HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.64–0.82); kidney progression HR 0.71 (0.62–0.81).
Limiting factor: Some CV secondary outcomes were underpowered and nonsignificant; low-albuminuria event counts were limited.
Would change: Longer follow-up and more CV events would strengthen secondary outcome inference.
External validity: 9/10
Broad CKD eligibility improves generalizability across diabetes and kidney-function strata.
Evidence: Participants recruited at 241 centers in eight countries with substantial non-diabetic and advanced CKD representation.
Limiting factor: Trial exclusions, run-in selection, 33% female representation, and limited precision at very low albuminuria constrain universality.
Would change: More evidence in underrepresented populations and low-event-risk CKD would raise the score.
Implementation value: 9/10
The regimen is simple and the effect size, endpoint definitions, and safety signals are directly interpretable for CKD treatment decisions.
Evidence: Empagliflozin 10 mg daily; absolute primary-event difference 42/1000 over ~2 years.
Limiting factor: Implementation still requires current labeling/guideline, renal monitoring, sick-day/perioperative, and local formulary context beyond this trial.
Would change: Current guideline/label crosswalk would convert evidence into a practice-ready recommendation.

Main strengths

  • Large, blinded, placebo-controlled randomized design with prespecified statistical plan.
  • Broad CKD phenotype with major representation of non-diabetic and advanced CKD.
  • Near-complete follow-up and high reported adherence.
  • Concordance between the event-based kidney outcome and eGFR-slope trajectory.

Main limitations

  • Early efficacy stopping shortened randomized exposure and may reduce precision for slower secondary outcomes.
  • Lower-than-expected cardiovascular event count reduced power for cardiovascular secondary/tertiary outcomes.
  • Low-albuminuria subgroups had fewer primary events, making event-based subgroup estimates imprecise despite favorable chronic eGFR-slope data.
  • The source packet omitted the separate Supplementary Appendix, limiting direct review of S-tables/S-figures and some sensitivity analyses.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this trial supports
Empagliflozin 10 mg daily materially lowers kidney-progression/CV-death risk in CKD populations broader than earlier diabetic/high-albuminuria trials.
What not to overclaim
Do not claim statistically precise event reduction in every low-albuminuria stratum; distinguish subgroup event estimates from chronic eGFR-slope evidence.
Safety use
Overall serious events were similar, but ketoacidosis, dehydration, genital/urinary infection, AKI, hyperkalemia, and amputation remain clinically relevant monitored outcomes.
What is still needed for practice
Current guideline/label eligibility, perioperative/sick-day management, renal monitoring, and local formulary interchange rules must be layered on separately.
