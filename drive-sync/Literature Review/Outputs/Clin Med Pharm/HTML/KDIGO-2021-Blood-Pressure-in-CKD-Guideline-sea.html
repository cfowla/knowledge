Executive synthesis

Verdict: foundational and highly implementable, but inseparable from standardized BP measurement and limited by evidence currency through April 2020.

The guideline's central operational change is not merely a lower blood-pressure number. It explicitly couples treatment targets to standardized office BP measurement. For most adults with high BP and CKD not receiving dialysis, it suggests an SBP target <120 mm Hg when tolerated, but warns that applying this target to non-standardized readings can be hazardous. It also formalizes albuminuria- and diabetes-stratified use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs, gives explicit monitoring and continuation rules for RAS inhibition, separates transplant and pediatric targets, and makes sodium restriction and physical activity the two principal lifestyle interventions.

The strongest interpretive constraint is that the adult SBP target is driven largely by cardiovascular and survival evidence, especially SPRINT, rather than convincing kidney-protection evidence at SBP <120 mm Hg. Direct evidence is less certain in diabetes, advanced CKD, marked proteinuria, very low DBP, white-coat hypertension, and extreme ages. The source therefore supports a measurement-plus-target-plus-monitoring strategy, not a universal isolated threshold.

Chapter 1 — Blood pressure measurement

Recommendation 1.1 (1B) prioritizes standardized office measurement over routine office measurement. KDIGO's rationale is practical: large outcome trials used standardized methods, routine office readings are generally—but not invariably—higher, and the individual relationship is too variable to support a simple correction factor.

Standardized preparation
  • Relax seated, feet on floor and back supported, for >5 minutes.
  • Avoid caffeine, exercise, and smoking for at least 30 minutes.
  • Empty the bladder; no talking during rest or measurement.
  • Remove clothing from the cuff site.
Technique and averaging
  • Use a validated, periodically calibrated device and correct cuff size.
  • Support the arm and position the cuff at right-atrial level.
  • At first visit measure both arms; use the higher-reading arm subsequently.
  • Separate repeated readings by 1–2 minutes and average at least 2 readings on at least 2 occasions.

AOBP may be preferred, but the definition of standardized BP is driven by preparation and technique rather than whether the device is automated. ABPM or HBPM is suggested as a complement to standardized office BP, not a replacement for the source's office-target evidence.

Source: Figure 2 and Recommendations/Practice Points 1.1–1.3, S23 and S26–S31.

Chapter 2 — Lifestyle interventions

KDIGO suggests sodium intake below 2 g sodium/day (<90 mmol sodium/day or <5 g sodium chloride/day) and at least 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity physical activity when compatible with cardiovascular and physical tolerance. It explicitly exempts sodium-wasting nephropathy from routine sodium restriction and cautions that potassium-rich DASH-style diets or salt substitutes may be inappropriate when potassium excretion is impaired.

Chapter 3 — Adults with CKD, with or without diabetes, not receiving dialysis

SBP target: <120 mm Hg, when tolerated, using standardized office BP

The target recommendation is Level 2, moderate certainty (2B). KDIGO gives higher weight to cardiovascular and survival benefit than to kidney protection. It also explicitly permits less intensive therapy for very limited life expectancy or symptomatic postural hypotension.

SPRINT anchor

In the overall 9,361-participant trial, intensive treatment reduced the primary cardiovascular outcome (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64–0.89) and all-cause death (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.60–0.90). SPRINT included 2,646 participants with CKD.

CKD subgroup

Mean eGFR was 47.9 ± 9.5 ml/min/1.73 m² and median ACR 13.3 mg/g. The CKD subgroup cardiovascular estimate was HR 0.81 (95% CI 0.63–1.05); mortality benefit was nominally significant. KDIGO notes SPRINT was not powered for subgroup analyses.

Do not detach the target from measurement. Practice Point 3.1.1 calls it potentially hazardous to apply the <120 target to non-standardized readings.

The guideline identifies important uncertainty in diabetes, CKD G4–G5, significant proteinuria, very low DBP, white-coat hypertension, and extreme ages. In adults ≥80 years in SPRINT, a secondary analysis favored intensive BP lowering for cardiovascular events (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.49–0.90) and all-cause mortality (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.48–0.93), but this remains subgroup evidence.

RAS inhibition: decision and monitoring rules

ACEi or ARB initiation is strongest for A3 albuminuria without diabetes and A2–A3 albuminuria with diabetes (both 1B); A2 without diabetes is 2C. KDIGO's implementation rules are unusually specific and clinically useful:

  • Use the highest approved tolerated dose used to achieve trial benefits.
  • Check BP, serum creatinine, and potassium within 2–4 weeks of initiation or dose increase.
  • Manage RASi-associated hyperkalemia when possible rather than reflexively reducing or stopping RASi.
  • Continue ACEi/ARB unless serum creatinine rises by more than 30% within 4 weeks after initiation or dose increase.
  • Consider dose reduction/discontinuation for symptomatic hypotension, uncontrolled hyperkalemia despite treatment, or to reduce uremic symptoms in kidney failure with eGFR <15 ml/min/1.73 m².
  • Avoid ACEi + ARB + DRI combinations in any combination (1B).

Chapter 4 — Kidney transplant recipients

The guideline retains a target of <130/80 mm Hg using standardized office measurement rather than extending the adult <120 mm Hg SBP target to transplant recipients. It recommends a dihydropyridine CCB or an ARB as first-line therapy (1C). The evidence base is thinner than for non-transplant CKD, and graft outcomes rather than cardiovascular target-trial evidence are central to the treatment-class discussion.

Figure 7 summarizes CCB versus placebo/no treatment for graft loss; the supplement separately profiles CCBs, ARBs, ACEi, beta-blockers, alpha-blockers, MRAs, and combination strategies in transplant recipients.

Chapter 5 — Children with CKD

KDIGO suggests lowering 24-hour ambulatory MAP to the ≤50th percentile for age, sex, and height (2C). It suggests annual ABPM plus standardized auscultatory office BP every 3–6 months. Where ABPM is unavailable, standardized manual auscultatory office SBP below the 90th percentile is a reasonable fallback.

The principal randomized evidence is the ESCAPE trial (385 participants), which compared 24-hour MAP <50th percentile with the 50th–99th percentile. KDIGO summarizes probable slowing of kidney disease progression without greater hypotension or acute GFR decline; the trial was not powered for all-cause mortality. In the source rationale, the composite of 50% GFR decline and ESKD favored intensive BP control (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.44–0.94). Both groups received ramipril.

ACEi or ARB is the preferred first-line class for high BP in children with CKD, with explicit recognition of proteinuria benefit, hyperkalemia risk, and fetal risk in pregnancy.

Guideline methods and evidence architecture

The source uses an independent Evidence Review Team, PICOM questions, systematic searching, risk-of-bias appraisal, evidence synthesis/meta-analysis where appropriate, and GRADE for certainty and recommendation strength. The data supplement maps development to Institute of Medicine standards and documents the search strategies and evidence profiles.

Recommendations

Used when systematic review evidence supports an actionable choice. Level 1 means strong; Level 2 means weak/conditional. Certainty is A–D.

Practice Points

Ungraded guidance used when a systematic review was not done, evidence was insufficient for a graded recommendation, or implementation/good-practice guidance was needed.

Evidence currency. The source's formal literature base was updated through April 2020. This SEA does not externally verify changes after that date. Any use for current 2026 treatment policy should therefore include a separate recency check.

Appraisal

10/10

Relevance

Directly addresses BP measurement, targets, lifestyle, and antihypertensive management across major CKD subpopulations.

8/10

Novelty

At publication, standardized measurement and the <120 mm Hg SBP target represented major changes from the 2012 guideline; novelty is now historical rather than current.

9/10

Method strength

Independent evidence review, explicit systematic reviews, GRADE, public review, and a 329-page data supplement provide strong methodological transparency.

7/10

Evidence strength

Strong for several central decisions, but the main adult SBP target leans heavily on SPRINT and its CKD subgroup, while transplant, pediatric, advanced CKD, diabetes, and other subgroups have thinner direct evidence.

8/10

External validity

Broad global CKD scope with adults, transplant recipients, and children, but maintenance dialysis is excluded and several clinically important subgroups were underrepresented in the key target trial evidence.

10/10

Implementation value

The source couples targets with measurement protocols, lifestyle thresholds, medication selection, RASi monitoring rules, and explicit exceptions.

Main strengths

  • Strong linkage between a treatment target and the measurement method that generated the evidence.
  • Clear GRADE language and explicit distinction between graded recommendations and Practice Points.
  • High implementation specificity for RASi dosing, creatinine/potassium monitoring, and continuation/discontinuation decisions.
  • Large, unusually detailed data supplement with search strategies and 104 clinical evidence/SoF tables beyond the three methods tables.
  • Separate guidance for transplant recipients and children rather than unqualified extrapolation from adult non-dialysis CKD.

Main limitations

  • The central adult SBP target depends heavily on SPRINT and its CKD subgroup; direct evidence is weaker in diabetes, advanced CKD, marked proteinuria, and several other high-risk subgroups.
  • The kidney-protection case for SBP <120 mm Hg is substantially weaker than the cardiovascular/survival case.
  • Maintenance dialysis is explicitly outside scope.
  • Many implementation statements are ungraded Practice Points rather than independently graded treatment effects.
  • Evidence searches are now historically dated; the final update was April 2020.

Implementation takeaways

Measure first

Do not operationalize the <120 mm Hg target unless the clinical workflow can reproduce standardized measurement.

Make RASi monitoring explicit

Encode 2–4 week creatinine/potassium follow-up, the >30% creatinine-rise rule, and hyperkalemia mitigation before discontinuation.

Separate populations

Adult non-dialysis CKD, transplant recipients, and children have distinct BP targets and evidence bases.

Keep certainty attached

Store recommendation strength/certainty separately from Practice Point status; do not flatten all guidance into equivalent rules.
