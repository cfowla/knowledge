One-page synthesis

KEYNOTE-811 tested pembrolizumab versus placebo on top of trastuzumab and platinum-fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy. The first interim analysis showed a large improvement in objective response. ORR was 74.4% with pembrolizumab and 51.9% with placebo, an absolute difference of 22.7 percentage points, 95% CI 11.2 to 33.7, P=0.00006. Complete response was 11.3% versus 3.1%, and tumor shrinkage was deeper by several measures. Duration-of-response estimates also favored pembrolizumab at this cutoff.

The main constraint is timing. PFS and OS were the trial's dual primary endpoints, but neither was reported in this manuscript. Safety was broadly similar for overall grade 3-5 adverse events, 57.1% versus 57.4%, while immune-mediated events and infusion reactions were more common with pembrolizumab, 33.6% versus 20.8%. This makes the article strong evidence for an early response signal, not a complete account of long-term clinical benefit.

Verdict: Read soon. It is a key interim randomized trial report and useful for understanding why the combination moved quickly. For treatment decisions, pair it with mature KEYNOTE-811 survival results, the current label, and current gastric-cancer guidelines.

Section condensation

Summary and rationale

The paper starts from the established HER2-positive gastric/GEJ setting, where trastuzumab plus chemotherapy had become first-line therapy. The authors give a mechanistic rationale for adding PD-1 blockade, citing HER2 internalization and cross-presentation, HER2-specific T-cell responses, immune modulation by trastuzumab, and immunogenic effects of oxaliplatin.

Two earlier single-arm phase II experiences had suggested activity and manageable safety. KEYNOTE-811 was designed to test the combination in a randomized, double-blind phase III setting.

Interim results

By June 17, 2020, 434 participants had been randomized. The first 264 formed the protocol-specified ORR efficacy population. ORR was 74.4% versus 51.9%, with a 22.7 percentage-point difference. Complete responses were 11.3% versus 3.1%. Median best target-lesion change was -65% versus -49%.

Safety included 433 treated participants. Grade 3-5 adverse events were almost identical between arms. Immune-mediated events and infusion reactions were more frequent with pembrolizumab. Seven versus ten participants died from adverse events; investigators considered two deaths in each group treatment-related.

Discussion

The authors interpret the ORR result as clinically meaningful and consistent with synergy between HER2 and PD-1 blockade. They describe response depth and durability as better with pembrolizumab and the safety profile as manageable.

The discussion itself states the major unresolved point. PFS and OS were still pending. Biomarker analysis was also early. Only MSI-high status and PD-L1 expression had been explored, and the PD-L1 CPS below 1 subgroup was small and imprecise.

Methods and protocol supplement

Participants were randomized 1:1 to pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks or placebo, each with trastuzumab and investigator-selected FP or CAPOX. Randomization was stratified by region, PD-L1 CPS, and chemotherapy. Treatment could continue for up to 35 cycles. Imaging began at week 6 and repeated every 6 weeks. BICR RECIST 1.1 drove efficacy endpoints.

The supplement adds detailed cardiac, laboratory, immune, infection, neurologic, and prior-treatment eligibility restrictions. It also specifies the PFS/OS log-rank and Cox methods, ORR Miettinen-Nurminen analysis, censoring rules, interim monitoring, multiplicity plan, and approximately 692-participant global-cohort target.

Figures and tables

Fig. 1. Best percentage change in target-lesion size

Waterfall plots compare evaluable participants in the pembrolizumab and placebo groups. The pembrolizumab distribution is shifted toward larger tumor reductions. The manuscript reports median best change of -65% versus -49%, and at least 80% reduction in 32.3% versus 14.8%.

Only 124 pembrolizumab and 122 placebo participants had both measurable baseline disease and an evaluable post-baseline measurement for this figure.

Table 1. Confirmed objective response

ORR was 74.4% versus 51.9%. Complete response was 11.3% versus 3.1%, partial response 63.2% versus 48.9%, and disease control 96.2% versus 89.3%.

Table 2. Adverse-event summary

Any-grade adverse events were nearly universal in both groups, 97.2% versus 98.1%. Grade 3-5 events were 57.1% versus 57.4%. Serious events were 31.3% versus 38.4%. Events leading to death were 3.2% versus 4.6%.

Extended Data Fig. 1. ORR subgroup differences

The overall ORR difference was 22.7 percentage points. Most subgroup point estimates favored pembrolizumab, but several intervals were wide. The PD-L1 CPS at least 1 subgroup showed a difference of 25.2 percentage points, 95% CI 12.8 to 36.9. The CPS below 1 subgroup showed 4.6 percentage points, 95% CI -27.6 to 35.4.

These subgroup estimates are less precise than the overall result and were not the trial's primary endpoint.

Extended Data Tables 1-3. Follow-up, disposition, baseline balance

Follow-up and disposition support the interim timing. Baseline characteristics were broadly balanced. The ITT population was 81.3% male, 68.4% had stomach primary tumors, 80.6% had HER2 IHC 3+ disease, and 86.6% received CAPOX.

Extended Data Tables 4-6. Detailed safety

Common adverse events included diarrhea, nausea, and anemia. Possible immune-mediated events and infusion reactions were more frequent with pembrolizumab, 33.6% versus 20.8%. Pneumonitis occurred in 5.1% versus 1.4%. Treatment-related deaths were 0.9% in each group.

6/10

Evidence strength

The ORR effect is large and statistically precise, but it is a secondary response endpoint from an early interim cut while PFS and OS were still pending.

Evidence basis. ORR difference 22.7 percentage points, 95% CI 11.2 to 33.7, P=0.00006; deeper tumor shrinkage and more complete responses were also reported.

Limiting factor. Response is not the trial's dual primary endpoint and does not establish survival benefit in this source.

What would change it. Mature PFS/OS results with consistent safety would materially raise the score.

7/10

External validity

The trial is multinational and uses common HER2 testing and chemotherapy backbones, but eligibility selects fit patients with ECOG 0-1 and protocol cardiac/organ-function requirements.

Evidence basis. 168 centers across 19 countries; 434 randomized participants in the interim database.

Limiting factor. The population was 81.3% male, and the protocol excludes several comorbid or higher-risk groups.

What would change it. Broader real-world evidence and subgroup precision would raise the score.

6/10

Implementation value

The paper and protocol provide enough treatment and monitoring detail to understand the regimen, but this 2021 interim report should not be used alone as current practice guidance.

Evidence basis. Doses, schedules, HER2 testing, imaging, safety collection, and analysis methods are explicit.

Limiting factor. Current regulatory status, guideline position, mature survival evidence, and later label changes were not verified in this source-only pass.

What would change it. Pairing the source with current guidelines, label, and mature KEYNOTE-811 results would raise the score.

Main strengths

Randomized and double-blind allocation, placebo control, central HER2 testing, blinded independent central response review, prespecified interim analysis, explicit multiplicity control, and a large treatment effect on ORR all support the validity of the reported interim finding.

Main limitations

The paper reports a secondary response endpoint before the dual primary endpoints matured. The ORR efficacy population is a fixed early subset of 264 participants. The trial also selects ECOG 0-1 patients with substantial cardiac and organ-function eligibility requirements. Sponsor funding, sponsor-employed coauthors, stock ownership, and broad industry relationships require attention when interpreting the framing, even though the design includes several bias-control measures.
