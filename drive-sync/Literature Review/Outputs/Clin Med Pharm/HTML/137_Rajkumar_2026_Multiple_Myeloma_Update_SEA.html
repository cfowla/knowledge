One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Modern multiple myeloma care is organized around precise diagnostic thresholds, cytogenetic risk, transplant eligibility/frailty, anti-CD38-based quadruplet induction, risk-adapted maintenance, and increasingly early use of CAR-T or bispecific immunotherapy at relapse. High-risk smoldering myeloma has moved from observation-only toward preventive daratumumab in the author’s framework.

Diagnosis and risk. The review retains IMWG myeloma-defining events: CRAB end-organ injury plus marrow plasmacytosis ≥60%, involved/uninvolved FLC ratio ≥100 with involved FLC ≥100 mg/L, or >1 MRI focal lesion, on a background of ≥10% clonal marrow plasma cells or plasmacytoma. High-risk disease is defined primarily by del(17p)/p53, bi-allelic del(1p), combinations of adverse IgH translocations with gain(1q) or del(1p), or gain(1q)+del(1p). mSMART adds plasma cell leukemia, high S phase, extramedullary disease, and double-hit status.

Newly diagnosed therapy. Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd is the preferred backbone for fit/ASCT-eligible patients and for many non-frail transplant-ineligible patients. Standard-risk patients may defer ASCT if stem cells are collected early; high-risk patients are steered toward early ASCT. Maintenance centers on lenalidomide plus daratumumab or isatuximab, with lenalidomide time-limited to 2 years in standard-risk disease and continued to progression in high-risk disease.

Relapse. Selection is driven by prior exposure/refractoriness and access. Cilta-cel, Tec-Dara, other BCMA bispecifics, belantamab combinations, and standard triplets are sequenced according to anti-CD38 and BCMA sensitivity. Infection prevention and toxicity management are central: monthly IVIG with bispecifics, prophylactic tocilizumab for CRS mitigation, vigilance for CAR-T neurologic/infectious/cytopenic complications, and eye toxicity with belantamab.

Smoldering myeloma. The Mayo 20/2/20 framework and additional biologic/imaging features identify high-risk SMM. AQUILA is the key practice-changing result in this review: 3 years of daratumumab improved 5-year progression-free survival and overall survival versus active monitoring. The author recommends daratumumab for newly diagnosed high-risk SMM while observing lower-risk SMM.

Section-by-section condensation

1. Disease overview

Multiple myeloma represents about 1% of cancers and 10% of hematologic malignancies. The review reports >36,000 U.S. diagnoses and almost 11,000 deaths annually, with median diagnosis age ~65 years. Bone morbidity, anemia, hypercalcemia, renal failure, infection, and extramedullary disease are key clinical manifestations.

MGUS precedes most myeloma, is common after age 50, and progresses at roughly 1% per year. SMM carries substantially higher time-dependent progression risk, especially with t(4;14), del(17p), and gain(1q).

2. Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires clonal marrow plasma cells ≥10% or plasmacytoma plus at least one myeloma-defining event. The source emphasizes modern imaging, SPEP/SIFE/FLC assessment, marrow cytogenetics, and use of MRI to detect focal marrow disease before lytic lesions. Plasma cell leukemia is defined by ≥5% circulating plasma cells in a conventional smear.

M protein is measurable at ≥1 g/dL serum or ≥200 mg/day urine. The author recommends monthly SPEP/FLC on therapy, every 3–4 months off therapy, and urine electrophoresis every 3–6 months. Mass spectrometry is presented as a higher-sensitivity alternative to SPEP/SIFE when available.

3. Molecular classification

The review treats myeloma as a group of cytogenetically distinct diseases. Hyperdiploid disease accounts for ~45%, IgH-translocated disease ~40%, with t(11;14) the most common translocation subtype. Primary events are distinguished from secondary gain(1q), del(1p), del(17p), and MYC abnormalities arising during progression.

4. Prognosis and risk stratification

Modern survival exceeds 10 years in transplant-eligible patients in cited cohorts, with 4-year survival >90%; patients >65 years have median survival ~7–8 years and 5-year survival >70%. The author cautions that these figures may underestimate current outcomes because they predate newer immunotherapies.

Risk is anchored in cytogenetics and augmented by mSMART markers. A central interpretive point is that broad risk categories may be less informative than specific genomic groups under modern therapy.

5. Treatment options

The therapeutic landscape now includes IMiDs, proteasome inhibitors, anti-CD38 antibodies, BCMA- and GPRC5D-directed bispecific antibodies, CAR-T therapies, and belantamab mafodotin. The review uses low-dose weekly dexamethasone as a toxicity-reduction default and provides a detailed regimen/dosing table.

6. Newly diagnosed myeloma

For fit/ASCT-eligible patients, Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd is preferred for 3–4 cycles; high-risk patients proceed to early ASCT, while selected standard-risk responders can collect stem cells and defer transplant. Acute cast nephropathy is treated more aggressively with daratumumab-VCd plus extracorporeal free-light-chain removal in the author’s approach. Once-weekly SC bortezomib is preferred for most patients.

Frailty rather than transplant eligibility alone guides intensity in older patients. Non-frail patients can receive quadruplets; frail patients generally receive DRd or Isa-Rd with dose reduction. Routine tandem transplant or post-ASCT consolidation is not recommended outside trials. Maintenance is lenalidomide plus anti-CD38 for most patients, with duration tailored to risk/MRD.

7. Relapsed myeloma

Relapse treatment is organized by timing, prior response, disease aggressiveness, performance status, and refractoriness. At least two active drugs are sought in triplets. Pomalidomide-based therapy is favored for lenalidomide-refractory disease, and salvage ASCT remains an option when prior remission was long or transplant was deferred.

The review gives CAR-T and bispecifics a central role in first and second relapse. Anti-CD38-sensitive patients are steered toward cilta-cel or Tec-Dara; anti-CD38-refractory patients toward cilta-cel or BCMA bispecifics. Later-line sequencing alternates BCMA-directed approaches with triplets, talquetamab, belantamab combinations, venetoclax for t(11;14), and alkylator-based regimens. Safety and infection prophylaxis are emphasized.

8. Supportive care

Bone protection uses zoledronic acid or pamidronate, with less-frequent dosing after stabilization; denosumab is an alternative in renal dysfunction and should be followed by bisphosphonate when stopped to mitigate rebound. Infection prevention includes early levofloxacin consideration, PJP prophylaxis with prolonged steroids/anti-CD38 therapy, antiviral prophylaxis with proteasome inhibitors/anti-CD38/elotuzumab, and IVIG with bispecific therapy.

9. Smoldering multiple myeloma

The review uses Mayo 20/2/20 criteria plus biologic and imaging features to identify high-risk SMM. Two or three Mayo factors correspond to a median time to progression of about 2 years; high-risk SMM has ~25% annual progression risk in the first two years.

AQUILA is presented as the pivotal new result: 3 years of daratumumab produced 5-year PFS 63.1% versus 40.8% with active monitoring and 5-year OS 93.0% versus 86.9% (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.27–0.98). The author recommends daratumumab for newly diagnosed high-risk SMM and observation for others.

Table 1 — IMWG diagnostic criteria

MGUS: M protein below diagnostic thresholds, marrow clonal plasma cells <10%, no attributable CRAB.
SMM: IgG/IgA M protein ≥3 g/dL or urine M protein ≥500 mg/24h and/or marrow clonal plasma cells 10–60%; no MDE/amyloidosis.
Multiple myeloma: ≥10% clonal marrow plasma cells or plasmacytoma + ≥1 MDE (CRAB or biomarker event).
Plasma cell leukemia: MM criteria + ≥5% circulating plasma cells.
Also defines IgM MGUS, light-chain MGUS, solitary plasmacytoma, and solitary plasmacytoma with minimal marrow involvement.

Table 4 — IMS/IMWG high-risk cytogenetic definition

Any one: (1) del(17p) and/or p53 mutation; (2) bi-allelic del(1p); (3) t(4;14), t(14;16), or t(14;20) plus gain(1q) or del(1p); (4) gain(1q)+del(1p).

Table 5 — mSMART risk stratification

High risk = IMS/IMWG high-risk cytogenetics OR additional markers: plasma cell leukemia (>5% circulating plasma cells), high S phase, extramedullary disease. “Double-hit” = two high-risk cytogenetic factors. Standard risk = none of these.

Principal strengths

  • Clinically complete: diagnosis through SMM, newly diagnosed, relapse, and supportive care.
  • Preserves practical dosing and sequencing rather than only discussing drug classes.
  • Integrates recent Phase III immunotherapy evidence into explicit decision pathways.

Principal limitations

  • Narrative, single-author review: no reproducible search strategy, study selection, risk-of-bias assessment, or GRADE-like certainty.
  • Some regulatory/approval statements and sequencing recommendations are time-sensitive and were not independently verified in this task.
  • Cross-trial comparison is tempting because results are tabulated side-by-side, but populations, prior exposure, endpoints, and follow-up differ.
  • Some recommended maintenance durations and treatment-discontinuation strategies remain areas of active study.
Do not use for: treating the review’s author preference as a formal guideline mandate, or making direct cross-trial efficacy rankings without returning to the primary trials.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Initial therapy
Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd is the organizing standard for fit patients; use frailty to reduce intensity.
ASCT
Early ASCT for high risk; standard-risk responders may defer if stem cells are collected early.
Maintenance
Lenalidomide + anti-CD38 for most; risk and MRD influence duration.
Relapse
Use refractoriness and prior target exposure to sequence cilta-cel, BCMA bispecifics, belantamab, and active triplets.
Infection prevention
Monthly IVIG with bispecifics; consider antibacterial, PJP, and antiviral prophylaxis in the populations specified by the review.
SMM
Identify high-risk disease with 20/2/20 plus additional factors; daratumumab is the author’s preferred preventive therapy for newly diagnosed high-risk SMM.
Clinical-use boundary. Confirm current drug labels, society guidance, local formulary, patient-specific contraindications, and specialist pathways before applying the treatment algorithms.
