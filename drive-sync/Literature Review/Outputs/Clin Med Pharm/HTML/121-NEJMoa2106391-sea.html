Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, international phase 3 trial.

Population

Adults with clear-cell RCC at protocol-defined intermediate-to-high or high recurrence risk after nephrectomy, including a small M1 NED subgroup.

Intervention

One-page synthesis

Core result. At the first prespecified interim analysis, adjuvant pembrolizumab reduced the hazard of recurrence or death versus placebo. The hazard ratio was 0.68, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.87, with 24-month DFS of 77.3% versus 68.1%.

KEYNOTE-564 randomized 994 patients after nephrectomy, with or without metastasectomy, and followed them for a median of 24.1 months to the December 14, 2020 cutoff. The primary DFS analysis met the interim significance criterion. A sensitivity censoring analysis produced a similar hazard ratio of 0.67. The treatment effect direction was generally consistent across displayed subgroups, but several subgroup estimates were imprecise. The M1 NED subgroup contained only 58 patients.

Overall survival was not mature. There were 18 deaths with pembrolizumab and 33 with placebo, HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.30 to 0.96, but only 26% of the deaths planned for final analysis had occurred. Toxicity was higher with pembrolizumab. Grade 3 to 5 any-cause adverse events were 32.4% versus 17.7%, and treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was 20.7% versus 2.0%. No treatment-related deaths occurred. Patient-reported symptom and physical-function scores did not cross the source-defined thresholds for clinically meaningful deterioration.

Main uncertainty. This is an early DFS-positive analysis with substantial censoring after 2 years and immature OS. The trial supports the source-level efficacy claim but does not, by itself, establish the mature overall-survival effect or current clinical positioning.

Methods and design

The trial used 1:1 randomization and double blinding. Randomization was stratified by M0 versus M1 NED status. Within M0, the trial also stratified by ECOG performance status and US versus non-US location. Efficacy used the intention-to-treat population. Safety used the as-treated population.

The final protocol amendment 564-04, dated October 13, 2020, changed the DFS censoring approach and preserved a prespecified alternative censoring sensitivity analysis. The statistical plan used Kaplan-Meier estimates, stratified log-rank testing, and a stratified Cox model. Multiplicity tested DFS first, then passed alpha to OS only after DFS success. The protocol planned about 332 DFS events for final DFS analysis and about 200 deaths for final OS.

Protocol integrity note. Amendment 564-04 says the phrase "from a subset" was removed so all imaging scans would receive BICR, yet section 9.2.1 still describes a blinded audit for a subset. Section 10.2 omits the subset qualifier. The reported primary endpoint was investigator-assessed DFS, so this inconsistency does not change the primary result.

Results

Disease-free survival

260 recurrence or death events were reported, 109 versus 151. HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.87, P=0.002. 24-month DFS was 77.3% versus 68.1%.

Overall survival

51 deaths were reported, 18 versus 33. HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.30 to 0.96. 24-month OS was 96.6% versus 93.5%. The analysis was immature.

Safety

Grade 3 to 5 any-cause adverse events were 32.4% versus 17.7%. Treatment-related grade 3 to 5 events were 18.9% versus 1.2%.

Treatment burden

Full 17-cycle treatment was completed by 61.1% versus 73.6%. Adverse-event discontinuation was 20.7% versus 2.0%.

Local recurrence alone occurred in 3.4% versus 6.4%, and distant recurrence in 17.3% versus 23.5%. The alternative DFS censoring analysis gave HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.52 to 0.86. At 24 months, DFS restricted mean survival time differed by 1.86 months, 95% CI 0.95 to 2.77. The corresponding OS restricted mean difference was 0.19 months, 95% CI -0.13 to 0.50.

The most visible safety differences were thyroid and skin effects. Hypothyroidism occurred in 21.1% versus 3.6%, hyperthyroidism in 11.9% versus 0.2%, and pruritus in 22.7% versus 13.1%. Immune-mediated adverse events of any grade occurred in 34.6% versus 5.8%, with grade 3 or 4 events in 8.6% versus 0.6%.

Table 1. Baseline characteristics

The randomized groups were broadly balanced. Median age was 60 years in each group. Men were 70.0% and 72.1%. M0 intermediate-to-high risk disease accounted for 86.1% and 86.9%. M1 NED accounted for 5.8% in each arm. PD-L1 CPS at least 1 was present in 73.6% and 76.9%.

Table 2. Any-cause and treatment-related adverse events

Any-cause grade 3 to 5 adverse events were 32.4% versus 17.7%. Serious adverse events were 20.5% versus 11.3%. Adverse-event discontinuation was 20.7% versus 2.0%. Treatment-related grade 3 to 5 events were 18.9% versus 1.2%. No treatment-related deaths occurred.

Table 3. Common any-cause adverse events

The most common events included fatigue, 29.7% versus 24.2%; diarrhea, 25.4% versus 22.4%; pruritus, 22.7% versus 13.1%; hypothyroidism, 21.1% versus 3.6%; rash, 20.1% versus 10.7%; and hyperthyroidism, 11.9% versus 0.2%.

The largest visible between-arm differences were endocrine and dermatologic events. Hypothyroidism was 21.1% versus 3.6%, hyperthyroidism 11.9% versus 0.2%, pruritus 22.7% versus 13.1%, and rash 20.1% versus 10.7%.

At week 52, FKSI-DRS least-squares mean change was -1.12 versus -0.45, below the 3-point deterioration threshold. EORTC QLQ-C30 physical-functioning change was -1.81 versus -0.90, below the 10-point threshold. Completion at week 52 was roughly 62% to 66% of each outcome analysis population.

For both DFS and OS, curves cross early and then become reasonably parallel. The protocol prespecified proportional-hazards checks and supportive restricted mean survival time analysis if nonproportionality warranted.

Expanded baseline data confirm balance. Table S2 records 29 M1 NED patients per arm and identifies three bone or brain metastasis cases as protocol violations. Median treatment duration was 11.1 months and median cycle count was 17 in both as-treated groups.

The final protocol is dated October 13, 2020. Limitations and uncertainty

  • Numbers at risk after the median 24-month follow-up were small, so late Kaplan-Meier estimates were unstable.
  • Several subgroup analyses were small. M1 NED included 58 patients and could not support definitive subgroup interpretation.
  • Overall survival was immature at this cutoff, with only 26% of planned final deaths observed.
  • Extended enrollment caused substantial censoring beyond 2 years. Longer follow-up was required for cure proportions and mature OS.
  • The author disclosure forms and full data-sharing statement referenced by the article are not present in the Drive packet.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first for the trial-level evidence, but do not use the 2021 paper alone as a current 2026 practice standard.

Relevance 8/10

Direct phase 3 evidence for adjuvant pembrolizumab after nephrectomy in high-risk clear-cell RCC.

Evidence basis. A randomized placebo-controlled clinical endpoint trial with explicit efficacy and safety data.

Limiting factor. The source is from 2021 and current practice was not externally verified.

What would change it. A current guideline, label, and mature survival update would raise practice relevance.

Novelty 9/10

At publication, the trial tested a new adjuvant immune-checkpoint strategy in this setting.

Evidence basis. The source reports the first prespecified phase 3 interim result for KEYNOTE-564.

Limiting factor. Novelty is historical rather than a current-state claim.

What would change it. Later comparative adjuvant trials reduce the unique information value for current decisions.

Method strength 9/10

Randomization, placebo control, double blinding, prespecified interim testing, multiplicity control, and a large multinational sample support internal validity.

Evidence basis. 994 randomized participants, ITT efficacy analysis, external monitoring, protocol and final SAP available.

Limiting factor. Primary DFS was investigator-assessed and the first analysis had substantial censoring beyond 2 years.

What would change it. Longer follow-up and mature OS with consistent blinded-central review would strengthen the method package.

Evidence strength 8/10

The primary DFS endpoint crossed its prespecified interim boundary with a clinically material absolute separation and HR 0.68.

Evidence basis. 24-month DFS 77.3% versus 68.1%, HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.87, P=0.002.

Limiting factor. Overall survival was immature, and the first analysis cannot establish the long-term survival effect.

What would change it. Mature OS and durable late DFS would raise confidence.

External validity 7/10

The 21-country trial is broad geographically, but eligibility selected fit adults with clear-cell histology and specific high-risk categories.

Evidence basis. ECOG 0 or 1, mostly M0 intermediate-to-high risk, only 58 M1 NED participants.

Limiting factor. Small M1 NED and some subgroup cells limit precision outside the dominant trial population.

What would change it. Replication or mature subgroup data in less represented risk groups would raise the score.

Implementation value 7/10

The regimen and safety profile are detailed enough to understand the trial intervention and monitoring burden.

Evidence basis. Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks for up to 17 cycles, with detailed adverse-event tables and PRO data.

Limiting factor. A 2021 trial report alone is not a 2026 prescribing or formulary standard.

What would change it. Current labeling, guideline positioning, contraindications, and mature outcome updates are required for clinical implementation.

Best use. Primary evidence for the initial KEYNOTE-564 disease-free survival result, regimen details, safety burden, and trial design.
Do not use for. A stand-alone current guideline recommendation, current labeling, mature overall-survival effect, or precise inference for small subgroups.
