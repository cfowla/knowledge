One-page synthesis

The ACC scan reports two real-world target trial emulations in Swedish SCREAM data. In the GLP-1RA versus DPP-4 inhibitor comparison, 32,979 patients were included. Weighted HHF incidence was 12.1 versus 15.8 events per 1,000 person-years. Three-year HHF risk was 3.43% versus 4.30%, with a reported weighted HR of 0.77. In the GLP-1RA versus SGLT-2 inhibitor comparison, 30,104 patients were included. Weighted HHF incidence was 12.8 versus 12.6 events per 1,000 person-years. Three-year HHF risk was 3.56% versus 3.33%, with a reported weighted HR of 1.02.

The scan says the absolute HHF risk difference was largest at higher predicted baseline HF risk. It also reports consistency across most subgroups, individual agents, and per-protocol analyses. Positive-control MACE results were directionally lower for GLP-1RA versus DPP-4 inhibitor, HR 0.85, and similar for GLP-1RA versus SGLT-2 inhibitor, HR 0.98. Roughly 90% of the population reportedly did not have HF. These numbers are useful for rapid orientation. The journal scan does not provide confidence intervals, p values, outcome coding, confounder selection, weighting diagnostics, or enough eligibility detail to judge whether the emulation supports a causal treatment comparison.

Verdict: Skim deeply. Use this page as a pointer to the primary Circulation article and as a compact record of the reported effect magnitudes. Do not use the journal scan alone to choose therapy.

Source condensation

Lead finding and study setup

The ACC scan states that HHF risk was lower with GLP-1RAs than DPP-4 inhibitors and similar with SGLT-2 inhibitors in patients with T2D. It attributes the underlying study to Xu and colleagues and says the investigators used SCREAM population data from Sweden to emulate two treatment trials. Listed GLP-1RAs were exenatide, liraglutide, lixisenatide, dulaglutide, and semaglutide. The DPP-4 inhibitor list was sitagliptin, vildagliptin, and linagliptin. The SGLT-2 inhibitor list was dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, empagliflozin, and ertugliflozin.

GLP-1RA versus DPP-4 inhibitor

The first emulated trial included 32,979 patients with T2D. The median age was 64 years and 40% were women. GLP-1RA users made up 42% of the cohort and DPP-4 inhibitor users 58%. During 10 to 36 months of follow-up, weighted HHF incidence was 12.1 versus 15.8 events per 1,000 person-years. Three-year absolute HHF risk was 3.43% versus 4.30%, with a reported weighted HR of 0.77.

GLP-1RA versus SGLT-2 inhibitor

The second emulated trial included 30,104 patients. The median age was 63 years and 38% were women. GLP-1RA users made up 49% and SGLT-2 inhibitor users 51%. During 8 to 36 months of follow-up, weighted HHF incidence was 12.8 versus 12.6 events per 1,000 person-years. Three-year absolute HHF risk was 3.56% versus 3.33%, with a reported weighted HR of 1.02.

Risk strata, sensitivity analyses, and positive control

The scan reports the largest absolute HHF risk difference among patients with higher predicted baseline HF risk. It says findings were consistent across most subgroups, individual agents, and per-protocol analyses. The positive-control MACE analysis reported three-year risks of 5.49% versus 6.31% for GLP-1RA versus DPP-4 inhibitor, HR 0.85, and 4.88% versus 4.91% for GLP-1RA versus SGLT-2 inhibitor, HR 0.98.

Author interpretation

The study authors, as quoted in the ACC scan, argue that GLP-1RAs may reduce HHF risk in T2D regardless of prior HF and could therefore contribute to both primary and secondary prevention. That interpretation belongs to the cited study authors. The ACC summary does not supply enough causal-method detail to independently establish the claim.

Evaluation and appraisal

The scores rate this ACC journal scan as an evidence summary, not the underlying Circulation article. High clinical relevance does not substitute for missing primary-method detail.

Relevance: 8/10

Directly addresses hospitalization for heart failure when choosing glucose-lowering drug classes in type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis
The summary reports two active-comparator analyses and gives absolute risks, incidence rates, and hazard ratios.
Principal limit
It is a secondary summary, so clinical interpretation depends on the primary article.
What would change the score
Reading and appraising the primary article would raise confidence in using the finding.

Novelty: 5/10

The journal scan points to a 2026 real-world comparison of GLP-1RA with both DPP-4 and SGLT-2 inhibitors for HHF.

Evidence basis
The ACC summary frames the work as two target trial emulations using SCREAM data.
Principal limit
The summary does not establish how much the methods or conclusions differ from prior observational and randomized evidence.
What would change the score
A comparison with prior studies in the primary paper would clarify the incremental contribution.

Method strength: 3/10

The summary identifies a target trial emulation but omits the details needed to judge causal identification.

Evidence basis
SCREAM data, two emulated comparisons, subgroup analyses, single-agent analyses, per-protocol analyses, and a positive control are reported.
Principal limit
Eligibility, treatment assignment emulation, confounder set, weighting diagnostics, outcome coding, censoring, and missing-data methods are not described here.
What would change the score
The full protocol, covariate balance, model diagnostics, sensitivity analyses, and outcome definitions would materially raise this score.

Evidence strength: 4/10

The summary provides useful magnitudes but not uncertainty intervals or enough methods to test the causal claim.

Evidence basis
For GLP-1RA versus DPP-4i, the reported three-year HHF risk is 3.43% versus 4.30% with HR 0.77. For GLP-1RA versus SGLT-2i, it is 3.56% versus 3.33% with HR 1.02.
Principal limit
No confidence intervals or p values are printed in this journal scan, and residual confounding cannot be assessed from the summary.
What would change the score
Primary-study uncertainty estimates and bias analyses would raise or lower confidence.

External validity: 5/10

The population-based Swedish data may represent routine care, but this 2-page summary gives little information about who entered the emulated trials.

Evidence basis
The cohorts are large, 32,979 and 30,104 patients, and roughly 90% reportedly had no prior HF.
Principal limit
The summary does not provide race or ethnicity, kidney-function distribution, HF phenotype, diabetes duration, socioeconomic context, or health-system comparability.
What would change the score
Detailed baseline characteristics and replication outside Sweden would strengthen generalizability.

Implementation value: 4/10

The result can inform which primary article deserves attention but is not enough to change therapy by itself.

Evidence basis
The summary gives class comparisons and absolute three-year HHF risks that are clinically interpretable.
Principal limit
It lacks treatment-selection rules, safety tradeoffs, patient-level effect modifiers, and current guideline context.
What would change the score
Primary-study appraisal plus guideline and patient-specific review would be needed before practice implementation.

Main strengths

The scan names the primary article, identifies both active comparators, gives cohort sizes, follow-up ranges, class-level drug lists, HHF incidence rates, three-year absolute risks, hazard ratios, and positive-control MACE results. It is compact and easy to use as a retrieval pointer.

Main limitations

The scan omits confidence intervals and p values. It does not describe eligibility, the treatment-strategy emulation, time zero, outcome coding, confounder selection, weighting diagnostics, censoring, missing-data handling, negative controls, or full sensitivity analyses. It also gives too little baseline detail to judge transportability. The phrase "similar with SGLT-2 inhibitors" is a descriptive summary of the reported HR 1.02, not evidence of formal equivalence or noninferiority.

Implementation and practice use

Best use. Keep the scan as a quick evidence index and use its effect magnitudes to decide that the primary Circulation article merits direct review.

Do not use for. Do not treat the ACC scan as proof that GLP-1RAs and SGLT-2 inhibitors are equivalent for HHF. Do not use the scan alone to choose a glucose-lowering drug class, alter HF therapy, or override current guideline, label, safety, kidney, weight, glycemic, and patient-specific considerations.

Next evidence step. Read the cited primary article and its supplement. Verify effect uncertainty, target-trial specification, covariate balance, residual-confounding analyses, outcome definitions, agent-specific results, and the size of absolute benefit across baseline HF-risk strata before translating the result into practice.
