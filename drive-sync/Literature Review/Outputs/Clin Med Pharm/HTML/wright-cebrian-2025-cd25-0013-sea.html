Scope warning

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The article is a strong implementation aid for CKD screening and treatment in T2D. It should not replace current ADA, KDIGO, regulatory labeling, or a drug-specific formulary review.

The source turns a broad CKD-T2D evidence base into two practical workflows. The screening pathway uses both eGFR and UACR, confirms abnormal results over time, stratifies risk with the KDIGO grid, and escalates monitoring or nephrology referral as risk rises. The treatment pathway starts with lifestyle and comorbidity management, then layers RAS inhibition, SGLT2 inhibition, finerenone, and GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy according to kidney function, albuminuria, potassium, cardiorenal risk, tolerability, and patient preference.

The strongest feature is usability. The weakness is method. The article reports a 40-expert workshop and review of ADA, KDIGO, and ESC guidance, but it does not report a reproducible literature search, study-selection rules, formal consensus voting, evidence grading, or a method for managing conflicts of interest. Bayer supported the meeting, writing, and figure production. Bayer also manufactures finerenone and sponsored the FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD trials. The article is best treated as a navigation and implementation document, not as an independent adjudication of comparative treatment value.

Source-integrity issues. Figure 1 contains a UACR unit inconsistency. The prose and Figure 5 conflict at the finerenone eGFR 60 dose boundary. Figure 5 also presents a narrower albuminuria criterion for the SGLT2 branch than the surrounding prose.

Source condensation

Purpose and development

The authors aim to give primary care clinicians a simplified CKD-T2D guide built from expert discussion and current clinical guidance. The CardioRenal Initiative workshop included 40 clinicians across four specialties and three broad world regions. The discussion analyzed ADA, KDIGO, and ESC recommendations and produced screening, monitoring, and treatment algorithms. The source does not describe this work as a systematic review or formal guideline-development process.

Screening and monitoring

CKD is framed as persistent kidney abnormality for more than 3 months. The pathway uses both eGFR and UACR because they measure different dimensions of disease. People at risk should receive both tests. Normal or low-risk findings are rechecked at least yearly. eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 or UACR at least 30 mg/g prompts confirmation after about 3 months and clinical optimization between tests. Confirmed CKD is placed on the KDIGO eGFR-by-albuminuria grid, which drives monitoring frequency and referral.

After diagnosis, the authors recommend life-long assessment of eGFR, UACR, HbA1c, LDL cholesterol, and blood pressure at least annually, with more frequent review as risk rises. Stage 4 or 5 CKD warrants nephrology referral. Patient education is part of the algorithm and should explain that UACR measures kidney damage while eGFR measures kidney function.

Pillared treatment

Lifestyle modification and comorbidity management form the base. The four pharmacologic pillars are ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy, an SGLT2 inhibitor, finerenone, and a GLP-1 receptor agonist. The source links these drugs to cardiovascular, heart failure, kidney, and glycemic goals. It emphasizes maximum-tolerated RAS inhibition, SGLT2 initiation at eGFR at least 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, potassium and kidney-function monitoring with finerenone, and GLP-1 receptor agonist use for cardiorenal benefit rather than A1C alone.

The article uses major outcome trials as support rather than conducting a new synthesis. Reported examples include RENAAL and IDNT for RAS blockade, EMPA-KIDNEY, DAPA-CKD, CREDENCE, and SCORED for SGLT inhibition, FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD for finerenone, and FLOW for semaglutide. The review also cites CONFIDENCE as support for combined finerenone plus empagliflozin. These are secondary reports. Primary-study ATOM extraction would require the cited publications themselves.

Implementation barriers

The article attributes underdiagnosis and undertreatment to low screening, clinician workload, limited education, frequent guideline changes, treatment inertia, patient concerns, adherence difficulty, and health-system barriers. It proposes routine UACR testing, clearer education, multidisciplinary care when available, and simplified algorithms to make evidence-based treatment easier to execute.

Limitations and conflicts

The authors acknowledge that the algorithms are simplified and less extensive than the full source guidelines. Genetic screening and personalized genetic algorithms were not part of the workshop. More important for appraisal, the article does not report a reproducible evidence-selection or consensus method. Substantial sponsor and author-industry relationships require readers to separate the practical value of the algorithms from independent evidence grading.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Directly addresses CKD screening and cardiorenal pharmacotherapy in T2D for primary care.

Evidence basis
Full clinical focus, algorithms, and trial examples are directly applicable to CKD-T2D management.
Principal limit
It is not a formulary comparison of empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin.
What would change the score
A direct head-to-head comparative evidence synthesis would raise the formulary-specific score.

Novelty 6/10

The main contribution is simplification and integration of existing guidance rather than new efficacy evidence.

Evidence basis
The article combines a 40-expert workshop with ADA, KDIGO, and ESC guidance and produces practical algorithms.
Principal limit
Most treatment claims derive from existing guidelines and trials.
What would change the score
A prespecified consensus method or new comparative evidence would raise the score.

Method strength 4/10

Evidence strength 6/10

The treatment framework rests on major guideline recommendations and randomized outcome trials, but the article itself is a secondary expert synthesis.

Evidence basis
The review cites RENAAL, IDNT, EMPA-KIDNEY, DAPA-CKD, CREDENCE, FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD, FLOW, and meta-analyses.
Principal limit
Evidence is selectively summarized without a formal risk-of-bias or certainty framework, and sponsor relationships are substantial.
What would change the score
Formal evidence selection, certainty ratings, and balanced comparative synthesis would raise the score.

Prospective implementation testing across varied primary-care systems would raise the score.

Implementation value 9/10

Bottom line

Best use. A compact implementation reference for screening, risk stratification, and assembling guideline-supported CKD-T2D therapies in primary care.

Do not use for. Stand-alone guideline authority, independent evidence grading, finerenone dosing at boundary values without checking current labeling, or direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin formulary equivalence.

Confidence in appraisal. High for source content because the full supplied PDF, all five numbered figures, and Table 1 were reviewed. Moderate for ongoing practice currency because clinical standards continue to change.

External currency check as of 23 August 2026

The 2026 ADA Standards continue to recommend an SGLT2 inhibitor with demonstrated benefit for T2D and CKD at eGFR at least 20 mL/min/1.73 m2 and recommend a GLP-1 receptor agonist with demonstrated kidney benefit. They also now state that simultaneous SGLT2 inhibitor plus finerenone initiation can be considered in selected adults with T2D, UACR at least 100 mg/g, eGFR 30-90 mL/min/1.73 m2, and background RAS inhibition. This makes the source's broad four-pillar framework directionally current but adds a more specific combination-therapy recommendation that postdates the article.

Figure 4 is outdated on GLP-1 receptor agonist suicidality. On 13 January 2026, the FDA reported that its review did not identify increased suicidal ideation or behavior risk with GLP-1 receptor agonist medications and requested removal of the warning from affected product labels.

Implementation takeaways

Screen with two measures.
Use eGFR and UACR together. Do not infer CKD chronicity from one abnormal result.
Use risk to set cadence.
KDIGO eGFR and albuminuria categories determine follow-up intensity and referral.
Build the cardiorenal regimen.
Think beyond A1C. RAS inhibition, SGLT2 inhibition, finerenone, and GLP-1 therapy address different parts of risk.
Check current authority.
Use current ADA, KDIGO, and product labeling for exact thresholds, dose boundaries, safety language, and combination sequencing.
