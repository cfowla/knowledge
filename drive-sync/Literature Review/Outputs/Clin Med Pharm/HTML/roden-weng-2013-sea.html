One-page synthesis

Roden and colleagues randomized 899 previously untreated adults with type 2 diabetes and HbA1c 7% to 10% to placebo, empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or sitagliptin 100 mg once daily for 24 weeks. A separate 87-person open-label cohort with HbA1c above 10% received empagliflozin 25 mg. The primary endpoint was week-24 HbA1c change. The randomized trial used computer-generated allocation, stratification, triple-dummy masking, a prespecified hierarchical testing procedure, and sensitivity analyses.

Both empagliflozin doses met the primary endpoint. Adjusted HbA1c differences versus placebo were -0.74 percentage points for 10 mg and -0.85 for 25 mg, each p<0.0001. Sitagliptin was -0.73 versus placebo. Empagliflozin also reduced fasting glucose and bodyweight and lowered systolic blood pressure. Direct empagliflozin versus sitagliptin comparisons were exploratory, not confirmatory. Common adverse-event rates were similar across randomized groups, confirmed hypoglycemia was rare, and genital infections were more frequent with empagliflozin. The 24-week duration, drug-naive population, surrogate endpoints, sponsor involvement, and historical treatment context limit how far the results should be carried into current practice.

Verdict

Read soon. Strong historical phase 3 evidence for short-term empagliflozin metabolic efficacy and a useful anchor for the SGLT2 evidence timeline. Do not use it alone to justify current empagliflozin-to-dapagliflozin formulary equivalence or inpatient use.

Methods and population

The study ran at 124 sites in Belgium, Canada, China, Germany, India, Ireland, Japan, Switzerland, and the United States. Randomized participants had received no oral or injected anti-diabetes treatment for 12 weeks, had BMI no greater than 45 kg/m2, and generally had HbA1c 7% to 10%. Key exclusions included marked fasting hyperglycemia during run-in and eGFR below 50 mL/min/1.73 m2, with a 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 threshold in China.

Randomized treatment was 24 weeks of placebo, empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or sitagliptin 100 mg once daily. Allocation used blocks of four and stratification by region, baseline HbA1c category, and eGFR category. Patients, investigators, and analysts were masked. The primary ANCOVA used baseline HbA1c as a covariate; post-rescue values were set missing and the primary analysis used LOCF. MMRM and per-protocol analyses tested sensitivity.

Active-comparator boundary

At week 24, the adjusted HbA1c difference was 0.00 percentage points for empagliflozin 10 mg versus sitagliptin and -0.12 for empagliflozin 25 mg versus sitagliptin. Neither comparison was confirmatory, and the 25 mg comparison was not statistically significant. Empagliflozin produced larger fasting-glucose, weight, and systolic-BP reductions than sitagliptin in exploratory analyses.

Safety

Serious adverse events were uncommon across groups. Events consistent with urinary tract infection occurred in 15 of 224 participants on empagliflozin 10 mg, 12 of 223 on empagliflozin 25 mg, and 12 of 229 in the placebo treated set. Genital-infection events occurred in 7, 9, and 0 participants, respectively. Confirmed hypoglycemia occurred in one participant in each randomized group, and none required assistance.

Supplement

Observed-case MMRM and per-protocol ANCOVA analyses reproduced the primary HbA1c result. The supplement also reports sensitivity analyses for bodyweight and blood pressure and laboratory changes. Hematocrit rose by about 2.1 percentage points with both empagliflozin doses, and serum uric acid fell by 58 to 62 µmol/L. Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance

9/10

Directly relevant to the early efficacy and safety evidence base for empagliflozin monotherapy and to SGLT2 formulary evidence history.

Evidence basis. Randomized empagliflozin data with placebo and sitagliptin comparators.

Limiting factor. The trial predates cardiovascular and kidney outcome evidence and current inpatient guidance.

Would raise the score. A current head-to-head clinical-outcomes study or current formulary-specific implementation study would raise the score.

Novelty

8/10

At publication, this was an early phase 3 monotherapy evaluation with both placebo and an active DPP-4 comparator.

Evidence basis. The paper states that no other large trial had tested this exact previously untreated setting.

Limiting factor. Novelty is historical rather than current.

Would raise the score. A novel comparative-outcomes question would raise the present-day novelty score.

Method strength

8/10

Randomization, masking, triple-dummy treatment, prespecified hierarchy, stratification, and sensitivity analyses support the primary efficacy findings.

Evidence basis. 899 randomized participants, active and placebo controls, 89% completion, ANCOVA plus MMRM and per-protocol sensitivity analyses.

Limiting factor. LOCF was used in the primary analysis, active-comparator tests were exploratory, and the sponsor had a direct role in design and analysis.

Would raise the score. Longer blinded follow-up with modern estimands and prespecified active-comparator hypotheses would raise the score.

Evidence strength

7/10

The glycemic, weight, and systolic-blood-pressure effects are consistent and quantitatively precise over 24 weeks.

Evidence basis. Primary HbA1c differences were about -0.74 to -0.85 percentage points versus placebo with narrow confidence intervals; sensitivity analyses were concordant.

Limiting factor. Outcomes are short-term surrogates and the trial was not designed for cardiovascular, kidney, hospitalization, or mortality outcomes.

Would raise the score. Patient-centered clinical outcomes and longer safety follow-up would raise the score.

External validity

6/10

The multicountry design helps transportability, but the population was selected and differs from many current treatment pathways.

Evidence basis. 124 sites in nine countries, but participants were drug-naive, had eGFR at least 50 mL/min/1.73 m2 or at least 60 in China, and 64% were Asian.

Limiting factor. Metformin was already usual first-line therapy, the trial excluded lower eGFR, and treatment lasted 24 weeks.

Would raise the score. Broader renal function, contemporary background therapy, and longer follow-up would raise the score.

Implementation value

6/10

The study gives clear dose-specific metabolic effect sizes and common adverse-event patterns useful for historical formulary rationale.

Evidence basis. Both approved empagliflozin doses were tested with placebo and sitagliptin, with weight, blood pressure, hypoglycemia, UTI, and genital-infection reporting.

Limiting factor. It cannot establish present-day inpatient interchangeability or current practice recommendations by itself.

Would raise the score. Current direct comparative effectiveness, label, and guideline evidence would raise the score.

Internal validity

The primary efficacy result is credible for the study's 24-week glycemic question. Randomization, masking, an active comparator, multiplicity control, and concordant sensitivity analyses reduce several common sources of bias. LOCF is an older missing-data method, and sponsor involvement in design and analysis deserves attention, but neither point erases the large and consistent placebo-corrected HbA1c signal.

External validity

Applicability is narrower. Participants were drug-naive, kidney function thresholds excluded lower eGFR, 64% were Asian, mean BMI was about 28.4 kg/m2, and the comparison lasted 24 weeks. The authors themselves noted that metformin was already standard first-line therapy in most countries. The open-label high-HbA1c cohort should not be treated as randomized evidence.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Useful now

Use this trial to document the original dose-specific metabolic efficacy of empagliflozin, its weight and blood-pressure effects, and its early 24-week safety profile.

Do not overclaim

Do not infer empagliflozin-dapagliflozin interchangeability, inpatient safety, cardiovascular equivalence, kidney equivalence, or contemporary treatment recommendations from this trial.

Best next evidence

Pair this historical trial with current class guidance, labeling, cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials, and direct or high-quality comparative effectiveness evidence when making formulary decisions.
