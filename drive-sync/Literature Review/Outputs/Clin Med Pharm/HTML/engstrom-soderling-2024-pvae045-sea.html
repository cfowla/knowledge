Metadata and scope

Design
Active-comparator new-user observational cohort; nationwide linked registers in Sweden, Denmark, and Norway.
Population
199,371 adults aged 35–84 years with type 2 diabetes: 141,065 empagliflozin initiators; 58,306 dapagliflozin initiators.
Exposure/comparator
Empagliflozin vs dapagliflozin; recommended starting dose 10 mg for both during the study period.
Primary outcomes
MACE; heart failure hospitalization/death; serious renal events.
Secondary/safety
Outcome components, all-cause death, and diabetic ketoacidosis.
Scope warning
Supports comparative cardiorenal effectiveness in routine-practice type 2 diabetes. It does not alone establish full inpatient formulary interchangeability, equivalence across all modern indications, or comparative cost.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. This is unusually direct evidence for an empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin formulary question. Across nearly 200,000 Scandinavian new users, adjusted risks of MACE, heart failure, serious renal events, and DKA were closely similar.

The three coprimary adjusted hazard ratios were 1.02 (95% CI 0.97–1.08) for MACE, 1.05 (0.97–1.14) for heart failure, and 0.97 (0.87–1.07) for serious renal events. Absolute adjusted rate differences were 0.1, 0.2, and −0.4 events per 1000 person-years, respectively. Figure 2 showed very small adjusted absolute risk differences through 5 years. DKA was also similar (HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.94–1.33).

The design is stronger than a conventional prevalent-user cohort: it uses an active comparator, new users, country-specific 68-covariate propensity scores, overlap restriction, stabilized IPTW, subgroup analyses, laboratory-enhanced sensitivity analyses in Sweden/Denmark, and an as-treated sensitivity analysis. The central limitation is unchanged: treatment was not randomized, so residual confounding remains possible. The renal replacement therapy component favored empagliflozin (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.60–0.99), while Denmark's laboratory-enhanced serious-renal estimate moved above 1 (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.03–1.36); these isolated signals sit within an otherwise neutral overall pattern and should not be treated as definitive agent-specific superiority.

Source-integrity flags. The dapagliflozin serious-renal adjusted incidence rate is 4.1/1000 person-years in the abstract and Table 2 but 4.0/1000 in Results prose. The normalized result uses Table 2's 4.1. In addition, some Denmark adjusted incidence rates differ slightly between Supplementary Tables S8 and S11 despite identical event counts and arm sizes; the source gives no explanation.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper addresses a clinically important asymmetry: placebo-controlled outcome trials and resulting indications/guidelines have been interpreted differently for empagliflozin and dapagliflozin, even though differences in trial populations and endpoint definitions may account for some divergence. Pharmacologic differences could also, in principle, produce agent-specific outcomes. Direct randomized head-to-head outcome data were absent.

The stated objective was therefore to compare cardiovascular and renal effectiveness and safety of the two drugs using nationwide routine-care data, including the infrequent but clinically important safety outcome diabetic ketoacidosis.

Methods

Adults aged 35–84 years initiating either drug were identified from nationwide registers. Cohort entry spanned June 2014–June 2021 in Sweden/Denmark and June 2014–December 2018 in Norway. Prior SGLT2 use was prohibited. Exclusions attempted to remove non-diabetic HF/CKD prescribing and several severe-illness/healthcare-contact situations. Main follow-up treated the cohort-entry drug as fixed, analogous to intention-to-treat, through outcome, death, emigration, 5 years, or study end.

Confounding control used country-specific logistic propensity scores with 68 baseline covariates, overlap restriction, and stabilized IPTW; balance below 10% standardized difference was prespecified as acceptable. Cox models estimated HRs; Poisson identity-link models estimated absolute rate differences; adjusted Kaplan–Meier curves estimated 1-, 3-, and 5-year absolute risks. Prespecified subgroup and sensitivity analyses used subgroup-specific propensity scores, additional laboratory/clinical variables with multiple imputation in Sweden/Denmark, and an as-treated refill-based exposure definition with a 30-day grace period.

Results

After overlap restriction, 141,065 empagliflozin and 58,306 dapagliflozin users remained. Mean age was 63 years, 36% were women, and weighted measured covariates were reported as balanced. Median follow-up was 2.0 years for empagliflozin and 3.0 years for dapagliflozin.

The primary comparative results were neutral, with narrow CIs around unity for MACE, heart failure, and serious renal events. Most secondary outcomes were similarly neutral. Renal replacement therapy alone had an HR below 1 with a CI excluding 1 (0.77, 0.60–0.99); all-cause death was 1.06 (1.00–1.11). DKA was 1.12 (0.94–1.33). No significant interaction was reported by sex, major cardiovascular disease, HF, or CKD. Serious renal events showed an age interaction (P=0.03), but both age-stratum CIs crossed 1. Country-specific and sensitivity analyses were broadly concordant with the main results.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the study as evidence of no meaningful cardiorenal difference between the two drugs in routine-practice type 2 diabetes, noting that the precision was compatible with relative differences below 8% for MACE, 14% for HF, and 13% for serious renal events, and absolute differences below 1 event/1000 patient-years. They also consider DKA risk similar.

The strongest support comes from scale, active-comparator new-user design, broad register data, and robustness analyses. The main weaknesses are observational residual confounding, prescription-fill rather than ingestion exposure, limited validation for the specific renal codes, slight restriction of generalizability from the diabetes-identification exclusion, and inability to compare other SGLT2 inhibitors.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 10/10

Rationale: Direct head-to-head empagliflozin–dapagliflozin comparative cardiorenal evidence in routine-practice type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis: Nationwide three-country cohort; clinically aligned outcomes.

Limiting factor: Does not directly study inpatient therapeutic interchange or all current non-diabetes indications.

Would raise/lower: Direct randomized head-to-head data or a pragmatic interchange trial would raise confidence.

Novelty 9/10

Rationale: Large direct comparison addresses a question that placebo-controlled class trials cannot answer.

Evidence basis: 199,371 new users with active comparator design.

Limiting factor: Still observational and limited to two agents.

Would raise/lower: Prospective randomized comparative evidence would materially raise novelty and decisiveness.

Method strength 8/10

Rationale: Active-comparator new-user design, country-specific propensity scores, overlap restriction, IPTW, and prespecified sensitivity analyses reduce major biases.

Evidence basis: 68-covariate propensity model; additional laboratory adjustment; as-treated analysis.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding and outcome-code limitations remain possible.

Would raise/lower: Negative controls, stronger unmeasured-confounding analyses, and prospective randomization would raise the score.

Evidence strength 8/10

Rationale: Primary estimates are precise and clustered near the null across MACE, HF, serious renal events, and DKA, with concordant sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: Coprimary HRs 1.02, 1.05, and 0.97 with narrow CIs; absolute differences under 1/1000 PY.

Limiting factor: Observational design; isolated component/subgroup signals and source-reporting inconsistencies warrant caution.

Would raise/lower: Replicated head-to-head randomized or quasi-experimental evidence would raise the score.

External validity 8/10

Rationale: Nationwide routine-practice data from three Scandinavian countries and broad inclusion support applicability to adults with type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis: Population-based registers and nearly 200,000 patients.

Limiting factor: Generalizability to other health systems, younger/older patients, non-diabetes HF/CKD populations, and current prescribing eras is uncertain.

Would raise/lower: Replication in diverse contemporary health systems and non-diabetic cardiorenal populations would raise the score.

Implementation value 9/10

Rationale: Highly useful for formulary class-effect rationale because it directly compares the two agents on major cardiorenal outcomes and DKA.

Evidence basis: Head-to-head relative and absolute estimates plus subgroup and sensitivity analyses.

Limiting factor: It does not by itself establish full therapeutic interchangeability across indications, dosing, inpatient initiation, cost, or all safety domains.

Would raise/lower: Pairing with current labels/guidelines, inpatient evidence, and cost/formulary data would complete an interchange decision.

Bottom line

Best use: strong observational support that major cardiorenal effectiveness is broadly similar between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin in routine-practice adults with type 2 diabetes. Do not use for: a stand-alone declaration of universal therapeutic equivalence across all indications, inpatient initiation scenarios, dosing constraints, costs, or safety domains.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Formulary relevance
The study substantially reduces concern that the two drugs have large intrinsic differences in MACE, HF, or serious renal effectiveness among routine-care adults with type 2 diabetes.
What remains necessary
Pair this evidence with current FDA labeling, guideline indications, renal-function thresholds, inpatient-use guidance, operational/cost data, and indication-specific trials before approving therapeutic interchange.
Signals not to overclaim
Renal replacement therapy HR 0.77 (0.60–0.99), the age interaction for serious renal events, and Denmark's additional-adjustment SRE HR 1.19 (1.03–1.36) are isolated findings within a broad neutral pattern and require cautious interpretation.
Safety
DKA risk appeared similar (HR 1.12, 0.94–1.33), but the study does not comprehensively compare all clinically important SGLT2 adverse effects.
