Risk of Heart Failure Hospitalization for GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Versus DPP-4 Inhibitors or SGLT-2 Inhibitors in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes: A Target Trial Emulation

Xu Y, Huang T, Zhang Y, et al. Circulation. 2026;153:1086-1100. DOI: 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.125.075157. Original research article; observational comparative-effectiveness target trial emulation.

Primary question
Does starting a GLP-1RA change HHF risk compared with DPP-4i or SGLT-2i in routine care?
Population
Adults with type 2 diabetes in Stockholm SCREAM data.

Summary

The study emulated two target trials rather than one three-arm comparison. Target trial 1 compared new GLP-1RA and DPP-4i users; target trial 2 compared new GLP-1RA and SGLT-2i users. The primary outcome was hospitalization for heart failure. The design combined an active-comparator new-user framework, adjustment for 72 measured baseline confounders, competing-risk absolute-risk estimation, multiple imputation, and bootstrap confidence intervals.

Main finding. GLP-1RA initiation was associated with less HHF than DPP-4i (weighted HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.66-0.91; 3-year risks 3.43% vs 4.30%; RD -0.88 percentage points). Against SGLT-2i, the observed HHF rates were similar (weighted HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.85-1.18; 3-year risks 3.56% vs 3.33%; RD 0.23 percentage points).

The paper supports a comparative-effectiveness association. It does not establish randomized equivalence between GLP-1RA and SGLT-2i. No equivalence or noninferiority margin was specified, and residual confounding remains possible.

Single agents

Liraglutide vs DPP-4i: HR 0.77 (0.66-0.93). Semaglutide vs DPP-4i: HR 0.59 (0.31-0.94), with semaglutide follow-up truncated at 2 years. Neither liraglutide nor semaglutide showed a lower HHF rate than SGLT-2i in the reported estimates.

Risk-stratified absolute effects

Among participants without prior heart failure, the 3-year GLP-1RA vs DPP-4i RD was -0.06 percentage points (95% CI -0.41 to 0.35) at TRS-HFDM score 0 and -1.77 points (-3.23 to -0.13) at score at least 2. This is an absolute-risk pattern and should not be read as proof that the relative treatment effect differs by risk level.

Robustness checks

  • Positive control MACE reproduced the expected direction versus DPP-4i: weighted HR 0.85 (0.74-0.99).
  • Minimum-one-year-follow-up restriction: HHF HR 0.75 (0.63-0.88) vs DPP-4i and 0.90 (0.76-1.07) vs SGLT-2i.
  • First-position-only HHF: HR 0.73 (0.58-0.89) vs DPP-4i and 1.06 (0.88-1.27) vs SGLT-2i.
  • BMI quantitative-bias scenarios did not reverse the DPP-4i comparison; modeled point estimates ranged 0.67-0.77. For the SGLT-2i comparison, modeled estimates ranged 0.88-1.02.
  • Alternative Lund-Malmo and EKFC eGFR equations reproduced the main pattern.
Positive-control boundary. Agreement with a known MACE effect increases confidence but does not prove absence of residual confounding for HHF. The supplied podcast discussion also notes that target-trial emulation is not a substitute for randomization and that a negative-control outcome would have been useful.

Evaluation and appraisal

What is strong

  • Explicit target-trial specification aligns eligibility, treatment assignment, time zero, follow-up, estimands, and analyses.
  • Active-comparator new-user design reduces several common pharmacoepidemiology biases.
  • Laboratory and socioeconomic data strengthen confounder adjustment beyond claims-only analyses.
  • Balance diagnostics, competing-risk estimates, positive control, per-protocol analysis, quantitative bias analysis, stricter outcome definition, and alternate eGFR equations address different failure modes.
  • Both relative and absolute effects are reported, including risk-stratified absolute differences.

What limits causal confidence

  • Random treatment assignment was not available. Conditional exchangeability is an identifying assumption, not an observed property.
  • Important potential confounders were unavailable: BMI, smoking, blood pressure, and LVEF. The BMI analysis covers only specified scenarios and does not address all unmeasured variables jointly.
  • UACR was missing in about one quarter to one third of participants; other key laboratory variables also required imputation.
  • Drug discontinuation was common. Per-protocol estimates require additional assumptions about time-varying confounding and censoring.
  • Stockholm data may not transfer directly to systems with different access, prescribing, cost, adherence, race/ethnicity composition, and baseline HF risk.
  • The supplied materials do not describe a proportional-hazards diagnostic for the Cox models.
Interpretation boundary. HR 1.02 with a 95% CI of 0.85-1.18 does not demonstrate formal equivalence of GLP-1RA and SGLT-2i. The defensible statement is that the study observed no meaningful difference under its design and precision.

Clinical meaning

Within this source, the clearest signal is the GLP-1RA vs DPP-4i comparison: lower relative HHF risk plus a modest absolute 3-year difference, larger among patients with higher baseline HF risk. The SGLT-2i comparison is better viewed as a head-to-head observational comparison with compatible rates, not as evidence that the two classes are interchangeable for every patient or HF phenotype.

The article itself calls for replication and randomized confirmation. It also cannot separate HFpEF from HFrEF. Any treatment choice that depends on current guidelines, approved indications, kidney function, obesity treatment, cost, or patient-specific contraindications requires information outside this source packet.
