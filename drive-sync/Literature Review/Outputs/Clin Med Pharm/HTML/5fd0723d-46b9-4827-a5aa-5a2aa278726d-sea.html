One-page synthesis

Core thesis: The postdischarge burden after chronic/subacute SDH is broader than surgical recurrence. In this cohort, 29.0% were readmitted within 90 days, only 22.5% of readmissions involved surgical SDH recurrence, and only 44.0% were directly SDH-related. Infection, cardiovascular, stroke/VTE, and other readmissions contributed substantial mortality and functional loss.

Among 22,387 qualifying hospitalizations, 6,497 patients were readmitted within 90 days. Infection readmissions were especially consequential: mortality was 9.6%, compared with 2.9% for surgical SDH readmissions, and among patients initially discharged with routine self-care, 45.5% were newly disabled after an infection readmission. Surgical recurrence remained the most resource-intensive readmission type by length of stay and cost, but it was not the dominant source of postdischarge morbidity.

The machine-learning pipeline used a 7-class LightGBM model as a feature transformer, generated 350-dimensional patient-level SHAP attribution profiles from the top 50 features across 7 classes, and clustered those profiles with K-means. Five phenotypes were labeled low acuity, elderly/frail, atrial fibrillation, young/healthy, and high acuity. The clusters showed differing observed-to-expected readmission patterns, including elevated surgical recurrence in the low-acuity cluster, elevated infection in elderly/frail and high-acuity clusters, and markedly elevated cardiovascular and stroke/VTE readmissions in the atrial-fibrillation cluster.

Central interpretive limit: the clusters were constructed from model attributions to the same readmission classes later used to characterize their readmission profiles. The resulting O/E differences demonstrate within-dataset phenotype separation, not independent validation of future risk or clinical utility.

Section condensation: Methods

Population and outcome construction

The study used NRD data from 2016-2022 and included adults with nonelective SDH admissions coded as chronic, subacute, or acute-on-chronic. The analysis excluded index deaths, DNR patients, nonresidents, records with missing key fields, October-December discharges, index-admission MMAE, and elective MMAE as the first readmission. NRD linkage follows patients only within the same state and calendar year.

The primary outcome was first all-cause readmission within 90 days. Readmissions were assigned hierarchically to surgical SDH, nonsurgical SDH/seizure, stroke/VTE, cardiovascular, infection, other, or not readmitted. Secondary impact measures were length of stay, inflation-adjusted cost, in-hospital mortality, functional decline, new disability, and inability to return home.

Machine-learning phenotyping

A multinomial LightGBM model predicted the 7-class outcome from index-admission demographic, comorbidity, SDH, procedure, diagnosis/CCSR, and hospital features. Training used 5-fold stratified cross-validation, 100-iteration Bayesian hyperparameter optimization, and post hoc temperature scaling. The authors explicitly describe the model as a feature-transformation tool rather than a clinical prediction instrument.

SHAP values for the top 50 features across all 7 outcome classes formed a 350-dimensional vector for each patient. K-means clustering was applied to these vectors; k was selected using an elbow plot, BIC, silhouette width, 100-resample consensus stability, and clinical/pathophysiologic congruency. Cluster comparisons used z-scored feature means and O/E readmission ratios with exact Poisson 95% CIs.

Statistical analysis

Continuous and categorical comparisons used Kruskal-Wallis and chi-square tests, respectively, with standardized mean differences to quantify baseline effect sizes. Two-sided p<0.05 was the stated significance threshold. Analyses were performed in R 4.5.2.

The primary PDF does not specify whether the final SHAP matrix was generated strictly from out-of-fold predictions for every patient. That is an appraisal uncertainty because fully out-of-fold attribution generation would reduce optimism and leakage concerns in downstream clustering.

Section condensation: Results

Cohort and readmission spectrum

Of 42,825 nonelective SDH admissions screened, 22,387 met criteria. Mean age was 70.8 years, 29.6% were female, and 58.9% underwent surgery. Readmission occurred in 6,497 patients (29.0%). Readmitted patients had greater comorbidity burden and were less likely to have undergone surgery during the index hospitalization.

Surgical SDH accounted for 22.5% of readmissions and nonsurgical SDH/seizure for 21.5%; together, directly SDH-related events were 44.0%. Other causes accounted for 30.9%, infection 14.1%, cardiovascular causes 6.4%, and stroke/VTE 4.5%. Overall, 66.8% of readmissions occurred in the first 30 days, although cardiovascular and infection events were relatively less concentrated in that earliest period.

Clinical impact of readmission

Across all readmissions, in-hospital mortality was 4.2%, functional decline 22.9%, new disability among routine index discharges 36.3%, and inability to return home among prior home discharges 25.3%. Surgical SDH readmissions had the longest and most costly episodes, but mortality was 2.9%.

Infection readmissions had the highest mortality at 9.6% and the highest new-disability rate among routine index discharges at 45.5%. Cardiovascular mortality was 5.8%. Infection also had the highest inability-to-return-home rate (31.5%), followed by surgical SDH (28.3%).

Model and phenotypes

The multinomial model's overall AUC was 0.66 (95% CI 0.64-0.68), which the authors characterize as modest but adequate for feature transformation. Five clusters were selected: low acuity 39.8%, elderly/frail 16.6%, atrial fibrillation 16.9%, young/healthy 14.5%, and high acuity 12.1%.

Within-dataset O/E patterns aligned with cluster labels. Low-acuity patients had higher surgical SDH O/E (1.29, 95% CI 1.19-1.38); elderly/frail patients had higher infection O/E (1.71, 1.51-1.92); the AF cluster had higher stroke/VTE (1.91, 1.52-2.29) and cardiovascular (2.43, 2.08-2.80) O/E; and the high-acuity cluster had higher infection (1.80, 1.55-2.05) and other-cause readmissions (1.46, 1.31-1.61).

Discussion, limitations, and conclusion

The authors argue that cSDH trials and care models should not treat surgical recurrence as the sole major outcome. They highlight the AF phenotype as a population facing unresolved anticoagulation tradeoffs and elderly/frail and high-acuity phenotypes as populations with substantial infection burden. These treatment-related points are hypotheses and implications, not interventions tested by this study.

Reported limitations include claims-data absence of imaging/labs/medication dose/outpatient detail, uncaptured out-of-hospital mortality, exclusion of MMAE/DNR/late-year patients, missing race/ethnicity and area-deprivation data, modest model discrimination, and the exploratory nature of clustering. The authors call for prospective and independent external validation.

Workflow — supervised attribution to unsupervised phenotype

Index-admission features
  -> 7-class LightGBM model
     -> cross-validation + Bayesian tuning + temperature calibration
        -> SHAP values for top 50 features x 7 classes
           -> 350-dimensional patient attribution profile
              -> K-means clustering
                 -> 5 phenotype labels
                    -> within-cohort cluster characteristics and O/E readmission profiles

Failure modes / governance: no prospective assignment, no external cluster validation, no demonstrated bedside threshold, uncertain strictly out-of-fold SHAP generation, and phenotype naming includes clinical interpretation. Human review is therefore required before translating clusters into decision support.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best use is to broaden the conceptual outcome set for cSDH research and to generate hypotheses for phenotype-specific postdischarge surveillance. Do not use it as an individual risk predictor or to choose anticoagulation timing, MMAE, surgery, or prophylactic interventions.

Directly addresses post-cSDH readmission burden and uses an interpretable ML phenotyping workflow.

Evidence basis: Large clinical cohort; readmission and functional outcomes; clinically recognizable clusters.

Implementation value

The findings can inform trial design and follow-up priorities but do not provide a validated bedside tool.

Evidence basis: Distinct infection, cardiovascular, stroke/VTE, and recurrence patterns suggest different postdischarge surveillance needs.

Principal limit: The model was explicitly not intended as a clinical prediction instrument, and the clusters lack prospective validation or intervention thresholds.

Would raise score: A validated scoring/assignment method linked to tested interventions would materially increase implementation value.

Major strengths

  • Large multi-year all-payer readmission cohort with clinically consequential outcomes, not merely repeat surgery.
  • Clear hierarchical readmission taxonomy and functional-disposition outcomes with denominators reported.
  • Machine-learning pipeline is unusually explicit about its feature-transformation purpose and includes calibration, tuning, cluster-stability checks, and interpretable attribution features.
  • Figures and Table 2 make the mismatch between surgical recurrence and total postdischarge burden easy to see.

Major limitations and safety boundaries

  • No causal inference: associations between patient characteristics, clusters, and readmission types do not show that modifying those features will prevent events.
  • Outcome-informed phenotypes: SHAP features encode the supervised model's readmission-class structure. Showing that the resulting clusters have different readmission profiles is partly expected and should not be treated as external validation.
  • Claims-data phenotype ceiling: imaging, neurologic exam, medication intensity, laboratory data, outpatient follow-up, frailty measures, and adherence are absent.
  • Readmission capture limits: same-state and calendar-year linkage can miss events outside those boundaries; out-of-hospital deaths are unobserved.
  • Contemporary applicability: MMAE patients were excluded, limiting direct relevance as MMAE use expands.
  • Clinical speculation: discussion of anticoagulation resumption, MMAE, infection prophylaxis, and targeted follow-up is hypothesis-generating. None of these strategies was compared or validated here.

Clinical / pharmacy translation

The AF phenotype is clinically notable because it concentrates stroke/VTE and cardiovascular readmissions, but the study does not evaluate when or how to resume anticoagulation after SDH. The paper should therefore be used to identify a research and care-coordination gap, not to justify a specific restart interval. Likewise, the elevated infection burden supports attention to mobility, aspiration, urinary, wound, and postdischarge surveillance pathways, but no prophylactic medication or monitoring protocol was tested.

AI/ML interpretation

The strongest methodological idea is using class-specific SHAP attribution patterns as a patient representation before clustering. That can reveal heterogeneity in how predictors contribute to different outcome classes. The key caveat is that this representation is learned from the outcomes themselves; cluster-outcome separation within the same dataset is therefore descriptive rather than a clean validation target. Reproducibility would be stronger with an explicitly out-of-fold embedding pipeline, alternative-representation sensitivity analyses, locked clustering rules, and external assignment of new patients to the learned phenotypes.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

When reviewing cSDH outcomes or trial designs, include all-cause readmission, infection, cardiovascular/thromboembolic events, functional decline, and disposition alongside surgical recurrence.

Do not use now

Do not assign a patient to one of these clusters for treatment selection, or use the paper to determine anticoagulation restart, MMAE timing, infection prophylaxis, or discharge destination.

Research next

Externally validate the phenotypes in a contemporary cohort that includes MMAE, richer clinical/radiographic variables, out-of-hospital mortality, and explicit out-of-fold model-attribution generation.

Trial design implication

Prespecify outcomes that capture overall morbidity and patient function rather than relying primarily on hematoma recurrence or repeat surgery.
