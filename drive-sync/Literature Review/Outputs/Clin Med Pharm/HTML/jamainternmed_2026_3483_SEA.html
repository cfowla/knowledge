One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Medication regimens were frequently unchanged even when older adults' HbA1c values were outside health-status-specific ADA goal ranges. Deintensification for below-goal HbA1c increased over 2019-2023 but remained uncommon, while intensification for above-goal HbA1c remained low.

The study analyzed 89,987 observations from adults aged 65 years or older with T2D in Cleveland Clinic EHR data from 2019-2023. Health status was categorized using terminal conditions and chronic-condition burden, and HbA1c status was defined relative to the health-status-specific ADA goals used by the investigators. The primary outcomes were regimen intensification and deintensification based on changes in diabetes drug-class count or noninsulin dose within 3 months after versus 3 months before the index HbA1c.

In 2023, intensification occurred in 32.8% of patients with above-goal HbA1c versus 16.1% with at-goal HbA1c; deintensification occurred in 20.3% versus 16.3%. For below-goal HbA1c, deintensification was 16.9% versus 16.3% at goal, while intensification was 7.7% versus 16.1%. Below-goal deintensification rose from 9.4% in 2019 to 16.9% in 2023. The practical signal is a persistent gap between health-status/glycemic-status stratification and medication adjustment, but the EHR design cannot determine whether non-adjustment reflected clinical inertia, patient preference, competing indications, hypoglycemia history, or other individualized reasoning.

Section condensation

Introduction / objective

The authors frame older adults with T2D as heterogeneous in life expectancy, comorbidity, and functional status, and cite ADA guidance supporting health-status-specific glycemic goals. The study question is whether medication regimens are being adjusted in a manner that reflects both health status and observed glycemic control.

Methods

Patients were ≥65 years with T2D, ≥1 diabetes medication, and a first HbA1c measurement in each study year from 2019-2023. Poor health required at least 1 terminal condition; intermediate health required ≥3 chronic conditions; good health met neither criterion. Investigators used HbA1c goals of <7.5%, <8%, and <9% for good, intermediate, and poor health, respectively, and defined “at goal” as the goal threshold down to 1.5 percentage points below it.

Intensification/deintensification reflected an increase/decrease in drug-class count or noninsulin dose within 3 months after versus before the index HbA1c. Insulin-dose change itself was not captured, although long- and short-acting insulin counted as separate classes. Annual prevalence was stratified by glycemic and health status, with mixed-effect logistic regression for trends and a Bonferroni-adjusted P<.025 threshold. Three sensitivity analyses addressed insulin use, cardio-renal indications for SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA, and restriction to sulfonylurea/insulin users.

Results

The sample included 89,987 observations (mean age 71.6 years; 50.5% male). Overall, 48.4% were below goal, 37.8% at goal, and 13.8% above goal; 30.6% were in poor health. In 2023, 48.9% of all patients, 34.7% of below-goal patients, and 43.9% of poor-health/below-goal patients were prescribed sulfonylureas and/or insulin.

For above-goal HbA1c, 2023 intensification was 32.8% versus 16.1% at goal, and deintensification was 20.3% versus 16.3%. From 2019-2023, above-goal intensification was statistically unchanged (30.7% to 32.8%; P=.14) while deintensification rose (14.6% to 20.3%; P<.001). For below-goal HbA1c, 2023 deintensification was similar to at goal (16.9% vs 16.3%; P=.338), while intensification was lower (7.7% vs 16.1%; P<.001). Both below-goal intensification and deintensification increased over time, primarily in intermediate- and poor-health groups. Sensitivity analyses were similar.

Discussion / conclusion

The authors emphasize that below-goal deintensification roughly doubled but remained uncommon, and they interpret intensification among frail/medically complex patients with below-goal HbA1c as concerning. Conversely, they view the low intensification rate among otherwise healthy patients with above-goal HbA1c as a missed opportunity for glycemic control.

They conclude that most older adults outside the ADA-recommended HbA1c range did not have medications adjusted and call for better guideline adherence and safer medication options. Their explanation remains hypothesis-generating because the dataset lacks clinician rationale, patient preference, functional status, and complete insulin-dose changes.

Limitations and disclosures

Reported limitations include inability to assess insulin-dose changes or decision rationale, use of only the first annual HbA1c, lack of ADL data, potential comorbidity undercoding, and possible nonglycemic indications for medications. The study is also structurally limited by a single-health-system EHR design, which is an appraisal inference rather than an author-reported limitation.

Disclosures include grants or personal fees reported by Misra-Hebert, Taksler, and Rothberg as specified in the article; no other disclosures were reported. Data-sharing details are directed to Supplement 2, which was not embedded in the retrieved PDF.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best use: contemporary benchmark for quality-improvement, deprescribing, and medication-stewardship questions in older adults with T2D. Do not use as a stand-alone clinical decision rule or as causal evidence that a specific medication change improves outcomes.

Directly addresses medication adjustment quality in older adults with T2D, a common pharmacotherapy and deprescribing problem.

Evidence basis: Large contemporary EHR cohort with health-status and glycemic-status stratification.

Limiting factor: Does not test a pharmacist-led or medication-specific intervention.

Would raise score: Higher if linked to actionable medication-level decision pathways or outcomes.

Evidence strength6.5/10

Clinical-practice appraisal

Clinical question / PEO
Population: adults ≥65 years with T2D on diabetes medication.
Exposure: health-status-specific HbA1c category and study year.
Comparator: at-goal HbA1c / other health strata.
Outcomes: medication intensification and deintensification.
Internal validity
Clear definitions and large sample support descriptive estimates. However, medication changes are imperfectly captured, glycemic status uses a single annual HbA1c, and decision rationale is unmeasured.
Practice translation
The study can identify audit targets: persistent below-goal HbA1c with high hypoglycemia-risk therapy, and above-goal HbA1c without intensification in otherwise healthier patients. Any patient-level action still requires individualized assessment.
Use now?
Use now for quality-improvement hypothesis generation, medication-stewardship metrics, and literature framing. Do not use alone to trigger automatic deprescribing/intensification.
Key appraisal constraint: “Below goal” and “above goal” are study-defined categories based on ADA goals and health-status classification; the EHR cannot establish that every observed non-adjustment or adjustment was inappropriate. Patient preference, hypoglycemia history, life expectancy, functional status, competing indications, and clinical trajectory may alter appropriateness.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Potential stewardship metric
Track older adults with below-goal HbA1c who remain on sulfonylurea and/or insulin, but require clinical review rather than automatic action.
Bidirectional problem
The paper highlights both possible overtreatment (below-goal without deintensification) and undertreatment (above-goal without intensification), preventing a one-direction deprescribing narrative.
Data requirements for a safer CDS build
Add hypoglycemia history, insulin dose, ADLs/frailty, renal function, patient goals, competing drug indications, and clinician rationale before classifying a regimen as mismatched.
What to read next
The linked supplement is necessary to audit detailed coding definitions and data-sharing conditions. Multisite or interventional evidence would be needed to support generalizable workflow changes.
