Comparing Effectiveness and Safety of SGLT2 Inhibitors vs DPP-4 Inhibitors in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes and Varying Baseline HbA1c Levels

Metadata and scope

Design
New-user active-comparator observational comparative effectiveness/safety study with propensity-score matching.
Population
144,614 eligible commercially insured/Medicare Advantage adults with T2D; 87,274 matched.
Exposure/comparator
SGLT2 inhibitor initiation vs DPP-4 inhibitor initiation.
Data
Optum Clinformatics claims linked to outpatient laboratory results; April 2013-June 2021.
Primary outcomes
Modified MACE and hospitalization for heart failure.
Safety
Hypovolemia, fractures, falls, genital infections, DKA, AKI, lower-limb amputation.
Scope warning: This is comparative observational evidence. It supports association and transportability questions but does not provide randomized causal proof. The comparison is specifically SGLT2i vs DPP-4i.

One-page synthesis

The study asks whether the comparative cardiovascular and safety profile of SGLT2 inhibitors changes across baseline HbA1c levels. In the 87,274-patient matched cohort, SGLT2i initiation was associated with lower modified MACE (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75-0.95; RD -3.02/1000 person-years) and substantially lower HHF (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.35-0.57; RD -4.37/1000 person-years). The relative estimates for MACE (HR range 0.83-0.88) and HHF (0.44-0.48) were similar across HbA1c strata, with no evidence of heterogeneity.

Safety findings align with the established SGLT2i profile: genital infections were more frequent (HR 2.17, 95% CI 1.98-2.36), DKA was more frequent (HR 1.73, 95% CI 1.06-2.43), and AKI was less frequent (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.66-0.81). Hypovolemia, nonvertebral fractures, falls, and amputations were not clearly different. Genital-infection risk was the notable HbA1c interaction, peaking in the 7.5%-9% stratum (HR 3.10, 95% CI 2.68-3.58; RD 46.22/1000 person-years). The most useful interpretation is not “SGLT2i are equally safe for everyone,” but that higher baseline HbA1c did not systematically magnify the measured adverse-event profile relative to DPP-4i.

Methods condensation

Study design and cohort

Eligible adults had T2D, newly initiated either drug class, and had a recent HbA1c result. The source dataset supplied nationwide commercial/Medicare Advantage claims with linked outpatient labs for a subset of beneficiaries. The study excluded patients with several conditions likely to complicate treatment choice or outcome ascertainment, including ESRD/dialysis/transplant, type 1 diabetes, secondary/gestational diabetes, selected malignancy, and nursing-home residence. The main analytic cohort was split into baseline HbA1c <7.5%, 7.5%-9%, and >9% strata.

Confounding control and follow-up

A logistic propensity score for SGLT2i vs DPP-4i was estimated separately within each HbA1c stratum using 128 prespecified covariates, followed by 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with a 0.01 caliper. The as-treated follow-up began the day after initiation and censored at discontinuation, switching/augmentation, outcome, death, disenrollment, or end of data. Balance was assessed with standardized differences; laboratory variables not entered into the PS model were also examined after matching.

Methodological strengths: active comparator, new-user design, subgroup-specific PS estimation, extensive measured confounding control, direct balance checks, and sensitivity analyses targeted to informative censoring, time-lag bias, comparator-specific HHF concern, and recurrent safety outcomes.

Results condensation

Population

Among 144,614 eligible adults, 60,523 initiated SGLT2i and 84,091 initiated DPP-4i. Matching retained 87,274 patients: 24,052 with HbA1c <7.5%, 32,290 with HbA1c 7.5%-9%, and 30,932 with HbA1c >9%. Before matching, treatment groups differed on age, obesity, eGFR, diabetic nephropathy/CKD and concomitant glucose-lowering therapies; after matching, measured characteristics and the propensity-score distributions were well aligned.

Effectiveness

Modified MACE occurred at 17.13 vs 20.18 events/1000 person-years (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75-0.95; RD -3.02, 95% CI -5.23 to -0.80). HHF occurred at 3.68 vs 8.08/1000 person-years (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.35-0.57; RD -4.37, 95% CI -5.62 to -3.12). Myocardial infarction and stroke were not individually clearly different, whereas all-cause mortality was lower (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.59-0.88). MACE and HHF estimates were directionally consistent across HbA1c strata, and the cumulative-incidence curves separated early.

Safety

Genital infection was higher with SGLT2i (65.41 vs 27.99/1000 person-years; HR 2.17, 95% CI 1.98-2.36; RD +37.53/1000 person-years). DKA was also higher (HR 1.73, 95% CI 1.06-2.43), while AKI was lower (20.54 vs 28.05/1000 person-years; HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.66-0.81; RD -7.60/1000 person-years). Hypovolemia, nonvertebral fracture, falls and lower-limb amputation estimates crossed the null. Genital infections showed effect heterogeneity across HbA1c strata; other safety outcomes did not.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: high-value comparative-effectiveness evidence for whether baseline HbA1c should materially alter expected SGLT2i vs DPP-4i cardiovascular/safety tradeoffs. Do not use as stand-alone proof of causality or as a substitute for current guideline/label contraindication and monitoring decisions.

Relevance — 9/10

Directly addresses a common pharmacotherapy decision involving SGLT2i benefit-risk across glycemic control.

Evidence basis: Large routine-care T2D cohort with cardiorenal and safety outcomes.

Limiting factor: Comparator-specific and observational.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with direct replication in another lab-linked claims/EHR source.

Novelty — 7/10

The main added value is explicit effect-modification analysis across baseline HbA1c strata, especially safety.

Evidence basis: Three large HbA1c subcohorts with matched comparisons.

Limiting factor: SGLT2i cardiorenal benefits and major adverse effects were already established.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with a prespecified externally replicated effect-modification result.

Method strength — 7/10

Active-comparator new-user design, 128-covariate PS matching, balance checks, and multiple sensitivity analyses are strong for RWE.

Evidence basis: 87,274 matched patients; PS re-estimated within strata; eTables 8-9 challenge key biases.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding, treatment discontinuation, and claims outcome limitations remain.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with target-trial emulation using richer clinical covariates or randomized evidence for the HbA1c interaction question.

Evidence strength — 7/10

Overall MACE, HHF, genital-infection, DKA, and AKI signals are quantitatively coherent and mostly precise.

Evidence basis: HRs, 95% CIs, absolute RDs, event counts, and sensitivity analyses are reported.

Limiting factor: Observational design; some subgroup estimates are imprecise; mean follow-up is about 8 months.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with longer follow-up and independent replication of subgroup safety effects.

External validity — 7/10

Routine-care population is broader than CVOT populations and includes varying cardiovascular risk and HbA1c.

Evidence basis: Commercial/Medicare Advantage US claims with lab linkage and feasibility comparison.

Limiting factor: Requires available HbA1c/creatinine data and excludes ESRD, nursing-home, and several other groups.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with Medicaid, uninsured, nursing-home, advanced CKD, and non-US validation.

Implementation value — 8/10

Supports not withholding SGLT2i solely because HbA1c is high while retaining attention to genital infection and DKA risk.

Evidence basis: Benefits for MACE/HHF and AKI and expected adverse-event pattern were consistent across most HbA1c strata.

Limiting factor: Does not establish superiority for every patient or replace individualized contraindication/monitoring decisions.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with direct decision-impact studies and contemporary class-specific guideline integration.

Key validity concerns

  • Residual confounding remains possible despite extensive PS adjustment; diabetes duration and BMI are specifically acknowledged as unavailable/unmeasured.
  • Average on-treatment follow-up is short relative to CVOTs; treatment discontinuation drives much censoring.
  • Subgroup estimates for uncommon outcomes, especially DKA, are less precise.
  • Claims-based outcomes can be misclassified; cardiovascular death cannot be isolated because cause of death is unavailable.
  • The lab-linked requirement may select a population with more documented testing, although eTable 1 supports broad measured similarity to eligible patients without HbA1c data.

Sensitivity interpretation

The 1-year ITT-like MACE estimate attenuated to HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.82-1.01), which weakens a strong causal interpretation of the MACE association. HHF remained robust (ITT-like HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.46-0.68), and remained lower when restricting to metformin/no insulin and when using sitagliptin alone as comparator. This pattern makes the HHF finding more persuasive than the modest MACE difference.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this supports

Baseline HbA1c alone should not be assumed to erase SGLT2i cardiovascular/HHF benefit relative to DPP-4i. The study directly addresses the concern that poorer glycemic control might systematically worsen SGLT2i tolerability.

What to monitor

Genital infection and DKA remain clinically relevant harms; the genital-infection absolute excess is large, and the 7.5%-9% subgroup had the highest observed excess.

What not to overclaim

The study does not prove equal safety across every HbA1c level, does not establish long-term comparative effects, and does not generalize automatically to patients excluded from the cohort.

Strongest signal

HHF reduction is large, precise, consistent across HbA1c strata, and robust across sensitivity analyses. The MACE effect is smaller and more sensitive to analysis assumptions.
