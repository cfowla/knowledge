One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. DECLARE–TIMI 58 is the foundational dapagliflozin cardiovascular outcomes trial and a key piece of the evidence showing that SGLT2 benefit is more consistent for heart-failure hospitalization and renal progression than for atherosclerotic events.

Core finding. Dapagliflozin met MACE cardiovascular-safety noninferiority but did not significantly reduce MACE (8.8% vs 9.4%; HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.84–1.03). It did reduce cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure (4.9% vs 5.8%; HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73–0.95), driven by hospitalization for heart failure (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.61–0.88), while cardiovascular death itself was neutral (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.82–1.17).

Renal and safety signal. The prespecified renal composite was lower (4.3% vs 5.6%; HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.67–0.87), and the renal-specific composite excluding cardiovascular death was lower still (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.43–0.66), but these sit outside the successful co-primary efficacy claim and require multiplicity-aware interpretation. Diabetic ketoacidosis and serious/discontinuing genital infection were increased, whereas acute kidney injury and major hypoglycemia were lower; Table 2 safety P values were unadjusted for multiple comparisons.

Why the design matters. The trial originally had one primary efficacy outcome, MACE. After external EMPA-REG OUTCOME findings, cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure was added as a second primary efficacy endpoint before the DMC reviewed comparative MACE efficacy data. Alpha was split without increasing sample size. The amendment was transparently documented and regulator-facing, but it is still an important interpretive feature.

Section condensation

Background and rationale

The trial addressed dapagliflozin cardiovascular safety and potential efficacy in a broad type 2 diabetes population. Prior SGLT2 outcome trials had already suggested prominent heart-failure and renal effects, motivating evaluation beyond atherosclerotic MACE.

The key design inflection occurred during the trial: external EMPA-REG OUTCOME results prompted a protocol amendment adding cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure as a co-primary efficacy endpoint while retaining MACE safety and efficacy assessments.

Trial design and oversight

DECLARE–TIMI 58 randomized 17,160 participants 1:1 after a placebo run-in to dapagliflozin 10 mg daily or placebo at 882 sites in 33 countries. Participants had type 2 diabetes, age ≥40 years, HbA1c 6.5% to <12%, creatinine clearance ≥60 mL/min, and either established ASCVD or multiple cardiovascular risk factors.

The sponsor supported the trial and participated in design, while the complete raw database was provided to the TIMI Study Group for independent analysis. The protocol/SAP package documents revision history, final analysis sets, event-adjudication strategy, Cox modeling, sensitivity analyses, and the closed testing/multiplicity framework.

Population, exposure, and follow-up

The randomized cohort was 40.6% established ASCVD and 59.4% multiple-risk-factor primary-prevention/high-risk participants. Baseline HbA1c was 8.3%, mean eGFR about 85 mL/min/1.73 m², and about 10% had prior heart failure. Background cardiometabolic treatment was substantial, including ACE inhibitor/ARB use in about 81% and statin/ezetimibe use in about 75%.

Median follow-up was 4.2 years. Trial-drug discontinuation was lower with dapagliflozin than placebo (21.1% vs 25.1%); consent withdrawal and loss to follow-up were uncommon. The supplement’s Figure S1 makes the run-in selection visible: 25,698 entered run-in before 17,160 were randomized.

Cardiometabolic effects

Dapagliflozin produced modest sustained separation in HbA1c, body weight, and blood pressure. Average HbA1c was 0.42 percentage points lower, weight reduction 1.8 kg greater, systolic blood-pressure reduction 2.7 mm Hg greater, and diastolic reduction 0.7 mm Hg greater than placebo.

These intermediate effects support biological activity but do not explain away or substitute for the randomized clinical endpoints. Open-label GLP-1 receptor agonist and SGLT2 inhibitor use occurred during follow-up and was more common in the placebo arm for open-label SGLT2 therapy.

Cardiovascular and renal outcomes

The MACE safety criterion was met (P<0.001 for noninferiority), but superiority was not (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.84–1.03; P=0.17). The other co-primary efficacy outcome, cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure, was reduced (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73–0.95; P=0.005), driven by hospitalization for heart failure (HR 0.73), not cardiovascular death (HR 0.98).

The renal composite was lower (HR 0.76) and the stricter renal-specific composite excluding cardiovascular death had HR 0.53. All-cause mortality was neutral (HR 0.93). Because MACE superiority failed and secondary comparisons were not all protected by the primary hierarchy, the strongest practice-level inference from this source is the heart-failure hospitalization signal; renal findings are compelling but must be interpreted within the testing structure.

Subgroups and sensitivity analyses

The heart-failure composite effect was similar in established ASCVD and multiple-risk-factor strata (interaction P=0.99). Supplementary analyses also showed broadly similar renal and heart-failure patterns across enrollment stratum, baseline heart-failure history, and renal-function categories, without persuasive interaction signals for the principal outcomes.

Competing-risk and per-protocol sensitivity analyses for the primary outcomes remained close to the main estimates. Figure S3 contains many exploratory subgroup comparisons; nominal interaction findings should not be elevated to definitive treatment-effect modifiers.

Safety

Dapagliflozin increased diabetic ketoacidosis (0.3% vs 0.1%; HR 2.18) and serious/discontinuing genital infection (0.9% vs 0.1%; HR 8.36). Amputation, fracture, volume depletion, urinary tract infection, overall cancer, hypersensitivity, and hepatic-event estimates did not show clear excesses.

Serious adverse events overall were lower (34.1% vs 36.2%; HR 0.91), as were acute kidney injury (1.5% vs 2.0%; HR 0.69) and major hypoglycemia (0.7% vs 1.0%; HR 0.68). These safety comparisons are informative but Table 2 explicitly states that P values and 95% confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpreted the totality as showing a more robust SGLT2 effect on heart-failure and renal outcomes than on atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. They did not demonstrate reduced cardiovascular or all-cause mortality.

Important limitations are the mid-trial co-primary endpoint amendment, alpha splitting without sample-size increase, placebo run-in selection, exclusion of screening creatinine clearance <60 mL/min, and multiplicity around many secondary and safety findings. These do not negate the randomized heart-failure signal but constrain overgeneralization.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance — 10/10

Direct landmark evidence for dapagliflozin cardiovascular, heart-failure, renal, and safety effects in type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis: Large broad CVOT with prespecified outcomes.

Principal limit: Does not directly address inpatient initiation or all contemporary indications.

Would change score: Current-practice synthesis with later HF/CKD trials would determine present-day implementation.

Novelty — 9/10

At publication it materially extended SGLT2 outcomes evidence to a majority primary-prevention/high-risk cohort.

Evidence basis: 10,186 participants lacked established ASCVD.

Principal limit: Class-level HF/renal signals had already emerged with empagliflozin and canagliflozin.

Would change score: A head-to-head or mechanistic discriminator would raise novelty further.

Method strength — 9/10

Large randomized double-blind placebo-controlled event-driven trial with blinded adjudication and prespecified SAP.

Evidence basis: 17,160 randomized; 69,547 patient-years; low loss to follow-up.

Principal limit: Mid-trial primary-endpoint amendment and alpha split are nontrivial design complications.

Would change score: An unchanged original primary hierarchy would remove the main design caveat.

Evidence strength — 9/10

Heart-failure composite and renal effects are supported by precise randomized estimates; MACE and mortality nulls are also informative.

Evidence basis: CVD/HHF HR 0.83; HHF HR 0.73; renal composite HR 0.76.

Principal limit: Many secondary/safety comparisons were not multiplicity-adjusted; renal endpoint included cardiovascular death.

Would change score: Replication in dedicated HF/CKD trials and multiplicity-protected renal outcomes strengthen causal translation.

External validity — 8/10

Broad global diabetes population included both established ASCVD and multiple-risk-factor patients.

Evidence basis: 882 sites, 33 countries; 59.4% without established ASCVD.

Principal limit: CrCl <60 mL/min excluded and placebo run-in selected for adherence.

Would change score: More advanced CKD and less selected real-world populations would broaden applicability.

Implementation value — 9/10

Clarifies where dapagliflozin benefit is most consistent: HF hospitalization and renal progression rather than MACE or mortality.

Evidence basis: Effect pattern, subgroup analyses, and detailed safety table are clinically actionable.

Principal limit: Trial predates later dedicated HF/CKD indications and does not by itself define current inpatient formulary use.

Would change score: Integration with subsequent DAPA-HF/DELIVER/DAPA-CKD and current labeling/guidelines would complete practice translation.

Best use: landmark causal evidence for dapagliflozin’s HF-hospitalization and renal signal in broad type 2 diabetes cardiovascular-risk populations.
Do not use for: claiming MACE, cardiovascular-mortality, or all-cause mortality benefit; treating every exploratory subgroup/safety P value as confirmatory; or making a present-day inpatient formulary recommendation without later dedicated HF/CKD and current regulatory/guideline evidence.

Confidence in appraisal: high. The main article, supplementary appendix, and final protocol/SAP package were available and internally reconciled.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Preserve
The distinction between MACE neutrality and HF-hospitalization benefit.
Preserve
The renal composite definition and the stricter renal-specific composite separately.
Monitor
DKA and genital infection risk; avoid treating nominal safety comparisons as multiplicity-protected.
Contextualize
CrCl ≥60 eligibility and placebo run-in when translating to CKD or less-selected populations.
Read next
DECLARE design/baseline papers, CANVAS/EMPA-REG, then dedicated dapagliflozin HF and CKD trials.
Do not overclaim
No demonstrated reduction in MACE, cardiovascular death, or all-cause mortality in this source.
