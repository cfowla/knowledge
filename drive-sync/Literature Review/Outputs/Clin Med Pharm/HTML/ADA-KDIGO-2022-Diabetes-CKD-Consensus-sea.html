2. Synthesis

Core thesis. ADA and KDIGO were substantially aligned in 2022 on CKD screening/diagnosis, individualized glycemic monitoring, lifestyle treatment, and layered pharmacotherapy. The report converts that alignment into seven joint consensus statements centered on RAS inhibition, statins, metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and nonsteroidal MRA therapy.

The report is particularly useful as an implementation bridge between two larger guidelines. Its clinical value is the compression of a broad CKD/diabetes treatment architecture into a small set of jointly endorsed actions, while retaining important thresholds such as eGFR ≥30 for metformin, eGFR ≥20 for SGLT2 inhibitor initiation, and eGFR ≥25 plus normal potassium and persistent albuminuria for nonsteroidal MRA therapy.

3. Seven joint consensus statements

#Consensus statement
1All patients with T1D or T2D and CKD should receive a comprehensive plan, agreed by clinicians and the patient, that optimizes nutrition, exercise, smoking cessation, and weight and layers evidence-based pharmacologic therapies to preserve organ function and attain individualized glycemia, blood pressure, and lipid targets.
2An ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended for patients with T1D or T2D who have hypertension and albuminuria, titrated to the maximum antihypertensive or highest tolerated dose.
3A statin is recommended for all patients with T1D or T2D and CKD: moderate intensity for primary ASCVD prevention and high intensity for known ASCVD and some patients with multiple ASCVD risk factors.
4Metformin is recommended for patients with T2D, CKD, and eGFR ≥30 ml/min per 1.73 m²; reduce the dose to 1,000 mg/day when eGFR is 30–44 and in selected patients with eGFR 45–59 who are at high risk of lactic acidosis.
5An SGLT2 inhibitor with proven kidney or cardiovascular benefit is recommended for patients with T2D, CKD, and eGFR ≥20 ml/min per 1.73 m²; once initiated, it can be continued at lower eGFR.
6A GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven cardiovascular benefit is recommended for patients with T2D and CKD who do not meet their individualized glycemic target with metformin and/or an SGLT2 inhibitor or who cannot use those drugs.
7A nonsteroidal MRA with proven kidney and cardiovascular benefit is recommended for patients with T2D, eGFR ≥25 ml/min per 1.73 m², normal serum potassium, and albuminuria ACR ≥30 mg/g despite the maximum tolerated dose of a RAS inhibitor.

4. Screening, monitoring, and treatment architecture

Annual CKD screening uses both eGFR and urine ACR, starting at T2D diagnosis and after five years of T1D. Persistent abnormalities for at least three months define CKD. The report favors the 2021 race-free CKD-EPI creatinine equation and notes a role for cystatin C to improve precision. Figure 1 visually links screening to confirmation and immediate evidence-based treatment; Figure 2 maps eGFR/albuminuria risk to monitoring and referral intensity.

Figure 3 is the report’s most useful implementation visual: healthy diet, physical activity, smoking cessation, and weight management sit above first-line SGLT2i/metformin/RASi/statin therapy, with GLP-1 RA, nonsteroidal MRA, additional antihypertensives, antiplatelet therapy, and lipid therapy layered according to risk. Figure 4 frames multidisciplinary care, harmonized guidelines, education, risk mitigation, and self-management as responses to implementation barriers.

5. Evidence interpretation

Consensus statements were created only where the two organizations’ recommendations aligned and were supported by high-quality RCT evidence, but the report deliberately does not repeat evidence grades; the underlying ADA and KDIGO guidelines remain the grading source. Quantitative trial results quoted in the report—such as FIDELITY finerenone outcomes—are secondary summaries and should not be misclassified as data generated by this consensus publication.

Strengths. Clear joint endorsement, practical thresholds, strong organ-protection framing, explicit screening workflow, and substantial alignment between independent guideline processes. Limitations. It is a consensus synthesis rather than a primary study or a fresh systematic review, and the article itself identifies expert opinion as the nature of an ADA consensus report. Evidence levels must be recovered from the underlying guidelines.
