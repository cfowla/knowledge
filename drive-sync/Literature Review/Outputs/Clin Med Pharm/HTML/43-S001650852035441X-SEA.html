One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In the PIANO cohort, in utero exposure to biologics, thiopurines, or their combination was not associated with higher adjusted odds of the principal pregnancy/neonatal outcomes evaluated, while active maternal IBD was associated with spontaneous abortion and preterm birth predicted infant infection.

PIANO enrolled 1712 pregnant patients with IBD, with 1490 completed pregnancies and 1431 live births. Completed pregnancies were classified as unexposed to biologic/thiopurine therapy (n=379), biologic-exposed (n=642), thiopurine-exposed (n=242), or combination-exposed (n=227). Primary outcomes were congenital malformations, spontaneous abortion, preterm birth, low birth weight, and infant infections. Adjusted pregnancy models controlled for maternal age, steroid use, and disease activity. Across the primary adjusted comparisons, confidence intervals for preterm birth, low birth weight, and congenital malformation all included the null for each medication class. Combination therapy had a higher adjusted odds of cesarean section (OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.1-2.5), an outcome strongly susceptible to indication/disease phenotype; supplemental data show perianal IBD was especially common as a cesarean indication in the combination group.

In the SAB analysis among women enrolled before 20 weeks, biologic, thiopurine, and combination exposure were not predictive, whereas active disease (HR 3.41, 95% CI 1.51-7.69) and prior SAB (HR 2.17, 95% CI 1.05-4.49) were. Any first-year infection occurred in 47% unexposed, 45% biologic-exposed, 46% thiopurine-exposed, and 47% combination-exposed live births (P=.87); adjusted ORs were 0.92, 0.90, and 0.93, respectively. Preterm birth independently increased infection risk (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.19-2.51). Placental transfer differed by biologic: median infant/maternal concentration ratios were 2.4 for infliximab, 1.3 for adalimumab, and 0.0 for certolizumab pegol, but serum concentration category was not associated with first-year infection in the subset with measurements. Developmental analyses showed no coherent adverse pattern through 48 months, although isolated nominally significant domain differences occurred.

Main constraints: maternal self-report and no objective inflammatory markers; residual confounding by disease activity/indication; enrollment could occur late in pregnancy, under-ascertaining early SAB; 8.5% of enrolled women had no completed pregnancy outcome while no longer pregnant; rare outcomes have limited power (supplemental detectable RR about 1.6-2.3 for several outcomes); many secondary/subgroup comparisons were performed without a reported multiplicity adjustment.

Section condensation

Background and aims

Active IBD during pregnancy is associated with pregnancy loss, preterm birth, and other adverse outcomes, creating a clinical tradeoff when medication safety data are uncertain. The paper focuses on thiopurines and biologics (TNF antagonists, anti-integrins, and anti-IL-12/23 agents), with particular concern about placental transfer and infant immune/infectious outcomes.

The study aimed to compare maternal and infant outcomes across drug exposure categories, measure biologic transfer into cord/infant serum, and assess developmental milestones. The clinical question is therefore not simply drug safety in isolation: it explicitly weighs potential fetal/infant exposure against harm from uncontrolled maternal disease.

Methods: study design, exposure, outcomes

PIANO was a prospective observational cohort across 30 U.S. IBD centers. Women with IBD and singleton pregnancies could enroll during pregnancy. Medication exposure included use in the 3 months before the last menstrual period or at any time during pregnancy. Groups were unexposed (no biologic/thiopurine; mesalamine, corticosteroids, and antibiotics could still be used), thiopurine, biologic, and combination. Disease activity used HBI for Crohn disease and SCCAI for ulcerative colitis; flare required active disease plus treatment escalation, surgery, or hospitalization.

Primary outcomes were congenital malformations, SAB, preterm birth <37 weeks, LBW <2500 g, and infant infections. Serious infection required hospitalization. ASQ3 assessed five developmental domains. Delivery serum studies measured maternal/cord/infant biologic concentrations. Logistic models adjusted pregnancy outcomes for maternal age, steroid use, and disease activity; SAB used Cox models in women enrolled before 20 weeks; infection models accounted for preterm birth and other confounders. Complete-case analysis was the primary missing-data approach.

Results: cohort and pregnancy outcomes

Of 1712 enrolled patients, 1490 completed pregnancy follow-up and 1431 had live births. The four exposure groups were clinically imbalanced: Crohn disease was overrepresented in biologic and combination groups, while ulcerative colitis was overrepresented among the unexposed. This makes covariate adjustment essential but does not guarantee elimination of confounding.

Adjusted Table 2 estimates did not show increased preterm birth, LBW, or congenital-malformation odds for biologic, thiopurine, or combination therapy; all corresponding 95% CIs included 1.0. Combination therapy was associated with cesarean section (OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.1-2.5). In the pre-20-week SAB cohort, active disease and prior SAB—not medication class—were independent risk factors.

Results: infections and disease activity

First-year serious and nonserious infection rates were similar across exposure groups. Adjusted ORs for any first-year infection were 0.92 (biologic), 0.90 (thiopurine), and 0.93 (combination), with all CIs including 1.0. Preterm birth was the independent factor associated with infection (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.19-2.51). Most infections were nonserious, dominated by otitis media and upper respiratory infections.

Ulcerative colitis patients had more disease activity than Crohn disease patients during pregnancy. Women unexposed to biologics/immunomodulators were more likely to flare in several time windows. Exposure to more than one biologic was associated with more flares (47% vs 20%), likely reflecting treatment changes driven by active/refractory disease rather than a drug-caused flare.

Results: placental transfer, growth, development

Placental transfer varied markedly by agent. Infliximab had a median infant concentration of 27.1 µg/mL and infant/maternal ratio 2.4; adalimumab 9.4 µg/mL and ratio 1.3; certolizumab pegol 0.0 µg/mL and ratio 0.0. Most agents except certolizumab remained detectable in infants at birth, but birth concentration category was not associated with serious or any infection through 12 months in the measured subset.

Infant height/weight trajectories showed no consistent adverse exposure pattern; thiopurine-exposed infants had higher mean birth-weight percentile. At 12 months and in supplemental 24-48 month analyses, ASQ3 results did not show a coherent adverse developmental signal. Isolated nominal P<.05 domain differences occurred in both favorable and unfavorable directions, which is important when interpreting the authors’ global “no difference” conclusion.

Discussion, limitations, conclusion

The authors interpret the cohort as strong reassurance that continuing biologics and thiopurines during pregnancy does not increase the studied adverse outcomes, and emphasize disease control because active disease was associated with SAB. They argue against withholding biologics solely because of pregnancy, including concerns about late-pregnancy placental transfer.

Key limitations are self-reported outcomes and disease activity, no objective inflammatory biomarkers, possible selection bias from loss to follow-up, late enrollment that misses early SAB, residual confounding by indication/severity, and limited power for uncommon outcomes. The supplement's post-hoc power analysis indicates that clinically relevant risk increases below roughly 1.6-2.3 for several uncommon outcomes could have gone undetected.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first for evidence on IBD medication exposure in pregnancy. Best use: a large prospective comparative safety cohort and a source for placental-transfer estimates. Do not use for: proving absence of small teratogenic/infectious risks, establishing causality, or substituting for current pregnancy/IBD treatment guidance.

Implementation value

The study is highly usable for counseling about continuation and for understanding placental transfer, with clear exposure/outcome definitions.

Evidence basis: Clinically interpretable ORs/HRs, first-year infection data, biologic concentration ratios, long-term ASQ3 follow-up.

Principal limit: Observational evidence should be integrated with current guidelines and individual disease control needs.

Would change: Current guideline alignment and newer agent-specific safety data would raise it.

Strengths

  • Prospective, multicenter design with a large pregnancy cohort for this clinical question.
  • Explicit comparison groups and adjustment for disease activity and steroid use.
  • Maternal, pregnancy, neonatal, infection, growth, PK, and developmental outcomes in one program.
  • Supplement provides useful analytic definitions, sensitivity analyses, attrition details, and detectable-effect power estimates.

Limitations and risk of bias

  • Confounding by indication/severity: exposure groups differed substantially by CD/UC phenotype and likely disease severity.
  • Measurement: maternal self-report and no objective inflammatory markers can misclassify disease activity and some outcomes.
  • Selection/left truncation: enrollment could occur late in pregnancy; early SABs may be missed. Attrition was nonzero.
  • Rare outcomes: wide intervals and post-hoc power limits mean the study mainly excludes large, not small, increases in uncommon outcomes.
  • Multiplicity: numerous subgroup, agent, growth, and developmental comparisons produce isolated nominal P<.05 findings.
  • Agent-specific precision: infliximab/adalimumab dominate exposure; golimumab, natalizumab, ustekinumab, and vedolizumab subsets are small.

Confidence in appraisal: high for the supplied source’s design/results and moderate for practice translation because contemporary guidance and newer therapies are outside this source-only evaluation.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use the source for
Counseling that the cohort did not detect increased major pregnancy/neonatal risk from biologics or thiopurines; explaining that uncontrolled disease itself matters.
Monitor the caveat
“No association” is not equivalent to “no possible risk,” especially for rare malformations/SAB and newer individual agents.
PK implication
Placental transfer differs markedly by agent; certolizumab pegol had minimal infant concentration while infliximab commonly exceeded maternal concentration at delivery.
Development
No coherent adverse ASQ3 signal through 48 months, but long-term subgroup sizes are much smaller than the pregnancy cohort.
Clinical translation: The source supports continuation rather than reflexive discontinuation when disease control requires biologic/thiopurine therapy, but treatment choice/timing should still follow current guidelines, current product-specific pregnancy data, and patient-specific disease activity. This appraisal did not externally verify 2026 guideline status.
