Scope warning. The randomized trial supports a large reduction in the composite 7-day KDIGO AKI endpoint in a selected elective cardiac-surgery population. It does not establish improvement in 30-day kidney events, cardiovascular outcomes, length of stay, disability, quality of life, or long-term kidney function. It also does not establish safety or benefit for routine perioperative use in patients already taking SGLT2 inhibitors.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first, but do not translate directly into routine perioperative prescribing yet. The randomized result is large and internally consistent for the prespecified composite AKI endpoint. The main uncertainty is what the effect means clinically. The benefit is concentrated in urine-output-defined AKI, while creatinine-only AKI was essentially unchanged and no prespecified clinical secondary outcome improved.

What the trial found

AKI occurred in 28% with dapagliflozin and 52% with placebo, relative risk 0.54, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.65, P<.001. The reported absolute risk difference was -23.98 percentage points. Stage 1 and stage 2 AKI were lower, but stage 3 events were rare and not reduced.

What drives the result

Supplement 2 is the key interpretive document. Creatinine-only AKI was 14% versus 15%, relative risk 0.93. Urine-output-only AKI was 21% versus 48%, relative risk 0.44. The composite result therefore cannot be read as evidence of a similarly large creatinine-based kidney-protection effect.

What did not improve

There was no significant difference in de novo atrial fibrillation, MACE30, MAKE30, ICU stay, hospital stay, days at home, 30-day quality of life, or disability. The trial was underpowered for uncommon outcomes such as stage 3 AKI and MAKE30.

Safety and implementation tension

One dapagliflozin participant developed ketoacidosis, with none in the placebo group. Current 2026 American Diabetes Association hospital standards still recommend holding SGLT2 inhibitors for 3 to 4 days before elective surgery. This trial tests the opposite strategy in a screened population, so guideline change would require reconciliation of benefit, DKA risk, and confirmatory evidence.

Methods and protocol

Clinical question

Among adults undergoing elective cardiac surgery, does starting dapagliflozin 10 mg the day before surgery and continuing through postoperative day 2 reduce KDIGO AKI during the first 7 postoperative days compared with placebo?

Design and population

MERCURI-2 was an investigator-initiated randomized superiority trial at seven Dutch hospitals. Enrollment ran from June 8, 2023, through January 27, 2025, with final follow-up on May 16, 2025. Adults scheduled for elective cardiac surgery were eligible. The trial excluded current SGLT2 inhibitor users, type 1 diabetes, selected insulin-treated patients with type 2 diabetes and BMI below 25, eGFR below 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, previous diabetic ketoacidosis, systolic blood pressure below 100 mm Hg, SGLT2 allergy, and pregnancy-related risk states.

Randomization was 1:1 using computer-generated blocks of 4, 6, and 8 with stratification by sex, type 2 diabetes, and site. Only trial pharmacists saw allocation. The dapagliflozin arm received 10 mg orally the evening before surgery, the morning of surgery, postoperative day 1, and postoperative day 2. The placebo schedule matched this exactly.

Primary and secondary outcomes

The primary outcome used the full KDIGO AKI definition. Baseline creatinine was the most recent value before study-drug initiation, usually the admission measurement on the day before surgery. Serum creatinine was measured daily for up to 7 days while hospitalized. Urine output was recorded hourly while a urinary catheter remained in place.

Prespecified secondary outcomes included stage 3 AKI, maximum postoperative eGFR change, de novo atrial fibrillation, MACE30, MAKE30, ICU and hospital length of stay, days at home through day 30, EQ-5D-3L quality of life, WHO-DAS disability, and selected safety events. Several prespecified secondary endpoints had not yet been analyzed at publication, including healthcare and productivity costs, perioperative glucose, hemodynamics, cardiac function, and urinary oxygenation.

Internal source inconsistency. The Methods prose transposes the MACE30 and MAKE30 definitions. Table 2 footnotes define MACE30 as cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, or heart-failure readmission, and MAKE30 as kidney-related death, renal replacement therapy, or persistent serum creatinine at least 200% of baseline. The Limitations section also uses MAKE30 for the kidney composite. This appraisal and the normalized atoms follow Table 2.

Statistical plan

The sample-size calculation assumed 22% AKI with placebo and relative risk 0.64 with SGLT2 inhibition. A total of 784 participants provided 80% power at two-sided alpha .05. The trial reported risk differences and relative risks with 95% confidence intervals for dichotomous outcomes and used distribution-appropriate methods for continuous outcomes. The primary outcome was adjusted for sex in a logistic model, and a per-protocol analysis tested robustness. No imputation for missing data was used. Secondary outcomes were not adjusted for multiplicity.

Supplement 1 protocol contribution

The attached protocol is Version 11.0 dated September 20, 2024. It confirms the trial identifier, phase IV design, sponsor, randomization and blinding framework, intervention schedule, safety surveillance, and detailed study procedures. It also documents specific risk-mitigation logic around diabetes and ketoacidosis. The protocol is useful mainly for design fidelity and safety context. The published article remains the primary result source.

Results

Participants and adherence

Of 2551 patients screened, 800 were eligible and consented, and 784 were randomized. Six randomized participants did not undergo surgery. Median age was 68 years. The cohort was 76% male and 97% White, with median BMI 27 and median eGFR 80 mL/min/1.73 m2. Twelve percent had type 2 diabetes and 6% had heart failure. In Supplement 2, 344 dapagliflozin participants and 339 placebo participants took all four doses.

The randomized groups were broadly balanced. Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance
9.5 / 10

Rationale. The question is directly relevant to perioperative pharmacotherapy and AKI prevention.

Evidence basis. Large multicenter randomized clinical trial with a simple drug intervention and common clinical endpoint.

Limiting factor. It applies to a narrow elective cardiac-surgery population.

Would raise. Confirmatory trials in broader surgical and kidney-risk populations.

Novelty
9.0 / 10

Rationale. Starting an SGLT2 inhibitor immediately before surgery runs against prevailing perioperative practice and produced a large randomized effect.

Evidence basis. Four-dose perioperative regimen tested against placebo across seven sites.

Limiting factor. Prior pilot and smaller SGLT2 cardiac-surgery studies already suggested benefit.

Would raise. Mechanistic confirmation that links the early urine-output signal to durable kidney protection.

Method strength
8.5 / 10

Rationale. Randomization, double blinding, matching placebo, multicenter enrollment, high adherence, and prespecified KDIGO measurement provide strong internal validity.

Evidence basis. 784 randomized participants, 392 per arm, balanced baseline data, primary and per-protocol consistency.

Limiting factor. No multiplicity adjustment for secondary outcomes, prespecified primary analysis omitted some stratification factors, and the Methods section contains a MACE/MAKE definition error.

Would raise. Fully stratification-adjusted prespecified analysis and cleaner outcome-definition reporting.

Evidence strength
7.5 / 10

Rationale. The primary composite result is statistically strong, but its clinical meaning is weaker than the headline effect suggests.

Evidence basis. RR 0.54 with narrow CI and consistent per-protocol result; stage 2 AKI also fell.

Limiting factor. Creatinine-only AKI was 14% versus 15%, stage 3 events were rare, and no 30-day clinical secondary outcome improved.

Would raise. Replication with creatinine-based or biomarker-supported kidney injury and powered patient-centered outcomes.

External validity
5.5 / 10

Rationale. Seven sites help, but the enrolled population is much narrower than the population in which perioperative SGLT2 decisions usually arise.

Evidence basis. 97% White, 76% male, elective surgery only, current SGLT2 users excluded, few patients with diabetes, heart failure, or advanced CKD.

Limiting factor. The trial does not answer whether to continue, stop, or restart SGLT2 therapy in existing users.

Would raise. Diverse multinational trials including current SGLT2 users, urgent surgery, advanced CKD, and higher DKA-risk groups.

Implementation value
6.5 / 10

Rationale. The regimen is operationally simple, but implementation would collide with current perioperative SGLT2 safety guidance.

Evidence basis. Four oral doses, high adherence, one ketoacidosis event, and a large composite AKI effect.

Limiting factor. Current ADA 2026 guidance advises holding SGLT2 inhibitors 3 to 4 days before elective surgery.

Would raise. Guideline reassessment, independent replication, formal DKA-risk protocol, and evidence of benefit on creatinine-based or patient-centered outcomes.

Main strengths

  • Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with seven participating hospitals.
  • Full KDIGO surveillance, including hourly urine output while catheterized.
  • Large effect estimate with narrow confidence interval for the primary outcome.
  • High adherence and a per-protocol result that closely matches the primary analysis.
  • Supplement 2 provides the component analysis needed to understand the composite endpoint.

Main limitations

  • The primary result is driven mainly by urine-output-defined AKI rather than serum-creatinine-defined AKI.
  • No significant benefit appeared in MACE30, MAKE30, length of stay, days at home, quality of life, or disability.
  • Stage 3 AKI and MAKE30 were too uncommon for adequate power.
  • The sample was 97% White and 76% male and excluded current SGLT2 inhibitor users.
  • Secondary endpoints were not multiplicity-adjusted.
  • The published Methods section transposes the MACE30 and MAKE30 definitions.

Practice translation

Do not use this trial alone to start dapagliflozin the day before elective surgery as routine care. The evidence is strong enough to challenge current assumptions, but the trial does not yet settle the safety and outcome tradeoff that existing perioperative guidance was designed to manage.

Source-derived implications

  • The four-dose dapagliflozin regimen markedly reduced the composite 7-day KDIGO AKI endpoint.
  • There was no convincing creatinine-only AKI benefit and no improvement in the measured 30-day clinical outcomes.
  • One ketoacidosis event occurred in the dapagliflozin group. The trial excluded several groups at higher perceived ketoacidosis risk.

External verification

The American Diabetes Association 2026 Standards of Care in the Hospital state that SGLT2 inhibitors should be held for 3 to 4 days before elective surgery and that patients undergoing nonelective surgery should be monitored closely for DKA. This is directly opposite the MERCURI-2 dosing strategy. The accompanying JAMA editorial treats the trial as highly persuasive but recommends limiting the conclusion to stable elective cardiac-surgery patients until confirmatory studies are available.

Best current use. Treat MERCURI-2 as a practice-challenging efficacy signal and a priority trial for protocol committees to review. Before changing local policy, require replication or guideline-level reassessment, and define explicit patient-selection, fasting, ketone-monitoring, and DKA-response procedures.

What not to overclaim

The study does not show that perioperative dapagliflozin prevents clinically important kidney failure, shortens hospitalization, improves 30-day recovery, or is safe for people already taking SGLT2 inhibitors. It also does not show that the same effect applies to urgent surgery, other SGLT2 inhibitors, or noncardiac surgery.
