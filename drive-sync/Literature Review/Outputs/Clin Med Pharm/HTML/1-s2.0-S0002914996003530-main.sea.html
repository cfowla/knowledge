One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In rt-PA-treated acute myocardial infarction, serial markers of thrombin activity/generation and fibrinogenolysis tracked with angiographic and clinical outcomes. Early FPA and TAT elevations were associated with death and suboptimal 90-minute coronary flow; F1.2 was associated with TIMI 0/1 flow; later Bbeta1-42, TAT, and F1.2 elevations were associated with recurrent ischemia. The authors proposed that hemostatic-marker profiles might eventually help identify higher-risk patients.

The treatment context is directly relevant to a heparin deep dive: 84 patients received unfractionated heparin as a 5,000 U IV bolus followed by 1,000 IU/h, with aPTT maintained at 65-90 seconds. The comparator was recombinant desulfatohirudin across four dose regimens. All patients also received front-loaded alteplase, aspirin, and metoprolol. Serial biomarker samples were collected before therapy and at 1, 12, and 24 hours; angiography was performed at 90 minutes and 18-36 hours.

The most useful signal is mechanistic/prognostic rather than therapeutic. Figure 1 showed broadly similar marker trajectories by treatment despite a striking 1-hour TAT divergence. Figures 2-5 linked selected marker levels to mortality, inadequate TIMI flow, bleeding, and recurrent ischemia. Table II is more sobering than the unadjusted associations: after adjustment for treatment, age, and gender, 1-hour log FPA remained associated with hemorrhage (OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.09-2.41), whereas adjusted 1-hour log F1.2 and log TAT estimates crossed 1. Bbeta1-42 at 12 hours remained associated with procedural bleeding (OR 1.48 per 60 micrograms/L, 95% CI 1.01-2.16). The study is explicitly exploratory, has extensive missing marker data, pools heterogeneous treatment groups, and does not correct p values for multiple testing.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames thrombin activation as a possible contributor to failed initial reperfusion and early reocclusion after thrombolysis. The investigators measured FPA, TAT, F1.2, and Bbeta1-42 in TIMI-5 to probe coagulation/fibrinogenolysis and to determine whether the markers correlated with clinical outcomes.

It reports that 1-hour FPA and TAT were associated with mortality and TIMI 0/1/2 flow, 1-hour F1.2 with TIMI 0/1 flow, and all three procoagulant markers with hemorrhagic events; late F1.2, TAT, and Bbeta1-42 were associated with recurrent ischemia. The abstract concludes that selected markers may be useful predictors, but this claim is hypothesis-generating rather than validated clinical prediction.

Introduction / background

The authors emphasize paradoxical thrombin activation during thrombolytic therapy and the possibility that persistent thrombin contributes to incomplete reperfusion or reocclusion. Heparin had been used as adjunctive therapy with rt-PA, while direct thrombin inhibition with hirudin was under investigation.

The ancillary study's purpose was mechanistic and prognostic: characterize in-vivo thrombin/fibrinogenolytic activity during rt-PA therapy and assess whether hemostatic markers could predict clinically relevant events. This is distinct from the parent TIMI-5 question of antithrombotic treatment comparison.

Methods

TIMI-5 randomized 252 patients. Heparin was given as 5,000 U IV then 1,000 IU/h, adjusted to aPTT 65-90 seconds. Hirudin was open-label in four regimens: 0.15 mg/kg bolus + 0.05 mg/kg/h (n=50), 0.1 + 0.1 (n=33), 0.3 + 0.1 (n=29), or 0.6 + 0.2 mg/kg/h (n=50). All patients received front-loaded alteplase (15 mg bolus, 0.75 mg/kg over 30 min to max 50 mg, then 0.50 mg/kg over 60 min to max 35 mg), aspirin 160 mg initially and daily, and metoprolol. Angiography occurred at 90 minutes and 18-36 hours; PTCA was discretionary for TIMI 0/1 flow at 120 minutes, and hirudin patients receiving PTCA were switched to heparin.

Blood was sampled before antithrombotic therapy and at 1, 12, and 24 hours. Samples were handled with EDTA, aprotinin, and PPACK, processed rapidly, frozen at -70 C, and assays were performed blinded. Highly skewed marker distributions were log10 transformed and also analyzed by tertiles. Methods included repeated-measures ANOVA, t tests, chi-square tests, correlation coefficients, and stepwise logistic regression. The authors explicitly state that p values were not adjusted for multiple comparisons and should be viewed as exploratory.

Results

Baseline FPA, TAT, and F1.2 means were above the listed normal ranges. FPA generally declined; TAT showed a marked 1-hour treatment-group divergence but no significant repeated-measures difference; F1.2 rose initially then declined; Bbeta1-42 rose sharply at 1 hour then returned toward baseline by 12 hours. More heparin than hirudin patients underwent PTCA overall (50% vs 32%; p=0.006), but the difference was not present before 24 hours and rescue PTCA within 24 hours was similar.

Higher early FPA/TAT values were associated with mortality and worse 90-minute TIMI flow; F1.2 and later FPA/Bbeta1-42 also related to selected reperfusion outcomes. Recurrent ischemia was enriched in higher late Bbeta1-42, TAT, and F1.2 tertiles. Hemorrhagic events were associated with higher early procoagulant markers, but 85.4% occurred at instrumented sites. In adjusted logistic models, 1-hour log FPA remained significantly associated with hemorrhage; adjusted log F1.2 and log TAT estimates did not reach conventional statistical significance. Bbeta1-42 at 12 hours remained associated with procedural bleeding. An adjusted inverse association between 12-hour log FPA and late TIMI 0/1 flow was also reported and acknowledged by the authors as difficult to reconcile with earlier findings.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret early procoagulant marker elevations as potentially reflecting thrombus burden and late elevations as possible indicators of ongoing thrombogenic activity/residual instability. They suggest that, if validated, a high-risk 1-hour marker profile might justify earlier catheterization/revascularization and that late markers might predict recurrent ischemia.

The article itself places substantial limits on that interpretation: approximately half of patients had valid measurements for each marker at all four time points; treatment groups were heterogeneous and frequently pooled; many comparisons were performed without multiplicity adjustment; and larger confirmatory studies were explicitly requested. The small number of deaths shown in Figure 2 (n=5 for each marker analysis) further limits stability of mortality associations.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best for historical mechanistic/prognostic context on thrombin generation during rt-PA plus adjunctive anticoagulation. Do not use this paper alone to select a modern anticoagulant, set UFH dosing, order these biomarkers routinely, or trigger catheterization based on marker values.

Validity and bias

  • Randomization boundary: treatment was randomized; biomarker-outcome relationships were not.
  • Missing data: only about half of patients had valid measurements for each marker at all four time points.
  • Multiplicity: no adjustment for multiple testing; authors explicitly call results exploratory.
  • Event scarcity: Figure 2 includes only five deaths in each marker comparison.
  • Procedural confounding: 85.4% of hemorrhagic events were at instrumented sites and temporally related to catheterization.
  • Treatment heterogeneity: outcome analyses often pooled UFH and four hirudin dose groups.
  • Model instability: log F1.2 and log TAT hemorrhage associations lost conventional significance after adjustment.
  • Direction inconsistency: the inverse 12-hour FPA association with late TIMI 0/1 flow was explicitly described as difficult to reconcile.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Preserve for heparin historyThe exact UFH regimen (5,000 U IV bolus, 1,000 IU/h, aPTT 65-90 s) is a useful historical comparator when tracing evolution of adjunctive anticoagulation during thrombolysis.
Mechanistic lessonThrombin-related activity persisted or rebounded despite thrombolysis, supporting the biological rationale for adjunctive antithrombotic therapy and for studying direct thrombin inhibition.
Do not overclaim hirudin superiorityAlthough TAT diverged at 1 hour and the highest hirudin dose showed a larger F1.2 decline in selected comparisons, the article reports no consistent significant between-treatment difference across markers and was not designed here as a definitive clinical-efficacy comparison.
Do not operationalize biomarkers from this paperNo validated cut point, calibration metric, discrimination analysis, or marker-guided intervention is provided. Associations are exploratory and should not be converted into CDS thresholds.
Bleeding signal needs contextEarly procoagulant markers correlated with bleeding, but most bleeding was procedural/instrumented. This is not evidence that higher thrombin activity directly causes spontaneous bleeding.
Contemporary verification requiredThe source is from 1996 and evaluates an rt-PA-era AMI pathway. Any present-day clinical translation should be verified against current primary evidence and guidance rather than inferred from this paper alone.
