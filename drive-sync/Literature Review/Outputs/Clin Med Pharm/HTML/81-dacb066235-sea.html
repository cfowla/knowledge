One-page synthesis

The supplement documents a large, dose-specific network meta-analysis of randomized trials for knee or hip osteoarthritis. The protocol deliberately excludes small trials by requiring an average of at least 100 participants per arm. It prespecifies pain as the primary outcome, physical function as a secondary efficacy outcome, and several safety outcomes. The statistical model is a Bayesian random-effects network model with a longitudinal random walk, so adjacent follow-up times borrow more strength than distant time points.

The primary treatment table gives a useful efficacy-safety map. Diclofenac 150 mg had a pain effect size of -0.56 and etoricoxib 60 mg -0.65, each with 99.9% posterior probability of exceeding the prespecified MID. Topical diclofenac 70-81 mg and 140-160 mg had pain effect sizes of -0.54 and -0.61, respectively. Opioid estimates were generally smaller and paired with larger adverse-event odds ratios. Oxymorphone 80 mg is the clearest extreme in the appendix, with an odds ratio of 19.34 for dropout due to adverse events and 7.42 for any adverse event.

Diagnostics support the efficacy model more strongly than the safety models. Pain and function each had 99% of standardized node residuals within 1.96 standard normal deviations, and the Q-Q plots were judged adequate. For safety, mean residual deviance modestly exceeded the number of data points. The consistency model also had a lower DIC than the inconsistency model for all five outcomes. One data-integrity issue remains unresolved: diflunisal 750 mg has 139 participants in appendix 7 but zero randomized participants in appendix 8.

Verdict: Read first. Best used as the dose-level methods and results companion to the parent article. It is especially valuable when auditing treatment estimates, dose-response assumptions, safety tradeoffs, or the network model.

Methods and design

The protocol uses large randomized trials, limits language to English, accepts mixed-joint or mixed-arthritis trials only when at least 75% of participants have knee or hip OA with retrievable cohort data, and requires a pain or algofunctional follow-up outcome. Two reviewers independently screen and extract data. The protocol specifies precedence rules for multiple trial reports and prefers intention-to-treat analyses.

The efficacy model uses standardized mean differences and a Bayesian random-effects network framework with a longitudinal random walk. Binary safety outcomes use odds ratios. The MID is 0.37 SD, stated as equivalent to 9 mm on a 100 mm VAS. Sensitivity work includes risk-of-bias meta-regression, dose-response models, alternative heterogeneity priors, and time-specific analyses.

Method note. The protocol page describing binary outcomes contains the phrase "odds ratio (RR)." The analysis tables and completed study use odds ratios. The source wording is internally inconsistent and was not silently corrected in the validation report.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9.5/10

Directly useful for dose-specific OA pharmacotherapy evidence and safety tradeoffs.

Basis. It compares 90 active regimens and preserves preparation and dose rather than collapsing drug classes.

Limit. The source is from 2021 and does not establish current regulatory or guideline status.

Would change with. A current-practice verification layer and newer comparative trials.

Novelty 8.5/10

The dose-specific joint efficacy and safety network adds more prescribing detail than class-level reviews.

Basis. The model integrates oral and topical NSAIDs, opioids, and paracetamol in one network.

Limit. Some methods extend the authors’ prior 2017 NSAID network meta-analysis.

Would change with. Independent replication with newer trials and patient-level modifiers.

Method strength 8.5/10

Prespecified eligibility, duplicate review, large-trial restriction, Bayesian random-effects modeling, time correlation, bias adjustment, and inconsistency checks form a strong review design.

Basis. Protocol pages 2-7 and appendices 4-6 specify the workflow and model; appendices 18-20 report diagnostics.

Limit. English-only inclusion and the ≥100-per-arm threshold trade breadth for lower small-study bias; several network nodes remain sparse.

Would change with. Broader language coverage, transparent code/data release, and external replication.

Evidence strength 8.0/10

Many dose estimates are precise and supported by a large network, but indirect evidence, sparse safety events, and trial-level bias remain important.

Basis. Web-appendix 7 reports 95% credible intervals for efficacy and safety; model fit is strong for pain/function.

Limit. Serious adverse-event intervals are often wide, and safety-model residual deviance exceeds the number of data points.

Would change with. More head-to-head large trials and better-powered patient-centered safety outcomes.

External validity 7.0/10

The large randomized-trial base supports broad knee/hip OA inference, but trial populations and route-specific evidence constrain application.

Basis. The protocol permits knee/hip OA and large randomized studies across many preparations.

Limit. Topical diclofenac evidence in the parent report is knee-specific, and trial eligibility can exclude smaller or less typical populations.

Would change with. Pragmatic trials across comorbidity strata, hip OA, and longer follow-up.

Implementation value 8.5/10

The supplement exposes dose-level effect and harm estimates that can inform evidence tables and CDS content.

Basis. Web-appendix 7 gives regimen-specific pain, function, and adverse-event estimates against a common comparator.

Limit. This is comparative evidence, not a patient-specific prescribing rule; current contraindications, labeling, and guidance require separate verification.

Would change with. A linked current guideline/regulatory layer and patient-specific risk model.

Confidence in appraisal: high for source interpretation, moderate for practice translation. The supplement is detailed enough to audit methods and estimates. Practice translation remains time-sensitive and was not externally updated because @VERIFY was not activated.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use

  • Populate dose-specific evidence tables.
  • Compare pain/function effects with dropout and adverse-event estimates.
  • Audit Bayesian network assumptions, priors, time correlation, and consistency.
  • Trace parent-article claims back to supporting supplementary estimates.

Do not use alone

  • Do not treat the network estimate as a patient-specific recommendation.
  • Do not infer current approval or guideline status from a 2021 paper.
  • Do not convert cited trial results into primary-study atoms without reading those trials.
  • Do not silently resolve the diflunisal participant-count conflict.
