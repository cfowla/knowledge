One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as a structured comparison of dual SGLT1/2 inhibition versus SGLT2 inhibition and as a warning about how sparse network evidence can generate apparently favorable class estimates. Do not use it as direct evidence that dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are interchangeable.

The review combined 111 RCT articles (103,922 patients) using frequentist network meta-analysis, with separate analyses for T1DM and T2DM. In T2DM, dual SGLT1/2 inhibitors were associated with lower estimated myocardial infarction (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.56–0.98) and stroke risk (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.47–0.92) versus SGLT2 inhibitors. However, the same class was associated with more diarrhea (RR 1.42, 95% CI 1.07–1.88) and severe hypoglycemia (RR 5.89, 95% CI 1.41–24.57). Most other cardiovascular, renal, metabolic, and safety comparisons were not statistically significant.

The headline cardiovascular results require caution. The authors state that MI and stroke comparisons were informed by only one to two SGLT1/2 inhibitor trials, notably SCORED and SOLOIST-WHF, whose populations had CKD or recent worsening heart failure. The network was highly unbalanced (100 SGLT2 versus 10 SGLT1/2 inhibitor articles), renal composite definitions varied, and the analysis used aggregate rather than individual-patient data. The supplied folder also lacks the article’s supplementary material, preventing direct review of search details, RoB plots, baseline/intervention tables, and T1DM supplemental figures.

Internal source discrepancy: the abstract reports the T2DM HbA1c class difference as 0.16% (95% CI 0.06–0.26), while the main Results and Table 2 report 0.21% (95% CI 0.07–0.35). The ATOM output preserves both values with distinct anchors.

Methods and evidence architecture

Four databases—PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and Scopus—were searched from inception to 15 January 2022. Eligible studies were RCTs of at least 12 weeks in patients with diabetes receiving an SGLT2 inhibitor or combined SGLT1/2 inhibitor and reporting cardiovascular, metabolic, renal, or safety outcomes. Four reviewers independently performed search/data extraction; disagreements were resolved by consensus. Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2 and pooled evidence with a modified network-meta-analysis GRADE approach.

The authors used Stata 16.0 for frequentist aggregate-data network meta-analysis. They assessed consistency with a global Wald test, used consistency or inconsistency models according to the prespecified p-value threshold, reported HR/RR/MD effect measures, inspected comparison-adjusted funnel plots for publication bias, and generated SUCRA rankings. Missing dispersion data were reconstructed using Cochrane methods, Wan’s method, or pooled-variance imputation when specified.

Interpretation constraint: no head-to-head randomized trial directly compared the dual SGLT1/2 class with the SGLT2 class. The central class-to-class estimates therefore depend on the network’s transitivity and comparability assumptions.

Other T2DM cardiovascular comparisons—mortality, cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, cardiovascular death/HF hospitalization composite, and MACE—were not statistically significant. The renal composite and eGFR comparison were also not significant; serum creatinine was higher with SGLT1/2 inhibitors by 6.40 µmol/L (95% CI 0.25–12.54). Among metabolic outcomes, the main text reports a 0.21 percentage-point advantage in HbA1c reduction for SGLT2 inhibitors; other measured metabolic comparisons were not significant.

For T1DM, cardiovascular and renal outcome analyses were frequently impossible because of insufficient studies. Available T1DM metabolic and safety comparisons did not show a clear class advantage. Osmotic diuresis could not be analyzed in either diabetes type from the available network.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

Directly addresses comparative effects of SGLT2 versus dual SGLT1/2 inhibition across cardiorenal, metabolic, and safety outcomes.

Evidence basis
111 RCT articles; clinically relevant outcomes.
Principal limit
It does not directly compare dapagliflozin with empagliflozin.
What would change it
Direct head-to-head or robust class-interchangeability evidence would raise the score.

Novelty 7/10

The authors present this as the first network meta-analysis directly comparing the two inhibitor classes.

Evidence basis
Broad multi-outcome class comparison and GRADE framework.
Principal limit
Novelty is mainly comparative synthesis rather than new primary data.
What would change it
Later replication with a larger dual-inhibitor evidence base would strengthen enduring novelty.

Method strength 6/10

Systematic search, PRISMA, RoB 2, GRADE, and explicit frequentist NMA methods are strengths.

Evidence basis
Four databases, pre-registration, independent extraction, consistency testing.
Principal limit
No head-to-head RCTs; aggregate data; very imbalanced network; supplement absent from the supplied packet.
What would change it
More direct trials, IPD, full supplement review, and stronger network balance would raise the score.

Evidence strength 5/10

Several estimates are precise enough to be informative, but the key positive signals are supported by sparse dual-inhibitor trials and often low/moderate GRADE.

Evidence basis
MI HR 0.74; stroke HR 0.65; diarrhea RR 1.42; severe hypoglycemia RR 5.89.
Principal limit
MI/stroke and severe-hypoglycemia signals depend on one or few SGLT1/2 trials; HbA1c estimate conflicts within the article.
What would change it
Replication across multiple independent dual-inhibitor trials would raise the score.

External validity 4/10

Generalizability of the headline cardiovascular advantages is limited by the specialized populations contributing the dual-inhibitor evidence.

Evidence basis
SCORED and SOLOIST-WHF dominate key SGLT1/2 cardiovascular comparisons.
Principal limit
Authors explicitly state uncertainty for the average T2DM patient.
What would change it
Broader T2DM trials and direct class comparisons would raise the score.

Implementation value 5/10

Useful for framing class tradeoffs and identifying uncertainty, but insufficient alone for formulary interchange decisions within the SGLT2 class.

Evidence basis
Quantifies efficacy and safety tradeoffs with GRADE.
Principal limit
It compares SGLT1/2 versus SGLT2 classes, not dapagliflozin versus empagliflozin.
What would change it
Direct within-class comparative effectiveness and current guideline/label synthesis would raise the score.
Main strengths: broad RCT evidence base, preregistration, multi-database search, independent extraction, explicit network-consistency testing, RoB 2, GRADE, and effect estimates with uncertainty.
Main limitations: no direct class-to-class RCT, sparse and specialized SGLT1/2 evidence for headline outcomes, severe network imbalance, heterogeneous renal composite definitions, aggregate-data analysis, absent supplement in the supplied packet, and the internal HbA1c discrepancy.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Useful for
Understanding how dual SGLT1/2 inhibition may differ from pure SGLT2 inhibition across multiple outcome domains; identifying uncertainty and safety tradeoffs.
Not sufficient for
Choosing dapagliflozin over empagliflozin, claiming within-SGLT2 class equivalence, or making a current formulary decision without newer/direct comparative evidence.
Read next
Direct dapagliflozin-versus-empagliflozin comparative studies, major individual SGLT2 cardiovascular/renal trials, and current guideline/label evidence relevant to the intended formulary population.
