One-page synthesis

Core thesis. NDM-1 had emerged as a transferable carbapenemase in Enterobacteriaceae, was already linked to international healthcare exposure, commonly traveled with additional resistance determinants, and left very few active drugs in 2010.

The review is valuable as a snapshot of the first phase of NDM-1 recognition. It explains why carbapenem resistance mattered, summarizes early reports from South Asia, the United Kingdom, North America, and other countries, and connects resistance detection to infection-control action. Its central warning aged well. NDM would not remain a geographically isolated import problem.

The treatment section did not age well enough for bedside use. Colistin and tigecycline were presented as the main agents with in vitro activity, with a hypothetical six-week colistin plus tigecycline regimen proposed for the case. Current guidance now prefers newer strategies for invasive NDM-producing Enterobacterales. The review should be read for history, mechanism, and early epidemiology, not as current therapeutic guidance.

Section condensation

The case

The article opens with a hypothetical 48-year-old man who received orthopedic care in Calcutta and later returned to Canada with a prosthetic-hardware complication. Cultures grew multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae, phenotypic carbapenemase testing was positive, and NDM-1 was confirmed by PCR and sequencing. The scenario is illustrative, not a reported patient.

Methods

The authors searched PubMed for "NDM-1" through October 2010. They identified 23 research articles and used 4. They excluded 19 because they judged them to lack new information or because they were not available in English. At least two authors reviewed each included article.

The review also searched Google News for "NDM-1" and "medical tourism" and used personal communication with experts. This approach made sense for a rapidly emerging threat, but it is not a reproducible systematic review and carries a high risk of selective evidence inclusion.

Emergence of carbapenem resistance and what NDM-1 is

The review places NDM-1 within the Ambler class B metallo-carbapenemases and stresses the clinical problem created by mobile resistance elements. It describes NDM-1 as conferring resistance to all β-lactams available in Canada at the time and notes frequent co-resistance to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

Early epidemiology centered on healthcare exposure in South Asia. By the end of 2009 the review reports at least 29 UK patients with NDM-1-positive E. coli or Klebsiella, 17 with travel to the Indian subcontinent and 14 with medical care there. A Mumbai center reported NDM-1 in 22 of 24 carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae isolates collected from August through November 2009. The authors also state that prevalence data from the Indian subcontinent were not available.

Spread and detection

The review identifies international travel and foreign hospitalization as risk factors and connects NDM-1 to the broader rise of medical tourism. It describes imported cases in several countries and uses Figure 2 to show that spread was already international by 2010.

For laboratory detection, the review says automated susceptibility platforms usually detected metallo-β-lactamase-mediated carbapenem resistance, but definitive mechanism identification required molecular confirmation. This distinction between phenotypic resistance and carbapenemase mechanism remains conceptually important.

Infection control

The article applies Canadian, US, and European carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae guidance to NDM-1. It advises asking about medical care in India or Pakistan when carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae are identified. It also notes that screening recommendations differed across jurisdictions.

Box 1 summarizes a Canadian response that includes clinical screening of roommates, retrospective laboratory review, escalation to infection-control and public-health experts when linked cases appear, and targeted active surveillance. These are historical recommendations from 2010 and should not substitute for current local policy.

Treatment and case revisit

In vitro activity was concentrated in colistin and tigecycline. The review reports 89% colistin and 65% tigecycline susceptibility among 37 British NDM-1 isolates. Clinical evidence was thin. Colistin reports were mostly retrospective case reports and small case series. Tigecycline studies were also small, and the review notes the FDA mortality warning.

The hypothetical case ends with a proposed six-week course of colistin plus tigecycline with prosthetic-hardware removal and renal monitoring. That recommendation reflects the treatment options of 2010. It should not be carried forward into current practice without replacement by contemporary guidance and isolate-specific susceptibility results.

Box 1. Canadian prevention and control measures

  • Clinically screen roommates with relevant specimens when a carbapenem-resistant gram-negative organism is detected more than 48 hours after admission.
  • Review the index patient's prior 6 to 12 months of laboratory records for evidence of earlier circulation.
  • If two or more patients carry the same strain, involve infection prevention, clinical microbiology, and public health and consider active surveillance of contacts.
  • Do not routinely culture healthcare workers, family members, or visitors. Reserve environmental sampling for major outbreaks.

2026 practice currency check

The source treatment regimen is obsolete as stand-alone guidance. The 2026 IDSA AMR guidance recommends aztreonam-avibactam and cefiderocol as preferred options for invasive NDM-producing Enterobacterales. If aztreonam-avibactam is unavailable, ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative. Tigecycline and eravacycline are alternatives only for selected infections outside the bloodstream and urinary tract. Source: IDSA 2026 AMR Guidance.

The early association with travel and medical care in South Asia also needs updating. CDC surveillance published in 2026 found blaNDM in 5.4% of sampled carbapenemase-producing CRE isolates in 2016 and 39.8% in 2023 across the US surveillance network. NDM is now a domestic epidemiologic problem as well as a travel-associated mechanism. Source: CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2026.

This currency check is external to the 2011 source. It does not alter the historical claims extracted from the article.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Read it for the early NDM-1 story, mechanism, and the logic connecting resistance detection to infection control. Do not use the 2011 treatment section for current therapeutic decisions.

Relevance 8/10

Highly relevant to the early NDM-1 emergence story and to the history of CRE recognition.

Evidence basis. The review organizes mechanism, epidemiology, detection, infection control, and treatment in one short source.

Principal limit. The clinical details are from 2010 and treatment has changed substantially.

What changes the score. Use current surveillance and treatment guidance alongside it.

Novelty 8/10

At publication, NDM-1 was newly recognized and the review assembled early international reports for a general clinical audience.

Evidence basis. The article appeared during the first wave of reports and included a contemporaneous global distribution map.

Principal limit. Much of the evidence was already published in the cited reports, especially Kumarasamy et al.

What changes the score. Novelty would be higher with original surveillance or molecular data.

Method strength 3/10

The search was explicit but not systematic and the inclusion process was highly selective.

Evidence basis. PubMed through October 2010, 23 research articles identified, 4 used, dual author review.

Principal limit. Nineteen articles were excluded for broad reasons, non-English work was excluded, and credibility was judged by the authors. News and personal communications were also used.

What changes the score. A reproducible search, explicit eligibility criteria, risk-of-bias assessment, and complete evidence table would raise the score.

Evidence strength 4/10

The core biological and epidemiologic signal was real, but much of the clinical evidence came from case reports, case series, early surveillance, and in vitro susceptibility data.

Evidence basis. The review preserved several quantitative early signals, including 22 of 24 Mumbai CRE isolates producing NDM-1 and high colistin susceptibility in early isolates.

Principal limit. Treatment claims relied on small retrospective reports and related carbapenemase evidence rather than NDM-specific comparative trials.

What changes the score. Prospective comparative treatment studies and larger representative surveillance would raise the score.

External validity 3/10

The source captured the first phase of NDM-1 spread but its geographic and treatment assumptions no longer generalize to current practice.

Evidence basis. Early cases clustered around South Asia and healthcare exposure abroad.

Principal limit. Current US surveillance shows substantial domestic expansion of blaNDM among carbapenemase-producing CRE.

What changes the score. Modern multicenter surveillance and current organism-specific treatment data are required for present-day use.

Implementation value 4/10

The detection and infection-control framing remains historically useful, but the antibiotic regimen in the case revisit should not guide current therapy.

Evidence basis. The article stresses mechanism identification, public-health involvement, and infection-control response.

Principal limit. The proposed colistin plus tigecycline regimen predates newer active agents and current guidance.

What changes the score. Use current IDSA guidance and contemporary susceptibility testing to make treatment decisions.

Main strengths

  • Short, clinically readable synthesis published during the first phase of NDM-1 recognition.
  • Preserves useful early quantitative signals and a clear susceptibility table.
  • Connects microbiology, travel exposure, laboratory recognition, infection control, and treatment constraints.

Main limitations

  • The review selected only 4 of 23 identified research articles and used broad, subjective inclusion criteria.
  • No formal risk-of-bias assessment, evidence grading, or reproducible study-selection flow is provided.
  • Many clinical claims rely on case reports, case series, in vitro data, and evidence from related carbapenemases rather than NDM-specific comparative treatment studies.
  • Published in 2011, before major changes in NDM epidemiology, diagnostics, and treatment options.

Confidence in appraisal: high. The entire six-page source, Table 1, Figures 1 and 2, Box 1, and the bibliography were inspected. Current treatment and epidemiology were checked against official 2026 sources.

Practice takeaways

Keep.

Mechanism identification matters. A CRE phenotype does not tell you which carbapenemase is present, and treatment can depend on that mechanism.

Update.

Travel remains useful history, but current NDM risk assessment cannot depend on South Asia exposure alone because domestic transmission has expanded.

Replace.

Replace the 2011 colistin plus tigecycline treatment logic with current IDSA guidance and isolate-specific susceptibility testing.

Read next.

The cited Kumarasamy 2010 study is the main early molecular and epidemiologic source behind Table 1 and both major figures and deserves separate primary-source extraction.
