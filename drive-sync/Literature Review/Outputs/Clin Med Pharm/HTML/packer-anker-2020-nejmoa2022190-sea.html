One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. EMPEROR-Reduced is a high-value confirmatory HFrEF trial. It establishes that empagliflozin added to recommended background therapy reduces the composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization, reduces recurrent heart-failure hospitalization, and slows on-treatment eGFR decline in a high-risk chronic HFrEF population with or without diabetes.
Source-derived core finding. Among 3730 randomized patients followed for a median of 16 months, the primary composite occurred in 19.4% with empagliflozin and 24.7% with placebo, HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.65-0.86; P<0.001). Total heart-failure hospitalizations were 388 versus 553, HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.58-0.85; P<0.001). eGFR declined by -0.55 versus -2.28 mL/min/1.73 m2/year, between-group difference 1.73 (95% CI 1.10-2.37; P<0.001).

The primary composite should not be read as proof of a cardiovascular-mortality effect. Cardiovascular death alone was 10.0% versus 10.8%, HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.75-1.12), while first heart-failure hospitalization was 13.2% versus 18.3%, HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.59-0.81). The trial's clearest cardiovascular benefit is prevention of heart-failure hospitalization.

The prespecified renal composite was less frequent with empagliflozin, 1.6% versus 3.1%, HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.32-0.77), but it was outside the multiplicity-protected hierarchy. The same caution applies to KCCQ, all-cause hospitalization, all-cause death, incident diabetes, and biomarker outcomes. These results are useful, but their confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Appraisal. Internal validity is strong because of randomization, double blinding, blinded adjudication, prespecified hierarchical testing, sensitivity analyses, high vital-status ascertainment, and independent replication of sponsor-run analyses. External validity is narrower for acute decompensated heart failure, systolic blood pressure below 100 mm Hg, dialysis or eGFR below 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, and patients poorly represented by the enrolled population.

Methods and analysis plan

Trial design and eligibility

Adults with chronic heart failure for at least 3 months, NYHA class II-IV symptoms, and LVEF 40% or less were eligible. The design deliberately enriched event risk with LVEF-dependent NT-proBNP thresholds. Without atrial fibrillation, the minimum NT-proBNP was 2500 pg/mL for LVEF 36-40%, 1000 pg/mL for LVEF 31-35%, and 600 pg/mL for LVEF 30% or less. Thresholds doubled with atrial fibrillation. A recent heart-failure hospitalization permitted the lower 600/1200 pg/mL thresholds across LVEF up to 40%.

Key exclusions included eGFR below 20 mL/min/1.73 m2 or dialysis, symptomatic hypotension or systolic pressure below 100 mm Hg, recent major cardiovascular events or surgery, recent acute decompensated heart failure requiring intravenous treatment or mechanical support, transplant/LVAD, selected cardiomyopathies, severe valvular disease, recent SGLT inhibitor use, and prior ketoacidosis.

Endpoints and multiplicity

The primary endpoint was time to first adjudicated cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization. The two key secondary endpoints were total first and recurrent heart-failure hospitalizations and eGFR slope during double-blind treatment. These three endpoints formed the confirmatory hierarchy. The design targeted 841 primary events for 90% power to detect a 20% relative reduction, with one planned interim analysis and overall two-sided type I error controlled at 0.0496.

Other prespecified outcomes, including the renal composite, KCCQ, total all-cause hospitalization, all-cause mortality, new diabetes, and laboratory measures, were outside the hierarchy. The article explicitly warns that their confidence intervals were not multiplicity-adjusted and should not be treated as definitive treatment effects.

Protocol and statistical-analysis-plan reconciliation

Final protocol: version 4.0, November 20, 2019, based on Global Amendment 03. Revised final TSAP: June 19, 2020. The TSAP history dates the major estimand and model changes to October 11, 2019. The June 2020 revision records censoring clarifications, COVID-19 sensitivity analyses, a wording clarification, and addition of baseline-eGFR subgroup categories.

The cumulative TSAP revisions specified the closeout-related efficacy estimand and corresponding all-postrandomization sensitivity analyses, added baseline-eGFR-by-time interaction to the renal slope model, changed LVEF from a continuous to a categorical covariate, aligned MMRM specifications, and clarified the win-ratio rules. The protocol states that treatment assignment remained blinded until database lock, apart from DMC access and emergency unblinding procedures.

Sponsor employees ran the statistical analyses under academic oversight; an independent statistician replicated and verified them. The first author had unrestricted data access. This structure does not remove sponsor involvement, but it materially strengthens auditability compared with sponsor-only analysis.

Subgroups

Diabetes did not identify a loss of primary-endpoint benefit: HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.60-0.87) with diabetes and 0.78 (0.64-0.97) without diabetes. The ARNI subgroup had HR 0.64 (0.45-0.89). The LVEF >30% subgroup had HR 0.99 (0.76-1.31), while more severely reduced-EF strata had lower point estimates. These subgroup analyses are exploratory for interaction and should not be used to claim differential efficacy without formal, multiplicity-aware interaction evidence.

Sensitivity analyses

The primary result was stable with multiple imputation for incomplete data, HR 0.75 (0.66-0.87), and with Fine-Gray competing-risk analysis, subdistribution HR 0.75 (0.66-0.86). A shared-random-effects renal-slope sensitivity model with all-cause mortality as a competing risk estimated a slope difference of 1.55 (0.92-2.19) mL/min/1.73 m2/year.

Evaluation and appraisal

10/10
Relevance

Directly answers whether empagliflozin improves major HFrEF outcomes independent of diabetes.

Evidence basis: randomized HFrEF efficacy, renal, safety, and subgroup data. Limit: no active SGLT2 comparator. Would change: lower if the decision concerned only acute inpatient initiation or direct formulary equivalence.

9/10
Novelty

At publication it extended disease-modifying SGLT2 evidence into a particularly high-risk, low-EF HFrEF population.

Evidence basis: trial rationale and comparison with prior HFrEF evidence. Limit: DAPA-HF had already established the class concept in HFrEF. Would change: higher only if it uniquely established the therapeutic class; lower if judged solely as replication.

9/10
Method strength

Randomization, double blinding, blinded adjudication, event-driven design, prespecified hierarchy, protocol/TSAP availability, and sensitivity analyses support strong internal validity.

Evidence basis: article, final protocol v4.0, TSAP history, Table S3. Limit: sponsor employees ran analyses, and the TSAP underwent documented revisions. Would change: higher with fully independent analysis execution; lower if major endpoint-model changes had occurred after unblinding.

9/10
Evidence strength

The primary endpoint and both key secondary endpoints are statistically and clinically coherent, with sensitivity analyses supporting robustness.

Evidence basis: HR 0.75 primary, HR 0.70 recurrent HF hospitalization, renal slope difference 1.73. Limit: cardiovascular mortality was not reduced, and the renal composite was outside the hierarchy. Would change: higher with a clear mortality effect or multiplicity-protected renal-event confirmation.

8/10
External validity

Large international enrollment supports broad applicability to stable chronic HFrEF, including patients with and without diabetes.

Evidence basis: 520 centers, 20 countries, broad HFrEF background treatment. Limit: risk-enriched selection, mostly NYHA II, about 24% women, and exclusion of eGFR <20, dialysis, SBP <100, and recent decompensation. Would change: higher with stronger representation of excluded/underrepresented groups.

9/10
Implementation value

The dose, population, endpoints, safety profile, and effect magnitudes are clear enough to inform HFrEF pharmacotherapy decisions.

Evidence basis: fixed 10 mg dose, clear event reductions, safety table, eligibility boundaries. Limit: does not establish head-to-head superiority or equivalence versus dapagliflozin. Would change: higher with direct comparative evidence relevant to formulary substitution.

Overall judgment

Best use: foundational evidence that empagliflozin is an effective HFrEF therapy regardless of diabetes, with strong hospitalization and renal-slope effects.
Do not use for: claiming a cardiovascular mortality benefit, treating the nonhierarchical renal composite as confirmatory, or inferring empirical equivalence between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin from cross-trial comparison.
Confidence in appraisal: high, because the main article, appendix, final protocol, and revised TSAP were all available and reconciled.

Practice translation

What the trial supports
  • Empagliflozin 10 mg daily reduces the primary composite in stable chronic HFrEF.
  • The most evident clinical event benefit is fewer heart-failure hospitalizations.
  • Benefit on the primary outcome was present with and without diabetes.
  • eGFR decline was slower during treatment.
What requires restraint
  • Do not present cardiovascular mortality as reduced.
  • Do not elevate the renal composite to the same inferential status as the hierarchy-protected endpoints.
  • Do not infer safety in dialysis, eGFR below 20, acute decompensation, or SBP below 100 from this trial.
  • Do not use Table S4 as a head-to-head drug comparison.

Formulary implication. EMPEROR-Reduced is strong evidence for empagliflozin's efficacy within HFrEF. By itself, it does not answer whether a dapagliflozin-only formulary is clinically interchangeable with empagliflozin for every patient or indication. That question requires the broader evidence set and any direct or suitably synthesized comparative evidence.

Limitations and uncertainty

  • Multiplicity: several attractive outcomes, especially the renal composite and KCCQ, were outside the confirmatory testing hierarchy.
  • Composite interpretation: the primary endpoint benefit was mainly explained by heart-failure hospitalization rather than cardiovascular death.
  • Selection: NT-proBNP enrichment excluded many screened patients and produced a higher-risk HFrEF cohort.
  • Representation: women were about one quarter of the cohort; NYHA IV was rare; severe renal dysfunction and low blood pressure were excluded.
  • Follow-up: 21 patients lacked final vital status and 42 had incomplete follow-up at some point, partly related to COVID-19 operations; sensitivity analysis was stable.
  • Sponsor role: sponsor employees performed statistical analyses. Academic oversight, unrestricted first-author access, and independent statistical replication reduce but do not erase this concern.
  • TSAP revisions: the packet documents analysis-plan revisions. The history table places the major estimand/model revisions in October 2019 and the June 2020 changes mainly in censoring clarification, COVID-19 sensitivity analysis, and subgroup specification.
  • Data-sharing details: the article says a data-sharing statement exists, but its text is not present in the supplied packet.

No unresolved central efficacy-number conflict was identified among the main article, appendix, and final analysis materials. Supplementary Table S4 carries an explicit correction notice for one descriptive DAPA-HF comparison value from a prior online version.
