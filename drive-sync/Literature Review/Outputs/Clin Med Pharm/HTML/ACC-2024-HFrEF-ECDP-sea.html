Summary

Bottom line. This ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway is a practical implementation companion for chronic ambulatory HFrEF. Its main clinical message is rapid use of the four core medication classes, individualized sequencing, frequent reassessment, and continued movement toward target or maximally tolerated doses. It also gives unusually detailed operational guidance on referral, adherence, access, social needs, comorbidity management, and palliative care.

The pathway concentrates on patients with clinical HFrEF and LVEF 40% or less who are not clinically unstable. It preserves the ten-pivotal-issues format used by earlier ACC HFrEF pathways and updates it for evidence and guidance available through the 2024 document-development period.

The medication framework is direct. Use an ARNI when feasible, an evidence-based beta-blocker, a mineralocorticoid antagonist, and an SGLT inhibitor. Do not wait for one class to reach target dose before adding the others. For de novo HFrEF, the committee aims to complete initiation and titration within about two to three months when tolerated. It supports direct de novo ARNI use, fixed-dose SGLT inhibitor use, and simultaneous initiation of several classes in selected patients.

Later sections move beyond drug selection. The pathway defines when to repeat imaging, when natriuretic peptides help, when invasive hemodynamics are reasonable, when to refer to advanced HF care, how to organize team-based titration, how to address nonadherence and medication cost, and how to integrate palliative care without abandoning good HF symptom management.

What the pathway recommends

Rapid core GDMT

Use the four core HFrEF classes early. There is no required sequence. Some patients can start several classes at once. The pathway repeatedly prefers broad early coverage over prolonged serial titration of one drug at a time.

ARNI

Sacubitril/valsartan is the preferred renin-angiotensin system inhibitor when feasible. Direct initiation without ACE inhibitor or ARB pretreatment is acceptable. A 36-hour washout is required after an ACE inhibitor. Lower blood pressure and advanced HF require more caution.

SGLT inhibitors

SGLT inhibitors are core HFrEF therapy whether or not diabetes is present. The pathway stresses that their HF benefit persists with impaired kidney function and that an early eGFR dip is expected. Volume status and diuretic dose still need attention.

Additional drugs and procedures

Hydralazine plus isosorbide dinitrate is added for persistently symptomatic African-American patients after core GDMT. Ivabradine is reserved for eligible patients in sinus rhythm with persistent heart rate elevation despite maximally tolerated beta-blocker therapy. Vericiguat is a selective option after recent worsening HF. Functional mitral regurgitation should be treated with optimized GDMT before transcatheter repair is pursued.

Monitoring and referral

The pathway supports frequent medication checks during titration, repeat ventricular imaging after three to six months of optimized therapy for device decisions, selective natriuretic peptide use, and selective invasive hemodynamic assessment. The referral table provides concrete advanced-HF triggers, including repeated admissions, persistent NYHA III or IV symptoms, hypotension, kidney dysfunction, arrhythmias, GDMT intolerance, and persistent LVEF 35% or less despite therapy.

Implementation

The strongest practical content may be outside the drug algorithm. The document gives detailed actions for team-based care, pharmacist participation, virtual titration, adherence assessment, medication-access work, prior authorization, social determinants of health, and longitudinal comorbidity management.

Palliative care

Palliative care is integrated rather than reserved for the final days of life. The pathway asks clinicians to address goals and quality of life throughout HF, continue symptom-directed HF therapy when useful, plan annually for future decisions, and individualize the transition toward comfort-focused care.

Evaluation and appraisal

Strengths

  • The medication sequence is clinically usable. It tells the reader when simultaneous starts are reasonable, what to monitor, and how quickly to move.
  • Figures 2 through 4 convert dense guideline material into actionable drug, monitoring, and referral workflows.
  • The pathway treats implementation failures as clinical problems. Cost, adherence, social needs, pharmacist involvement, remote care, and prior authorization receive concrete treatment.
  • The supplement makes the development process and conflict-management rules visible.
  • The document is careful about its role. It says clinical judgment and the current HF guideline take precedence.

Limitations

  • This is expert consensus guidance. It is not a new systematic review, and many of the implementation recommendations rest on mixed evidence, observational data, or committee judgment.
  • Trial data are summarized selectively to support the pathway. A reader who needs primary-study methods, subgroup validity, or precise adverse-event estimates still needs the cited trial reports.
  • Some special-population evidence is thin, especially for very old adults, frailty, and some racial groups.
  • The internal target-count and eGFR-threshold discrepancies show that this dense implementation document still requires careful source checking before turning individual thresholds into hard-coded clinical decision support.
  • No external 2026 currency verification was performed because @VERIFY was not activated.

Practice value

For HFrEF implementation, this is a high-value companion to a formal HF guideline. It is particularly useful for medication sequencing, rapid titration, referral criteria, team workflows, and access problems. It should not be used as a substitute for the underlying guideline, primary trial reports, current product labeling, or local protocol.
