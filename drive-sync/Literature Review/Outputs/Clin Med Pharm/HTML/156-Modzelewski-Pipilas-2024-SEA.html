One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In a propensity-score-matched TriNetX cohort of patients with heart failure who were new to SGLT2 inhibitor therapy, empagliflozin initiation was associated with a lower 1-year hazard of the composite of all-cause mortality or hospitalization than dapagliflozin. The difference was driven by hospitalization; all-cause mortality was not clearly different.

The study identified 28,075 new users (15,976 empagliflozin; 12,099 dapagliflozin). The body text and main baseline table report 11,007 matched patients per group. In that matched cohort, the composite occurred in 3,545 (32.2%) versus 3,828 (34.8%) patients (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.86-0.94; log-rank P<.001; E-value 1.36). Hospitalization was similarly lower with empagliflozin (29.7% vs 32.1%; HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.86-0.94), whereas mortality was 6.3% vs 6.9% (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.82-1.00). HFrEF and HFpEF subgroup analyses showed similar directional associations.

The signal is clinically interesting because both drugs are widely considered class alternatives in heart failure, but the causal interpretation is limited by residual confounding, incomplete characterization of heart-failure severity and treatment context, platform-constrained outcome definitions and variance estimation, and uncertain medication adherence or switching. The authors explicitly state that the findings should not be used to prefer empagliflozin over dapagliflozin within quadruple heart-failure therapy. A pragmatic head-to-head randomized trial would be the appropriate confirmatory design.

Internal source inconsistency. The abstract and Figure 1 text report 11,077 matched patients per group, while the Results body, main baseline table, Figure 2 at-risk counts, and supplement analyses use 11,007. The article also states that a July 24, 2024 correction fixed a numerical error. This retrieved corrected PDF still contains the 11,077/11,007 discrepancy; this appraisal uses 11,007 for matched outcome denominators because it is supported by the outcome percentages and multiple detailed source locations.

Section condensation

Introduction

Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin both reduce cardiovascular death and heart-failure hospitalization relative to placebo, but within-class equivalence is not guaranteed. Prior observational work suggested possible differences in metabolic effects and cardiac remodeling. The authors framed the comparative effect on patient-centered outcomes in heart failure as uncertain.

The specific objective was a direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin comparison for the composite of all-cause mortality or hospitalization.

Methods - cohort and exposure

TriNetX contributed deidentified electronic medical record data from 81 health care organizations. Eligible patients had heart failure (ICD-10 I50x), no prior SGLT2 inhibitor use, and new initiation of empagliflozin or dapagliflozin between August 18, 2021 and December 6, 2022. Day 0 was drug initiation. Patients were analyzed according to the initially received drug regardless of later changes.

The primary endpoint was time to all-cause death or hospitalization over days 1-365. Secondary endpoints were all-cause death, hospitalization, and last measured hemoglobin A1c. Prespecified adverse effects were urinary tract infection and diabetic ketoacidosis. Cause-specific death and hospitalization could not be obtained through the platform.

Methods - confounding control and analysis

Propensity scores were estimated by logistic regression from demographics, cardiac/diabetes diagnoses, medication use, kidney function, hemoglobin A1c, natriuretic peptides, ejection fraction, and prior hospitalization. Except age, covariates were entered categorically. Continuous laboratory values were converted to multiple category indicators, allowing inclusion of patients with missing values and allowing multiple categories to be positive when multiple measurements occurred.

The study used 1:1 greedy nearest-neighbor matching without replacement; SMD <0.1 indicated balance. Outcomes used Kaplan-Meier curves, log-rank tests, HRs, 95% CIs, and reported absolute risk differences. Censoring occurred after the last TriNetX fact. E-values assessed sensitivity to unmeasured confounding. HFrEF/HFpEF analyses and secondary outcomes were not multiplicity-adjusted and were explicitly hypothesis-generating.

Results

Among 744,914 eligible heart-failure patients naive to SGLT2 inhibitors, 28,075 initiated one of the two drugs. Before matching, several clinically important differences existed, including more systolic heart failure and greater use of angiotensin II inhibitors/sacubitril among dapagliflozin initiators. After matching, measured covariates were tightly balanced; the largest reported post-match SMD was 0.013.

At 1 year, the matched composite endpoint favored empagliflozin (32.2% vs 34.8%; HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.86-0.94). Hospitalization showed the same HR, while mortality did not clearly differ (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.82-1.00). HbA1c among those measured was identical at the rounded mean (6.8% in both groups; P=.64). Prespecified adverse events occurred in 5.9% vs 6.5% (P=.08). HFrEF and HFpEF subgroup composite HRs were 0.92 and 0.91, respectively.

Discussion

The authors interpret the association as a possible within-class difference, driven by hospitalization. They contrast the result with trial meta-analyses showing similar placebo-relative benefits for empagliflozin and dapagliflozin and discuss adherence, comorbidity burden, treatment interactions, and cardiac remodeling as possible explanations.

They recommend direct confirmation in a pragmatic comparative-effectiveness randomized trial rather than treating the observational result as proof of drug superiority.

Limitations and conclusion

Key limitations are residual confounding (including missing NYHA class), incomplete use of contemporary quadruple therapy, inability to identify cause-specific outcomes, inability to compute cluster-robust standard errors for matched-pair/practice clustering, and inability to determine heart-failure onset sufficiently to quantify potential immortal-time bias. The reported E-value of 1.36 implies only modest unmeasured confounding could potentially explain the observed HR.

The conclusion is deliberately narrower than the headline comparison: empagliflozin was associated with lower 1-year hospitalization rates, but the result requires confirmation and should not be used to establish a preferred SGLT2 inhibitor for guideline-directed therapy.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Useful comparative-effectiveness signal and a good example of real-world SGLT2 head-to-head analysis, but insufficient to change clinical preference between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin.
Relevance - 8/10

Directly addresses a common heart-failure pharmacotherapy choice. Evidence basis: large multi-institutional new-user cohort and patient-centered outcomes. Limiting factor: observational design. A head-to-head randomized trial would raise the score.

Novelty - 7/10

Provides a large direct comparison where most established evidence is placebo-controlled. Limiting factor: not the first observational comparison and mechanisms remain unexplained. Replicated head-to-head evidence would increase novelty/impact.

Method strength - 6/10

New-user design, broad covariate matching, balance checks, time-to-event methods, and E-value sensitivity are strengths. Residual confounding, platform-limited statistics, missing clinical severity, and uncertain adherence/switching reduce confidence.

Evidence strength - 5/10

The association is precise for the composite and hospitalization, but causality is not secure and mortality is not clearly different. The modest E-value and observational limitations cap certainty. Randomization or strong independent replication would raise this score.

External validity - 6/10

Large, primarily North American multi-organization EHR network improves breadth. Applicability is limited by incomplete quadruple-therapy uptake, unknown site-level practice patterns, and EHR-dependent phenotype/outcome ascertainment.

Implementation value - 4/10

The study is useful for hypothesis generation, formulary discussion context, and research prioritization, but the authors explicitly caution against selecting empagliflozin over dapagliflozin based on these data alone.

Major strengths

  • Large multicenter real-world cohort with new-user restriction.
  • Direct head-to-head comparison rather than indirect cross-trial inference.
  • Extensive measured-confounder adjustment and post-match balance reporting.
  • Patient-centered composite endpoint with separate hospitalization and mortality analyses.
  • Prespecified subgroup analyses in HFrEF and HFpEF with supplemental balance tables.

Major limitations

  • Residual confounding remains plausible; NYHA class and other severity/prescribing variables were not available.
  • E-value 1.36 indicates the observed association is not especially robust to an unmeasured confounder of moderate strength.
  • Outcome attribution is all-cause; cause-specific heart-failure hospitalization or cardiovascular mortality was unavailable.
  • Platform constraints prevented cluster-robust standard errors for matched pairs or practice clustering.
  • Medication exposure was based on initiation with an intention-to-treat-like assignment; adherence, discontinuation, dose, and switching are not characterized.
  • No multiplicity correction for secondary/subgroup analyses.
  • Internal matched-sample-count discrepancy persists in the corrected PDF.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Use as a comparative-effectiveness signal and as a reason to watch for replication or a head-to-head pragmatic trial.

Do not use for

Do not treat the HR 0.90 as proof that empagliflozin is intrinsically superior or use this paper alone to mandate formulary preference.

Pharmacy interpretation

When both agents are otherwise appropriate, established indication, patient-specific factors, renal function, tolerability, access, cost, and system formulary considerations remain more defensible selection drivers than this single observational comparison.

Research next step

Pragmatic randomized head-to-head trial with standardized heart-failure phenotype, cause-specific outcomes, adherence/switching capture, site clustering, and contemporary background therapy.
