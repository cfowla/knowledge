One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The paper is useful as a prospective national snapshot of UK ITP treatment during the COVID-19 pandemic. Its central comparative claim, that TPO-RA appeared more effective than corticosteroids, comes from nonrandomized unadjusted treatment groups with clear case-mix differences and several internal table inconsistencies.

The investigators collected 343 submitted cases and analyzed 335 eligible adults. Treatment was started in 318. First-line success was defined as avoiding another treatment line within 28 days. Among evaluable patients, success was 82.6% with TPO-RA, 50.8% with corticosteroids, and 32.7% with IVIG. The treatment groups were not comparable at baseline. TPO-RA was used mainly in relapsed disease, while corticosteroids and other strategies were used mainly in new diagnoses. Secondary ITP frequency also differed across groups.

COVID-19 was detected in 23 of 243 patients tested at diagnosis, 9.5%. Thirteen of those 23 required admission and five required intubation. Four patients on maintenance ITP therapy had concurrent COVID-19; all four were on mycophenolate, all were admitted, and two were intubated. Fourteen secondary ITP cases were attributed to COVID-19 vaccination. These subgroup findings are descriptive and based on small numbers.

Source-integrity warning. The supplied article and supplement contain denominator and arithmetic inconsistencies. The most consequential is Table II, where the corticosteroid fourth-line value is 33/172 even though the overall fourth-line value is 20/295 and the steroid subtype rows sum to 10. No value was silently corrected in this appraisal.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper frames the pandemic as a treatment problem for ITP because corticosteroids and immunosuppressants were viewed as potentially unfavorable during COVID-19 infection and vaccination. UK consensus guidance and an NHS England interim policy supported first-line TPO-RA use in newly diagnosed or relapsed ITP during this period.

The study aimed to audit real-world practice against that guidance and compare observed first-line treatment outcomes. The paper treats those comparisons as pragmatic real-world evidence, not as a randomized trial.

Methods

This was a prospective observational service-evaluation study across 24 NHS trusts. Eligible patients were adults older than 18 years with new or relapsed ITP during the one-year study window. Sites entered data into a prespecified online form.

First-line success meant no further treatment line within 28 days. Platelet response used a threshold above 30 x10^9/L at days 7, 14, and 28. The article reports local service evaluation or audit approval at participating centres. It does not describe randomized treatment allocation or an adjusted comparative-effectiveness model.

Results

The 335 eligible patients had median age 57 years. There were 213 new diagnoses and 122 relapses. Treatment started in 318. Of 307 evaluable first-line outcomes, 166 were successful. TPO-RA had the highest reported success proportion at 38/46, 82.6%, compared with 92/181, 50.8%, for corticosteroids and 17/51, 32.7%, for IVIG. Second-line therapy was used in 45.9% of evaluable cases, third-line in 19.7%, and the table reports fourth-line therapy in 6.8% overall.

Bleeding complications were reported in 143/318 and thrombotic complications in 14/318. The table reports thrombosis in 11/189 steroid-treated patients, 1/47 TPO-RA patients, and 1/51 IVIG patients. These are unadjusted event proportions. Baseline treatment selection differed substantially by new versus relapsed disease and by secondary ITP frequency, so cross-treatment comparisons are vulnerable to confounding by indication.

Discussion

The authors interpret the cohort as a timely national snapshot and argue that TPO-RA was a useful first-line option during pandemic conditions. They also state that corticosteroid success looked inferior to TPO-RA and note that 74.9% of the cohort received corticosteroids at some point.

The discussion acknowledges that the success definition may disadvantage IVIG, platelet-response timing may reflect more than one treatment line, and real-world data carry bias. The authors explicitly call for clinical trials to confirm TPO-RA first-line benefit.

Source-integrity findings

  1. Table I labels the overall column n=343 and reports 23/343 positive COVID-19 tests, while the Results section defines the eligible cohort as n=335 and separately reports 23/335 overall; the tested subgroup denominator is 243.
  2. Table I prints post-COVID-19 vaccination as 0/3 (100%) in the maintenance-therapy column, which is arithmetically inconsistent with the numerator and denominator.
  3. Table II reports fourth-line treatment in 33/172 corticosteroid cases (19.2%) even though the overall fourth-line row is 20/295 (6.8%), and the prednisolone and dexamethasone fourth-line subrows sum to 10 cases.
  4. The Table II thrombosis footnote says there were seven venous thromboses but then enumerates three deep vein thromboses, four pulmonary embolisms, one portal vein thrombosis, and one sinus venous thrombosis, which totals nine venous events.
  5. The Results prose states that 23/30 second-line TPO-RA cases avoided third-line treatment at 76.1%, whereas Figure S1 prints 23/30 as 76.7%.
  6. The article and Table S1 report 76/335 secondary ITP as 22.3%, although that fraction is approximately 22.7%; Table S1 also contains a drug-induced row of 6/78 despite the stated secondary-ITP total of 76.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

High for historical ITP practice and pandemic-era treatment selection.

Evidence basis. National prospective real-world cohort with treatment, safety, COVID-19, and vaccine data.

Limiting factor. The setting is specific to pandemic-era UK policy and is not current-practice verification.

Would change with. A current guideline or replication showing that the findings remain applicable in present practice.

Novelty 7/10

The source provides a timely multicentre national snapshot and unusual first-line TPO-RA use during a policy exception.

Evidence basis. Twenty-four NHS trusts contributed prospective cases during the first pandemic year.

Limiting factor. Novelty is context dependent and much of the treatment evidence predates the pandemic.

Would change with. Independent national datasets or comparative analyses showing a reproducible practice shift.

Method strength 5/10

Prospective multicentre collection and prespecified questions are strengths, but treatment assignment was observational.

Evidence basis. Defined eligibility, prospective form, explicit outcome rule, and multicentre participation.

Limiting factor. No randomization, no adjusted comparative model, missing data, and internal table inconsistencies.

Would change with. Prespecified adjusted analyses, robust missing-data handling, and source tables without arithmetic contradictions.

Evidence strength 4/10

The source supports descriptive practice patterns but only weakly supports comparative efficacy claims.

Evidence basis. Large differences in unadjusted treatment-success proportions are reported.

Limiting factor. Confounding by indication is obvious from baseline group differences, and some key tables contain inconsistent denominators or impossible totals.

Would change with. Randomized or well-adjusted comparative evidence with consistent denominators and uncertainty estimates.

External validity 6/10

The 24-centre national network improves UK representativeness for the study period.

Evidence basis. Tertiary and district hospitals participated, and the age/secondary-ITP profile was compared with prior national data.

Limiting factor. Pandemic policy, testing patterns, vaccine timing, and off-label TPO-RA access limit transportability.

Would change with. Replication outside the pandemic policy environment and across health systems.

Implementation value 5/10

Useful for understanding how ITP services adapted and how treatment-line outcomes can be tracked.

Evidence basis. The article gives treatment groups, success rules, response timing, indications, and safety events.

Limiting factor. It does not provide causal evidence strong enough to choose TPO-RA over corticosteroids in current practice.

Would change with. Current guideline alignment plus stronger comparative evidence and standardized treatment algorithms.

Clinical translation. This source alone should not be used to establish TPO-RA superiority as first-line ITP therapy. The paper itself calls for clinical trials. Current guideline and regulatory status were not checked because @VERIFY was not activated.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Best use
Historical real-world evidence on UK ITP management during COVID-19, including treatment selection, steroid weaning, COVID outcomes, and treatment-line flow.
Do not use for
A causal claim that TPO-RA is more effective or safer than corticosteroids, or as a current treatment recommendation.
Data-design lesson
Keep treatment success denominators explicit. Separate missing outcomes from failures. A denominator change from 189 to 181 materially changes the corticosteroid success percentage.
Read next
Prioritize the pandemic ITP guidance, direct COVID-ITP studies, vaccine-associated thrombocytopenia study, and TPO-RA trials listed in the reference task queue.
