One-page synthesis

Core thesis: An equation that reduces the weight-based UFH starting dose as age and BMI increase correlated more strongly with the eventual dose associated with two consecutive therapeutic aPTTs than the institution's empiric weight-based starting dose.

The investigators derived a linear equation in 40 retrospectively identified patients and then calculated the equation prospectively, without using it to direct care, in an observational validation cohort. In the phase II intention-to-treat cohort (N=101), the predictive dose correlated with therapeutic dose at r=0.703 versus r=0.532 for the empiric dose; the reported comparison reached P=.05. Figure 3 reports R² values of 0.4943 versus 0.2829. The per-protocol comparison showed a similar direction (r=0.696 versus 0.532) but did not reach conventional statistical significance (P=.057).

The study is best interpreted as an institution-specific prediction-model pilot. The endpoint is correlation with eventual therapeutic dose, not time to therapeutic anticoagulation, bleeding, thrombosis, or mortality. Most patients initially identified were excluded because they did not achieve two consecutive therapeutic aPTTs on a consistent dose or met other criteria, and the equation was never prospectively used as the treatment algorithm. The authors appropriately call for a large prospective trial with clinical outcomes.

Key constraint: The retrieved PDF references Supplementary Appendix 1 for additional regression-derivation details, but that supplement is not contained in the file. Model diagnostics beyond the main-text coefficients, R², and P values cannot be fully appraised from this PDF alone.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames UFH dosing as pharmacokinetically variable and proposes adding age and BMI to weight-based dosing. It describes a retrospective derivation phase and a prospective observational validation phase targeting an aPTT of 60-80 seconds.

The abstract reports r=0.703 for predicted versus therapeutic dose and r=0.532 for empiric versus therapeutic dose (P=.05), concluding that prediction improved and that randomized prospective validation is still required.

Introduction

The introduction emphasizes UFH's rapid onset, reversibility, and titratability but notes substantial interpatient variability related to binding/elimination, antithrombin levels, clot burden, distribution, obesity, age, and monitoring assay variability. The study's institutional context uses a nurse-driven nomogram and an aPTT target of 60-80 seconds for selected indications.

The authors hypothesize that age and BMI explain residual variability not captured by weight alone and define the project as derivation followed by institution-specific internal observational validation.

Methods

Phase I retrospectively screened September 2015 patients and included those achieving two consecutive aPTTs of 60-80 seconds on the same UFH dose. Phase II prospectively observed patients beginning October 2015 until the prespecified pilot sample was reached. Patients treated for less than 24 hours, started at an outside hospital, or treated with nomogram alterations were excluded. A per-protocol subset further restricted indications.

Patients in phase II received standard-care empiric weight-based dosing; the age/BMI equation was calculated simultaneously but not used to dose them. The primary endpoint compared correlation of empiric versus therapeutic dose with correlation of predictive versus therapeutic dose. Multiple linear regression/ANOVA were used for derivation, and a two-tailed Fisher transformation was used for the correlation comparison.

Results

The derivation cohort included 40 of 169 identified patients; phase II included 101 of 362 in the ITT cohort and 73 in the PP cohort. The equation was Dose (units/kg/h) = -0.275 × BMI (kg/m²) - 0.143 × Age (years) + 33.06. Derivation R² was 0.303; BMI P=.005, age P=.01, and overall model P=.001.

In phase II ITT, r was 0.703 for predictive versus therapeutic dose and 0.532 for empiric versus therapeutic dose (P=.05). Figure 3 reports R²=0.4943 and 0.2829, respectively. PP results were directionally similar (r=0.696 vs 0.532) but P=.057.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret age and BMI adjustment as explaining more UFH dose variability and hypothesize that age- and adiposity-related changes in distribution contribute. They report no significant hemorrhagic or thromboembolic complications, but no event counts or comparative clinical-outcome analysis are provided.

Limitations explicitly include observational design, correlation rather than causation, few patients with BMI >35 kg/m², variability of the aPTT assay, and heterogeneity among patients with cancer. The paper calls for prospective clinical-outcome validation and suggests future comparison with chromogenic anti-factor Xa monitoring.

Critical appraisal

Selection boundary: patients had to reach the study's therapeutic aPTT definition to contribute a therapeutic dose. In phase II, 186 of 362 identified patients were excluded for failure to achieve two consecutive therapeutic aPTTs on a consistent dose, and another 75 met exclusion criteria. The reported predictive performance therefore applies to a selected subset in whom the target dose could be observed.

The validation is temporal and prospective in patient identification, but not interventional: clinicians did not dose patients according to the predictive equation. This distinction limits claims about faster target attainment, reduced overshoot, bleeding prevention, or thrombosis prevention. The study demonstrates association with a retrospectively observable target dose, not effectiveness of a new dosing pathway.

Prediction-model reporting is limited in the main PDF. Correlation and R² are provided, but calibration, absolute prediction error, prediction intervals, coefficient uncertainty, sensitivity analysis, and external validation are not reported in the main text. Supplementary Appendix 1 may contain additional derivation details, but it is unavailable in the retrieved source. The ITT correlation comparison is exactly at P=.05 and the PP comparison is P=.057, making the inferential signal modest.

The source's own external-validity caveats are substantial: single-center design, one aPTT assay/target, few patients with BMI >35 kg/m², heterogeneous indications, and cancer-treatment heterogeneity. The equation also has no reported prospective guardrails or validated use range in the main text.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Use now?
No as a stand-alone dosing protocol. The source supports a prediction hypothesis, not demonstrated clinical superiority.
Useful concept
Age and adiposity may account for some residual variability left by weight-only UFH dosing; explicit predictive modeling can be tested against eventual stable dose.
What a modern validation should add
External sites, broader BMI ranges, anti-Xa and aPTT strata, calibration/error metrics, prespecified dose bounds, time-to-therapeutic endpoints, bleeding/thrombosis outcomes, and prospective algorithm use.
What not to overclaim
Improved correlation does not by itself prove faster anticoagulation, fewer supratherapeutic values, less bleeding, or fewer recurrent thrombotic events.
