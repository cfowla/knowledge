One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. This is a methodologically serious pharmacovigilance re-analysis of a prior RESPOND safety signal. It strengthens the case that the early CVD signal after switching to INSTI-based ART deserves continued study, but it does not establish class-wide causal cardiotoxicity or justify changing ART solely on this paper.
Core finding — treatment-experienced

Switching to an INSTI was associated with higher CVD risk at 1 year (ITT RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.16–2.00; RD +0.26 percentage points) and 2 years (RR 1.30, 1.03–1.62; RD +0.29 points), with the ITT estimate converging by 4 years and reversing numerically by 6 years (RR 0.93, 0.78–1.09).

Core finding — treatment-naïve

Among ART-naïve participants, the ITT curves gradually separated: RR 1.07 at 1 year, 1.21 at 2 years, and 1.87 (1.06–3.26) at 6 years, corresponding to an absolute 6-year RD of +1.03 percentage points. Only 95 CVD events occurred, so precision is limited.

What improves credibility

Explicit target-trial framework; monthly sequential trials for switchers; central CVD event validation; ITT and PP estimands; extensive baseline adjustment; bootstrapped CIs; alternative g-computation sensitivity analysis.

What weakens causal interpretation

Residual confounding/channeling remains plausible; some cohorts contributed stratified samples by INSTI use; the full-cohort sensitivity analysis materially attenuated the early association; treatment classes and comparator regimens were heterogeneous.

Clinical meaning

Relative effects are attention-grabbing, but absolute differences are small. The study itself concludes that INSTIs should remain recommended and calls for agent-specific and mechanistic studies rather than a practice reversal.

Important nuance. The paper’s “transient first two years” framing fits the treatment-experienced ITT analysis, but the per-protocol estimate remains statistically elevated at 4 years (RR 1.29, 95% CI 1.07–1.54; RD +0.53 percentage points). The estimand matters.

Section condensation: methods

Data source and eligibility

RESPOND combines prospectively recorded routine clinical/laboratory data from 19 cohorts across Europe and Australia. ART-naïve participants had to be age ≥18 years, ART-naïve after January 2012, have detectable HIV RNA (≥200 copies/mL), and have CD4/HIV RNA measurements within one year before through 12 weeks after ART start. Participants with missing sex/gender, unknown ART, or initial maraviroc/enfuvirtide were excluded.

Treatment-experienced participants had to be on INSTI-free ART after January 2012, have no prior INSTI exposure, and have suppressed HIV RNA (<200 copies/mL). A history of CVD was excluded; Figure 1 also shows exclusion of 5,026 people with unknown CVD history. This creates a primary-prevention-like switch cohort rather than a population including established CVD.

Target-trial emulation and follow-up

For ART-naïve participants, baseline was initial ART start. For treatment-experienced participants, the investigators emulated a new trial every month from January 2012 through December 2021, reassessing eligibility and baseline covariates each month. Individuals could therefore enter multiple nested trials, and the overlapping trials were pooled. Treatment assignment was based on ART at each trial baseline, with no post-baseline grace period.

ITT follow-up continued until first CVD event, death, loss to follow-up, or final cohort visit; absence of an HIV RNA measurement for ≥18 months defined loss to follow-up. PP analyses artificially censored participants at strategy deviation. This design directly addresses the lack of a natural baseline among people who “continue” existing ART.

Outcome ascertainment

The endpoint was a composite of myocardial infarction, stroke, and invasive coronary/carotid procedures. Events occurring within one year before or after RESPOND enrollment were reported using standardized case-report forms and centrally validated by a trained physician using established algorithms. Earlier historical events were recorded but were not centrally validated.

Statistical analysis

Pooled logistic regression estimated adjusted risks, RDs, and RRs. Stabilized inverse-probability treatment weights incorporated demographic, HIV, cohort, calendar-time, renal, metabolic, smoking, lipid, and ART variables; the experienced analysis additionally modeled nested-trial indicators and excluded CVD history/HIV RNA because of eligibility restrictions. PP analyses added censoring or cumulative treatment weights. Weights were truncated at the 99th percentile.

Confidence intervals used 500 bootstrap samples. Missing data were not imputed; missingness was modeled as a separate category. Treatment-experienced sensitivity analyses used parametric g-computation, restricted trials to the later prospective period, and restricted to four cohorts that shared data on all participants. Subgroups examined sex/gender, age, and baseline abacavir use.

Section condensation: results

Treatment-naïve cohort

Of 8,444 ART-naïve people after January 2012, 7,111 were eligible; 3,354 (47%) started INSTI-based ART and 3,757 (53%) started other ART. Median age was 38 years; 19% were women and 8.5% Black. INSTI initiators were disproportionately treated in later calendar years and more often had hypertension, diabetes, eGFR <60 mL/min, and initial TAF or abacavir exposure. Dolutegravir accounted for 55.1% of initial INSTI use.

Over median follow-up of 62 months (IQR 36–85), 95 CVD events occurred: 38 MIs, 37 strokes, and 20 invasive procedures. The adjusted ITT RR rose from 1.07 at year 1 to 1.87 at year 6, with the 6-year RD +1.03 percentage points (95% CI 0.08–2.07). PP estimates were larger and statistically elevated from year 2 onward, but event scarcity produces wide uncertainty.

Treatment-experienced cohort

Among 28,638 people with suppressed HIV RNA on INSTI-free ART, 22,921 unique people were included after excluding 691 with CVD history and 5,026 with unknown CVD history. Because individuals entered repeated monthly trials, the analysis contained 1,299,172 non-unique trial records: 11,278 switching to INSTI and 1,287,894 remaining on other ART. The unique population had median age 49 years; 28% were women and 12% Black. Dolutegravir represented 58% of INSTI switches.

Across median 37 months of follow-up, 800 unique CVD events occurred (311 MIs, 256 strokes, 233 invasive procedures). ITT risk was higher after switching at 1 and 2 years, but the difference attenuated by year 4 and was absent by year 6. PP estimates were larger: RR 1.82 at 1 year, 1.58 at 2 years, 1.29 at 4 years, and 1.16 at 6 years, with the 6-year CI crossing 1.

Subgroups and sensitivity analyses

No statistically evident effect modification was reported by age (interaction p=0.19), sex/gender (p=0.96), or baseline abacavir use (p=0.72). Parametric g-computation produced similar ITT results, and restricting to trials after January 2017 was described as consistent with the main findings.

Most important sensitivity result: restricting to four cohorts that shared data on all participants with RESPOND (6,887 participants; 233 events) attenuated the treatment-experienced association and removed statistical significance: RR 1.15 (95% CI 0.64–1.63) at year 1 and 0.97 (0.57–1.33) at year 2. This materially lowers confidence that the primary early signal is robust to cohort inclusion/selection structure.

Table 1 — Baseline characteristics, ART-naïve (pages 8–9)

The table shows substantial calendar-time and treatment-channel differences before weighting. INSTI initiators started therapy later (44% after 2016 vs 10% among non-INSTI initiators; only 18% before 2015 vs 69%), and more commonly had hypertension (18% vs 10%), diabetes (2.7% vs 1.8%), and use of ABC (25% vs 13%) or TAF (28% vs 5.7%). Several key covariates had substantial missingness overall: BMI 36%, CVD history 32%, smoking 30%, total cholesterol 28%, eGFR 27%, and HDL 38%.

Appraisal: these imbalances justify aggressive confounding adjustment, but the combination of strong calendar trends and missing-indicator handling means residual confounding remains plausible even after weighting.

Section condensation: discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the treatment-experienced analysis as confirming the early CVD signal from their prior RESPOND report under a stronger target-trial design, noting that the pattern was similar with g-computation. They argue that the early timing does not fit conventional atherosclerotic development and therefore warrants mechanistic study. For ART-naïve patients, they describe the progressively increasing divergence as more biologically compatible with longer-term metabolic effects of INSTIs, although the small event count limits confidence.

The manuscript openly acknowledges non-causal alternatives: confounding by indication, channeling of higher-risk people to INSTIs, differential healthcare contact after switching, and cohort-selection bias. The authors also note that results are class-level while dolutegravir dominated exposure, so the findings may reflect dolutegravir more than a homogeneous INSTI effect. They conclude that absolute risk differences are small, that benefits of INSTI-based ART may outweigh the signal, and that INSTIs should remain recommended while future work evaluates individual agents and mechanisms.

Internal numerical consistency checks

Two manuscript discrepancies should be preserved when citing.

  • ART-naïve 1-year ITT RR: abstract and Table 2 report 95% CI 0.29–4.93, while the narrative Results text on page 9 reports 0.20–4.93.
  • Treatment-experienced 2-year ITT RR: abstract reports 1.07–1.62, while the Results text and Table 2 report 1.03–1.62.

This appraisal uses the Table 2 values for its quantitative summary and flags the conflicting prose rather than silently reconciling it.

Principal strengths

  • Target-trial design directly addresses baseline alignment problems in ART switching comparisons.
  • Large treatment-experienced population with repeated monthly eligibility assessment.
  • Central validation of CVD endpoints and clinically meaningful outcome components.
  • ITT and PP estimates make exposure-strategy assumptions visible.
  • Alternative causal estimator and cohort-restriction sensitivity analyses explicitly probe robustness.
  • Absolute risks and RDs are reported alongside relative effects.

Principal limitations

  • Residual confounding, confounding by indication, and treatment channeling remain plausible despite weighting.
  • Selection structure is consequential: restricting to cohorts sharing all participants attenuated the main early association.
  • Missingness is high for several cardiovascular covariates in the ART-naïve cohort, and missing values were handled as categories rather than imputed.
  • Only 95 CVD events occurred in the ART-naïve cohort; long-term estimates are therefore vulnerable to sparse-event instability.
  • INSTI is analyzed as a class although dolutegravir comprises ~55–58% of exposed participants.
  • Comparator ART is heterogeneous and changes substantially across calendar time.
  • The composite includes invasive procedures, which may partly reflect diagnostic intensity/healthcare contact.
  • Supplementary target-trial specifications and diagnostics were not available in the uploaded source.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use now

Use this as a pharmacovigilance signal and as evidence that early CVD risk after INSTI switching remains unresolved under a stronger causal-inference design.

Do not use for

Do not treat the study as proof of class-wide INSTI cardiotoxicity, an individual-agent ranking, or a standalone reason to discontinue/switch otherwise appropriate INSTI therapy.

For literature synthesis

Separate ART-naïve vs treatment-experienced and ITT vs PP. Preserve absolute RDs alongside RRs and explicitly include the attenuated full-cohort sensitivity analysis.

Best next evidence. The source itself points to two priorities: adequately powered comparisons of individual INSTIs/comparator regimens and mechanistic studies capable of explaining (or falsifying) the rapid early risk pattern after switching.
Bottom line: credible enough to keep the cardiovascular safety signal active; not strong enough to make it causal or practice-changing. Confidence in this appraisal: moderate-high for the main manuscript, reduced by absence of the referenced supplement.
