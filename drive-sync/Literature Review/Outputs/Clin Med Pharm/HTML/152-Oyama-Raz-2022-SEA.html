Scope warning. This report evaluates treatment-effect consistency across baseline medication strata. Background cardiovascular drugs were not randomized, subgroup analyses were not powered for definitive interaction claims, and nominal P values were not multiplicity adjusted.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In 17,160 participants with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk, dapagliflozin’s reductions in cardiovascular death/hospitalization for heart failure and kidney disease progression were generally consistent whether or not participants were receiving ACEI/ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics, or MRAs. Selected safety outcomes—volume depletion symptoms, acute kidney injury, and marked hyperkalemia—also showed no convincing heterogeneity by these therapies.

The most notable nominal interaction was for the kidney-specific composite by baseline diuretic use (interaction P=.003), with a numerically larger relative benefit among nonusers; because the analysis tested many subgroups without multiplicity correction, the authors treat this cautiously. Physiologic effects on systolic blood pressure and eGFR were also similar across medication strata. The parent trial protocol/SAP confirms randomized dapagliflozin assignment, ITT efficacy analysis, and prespecified subgroup interaction modeling, but it also documents the exploratory status of unadjusted subgroup P values.

Source-level inconsistency preserved, not reconciled: the abstract/narrative reports the subgroup receiving ACEI/ARB + beta-blocker + diuretic as n=4,243 with CV death/HHF HR 0.76 (0.62-0.93) and kidney HR 0.62 (0.44-0.87), whereas Figure 2 labels “All 3” as n=3,654 with HR 0.75 (0.59-0.94) and 0.70 (0.48-1.02), respectively. Both representations are retained in the ATOM output and flagged for review.

Methods

Population and randomized treatment

DECLARE-TIMI 58 randomized 17,160 participants with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD or multiple cardiovascular risk factors to dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily or matching placebo. Entry required creatinine clearance ≥60 mL/min. The trial ran from May 2013 through September 2018; the secondary analysis was conducted from February 2021 through May 2022.

Secondary-analysis framework

Baseline medication subgroups were ACEI/ARB, beta-blocker, diuretic (loop/thiazide/other, excluding MRA), and MRA. Efficacy was analyzed by randomized assignment using Cox models, with treatment-by-subgroup interaction testing. Repeated-measures mixed models estimated longitudinal differences in systolic blood pressure and eGFR. No multiplicity adjustment was used. Efficacy followed ITT; selected safety analyses used participants receiving at least one dose and prespecified on-treatment windows.

Outcomes

Key outcomes were cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure, hospitalization for heart failure alone, and a kidney-specific composite of persistent ≥40% eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, or kidney-related death. The article’s kidney-specific definition should not be conflated with an earlier protocol renal composite that also incorporated cardiovascular death.

Results

Baseline therapy

ACEI/ARB use was 13,950/17,160 (81%), beta-blocker 9,030 (53%), diuretic 6,205 (36%), and MRA 762 (4%). No participant used sacubitril/valsartan at baseline. Medication-user groups differed clinically, reflecting background disease burden rather than randomized exposure to these therapies.

Physiologic effects

At 48 months, placebo-corrected systolic BP reductions were about 2 mm Hg across most medication strata, and eGFR differences favored dapagliflozin by roughly 1-2 mL/min/1.73 m²; none of the Figure 1 interaction tests suggested material heterogeneity. The article states body weight, HbA1c, and diastolic BP effects were also consistent, but the cited Supplement 2 containing those details was not present in the retrieved folder.

Clinical efficacy and safety

Figure 2 shows generally similar hazard ratios across baseline medication use for cardiovascular death/HHF, HHF, and the kidney-specific composite. The only strong nominal interaction was kidney outcome by diuretic status (P=.003), with HR 0.74 (0.54-1.01) among diuretic users versus 0.39 (0.29-0.53) among nonusers. Figure 3 shows no convincing treatment interaction for volume depletion, AKI, or hyperkalemia, and AKI was generally lower with dapagliflozin.

Protocol and Statistical Analysis Plan reconciliation

Protocol scaffold

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3b trial
~17,150 planned participants / ~1,200 centers
Dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily vs placebo
Placebo run-in → randomization → longitudinal visits/phone follow-up
Initial gate: MACE noninferiority (HR margin 1.3)
If met: superiority testing for MACE and CV death/HHF
Safety surveillance: serious AEs + prespecified events of special interest

SAP analysis sets and subgroup logic

Full analysis set: all randomized participants → analyze by randomized treatment (ITT)
Safety set: ≥1 dose + postdose data
Risk windows: generally 30 days for efficacy/SAEs; 7 days for nonserious selected AEs
Subgroup model: Cox model + treatment × subgroup interaction
Multiplicity: subgroup interaction P values not adjusted → exploratory/descriptive

The SAP planned approximately 1,390 MACE events and ~770 CV death/HHF events, but those power statements concern the trial-level confirmatory objectives—not medication-subgroup interaction testing. The 2022 report therefore appropriately acknowledges limited power within individual medication strata.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as supportive evidence that dapagliflozin’s established cardiorenal benefits and selected safety profile are generally preserved across common background cardiovascular therapies. Do not use it to claim that specific background drugs modify dapagliflozin efficacy or to infer causal effects of those background drugs.

Relevance 8/10

Directly addresses whether dapagliflozin benefits and selected safety signals are preserved across common cardiovascular background therapies in T2D.

Evidence basis

Principal limit

Would raise confidence

Supports adding dapagliflozin without routine avoidance solely because patients take these background cardiovascular drugs.

Would raise confidence

Main strengths

  • Very large randomized parent trial with blinded treatment assignment.
  • Prespecified subgroup analysis and explicit interaction testing.
  • Clinical cardiovascular and kidney endpoints plus selected safety outcomes.
  • Protocol/SAP available for independent verification of trial-level analysis principles.

Main limitations

  • Subgroups not powered for definitive within-stratum or interaction conclusions.
  • No multiplicity adjustment across many subgroup comparisons.
  • Background medication dose and subsequent titration not captured in a way that supports dose-interaction analysis.
  • Small MRA subgroup and no baseline sacubitril/valsartan.
  • Parent trial excluded CrCl <60 mL/min, constraining direct extrapolation to more advanced CKD.
  • Unresolved all-three subgroup discrepancy between abstract/narrative and Figure 2.
  • Supplement 2 and Supplement 3 were absent from the retrieved source package.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Reasonable use: consider the report as reassurance that dapagliflozin generally retains benefit when added to common ACEI/ARB, beta-blocker, diuretic, or MRA regimens, without a consistent signal of excess AKI, marked hyperkalemia, or volume depletion attributable to those combinations.

Do not overclaim: the analysis does not establish that any one background medication potentiates or attenuates dapagliflozin, does not specify medication-dose adjustment strategies, and cannot substitute for patient-level monitoring when combining agents that alter blood pressure, volume status, kidney function, or potassium.

High-value follow-up: reconcile the missing Supplement 2 and the all-three subgroup discrepancy against an authoritative publisher copy/correction before using the exact subgroup numbers in a derivative evidence product.
