One-page synthesis

Core thesis. CKD care in diabetes should combine repeated UACR/eGFR risk staging with intensive risk-factor management and layered cardiorenal therapy: optimized glycemia and blood pressure, maximally tolerated RAS blockade when albuminuria/hypertension is present, SGLT2 inhibition beginning at eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² in type 2 diabetes, GLP-1–based therapy with demonstrated benefit, and finerenone for albuminuric CKD when appropriate.

Section 11 operationalizes CKD care around both eGFR and albuminuria. It emphasizes annual screening (more frequently in established CKD), ≥30% albuminuria reduction when UACR is ≥300 mg/g, BP <130/80 mmHg when safe, and referral for rapidly worsening markers or eGFR <30. Figure 11.1 integrates GFR and albuminuria strata into screening/treatment/referral frequency; Tables 11.1–11.3 address alternative kidney disease, CKD complication surveillance, and albuminuria-lowering interventions.

The pharmacotherapy section is anchored by randomized outcome evidence for SGLT2 inhibitors and finerenone, plus GLP-1 kidney-outcome evidence including FLOW. The chapter extends practical guidance into lower eGFR, dialysis, pregnancy, and combination therapy. Evidence is strongest for SGLT2/RAS/finerenone cardiorenal outcomes and less mature for dialysis GLP-1 therapy, simultaneous combination initiation based on albuminuria, and some pregnancy/kidney-failure edge cases.

Section condensation

Epidemiology, assessment, and diagnosis

CKD is defined by persistent albuminuria, eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m², or other kidney-damage markers and affects roughly 20–40% of people with diabetes. The chapter favors random spot UACR and routine eGFR, highlights UACR biological variability, and recommends confirming elevated albuminuria with repeated specimens. Combined creatinine/cystatin C estimation can improve accuracy when creatinine generation is atypical.

CKD attributed to diabetes remains a clinical diagnosis unless biopsy establishes etiology. Active urinary sediment, rapid eGFR decline, rapidly rising proteinuria, nephrotic syndrome, or atypical retinopathy patterns should prompt consideration of another or additional cause and possible nephrology evaluation.

Staging, AKI, and surveillance

Risk staging requires both eGFR and albuminuria. Albuminuria modifies cardiovascular, CKD-progression, and mortality risk at every eGFR level, while eGFR determines medication dosing constraints and complication surveillance. AKI is distinguished from expected hemodynamic creatinine changes during RAS blockade; a creatinine increase up to 30% without volume depletion is not, by itself, an indication to stop therapy.

Established CKD requires repeated UACR/eGFR monitoring plus attention to potassium, medication dosing, nephrotoxins, anemia, acidosis, volume status, and mineral-bone disease. Monitoring frequency increases from roughly every 6–12 months in G3 to every 1–3 months in G5.

Nutrition and glycemic management

Protein intake is 0.8 g/kg/day for G3+ nondialysis CKD and 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day may be needed on dialysis. High protein intake (>20% of calories or >1.3 g/kg/day) is discouraged; sodium restriction below 2,300 mg/day may aid BP/CV risk management. Nutrition targets should be individualized, especially as eGFR declines.

Glycemic optimization reduces albuminuria/CKD progression, but kidney benefits have long time horizons and intensive treatment can increase hypoglycemia risk in CKD. A1C is less reliable in advanced CKD; glycated albumin and fructosamine can supplement assessment.

Blood pressure and RAS blockade

The recommended treated BP goal is <130/80 mmHg when safely attainable, with consideration of SBP <120 mmHg. ACE inhibitor/ARB therapy is foundational when hypertension coexists with albuminuria, especially UACR ≥300 mg/g and/or eGFR <60, and should be titrated to the maximally tolerated dose.

Routine ACE inhibitor/ARB use is not recommended for primary prevention when BP, UACR, and eGFR are normal. Potassium and eGFR monitoring are integral; mild creatinine rises should not trigger premature discontinuation without volume depletion or clinically important hyperkalemia.

SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1–based therapy

SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended in type 2 diabetes + CKD from eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² and can be continued until kidney failure. The source emphasizes kidney/CV benefit even when glucose-lowering efficacy wanes at lower eGFR. GLP-1–based therapy is also recommended for CKD/CV risk reduction and FLOW supplies direct semaglutide kidney-outcome evidence.

Medication choice should account for kidney clearance and adverse effects; exenatide and lixisenatide have important low-eGFR limitations. The chapter distinguishes strong outcome evidence from secondary CVOT kidney analyses and from less mature tirzepatide kidney-outcome evidence.

Finerenone and combination therapy

Finerenone is recommended for albuminuric CKD when eGFR is ≥25 mL/min/1.73 m², with potassium monitoring 1 month after initiation. FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD, and FIDELITY provide concordant kidney and cardiovascular benefit with a clinically relevant hyperkalemia tradeoff.

Layered therapy is presented on background maximally tolerated RAS blockade. CONFIDENCE provides randomized evidence that initial finerenone + empagliflozin produces larger UACR reduction than either alone, supporting Recommendation 11.9; the chapter does not present a completed hard-outcome combination trial.

Pregnancy, kidney failure, dialysis, and referral

Potentially fetotoxic kidney-protective drugs should be avoided without reliable contraception and changed before conception. In pregnancy, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors, MRAs, SGLT2 inhibitors, and neprilysin inhibitors are contraindicated; methyldopa, labetalol, and long-acting nifedipine are listed as safer antihypertensive options.

SGLT2 inhibitors can be continued below eGFR 20 until dialysis when tolerated; GLP-1–based therapy not dependent on kidney clearance may be used on dialysis, although evidence is less mature. Nephrology referral is recommended for rapidly worsening kidney markers, eGFR <30, uncertain etiology, difficult management issues, or kidney-replacement planning.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: current operational CKD management in adults with diabetes and as a map to the primary cardiorenal evidence base. Do not use as a substitute for direct reading of cited primary studies when exact effect estimates, subgroup validity, or safety estimates will drive a high-stakes decision.

Relevance

Rationale: Directly actionable 2026 CKD risk-management guidance for diabetes care.

Evidence basis: Nineteen graded recommendations span screening, BP/RAS, SGLT2/GLP-1, finerenone, dialysis, pregnancy, and referral.

Limiting factor: High relevance does not itself establish certainty.

Raise/lower: Lower if the use case is outside adult diabetes/CKD management.

External validity

Rationale: The recommendations cover broad CKD severity and clinically common risk strata.

Evidence basis: Trials include eGFR down to 20–25 and substantial albuminuria; the chapter addresses dialysis and pregnancy separately.

Limiting factor: Some trials enriched for albuminuria, cardiovascular disease, or selected populations; dialysis evidence is less mature.

Implementation value

Rationale: The chapter supplies explicit thresholds, monitoring intervals, drug sequencing, referral triggers, and a treatment workflow.

Evidence basis: Figures 11.1/11.2 and Tables 11.1–11.3 translate evidence into operational care decisions.

Limiting factor: Cross-referenced medication details and full methodology live in other Standards sections.

Raise/lower: Would fall if used without the cross-referenced sections or local medication/monitoring constraints.

Main limitations. The detailed ADA evidence-grading/development method is external to the chapter; evidence maturity varies substantially across recommendations; dialysis GLP-1 and simultaneous combination therapy are less mature than SGLT2/finerenone hard-outcome evidence; pregnancy recommendations necessarily rely on safety constraints rather than randomized efficacy trials.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Screen and stage with two axes

Use UACR and eGFR together; repeat UACR when needed to confirm persistent albuminuria and intensify monitoring as risk category rises.

Do not stop RAS therapy reflexively

Titrate ACE inhibitor/ARB to tolerated evidence-based doses and distinguish expected hemodynamic creatinine changes from true AKI.

Layer kidney-protective therapy

For T2D + CKD, prioritize SGLT2 inhibition from eGFR 20, add GLP-1 therapy when appropriate, and use finerenone for eligible albuminuric CKD with potassium monitoring.

Recognize lower-certainty edges

Dialysis GLP-1, pregnancy transitions, and early combination therapy require careful attention to evidence grade, kidney clearance, safety, and individualized context.

Refer before crisis

Rapid marker deterioration, eGFR <30, uncertain etiology, difficult CKD complications, or replacement-therapy planning are explicit nephrology triggers.
