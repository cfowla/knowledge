One-page synthesis

Core update. ESC added strong SGLT2 inhibitor recommendations for HFmrEF and HFpEF, formalized rapid pre-discharge and early post-discharge optimization after acute heart failure, added SGLT2 inhibitor and finerenone recommendations for patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, and strengthened intravenous iron guidance for HFrEF and HFmrEF with iron deficiency.

The chronic heart-failure change is driven by EMPEROR-Preserved and DELIVER. Both trials met their composite primary endpoints, but the guideline repeatedly states that the main benefit was fewer heart-failure events rather than lower cardiovascular mortality. That distinction matters because the recommendation wording names the composite outcome.

The acute-heart-failure update is more selective. ADVOR improved decongestion and CLOROTIC increased weight loss, but neither produced a guideline recommendation because clinical outcome and safety evidence remained insufficient. EMPULSE supports starting empagliflozin after stabilization. STRONG-HF produced the new Class I, Level B recommendation for rapid treatment optimization and close follow-up, but the source also lists three limits on generalizability and interpretation.

For cardiorenal prevention, the update combines chronic-kidney-disease trials and heart-failure evidence. The SGLT2 recommendation is strongest in T2DM plus CKD populations resembling trial entry criteria. Finerenone is recommended to prevent heart-failure hospitalization, with the signal supported most clearly by FIGARO-DKD and the pooled FIDELITY analysis. Hyperkalaemia remains a treatment-specific safety issue.

For iron deficiency, the update separates symptom and quality-of-life benefit from hospitalization prevention. Intravenous iron is Class I, Level A for symptoms and quality of life. Ferric carboxymaltose or ferric derisomaltose is Class IIa, Level A for hospitalization reduction. The source does not show a mortality benefit.

Acute heart failure

  • ADVOR. Acetazolamide improved successful decongestion, 42.2% versus 30.5%, but did not improve HF rehospitalization or all-cause death. ESC did not issue a recommendation and asked for more outcome and safety data.
  • CLOROTIC. Hydrochlorothiazide increased weight loss but not dyspnoea, increased creatinine rises, and did not improve rehospitalization, mortality, or length of stay. No recommendation was made.
  • EMPULSE. Empagliflozin after clinical stabilization improved a 90-day hierarchical clinical-benefit endpoint, win ratio 1.36. Adverse-event rates were similar.
  • COACH. The care strategy reduced all-cause death or cardiovascular hospitalization, HR 0.88. The Task Force requested multinational confirmation before recommending the approach.
  • STRONG-HF. High-intensity care reduced HF readmission or all-cause death at 180 days, 15.2% versus 23.3%, adjusted RR 0.66. The mortality component alone was not reduced.
STRONG-HF generalizability. The trial selected patients using NT-proBNP criteria and an in-hospital decline, the usual-care group often received less than half of full target doses of key neurohormonal therapies, and SGLT2 inhibitors were not mandated because the trial began before current evidence.

CKD and type 2 diabetes

DAPA-CKD supports the SGLT2 recommendation with a reduction in HF hospitalization or CV death, HR 0.71. EMPA-KIDNEY showed a kidney and cardiovascular primary benefit but did not significantly reduce HF hospitalization or CV death, HR 0.84. The guideline uses the wider trial and meta-analysis context to recommend SGLT2 inhibitors for T2DM plus CKD populations matching the evidence base.

For finerenone, FIDELIO-DKD improved the kidney endpoint without a significant HF-hospitalization-alone signal. FIGARO-DKD reduced the cardiovascular composite, driven by fewer HF hospitalizations. FIDELITY then supported both the cardiovascular composite and HF hospitalization alone. The guideline recommends finerenone to reduce HF hospitalization in T2DM plus CKD. Hyperkalaemia occurred more often with finerenone in both component trials.

Recommendation Table 4 gives detailed trial-specific CKD entry definitions. Applying the recommendation outside those kidney-function and albuminuria ranges requires separate clinical judgment and current product or regulatory guidance.

Iron deficiency

IRONMAN did not meet its primary composite endpoint at conventional statistical significance, RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.66 to 1.02, P=0.070. The update therefore relies on the full trial set and meta-analyses rather than one positive mortality trial. In the Graham meta-analysis of 10 trials and 3373 patients, intravenous iron reduced total HF hospitalizations plus cardiovascular death and first HF hospitalization plus cardiovascular death, but did not reduce cardiovascular or all-cause mortality.

The source defines iron deficiency for this evidence as transferrin saturation below 20% or ferritin below 100 micrograms per liter. Recommendation Table 5 notes that most evidence applies to LVEF 45% or less.

Methods and governance

The update considered new evidence through 31 March 2023. Major randomized trials and meta-analyses were discussed and voted on. All approved recommendations reached at least 75% agreement. Members with declared interests in a topic were asked to abstain from voting on that topic.

The update is selective by design. Only findings expected to produce a new or changed Class I or IIa recommendation entered the recommendation tables, and trials primarily belonging to other ESC guideline domains could be deferred. That makes this document an update to the 2021 guideline, not a complete replacement.

The main paper states that the Task Force was funded entirely by ESC without healthcare-industry involvement. A separate 87-page declaration report records yearly expert declarations during development through 2022 and states that declarations were assessed before work and during development under ESC policy.

Appraisal

What the source does well

  • It states the evidence cutoff, voting process, and selection boundary for a focused update.
  • It separates recommendation class from level of evidence and ties changed recommendations to named trials and meta-analyses.
  • It repeatedly protects against a common composite-endpoint error by stating that a positive composite does not mean each component improved.
  • It preserves clinically important negative evidence. ADVOR, CLOROTIC, COACH, mortality components, and IRONMAN are not rewritten as uniformly positive findings.
  • The supporting evidence tables expose key populations, interventions, outcomes, effect estimates, and trial-level conclusions for the evidence families that drove the update.

What limits confidence or transfer

  • This is a focused update. It deliberately omits questions that did not trigger new or changed Class I or IIa guidance and therefore cannot stand alone as a full heart-failure management guideline.
  • Several strong recommendations use composites whose observed benefit is largely hospitalization reduction rather than mortality reduction.
  • STRONG-HF is based on a selected population and a usual-care comparator with low attainment of target doses. The protocol also predates mandated SGLT2 therapy.
  • Cardiorenal recommendations are anchored to trial-specific eGFR and albuminuria ranges. The evidence is not equally direct for every CKD phenotype.
  • The intravenous-iron evidence supports symptoms, quality of life, and hospitalization reduction. Mortality benefit is not established in the evidence summarized here.
Overall appraisal. The Focused Update is strong as a targeted change document. Its most practice-changing claims are well linked to randomized evidence and meta-analysis, and the authors are unusually explicit about composite endpoints and neutral mortality components. Its main limitation is the same feature that makes it usable: it is selective. Clinical use still requires the 2021 ESC heart-failure guideline and current regulatory, formulary, and patient-specific context.
