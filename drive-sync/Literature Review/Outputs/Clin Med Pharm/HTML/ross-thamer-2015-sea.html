One-page synthesis

Core finding: splitting the same total daily empagliflozin dose into twice-daily administration produced HbA1c lowering non-inferior to once-daily dosing at 16 weeks. The 12.5 mg BID vs 25 mg QD difference was -0.11 percentage points (95% CI -0.26 to 0.03), and the 5 mg BID vs 10 mg QD difference was -0.02 (95% CI -0.16 to 0.13); both passed the 0.35% non-inferiority margin.

All empagliflozin regimens reduced HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, weight, and systolic blood pressure versus placebo. HbA1c responder odds were also higher. Sensitivity MMRM analyses were directionally and quantitatively consistent with the primary LOCF ANCOVA.

Safety over 16 weeks was broadly similar across groups. Overall adverse-event proportions ranged from 41.7% to 50.0%; serious events were uncommon and no deaths occurred. UTI and genital-infection category rates were somewhat higher with empagliflozin than placebo but were mostly mild/moderate; confirmed hypoglycaemia was rare. The practical evidence is strongest for dosing-frequency equivalence at matched total daily empagliflozin dose, not for class interchangeability.

Section condensation

Introduction

The rationale was formulation-driven: metformin IR is commonly administered twice daily, so a fixed-dose combination would expose empagliflozin to BID administration. Prior healthy-volunteer PK/PD work suggested no relevant difference between BID and QD schedules, motivating a clinical efficacy/safety test.

Methods

After a 2-week placebo run-in, patients were randomized 2:2:2:2:1 to empagliflozin 12.5 mg BID, 25 mg QD, 5 mg BID, 10 mg QD, or placebo for 16 weeks on stable metformin IR. Randomization was stratified by region, HbA1c, and eGFR. The primary endpoint was week-16 HbA1c change; FPG was secondary, with weight and BP among exploratory endpoints. The prespecified non-inferiority margin was 0.35 percentage points.

Primary ANCOVA used treatment, region, and eGFR fixed effects with baseline HbA1c covariate; post-rescue values were censored and LOCF used for missingness. Hochberg controlled the one-sided type I error for the two primary NI tests. MMRM sensitivity analyses used observed cases with and without post-rescue values.

Results

Of 1,626 screened, 983 were randomized/treated and 965 entered the full analysis set. Baseline characteristics were balanced. Both matched-total-dose BID-vs-QD comparisons met non-inferiority, and post hoc reverse-direction tests also met the margin. All active arms were superior to placebo for HbA1c and FPG; weight and SBP also improved across all active regimens.

The MMRM sensitivity analyses retained the same conclusion: 12.5 mg BID vs 25 mg QD differences were -0.12 (95% CI -0.27 to 0.03) excluding post-rescue observations and -0.13 (-0.28 to 0.02) including them; 5 mg BID vs 10 mg QD differences were -0.02 (-0.17 to 0.13) and -0.04 (-0.19 to 0.11), respectively.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpreted the matched total daily doses as therapeutically equivalent for HbA1c lowering and proposed that empagliflozin could therefore be paired with BID metformin IR in a fixed-dose combination. This is supported for short-term glycemic effect, but the study does not test clinical-outcome equivalence or cross-drug interchangeability.

Key appraisal constraints: 16-week duration; surrogate glycemic endpoints; LOCF in the primary analysis; recent cardiovascular events and CrCl <60 mL/min excluded; manufacturer funding and substantial sponsor employment among authors. None of these invalidate the narrow NI result, but they constrain broader clinical interpretation.

Implementation / practice takeaways

  • Matched total daily empagliflozin doses can be split BID without a clinically meaningful loss of HbA1c efficacy over 16 weeks under the tested conditions.
  • The evidence supports fixed-dose-combination formulation logic with metformin IR; it does not establish that dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are interchangeable.
  • For formulary class-effect arguments, weight this as supportive pharmacologic/dosing evidence and pair it with outcome trials, direct/indirect comparative evidence, guideline statements, renal-function data, and inpatient-specific evidence.
  • Safety differences were small over 16 weeks, but the trial is underpowered for uncommon adverse events and too short for long-term safety inference.
