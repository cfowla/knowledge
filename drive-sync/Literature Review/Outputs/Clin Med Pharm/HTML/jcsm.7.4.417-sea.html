One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Feldman argues that the sodium oxybate risk-management program exposed a surveillance failure: despite a centralized pharmacy intended to collect adverse-event information, an SEC filing disclosed 74 additional patient deaths that had not been reported to the FDA. The letter contrasts that reporting gap with an earlier post-marketing safety publication that had reported 30 fatalities from 2002 through early 2008, most judged unrelated to the drug. Feldman explicitly states that the causal relationship of the 74 additional deaths to sodium oxybate was unresolved.

Why it matters. The letter uses the episode to criticize passive physician-dependent pharmacovigilance. It cites prior studies reporting physician adverse-event underreporting of 86% to 96%, lists attitudinal barriers to reporting, and argues that mandated risk-management programs should use independent third-party surveillance and prospective active surveillance rather than relying primarily on passive reporting. It also urges caution when sodium oxybate is used in patients with obesity or other risk factors for sleep-disordered breathing.

Main limitation. The source provides no denominator, patient-level data, exposure details, mortality-rate calculation, causal adjudication, or original methods. The most consequential quantitative claims come from cited materials rather than data generated by the letter. The author also disclosed investigator, consulting, and speakers-bureau relationships with Jazz Pharmaceuticals.

Section condensation

Risk-management program and prior mortality report

The letter states that FDA approval conditions required the sodium oxybate manufacturer to maintain a risk-management program that used a single central pharmacy to distribute the drug directly to patients and collect adverse-event information, including deaths, for FDA reporting. It then cites a prior post-marketing safety study reporting 30 fatalities among patients prescribed sodium oxybate from 2002 through early 2008; the letter notes that most were determined to be unrelated to the medication.

SEC disclosure and clinical interpretation

Feldman reports that a Jazz Pharmaceuticals SEC filing disclosed 74 additional deaths among patients who received sodium oxybate from 2003 through 2010 that had not been reported to the FDA, with the cause unspecified in most cases. The letter also reports the company's position that an adjusted annual all-cause mortality rate did not represent a new safety signal.

Feldman separates that company assessment from his own concern about the reporting and communication failure. He does not claim that the 74 deaths were caused by sodium oxybate; instead, he uses the unresolved deaths as a reason to reinforce caution in patients with obesity or other risk factors for sleep-disordered breathing.

Passive reporting limitations

The letter argues that the actual number of deaths is uncertain because passive safety systems depend on voluntary physician reporting. It cites studies indicating 86% to 96% underreporting of adverse events, including serious and fatal events, and summarizes reported reasons for non-reporting: clinicians may assume serious adverse events are already known, believe causality cannot be determined, think an individual report will not add knowledge, or believe only unusual events should be reported.

Reporting and surveillance recommendations

Feldman describes spontaneous adverse-drug-reaction reporting as a central component of FDA post-marketing surveillance and encourages reporting of known and suspected reactions to manufacturers and the FDA. He concludes that mandated risk-management programs should use independent third-party surveillance and recommends a prospective active-surveillance study for sodium oxybate rather than continued reliance on passive physician reporting.

Disclosure

The letter states that it was not industry supported. Feldman disclosed service as a principal investigator, consultant, and speakers-bureau member for Jazz Pharmaceuticals.
