One-page synthesis

Core finding. In 1490 completed pregnancies among women with IBD, biologic, thiopurine, and combination exposure was not associated with a clear increase in congenital malformations, spontaneous abortion, preterm birth, low birth weight, or infant infection through the first year. Active disease, not drug exposure, was strongly associated with spontaneous abortion. Preterm birth was associated with infant infection.

The cohort included 379 unexposed pregnancies, 242 thiopurine exposures, 642 biologic exposures, and 227 combination exposures. Adjusted pregnancy-outcome estimates were generally compatible with no major excess risk. The combination group had a higher adjusted odds of cesarean delivery, OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.1 to 2.5, but cesarean indication was influenced by IBD factors such as perianal disease and active disease. Any first-year infant infection occurred in 46% of live births and serious infection in 6%; adjusted infection odds did not increase with biologic, thiopurine, or combination exposure.

The placental-transfer subset showed expected drug-specific differences. Infliximab had the highest median infant concentration at delivery, while certolizumab pegol had negligible transfer. Birth drug concentration was not associated with infant infection in the supplemental analysis. ASQ3 developmental testing showed no consistent adverse pattern through 48 months. Isolated p-values at 12 and 24 months should not be over-read because many domains and time points were tested.

Main evidence limits. The study is observational. Disease activity was largely self-reported, later enrollment preferentially targeted therapies of interest, 202 women contributed a later pregnancy analyzed as a separate pair, and post hoc power shows the cohort could miss smaller increases in rare outcomes. Several newer-biologic groups were very small.

Source summary

Background and clinical question

Pregnancy commonly overlaps with the peak incidence years for Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis. Active IBD increases pregnancy risk, but fetal exposure and placental transfer created concern about continuing immunomodulators and biologics. The study asked whether biologic, thiopurine, or combination exposure changed major pregnancy and infant outcomes.

The clinical tension is confounding by disease control. Stopping therapy may reduce drug exposure while increasing IBD activity. The study therefore measured both medication exposure and disease activity and included the unexposed IBD group as the main comparator.

Methods

PIANO enrolled pregnant women with IBD and singleton pregnancies at 30 US centers from 2007 through 2019. Exposure meant biologic or thiopurine use during the 3 months before the last menstrual period or during pregnancy. The four groups were unexposed, thiopurine, biologic, and combination therapy. Outcomes included congenital malformations, spontaneous abortion, preterm birth, low birth weight, infant infection, growth, ASQ3 development, and placental drug transfer.

Regression models adjusted for relevant confounders. Spontaneous abortion used Cox models among women enrolled before 20 weeks. Infection models accounted for preterm birth, maternal age, and disease activity. The supplement documents attrition, repeated pregnancies, post hoc power, serum assay details, and sensitivity analyses. It also states that assays for non-infliximab and non-adalimumab agents had not all completed validation at the time of the plan.

Pregnancy outcomes

The study reports 133 congenital malformations, 42 spontaneous abortions, 91 low-birth-weight infants, and 132 preterm births. In Table 2, adjusted odds ratios for biologic, thiopurine, and combination exposure were not clearly elevated for spontaneous abortion, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, low birth weight, growth restriction, NICU admission, or congenital malformations. Combination therapy had an adjusted OR of 1.7, 95% CI 1.1 to 2.5, for cesarean delivery.

Among 944 pregnancies enrolled before 20 weeks, active disease had an HR of 3.41, 95% CI 1.51 to 7.69, for spontaneous abortion, and prior spontaneous abortion had an HR of 2.17, 95% CI 1.05 to 4.49. Medication class was not predictive in that model. Post hoc power suggests the study could detect only fairly large relative risks for several rare outcomes, including 1.64 for congenital malformations and 2.30 for spontaneous abortion.

Infant infection

Any infection during the first year occurred in 658 of 1431 live births, 46%, and serious infection in 79, 6%. Adjusted ORs for any infection were 0.92 for biologic exposure, 0.90 for thiopurine exposure, and 0.93 for combination exposure. Preterm birth was the only independent infection risk factor reported, OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.19 to 2.51.

Sensitivity analyses excluding otitis media, restricting biologic exposure, and examining individual biologics did not show a clear treatment-related infection signal. Supplemental Table S11 also found no association between birth serum drug concentration category and serious or any first-year infection.

Placental transfer

Serum measurements showed substantial transplacental transfer for most monoclonal antibodies. Median infant concentrations were 27.1 micrograms/mL for infliximab, 9.4 for adalimumab, 8.2 for vedolizumab, 5.0 for ustekinumab, 3.8 for natalizumab, 2.2 for golimumab, and 0 for certolizumab pegol. Median infant-or-cord to maternal ratios ranged from 0 for certolizumab to 2.4 for infliximab.

At the first later infant measurement, collected a median of 129.5 days after birth, 86.8% had concentrations below 2 micrograms/mL. The source contains several serum-table inconsistencies, including n=235 in main Table 4 versus n=234 in supplemental serum tables and an impossible natalizumab count in one Table 4 row. Those cells should not be reused without checking the original table.

Development and growth

At 12 months, ASQ3 scores did not show an adverse exposure pattern. The biologic group had a 3.5-point higher personal-social score versus unexposed infants, p=0.01, while other domains were not statistically different. At 24 months, the supplement shows two isolated findings, a -3.8-point fine-motor difference for combination exposure and a +2.2-point gross-motor difference for biologic exposure. No domain differences were statistically significant at 36 or 48 months.

Growth analyses likewise lacked a consistent adverse pattern. Thiopurine exposure was associated with higher mean birth-weight percentile. Adjusted analyses contained isolated significant comparisons for low weight or height at specific ages, but the direction and timing were not consistent enough to establish a coherent growth effect.

Disease activity and interpretation

Flares were more frequent in the unexposed group at several pregnancy and early postpartum time points. Exposure to more than one biologic was also associated with more pregnancy flare, which likely reflects a harder-to-control disease subgroup rather than a protective effect of medication assignment. The authors place disease control at the center of the risk-benefit interpretation.

The source conclusion supports continuing biologic and thiopurine therapy during pregnancy rather than stopping solely because of fetal exposure. That conclusion is plausible from this cohort but remains an observational inference. Current clinical use should check contemporary guidance and drug-specific evidence, especially for agents with small PIANO samples.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a high-value pregnancy-safety cohort for IBD therapy. Use it to estimate whether large safety signals are present and to understand the risk of uncontrolled disease. Do not use it as proof that every drug has zero fetal or infant risk.
Relevance: 9/10

Directly addresses medication safety during pregnancy in IBD.

Evidence: Large prospective IBD pregnancy cohort with maternal and infant outcomes.
Main limit: Does not address every individual drug with equal precision.
Would raise: More drug-specific prospective data would raise the score.

Novelty: 8/10

At publication, the cohort materially expanded prospective biologic pregnancy safety data.

Evidence: 1490 completed pregnancies and 869 biologic or combination exposures.
Main limit: Several biologic subgroups remained small.
Would raise: Larger samples for newer agents would raise the score.

Method strength: 7/10

Prospective multicenter follow-up and adjusted analyses are strong for an observational registry.

Evidence: 30 centers, prespecified outcomes, regression and sensitivity analyses.
Main limit: No randomization, self-reported disease activity, repeated pregnancies treated as independent pairs.
Would raise: Objective disease markers and models accounting for within-mother clustering would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 7/10

The data support absence of a large safety signal but cannot exclude smaller increases in rare outcomes.

Evidence: Adjusted estimates for major outcomes mostly span the null; post hoc power quantifies detectable risk.
Main limit: Rare outcomes and small subgroups produce wide confidence intervals.
Would raise: More events and pooled prospective replication would raise the score.

External validity: 7/10

Thirty US centers improve geographic breadth within specialty IBD care.

Evidence: Large multicenter cohort with Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis.
Main limit: The cohort was relatively affluent and highly educated.
Would raise: More diverse settings and populations would raise the score.

Implementation value: 8/10

The study gives a practical evidence base against routine discontinuation solely because of pregnancy.

Evidence: Exposure, disease activity, infant infection, placental transfer, and development were all examined.
Main limit: This 2021 study is not a current guideline and no external practice verification was performed in this run.
Would raise: Current guideline concordance and newer-biologic data would raise the score.

Validity

Strengths include prospective enrollment, multicenter follow-up, a large exposed cohort, prespecified pregnancy and infant outcomes, adjustment for disease activity, drug-level measurements, and sensitivity analyses. The main threats are residual confounding, self-reported activity, attrition, potential selection changes during enrollment, small drug-specific strata, and treating repeat pregnancies as independent observations.

Practice translation

The source supports maintaining effective IBD control during pregnancy and argues against routine discontinuation of biologics or thiopurines solely because of pregnancy. The data are strongest for the class-level question and older anti-TNF agents. They are less precise for rare outcomes and less common biologics. No current guideline or regulatory verification was performed in this production run.

Source-integrity notes

  • Main Table 4 reports n=235 for serum concentrations, while supplemental serum tables use n=234.
  • Main Methods says follow-up serum was obtained when infant drug was detectable; the supplement specifies a threshold above 2 micrograms/mL.
  • Main Table 4 lists natalizumab n=4 but one row displays 9/22, an internally impossible count for that column. This cell was not used in the extracted quantitative atoms.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use now

Use the study as class-level prospective safety evidence when discussing continuation of biologics or thiopurines in pregnancy, alongside current guidance and patient-specific disease control.

Do not overclaim

Null class-level estimates do not prove zero risk, especially for rare congenital outcomes or individual newer biologics with small samples.

Monitor the disease

Active IBD carried a strong spontaneous-abortion association in this cohort. Disease activity is part of the exposure-risk equation, not a background variable.

Interpret serum transfer by drug

Placental transfer differed sharply by biologic. Serum presence alone did not predict first-year infection in the available subset.
