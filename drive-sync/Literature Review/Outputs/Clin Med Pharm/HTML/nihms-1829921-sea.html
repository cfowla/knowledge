One-page synthesis

Bottom line. The randomized ECHELON-2 cohort retained an overall PFS and OS advantage for A+CHP versus CHOP at 5 years. The strongest and most stable disease-subtype signal is in systemic anaplastic large cell lymphoma. Non-ALCL estimates are much less certain, and the trial was not powered for comparisons within those histologies.

At 5 years, PFS favored A+CHP with HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.91, and estimated 5-year PFS of 51.4% versus 43.0%. OS favored A+CHP with HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.99, and estimated 5-year OS of 70.1% versus 61.0%. In sALCL, the PFS HR was 0.55 and the 5-year PFS rates were 60.6% and 48.4%. The OS HR in sALCL was 0.66 with a 95% CI that crossed 1.0. The AITL 5-year PFS point estimate was lower with A+CHP than CHOP, 26.6% versus 48.1%, and the AITL forest-plot PFS HR was 1.41 with a wide 95% CI. PTCL-NOS estimates were imprecise and near neutral for PFS.

The CD30 analysis does not show a clear response gradient within the trial's eligible range. Complete remission rates were similar above versus at or below the subtype median in AITL and PTCL-NOS, but the analysis was post hoc and underpowered. Because eligibility required CD30 of at least 10%, ECHELON-2 cannot establish efficacy below that threshold. Subsequent therapy was heterogeneous. Fewer A+CHP patients received later brentuximab vedotin than CHOP patients, and descriptive retreatment ORR was 59% versus 50%, but those analyses are small and nonrandomized.

Interpretation boundary. This is long-term evidence from the randomized trial, not a new prespecified confirmatory analysis. The 5-year update was exploratory, investigator-assessed after central review had closed, and its P values were nominal without multiplicity adjustment.
Source-integrity finding. The CHOP neuropathy severity paragraph reports 30 of 42 as 64%, 11 of 42 as 23%, and 1 of 42 as 2%. The counts sum to 42, but the printed percentages do not match the counts and do not sum to 100%. This appraisal preserves the source values and does not substitute calculated percentages.

Methods and validity

Design and participants

ECHELON-2 randomized adults with previously untreated CD30-positive PTCL to A+CHP or CHOP. Eligibility required ECOG performance status at most 2 and CD30 staining in at least 10% of neoplastic cells by local review. Eligible histologies included ALK-positive and ALK-negative sALCL, PTCL-NOS, AITL, adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma, enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma. ALK-positive sALCL required IPI at least 2. The study intentionally targeted 75% plus or minus 5% sALCL for European and Canadian regulatory requirements.

Patients received six or eight 21-day cycles. Consolidative SCT or radiotherapy was allowed at investigator discretion, with transplant intent set before cycle 1. The trial enrolled 452 patients across 17 countries, 226 per arm. Seventy percent had sALCL, 78% had IPI at least 2, and 80% had stage III or IV disease.

Endpoints and statistics

The primary PFS endpoint was originally assessed by blinded independent central review. For this update, investigators assessed PFS after the central reader closed. PFS included relapse or progression, death from any cause, or subsequent systemic chemotherapy for residual or progressive PTCL. OS, sALCL PFS, CR, and ORR were key alpha-controlled secondary endpoints in the original program. The update also examined post-frontline therapy, CD30 response relationships, and neuropathy resolution.

The cutoff was 5 November 2020. Kaplan-Meier methods estimated PFS, and stratified Cox regression produced A+CHP versus CHOP HRs. The prespecified strata were ALK-positive sALCL versus other histologies and IPI 0-1 versus 2-3 versus 4-5. Efficacy used ITT; safety used treated patients. The source explicitly calls the 5-year analysis exploratory and not prespecified and states that P values are nominal and not multiplicity adjusted.

Clinical trial appraisal

P: Adults with previously untreated CD30-positive PTCL, CD30 >=10%, ECOG <=2. Trial enrollment was enriched for sALCL.
I: A+CHP, six or eight 21-day cycles.
C: CHOP, six or eight 21-day cycles.
O: PFS, OS, CR/ORR, disease-subtype outcomes, subsequent therapy, CD30-response exploration, peripheral neuropathy, secondary malignancy.
Validity strengths: randomized double-blind phase III design, balanced groups, ITT efficacy analysis, long follow-up, survival endpoints.

CD30 exploratory findings

Most AITL patients with evaluable CD30 expression, 26 of 29, were between 10% and 30%; the median was 18%. PTCL-NOS ranged from 10% to 100%, with median 25%. In AITL treated with A+CHP, CR was 57% above the median and 53% at or below it, P=0.84. In PTCL-NOS, CR was 57% above the median and 71% at or below it, P=0.44. The source reports no apparent correlation between CD30 level and CR within these subgroups.

Transplant and subsequent therapy

Consolidative SCT was used in 22% of A+CHP patients and 17% of CHOP patients. Overall SCT use was 30% and 28%. Subsequent anticancer therapy was more common after CHOP, 45%, than after A+CHP, 31%. Common subsequent regimens included brentuximab vedotin monotherapy, 11% versus 21%, and platinum-based chemotherapy, 9% versus 19%. The first subsequent therapy produced ORR of 42% in both arms, with CR of 27% after A+CHP and 19% after CHOP.

After progression or relapse, 29 A+CHP patients and 54 CHOP patients received systemic brentuximab vedotin. ORR was 59% after A+CHP and 50% after CHOP. Median time to brentuximab vedotin treatment was 15.0 versus 8.2 months. These response analyses are descriptive and subject to selection, treatment-timing, and investigator-assessment effects.

Safety and follow-up

Peripheral neuropathy occurred in 52% with A+CHP and 55% with CHOP. At last follow-up, ongoing neuropathy remained in 21% and 19%. Among affected patients, 72% in A+CHP and 78% in CHOP had improved or resolved neuropathy. Outcomes for grade 2 or 3 neuropathy were confounded in 19 of 29 patients by death, withdrawal, or loss to follow-up. Secondary malignancies occurred in 6 A+CHP and 8 CHOP patients.

The source's CHOP grade distribution contains the percentage inconsistency described above. Counts were preserved; no recalculated values replace the source's printed percentages.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is the long-term randomized evidence for A+CHP versus CHOP in trial-eligible CD30-positive PTCL. Its strongest use is the overall and sALCL survival evidence. It should not be used as definitive evidence for individual non-ALCL histologies or CD30 below 10%.
Relevance: 9/10

Direct long-term comparative evidence for frontline PTCL treatment.

Evidence basis. Randomized ECHELON-2 cohort with 5-year PFS, OS, safety, and subsequent-therapy follow-up.

Limiting factor. The source is a 2022 follow-up and current 2026 practice was not externally verified.

Would change the score. Current guideline concordance and newer comparative evidence could raise or lower practical relevance.

Novelty: 8/10

The update adds mature survival, neuropathy, CD30, transplant, and retreatment information beyond the primary analysis.

Evidence basis. Five-year PFS and OS plus extended follow-up analyses.

Limiting factor. The central treatment comparison was already established in the original phase III report.

Would change the score. A prespecified mature analysis with new externally validated subtype findings would raise the score.

Method strength: 8/10

The underlying trial is randomized, double-blind, multicountry, and phase III.

Evidence basis. 1:1 randomization, masking, ITT efficacy analysis, survival endpoints, long follow-up.

Limiting factor. The 5-year update itself was exploratory, investigator-assessed for PFS after central review closed, and used nominal P values.

Would change the score. Prespecified long-term analysis with central endpoint review and multiplicity control would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 8/10

Overall PFS and OS estimates directly support a long-term A+CHP advantage in the randomized population.

Evidence basis. PFS HR 0.70 and OS HR 0.72 with 5-year absolute survival estimates.

Limiting factor. Rare histology analyses are underpowered and several subgroup confidence intervals are wide.

Would change the score. Consistent independent long-term trials or robust subtype-specific randomized data would raise the score.

External validity: 6/10

The multicountry population helps generalizability, but the cohort was intentionally enriched for sALCL and required CD30 at least 10%.

Evidence basis. 17 countries, broad eligible PTCL histologies, but 70% sALCL and regulatory enrollment targeting.

Limiting factor. No efficacy evidence for CD30 below 10%; low-count rare histologies limit subtype inference.

Would change the score. Prospective evidence in non-sALCL disease and CD30-low populations would raise the score.

Implementation value: 8/10

The article gives durable comparative efficacy, neuropathy, transplant, and subsequent-therapy data that can inform evidence review.

Evidence basis. Long-term efficacy and safety plus exact downstream treatment patterns.

Limiting factor. Current guideline and regulatory status were not verified in this run, and the report is not a stand-alone 2026 treatment directive.

Would change the score. Current-practice verification and linkage to contemporary guidelines would raise the score.

Independent appraisal

  • The randomized design strongly supports the overall treatment comparison.
  • The trial's sALCL enrichment matters. The overall result should not be treated as equally precise across all PTCL histologies.
  • The AITL PFS point estimate runs in the opposite direction from the overall effect, but its interval is wide and the study was not powered to settle AITL treatment effect.
  • The CD30 analysis is useful for hypothesis generation within the observed 10% to 100% range. It does not establish a threshold-independent effect.
  • Industry funding and sponsor-employed authors warrant attention. The randomized blinded design, predefined endpoints in the parent trial, and transparent reporting reduce but do not eliminate sponsorship concerns.

Practice and implementation takeaways

Use this source for

Long-term randomized PFS and OS evidence for A+CHP versus CHOP in trial-eligible CD30-positive PTCL, especially sALCL.

Do not use this source for

Definitive treatment-effect claims in individual non-ALCL histologies, efficacy below 10% CD30, or a stand-alone statement of current 2026 standard of care.
