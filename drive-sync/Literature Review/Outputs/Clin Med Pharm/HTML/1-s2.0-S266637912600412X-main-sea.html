One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Baseline QoL-B-RSS symptom burden may help characterize which treatment domain improves most in bronchiectasis: florensocatib consistently reduced exacerbations without meaningful symptom improvement, whereas inhaled tobramycin produced larger symptom gains, particularly in patients starting with higher symptom burden. The stratification claim remains hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

The post hoc analysis combines SAVE-BE, a phase 2 florensocatib trial, and TORNASOL, a phase 3 inhaled tobramycin trial. Patients were categorized using baseline QoL-B-RSS <60 (high symptom burden) versus ≥60 (low burden), with continuous analyses also performed. In SAVE-BE, pooled florensocatib reduced annualized exacerbation frequency versus placebo (RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.33–0.67), with similar statistically significant effects in low-burden (RR 0.40) and high-burden (RR 0.53) groups. Time to first exacerbation and exacerbation-free status also favored florensocatib in both groups, while week-24 QoL-B-RSS did not significantly improve in either subgroup.

In TORNASOL, tobramycin improved QoL-B-RSS at day 29 by 10.56 points in the high-burden subgroup and 6.03 points in the low-burden subgroup, with benefit sustained to day 85. At day 29, 63.5% versus 23.8% of high-burden patients and 34.8% versus 4.6% of low-burden patients achieved the ≥8-point MCID with tobramycin versus placebo. High-burden patients also had significant improvements in congestion, cough, sputum production, and purulence. However, confidence intervals between symptom-burden groups overlapped substantially, formal interaction tests did not establish a clear subgroup difference, and TORNASOL required Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection. The paper therefore supports a plausible phenotype-treatment hypothesis, not a validated clinical decision threshold.

Section condensation

Introduction

Bronchiectasis management aims to reduce both exacerbations and chronic respiratory symptoms. The paper frames symptom burden as a candidate pragmatic marker because bacterial load and inflammatory biomarkers are less routinely available, while QoL-B-RSS is validated and has been associated with exacerbation risk and response to some therapies.

The authors contrast two mechanistic treatment classes: DPP-1 inhibition targeting neutrophil serine protease activation and inhaled antibiotics targeting chronic airway infection. The stated question is whether baseline symptom burden identifies differential response to florensocatib versus inhaled tobramycin.

Methods

SAVE-BE included 224 adults with HRCT-confirmed clinically significant bronchiectasis, ≥2 exacerbations in the prior year, BMI ≥18 kg/m², and daily spontaneous sputum. Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to 20 mg florensocatib, 40 mg florensocatib, or placebo; the two active doses were pooled in the post hoc analysis. TORNASOL randomized 357 adults aged 18–75 years with symptomatic HRCT-confirmed bronchiectasis, P. aeruginosa isolation, and ≥1 exacerbation in the prior 2 years to nebulized tobramycin 300 mg twice daily or saline placebo in two 28-day on/off cycles.

QoL-B-RSS ranges 0–100, with lower scores representing worse symptoms and an 8-point MCID. High burden was defined as <60 and low burden as ≥60. SAVE-BE used GAM/negative-binomial modeling for exacerbation frequency, Cox modeling for time to first exacerbation, and ANCOVA for continuous outcomes. TORNASOL used GAM and mixed-effects models with Bonferroni correction. No formal sample-size calculation was performed for these post hoc analyses.

Results — SAVE-BE

Florensocatib reduced annualized exacerbation frequency overall (RR 0.47, 95% CI 0.33–0.67; p<0.0001), in low-burden patients (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.24–0.67), and in high-burden patients (RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.88). There was no significant modification by FEV1% predicted or prior exacerbation history. Exacerbation-free status and time to first exacerbation favored florensocatib in both burden groups.

At week 24, florensocatib reduced sputum weight in both burden groups and reduced sputum purulence significantly in the low-burden subgroup, but FEV1% predicted did not materially change. QoL-B-RSS did not significantly improve in either subgroup (low: +4.53 points vs placebo, p=0.0598; high: −2.75, p=0.3770), and individual symptom domains generally did not differ.

Results — TORNASOL

Tobramycin showed greater symptomatic improvement as baseline QoL-B-RSS decreased. At day 29, adjusted QoL-B-RSS mean differences versus placebo were 10.56 points (95% CI 5.19–15.92) in high-burden patients and 6.03 (2.39–9.67) in low-burden patients. At day 85 the differences were 8.13 and 6.03 points, respectively. BSI did not significantly modify the association (interaction p=0.40).

At day 29, MCID response occurred in 63.5% versus 23.8% of high-burden patients and 34.8% versus 4.6% of low-burden patients with tobramycin versus placebo. High-burden patients had significant gains in chest congestion, daily cough, sputum production, and sputum purulence; low-burden patients had significant gains in daily cough, sputum production, and purulence.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the findings as evidence that anti-inflammatory and anti-infective therapies may preferentially improve different disease domains. They suggest symptom burden could be a practical phenotype marker, but explicitly frame the results as hypothesis-generating and call for prospective validation.

Major constraints are the post hoc design; different parent-trial populations, interventions, follow-up schedules, and endpoints; binary symptom cutoff; potential circularity from using baseline QoL-B-RSS for both stratification and symptom endpoints in TORNASOL; pooling of florensocatib doses; limited power; missing BSI/bacterial colonization data in SAVE-BE; and possible insensitivity of composite QoL-B-RSS to domain-specific anti-inflammatory effects.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Strong for understanding treatment-domain dissociation and for generating a symptom-phenotyping hypothesis; insufficient to use QoL-B-RSS <60/≥60 as a treatment-selection rule.
Relevance — 8/10Directly relevant to bronchiectasis pharmacotherapy and treatment-response stratification. Limiter: does not establish a practice-ready decision rule. Higher with prospective phenotype-guided randomization.
Novelty — 7/10Uses a simple symptom scale to compare response patterns across anti-inflammatory and anti-infective trial datasets. Limiter: post hoc reuse of existing trials. Higher with prespecified validation.
Method strength — 6/10Parent data come from blinded randomized trials with appropriate modeling and sensitivity stratification. Limiter: post hoc subgrouping, pooled florensocatib doses, no post hoc power calculation, cross-trial heterogeneity.
Evidence strength — 5/10Within-trial treatment effects are convincing, but evidence that symptom burden truly modifies treatment response is weaker than the treatment effects themselves. Limiter: overlapping subgroup CIs and nonsignificant interaction analyses; prospective validation would raise the score.
External validity — 4/10Multicenter data are useful, but both trials were conducted in China and TORNASOL required chronic P. aeruginosa infection. Higher with international validation across microbiologic phenotypes and treatment settings.
Implementation value — 5/10QoL-B-RSS is simple and interpretable, and the 60-point threshold is operationally easy. Limiter: threshold is data-derived/rounded and not prospectively validated for treatment selection.

Validity judgment

Key inferential issue: the strongest data show that florensocatib reduces exacerbations in both symptom groups and tobramycin improves symptoms in both groups. The more ambitious claim — that baseline symptom burden identifies which therapy a patient should receive — rests on post hoc numerical differences and cross-trial contrasts rather than a direct randomized treatment-by-burden interaction comparing the two therapies.

Regression-to-the-mean / circularity concern: TORNASOL stratifies by baseline QoL-B-RSS and then analyzes change in the same measure. Baseline adjustment helps but does not eliminate the interpretive limitation; the authors explicitly acknowledge this.

Practice translation

The paper supports measuring symptom burden as a clinically meaningful phenotype descriptor and reinforces that exacerbation prevention and symptom relief are distinct treatment goals. It does not support selecting florensocatib versus inhaled tobramycin solely from a QoL-B-RSS threshold. The tobramycin findings are specifically derived from a population with P. aeruginosa isolation, while SAVE-BE did not collect baseline bacterial colonization status for the interaction analyses.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Use nowTrack symptom burden separately from exacerbation history; do not assume improvement in one domain predicts improvement in the other.
Research-usefulQoL-B-RSS <60/≥60 is a reasonable candidate stratifier for prospective trials of phenotype-guided bronchiectasis therapy.
Do not overclaimDo not interpret lower symptom burden as proven indication for DPP-1 inhibition or higher burden as a standalone indication for inhaled antibiotics.
Monitoring implicationFor anti-inflammatory strategies, include exacerbation outcomes and mucus/airway measures rather than relying on composite symptom scores alone.
Population boundaryTORNASOL symptom-response evidence applies to bronchiectasis with P. aeruginosa isolation; broader microbiologic phenotypes were not established here.
Next evidence neededPrespecified, adequately powered interaction analyses or prospective stratified randomization with external validation.
