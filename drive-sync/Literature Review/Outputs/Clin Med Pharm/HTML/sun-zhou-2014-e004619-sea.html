One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The review provides a useful quantitative summary of early dapagliflozin add-on efficacy. Its main pooled estimates are internally consistent across sensitivity analyses, but body-weight publication bias, missing-data methods in the underlying trials, universal industry sponsorship of eligible RCTs, and several internal reporting conflicts limit reuse without checking the source.

Twelve randomized trials entered quantitative synthesis. The pooled 10 mg dapagliflozin comparison favored dapagliflozin for HbA1c (MD -0.52 percentage points, 95% CI -0.60 to -0.45), fasting plasma glucose (MD -1.13 mmol/L, 95% CI -1.33 to -0.93), and body weight (MD -2.10 kg, 95% CI -2.32 to -1.88). Glycaemic outcomes had moderate heterogeneity. Body weight had low heterogeneity but significant funnel-plot asymmetry; trim-and-fill yielded -1.94 kg.

The review used prospective registration, PRISMA reporting, Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, GRADE, random-effects pooling, subgroup analyses, publication-bias testing, and two sensitivity strategies. Four RCTs had multiple unclear-risk items, most trials used LOCF, and all eligible RCTs were sponsored by Bristol-Myers Squibb or AstraZeneca.

Methods

The authors searched PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, the Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov, Google, and journals. Eligible studies were adult T2DM RCTs of dapagliflozin added to conventional antidiabetic therapy versus placebo on the same background treatment. Trials had to report HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, and body weight; HbA1c analyses required follow-up longer than eight weeks.

Two or more reviewers selected studies. One reviewer extracted data and another verified extraction. The review pooled adjusted mean differences with random-effects models, used I-squared for heterogeneity, Egger tests and funnel plots for publication bias, trim-and-fill adjustment, background-drug and duration subgroups, follow-up meta-regression, and sensitivity analyses excluding higher-risk and interim-report trials.

Figures, tables, and supplement

Figure 1 - study selection

380 citations were assessed. The review excluded 139 non-RCTs, seven PK/PD studies, and eight RCTs that failed intervention/comparator criteria; 12 RCTs entered quantitative synthesis.

Figure 2 - risk of bias

Four RCTs had more than one unclear-risk item. The recurring concern was absent reporting of blinding, described as detection-bias risk.

Figure 3 - HbA1c forest plot

Pooled MD -0.52 percentage points (95% CI -0.60 to -0.45), I-squared 56%. Background-drug subgroup effects ranged from -0.69 to -0.47 percentage points; duration subgroups were -0.53 (<=24 weeks) and -0.52 (>24 weeks).

Figure 4 - funnel plots

Egger tests were not significant for HbA1c or fasting plasma glucose. Body weight showed asymmetry (t=-3.11, p=0.0091); trim-and-fill changed the pooled weight estimate from -2.10 to -1.94 kg.

Figure 5 - fasting plasma glucose forest plot

Pooled MD -1.13 mmol/L (95% CI -1.33 to -0.93), I-squared 53.8%. Drug-subgroup effects ranged from -1.47 mmol/L with pioglitazone background to -0.93 mmol/L with metformin background.

Figure 6 - body-weight forest plot

Pooled MD -2.10 kg (95% CI -2.32 to -1.88), I-squared 12%. Duration subgroups were -2.63 kg (<=24 weeks) and -1.92 kg (>24 weeks).

Main tables

Table 1 - included RCT characteristics

Summarizes 12 trials of 2.5, 5, and 10 mg dapagliflozin across five background-therapy families. The review pooled the 10 mg comparisons. Trial-level findings remain secondary reports and were not converted into primary-study atoms.

Table 2 - GRADE

Authors rate HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose as high-quality evidence and body weight as moderate because of publication bias. Table 2 prints 14 studies for HbA1c and body weight despite the Results text describing 12 RCTs for each analysis.

Table 3 - meta-regression

HbA1c showed no significant duration association. Fasting glucose and body weight are described as duration-associated, but the printed coefficients and intervals contain internal conflicts that require source adjudication before reuse.

Source-integrity findings

  • The abstract reverses the ordering of the two sensitivity-analysis result sets relative to the Results section: the abstract assigns -0.56/-1.11/-2.23 to exclusion of low-quality trials and -0.50/-1.08/-2.08 to exclusion of interim trials, whereas the Results section assigns them in the opposite way.
  • GRADE Table 2 labels the HbA1c evidence as 3,986 participants from 14 studies, while the narrative Results section states that the HbA1c analysis included 12 randomized trials.
  • GRADE Table 2 labels the body-weight evidence as 4,008 participants from 14 studies, while the narrative Results section states that the body-weight analysis included 12 randomized trials.
  • Table 3 prints a fasting-plasma-glucose week coefficient of -0.01 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.004 to 0.012, an interval that is incompatible in sign with the printed point estimate; the Results prose also describes -1.52 as the follow-up-duration coefficient although Table 3 presents -1.52 as the intercept.
  • The body-weight meta-regression is described as significant with R-squared 1 and p<0.01, while Table 3 prints a week estimate of -0.01 with 95% CI -0.02 to 0.01, which crosses zero; the source does not reconcile the difference.

External validity 5/10

The included trials covered several common background therapies and follow-up from 12 to 104 weeks.

Evidence basis
Background drugs included metformin, insulin, glimepiride, pioglitazone, and metformin/sitagliptin.
Principal limit
Only 10 mg dapagliflozin was pooled for the main comparison, all trials were industry-sponsored, and eligibility required all three outcomes.
What changes the score
Higher with broader independent trials and less restrictive outcome-based eligibility.

Implementation value 4/10

The source quantifies expected glycaemic and weight effects but does not evaluate the wider clinical outcomes needed for a complete formulary decision.

Evidence basis
Pooled effect sizes and sensitivity analyses are directly reusable for historical efficacy context.
Principal limit
No cardiorenal effectiveness, hospitalization, mortality, or inpatient implementation outcomes were review endpoints.
What changes the score
Higher if paired with evidence addressing current clinical outcomes and inpatient use.

Systematic-review validity

Strengths

Prospective registration, multi-database searching, dual eligibility review, extraction verification, random effects, Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, GRADE, publication-bias analysis, and sensitivity analyses.

Main limits

Narrow search terms, only 12 eligible trials, LOCF and post-rescue exclusions in most trials, all eligible RCTs industry-sponsored, body-weight publication bias, and source inconsistencies.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use this source for the magnitude of early add-on dapagliflozin effects on glycaemia and body weight and for identifying the underlying randomized trials. The reference task queue is more valuable for current evidence work than treating the 2014 pooled estimate as a complete formulary rationale.

Do not use this review alone to infer cardiovascular, kidney, heart-failure, mortality, or inpatient outcomes because those outcomes were outside its review question. Resolve the flagged Table 2/Table 3 inconsistencies before reusing study counts or meta-regression coefficients.
