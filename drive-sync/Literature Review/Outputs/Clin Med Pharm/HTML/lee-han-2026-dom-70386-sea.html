Efficacy and safety of combining empagliflozin in people with type 2 diabetes mellitus uncontrolled with metformin and sitagliptin: A randomised, double-blind, multicentre, therapeutic confirmatory phase 3 clinical trial

Design

26 sites, South Korea

24-week controlled period + 28-week extension
Interventions
Empagliflozin 10 mg or 25 mg added to stable metformin + sitagliptin
Primary endpoint
Change in HbA1c at week 24

Scope warning. This trial establishes short-term glycemic efficacy and tolerability for adding empagliflozin to metformin+sitagliptin in a selected Korean T2DM population. It does not establish empagliflozin–dapagliflozin equivalence, inpatient interchangeability, or cardiovascular/renal clinical-outcome superiority.

One-page synthesis

In adults with T2DM inadequately controlled on stable metformin plus sitagliptin, adding empagliflozin 10 or 25 mg produced clinically and statistically clear HbA1c lowering over 24 weeks versus placebo. Adjusted mean HbA1c differences were -0.7 percentage points (95% CI -1.0 to -0.4) with 10 mg and -0.8 (-1.1 to -0.5) with 25 mg; both p<0.0001. FPG fell by about 32 mg/dL more than placebo. Glycemic effects persisted to week 52. At week 24, HbA1c <7% was achieved by 47.4%, 43.1%, and 21.4% in the 10 mg, 25 mg, and placebo groups, respectively.

Weight loss was roughly 3 kg at week 24 with either empagliflozin dose versus 0.4 kg with placebo. UACR decreased, but only the 25 mg week-24 comparison was marked significant versus placebo; these were exploratory surrogate findings. TEAE incidence was similar or numerically lower with empagliflozin than placebo, and no diabetic ketoacidosis occurred. The controlled evidence is strongest for glycemic efficacy; claims of “renal protection” and multifaceted superiority should be interpreted more cautiously because UACR/metabolic endpoints were exploratory, secondary/exploratory testing was not multiplicity-adjusted, and hard cardiorenal outcomes were not studied.

Methods and validity

Population and assignment

Adults aged 19-85 years with T2DM, HbA1c 7%-10%, BMI 18.5-40 kg/m², and stable oral therapy entered a 2-week placebo run-in. The main background regimen was metformin ≥1000 mg plus sitagliptin 100 mg. Important exclusions included eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m², dialysis/ESRD, type 1 or secondary diabetes, severe diabetic complications, recent major cardiovascular events or unstable heart failure, and FPG >270 mg/dL. Participants were randomized 1:1:1 using an interactive web system stratified by baseline HbA1c and eGFR.

Participants received empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or placebo for 24 weeks. Continuous empagliflozin groups remained on assigned doses during the 28-week extension; placebo participants switched to empagliflozin 10 mg. Rescue therapy was permitted for repeated FPG >240 mg/dL or investigator judgment.

Statistics

The primary endpoint used ANCOVA with randomization stratification factors as covariates. Family-wise type I error for the primary endpoint was controlled through prespecified gatekeeping: 10 mg versus placebo, then 25 mg versus placebo if the first test rejected. Secondary and exploratory outcomes were not multiplicity-adjusted. FAS efficacy used LOCF for missing values; safety used observed data. Planned enrollment was 165 based on a -0.7% difference, SD 0.93%, 90% power, alpha 0.05, and 15% dropout.

Sponsor and independence

Chong Kun Dang Pharmaceutical Company funded the study and supported investigational-drug supply, clinical monitoring, data management, statistical analysis, and medical writing. Several investigators reported industry relationships, including with the sponsor. These disclosures do not negate the randomized design, but they increase the importance of relying on prespecified endpoints, numerical tables, and transparent reporting rather than promotional interpretation.

Results

Flow and baseline

Of 230 screened, 172 were randomized and 162 (94.2%) completed the controlled period. Four completers declined extension, 158 entered extension, and 151 completed the full study. Baseline mean age was 60.4 years, 63.7% were men, HbA1c 7.8%, FPG 152.1 mg/dL, BMI 25.5 kg/m², and eGFR 89.3 mL/min/1.73 m².

Exploratory and safety findings

Week-24 body-weight changes were -3.0 kg and -2.9 kg with empagliflozin 10 and 25 mg versus -0.4 kg with placebo. UACR changes were -8.7, -19.9, and +6.1 mg/g; only the 25 mg comparison was marked statistically significant versus placebo. Metabolic-syndrome resolution at week 24 was 33.3%, 35.1%, and 9.5% for 10 mg, 25 mg, and placebo.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

Direct phase 3 evidence for empagliflozin add-on glycemic efficacy and tolerability in triple oral therapy.

Evidence basis
Randomized 24-week placebo comparison plus 52-week extension.
Principal limit
Does not directly address dapagliflozin interchangeability or hard cardiorenal outcomes.
Would raise score
A direct empagliflozin-vs-dapagliflozin trial with clinical outcomes.

Novelty 6/10

Adds prospective phase 3 evidence for metformin+sitagliptin+empagliflozin in a Korean population.

Evidence basis
Specific triple-therapy regimen and 52-week follow-up.
Principal limit
SGLT2/DPP-4/metformin combination efficacy was already supported by prior studies.
Would raise score
Demonstration of a clinically distinct benefit or head-to-head class comparison.

Method strength 8/10

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with prespecified primary gatekeeping and strong completion.

Evidence basis
172 randomized; 94.2% completed 24 weeks; primary ANCOVA; blinded treatment period.
Principal limit
LOCF imputation, modest sample, sponsor involvement, and unadjusted multiplicity for secondary/exploratory endpoints.
Would raise score
Larger independent replication with modern missing-data methods and multiplicity control for key secondary endpoints.

Evidence strength 7/10

Primary glycemic benefit is precisely estimated and strongly statistically supported.

Evidence basis
HbA1c adjusted differences -0.7 and -0.8 percentage points with narrow 95% CIs and p<0.0001.
Principal limit
Endpoints are mainly glycemic/surrogate; renal and metabolic claims rely on exploratory measures.
Would raise score
Patient-centered cardiovascular/renal outcomes or robust prespecified secondary control.

External validity 5/10

Population and regimen are clinically recognizable but geographically and biologically selected.

Evidence basis
26 Korean sites with adults on stable metformin+sitagliptin.
Principal limit
Korean-only population; eGFR <45 excluded; selected HbA1c/BMI ranges; fixed background regimen.
Would raise score
Replication across diverse regions, kidney function, age/frailty, and treatment backgrounds.

Implementation value 6/10

Useful for counseling on expected glycemic, weight, and short-term tolerability effects of adding empagliflozin.

Evidence basis
Absolute glycemic changes, target attainment, body weight, UACR, and AE frequencies are reported.
Principal limit
Not a direct formulary-equivalence study and not designed for inpatient use.
Would raise score
Direct class-interchange evidence, inpatient outcomes, and comparative effectiveness versus dapagliflozin.

Validity and interpretation

Main strength: the prespecified, blinded, randomized placebo comparison gives high confidence that empagliflozin caused the observed 24-week HbA1c and FPG reductions in this selected population.

Main limits: modest sample size, Korean-only population, eGFR below 45 excluded, LOCF efficacy imputation, no multiplicity adjustment beyond the primary gatekeeping hierarchy, and exploratory surrogate evidence for “renal protection.” The 28-week extension lacks a continuing placebo arm because placebo switched to empagliflozin.

The trial therefore supports adding empagliflozin for glycemic intensification when metformin+sitagliptin is insufficient. It should not be used alone to infer cardiovascular or kidney outcome benefit in this population, to establish dose-response superiority of 25 mg, or to claim therapeutic equivalence with dapagliflozin.

Implementation / practice takeaways

  • Expected incremental HbA1c lowering versus placebo at 24 weeks is about 0.7-0.8 percentage points on top of metformin+sitagliptin.
  • Weight loss of about 3 kg over 24 weeks is plausible in a population with mean BMI around 25.5 kg/m².
  • Short-term TEAE rates were not increased overall, but the sample is too small to characterize uncommon SGLT2 adverse events.
  • For a dapagliflozin-only formulary question, this is supporting evidence for empagliflozin efficacy—not comparative evidence of class interchangeability.
  • UACR, metabolic syndrome, waist, and BP should remain exploratory/surrogate findings; secondary/exploratory multiplicity was not controlled.

Bottom line: Use as high-quality regimen-specific glycemic evidence. Do not use as stand-alone evidence for cardiorenal outcome equivalence or inpatient interchange.
