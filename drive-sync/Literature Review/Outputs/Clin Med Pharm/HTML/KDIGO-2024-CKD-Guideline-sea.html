One-page synthesis

Core thesis: CKD care should combine accurate classification (CGA), risk prediction, layered disease-modifying therapy, drug stewardship, complication management, and patient-centered care models rather than rely on eGFR thresholds alone.

The 2024 update substantially modernizes the 2012 CKD guideline. The largest practice changes are broader use of cystatin C when creatinine-based eGFR is inadequate for consequential decisions; formal kidney-failure risk prediction to guide referral and KRT planning; strong SGLT2 inhibitor recommendations across diabetic and non-diabetic CKD; selected use of finerenone and GLP-1 receptor agonists; explicit medication-stewardship workflows; and a symptom/risk-based approach to referral and dialysis planning.

The source is methodologically strong as a guideline-development product, but evidence strength is heterogeneous. The formal guidance set contains 28 graded recommendations and 141 ungraded Practice Points. Only 6 recommendations are backed by high-certainty (A) evidence; 5 use very-low-certainty (D) evidence. Practical utility is therefore very high, while confidence must remain recommendation-specific.

Best use: foundational CKD reference, clinical decision support content, medication safety, referral/risk thresholds, and guideline-informed protocol design. Do not use for: assuming every Practice Point is RCT-derived, extrapolating the source to dialysis/transplant populations, or treating the 2024 Chapter 3 therapy section as permanently current without checking ongoing KDIGO updates.

Section-by-section condensation

Introduction, definitions, and special considerations

KDIGO retains the CGA framework: CKD is defined by kidney structure/function abnormality present for at least 3 months with health implications, and classified by cause, GFR category, and albuminuria category. The guideline emphasizes that risk rises across both lower GFR and higher albuminuria, while age, sex/gender, pregnancy/reproductive context, pediatrics, frailty, socioeconomic factors, and access to care affect interpretation and implementation.

The document is not a screening textbook and begins primarily from an established CKD diagnosis, although it gives detection/chronicity guidance. The risk heatmaps and CKD Prognosis Consortium analyses are used to frame absolute and relative risk rather than to justify a single age-adjusted redefinition of CKD.

Chapter 1 — Evaluation of CKD

Evaluation uses both GFR and albuminuria, confirmation of chronicity, and a cause-focused workup. Initial GFR assessment is creatinine-based eGFR; combined creatinine–cystatin C eGFR is recommended when creatinine-based estimates are less accurate and the answer affects decisions. Measured GFR is reserved for situations in which greater accuracy materially changes care. Race should not be used in eGFR computation.

Albuminuria testing prioritizes urine ACR and confirmation of abnormal results, with explicit preanalytic/analytic standards. Kidney biopsy is presented as an acceptable diagnostic test when clinically appropriate, and genetics is incorporated as an increasingly actionable component of causal evaluation. Point-of-care creatinine/albumin testing is acceptable when it improves access or pathway execution, provided laboratory-quality safeguards are maintained.

Chapter 2 — Risk assessment

Monitoring frequency is tied to CKD stage and progression risk. A >20% change in eGFR or doubling of ACR exceeds expected variability and warrants evaluation in the appropriate context. KDIGO moves beyond GFR-only referral thresholds by recommending externally validated kidney-failure risk equations in CKD G3–G5.

Risk thresholds are operationalized: roughly 3%–5% 5-year kidney-failure risk can support nephrology referral, >10% 2-year risk can support multidisciplinary care timing, and >40% 2-year risk can support modality education/access or transplant preparation. Disease-specific risk models are preferred when validated for the disease in question.

Chapter 3 — Delaying progression and managing complications

Management is deliberately layered: lifestyle/diet, blood-pressure control, renin–angiotensin system inhibition where indicated, SGLT2 inhibition, and selected add-on therapies such as finerenone and GLP-1 receptor agonists. SGLT2 inhibition receives the strongest treatment recommendations: adults with CKD and eGFR ≥20 ml/min/1.73 m² plus ACR ≥200 mg/g, or heart failure irrespective of albuminuria, receive a 1A recommendation; adults with eGFR 20–45 and lower albuminuria receive a 2B suggestion. The supplement rates kidney-failure evidence as high certainty for broad CKD populations, with pooled effect estimates around HR 0.60–0.72 and benefit present with and without diabetes.

Important safety/implementation details are explicit. RAS inhibitors are titrated to tolerated approved doses with creatinine/potassium checks after initiation or dose change; hyperkalemia should often be managed rather than reflexively stopping therapy. Finerenone is limited to selected T2D/CKD patients with normal potassium and persistent albuminuria, with structured potassium monitoring; pooled finerenone cardiovascular benefit was HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.78–0.95), while hyperkalemia-related withdrawal was more frequent than placebo. Uric-acid lowering is recommended for symptomatic hyperuricemia but not for asymptomatic hyperuricemia solely to slow CKD progression.

Chapter 4 — Medication management and drug stewardship

The chapter treats CKD as a medication-safety state: review nephrotoxins, OTC/herbal products, pregnancy potential, and drug accumulation; monitor eGFR/electrolytes and therapeutic levels when indicated; and perform structured medication review at transitions of care. eGFR is usually acceptable for drug dosing, while creatinine–cystatin C or measured GFR is reserved for narrow-therapeutic-index, toxicity-sensitive, or otherwise high-accuracy decisions.

The guideline cautions against permanent discontinuation after temporary “sick day” or perioperative holds. It explicitly recommends communication of restart plans. Contrast decisions should be based on indication and individualized AKI risk rather than CKD status alone, with specific guidance for iodinated and gadolinium-based agents.

Chapter 5 — Optimal models of care

Referral is broadened from a single eGFR threshold to cause, kidney-failure risk, albuminuria/hematuria, complications, and management needs. Patient-reported symptoms, malnutrition, multidisciplinary care, education, telehealth, and pediatric-to-adult transition are treated as core care-model components rather than optional add-ons.

Dialysis initiation is symptom- and context-based rather than anchored to a single eGFR: symptoms/signs, refractory volume/BP problems, metabolic abnormalities, nutritional decline, cognition, QoL, preferences, and laboratory findings are integrated. Preemptive transplant is prioritized for appropriate children, and comprehensive conservative care plus advance-care planning are explicitly supported as legitimate options.

Chapter 6 — Research recommendations

Research gaps span GFR/albuminuria measurement, genetics, risk-prediction implementation, combinations and sequencing of disease-modifying therapies, complications, medication management, referral, symptom management, models of care, pediatrics, sex/gender, and CKD etiology. The research agenda is substantial because many clinically important Practice Points sit outside a de novo systematic-review evidence base.

The chapter is useful as a map of where the guideline is most vulnerable to future revision. This is especially relevant in 2026 because KDIGO has already initiated a focused update to Chapter 3 to incorporate emerging evidence on newer CKD therapies.

Methods and evidence framework

The guideline used an independent Evidence Review Team, predefined clinical questions, systematic searching, risk-of-bias tools, GRADE certainty assessment, Work Group deliberation, and public review. The literature searches were conducted from July 2022 through April 2023 and updated in July 2023. The data supplement preserves search strategies, Institute of Medicine standards mapping, Summary-of-Findings tables, and PRISMA diagrams.

A key interpretive boundary is the distinction between graded recommendations and Practice Points. There are 28 graded recommendations (16 Level 1, 12 Level 2) but 141 Practice Points. Certainty among graded recommendations is mixed: 6 A, 8 B, 9 C, and 5 D. Therefore, the guideline is methodologically rigorous as a guideline-development product, but its clinical assertions do not all rest on equivalent evidence.

Finerenone

FIDELITY pooled cardiovascular outcome HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.78–0.95); HF hospitalization HR 0.78 (0.66–0.92). Permanent hyperkalemia-related withdrawal 1.7% vs 0.6%.

Aspirin primary prevention

Supplement Table S17: composite CV events RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.62–1.00), moderate certainty; major bleeding HR 1.31 (95% CI 1.01–1.70), low certainty. KDIGO therefore limits the strong aspirin statement to secondary prevention.

NOACs in CKD + AF

Supplement stroke evidence is low-to-moderate certainty for several outcomes; the guideline still recommends NOACs over warfarin for CKD G1–G4 with renal dose adjustment and procedural interruption considerations.

Main strengths

  • Explicit GRADE framework and recommendation-strength language.
  • Independent Evidence Review Team and transparent search/SoF/PRISMA supplement.
  • Strong linkage between evidence statements and implementable algorithms/thresholds.
  • Broad lifespan, resource, and patient-centered scope.
  • Clear separation of graded recommendations from ungraded Practice Points.

Main limitations

  • Search currency is largely through 2023; fast-moving pharmacotherapy evidence has continued to evolve.
  • Most formal guidance statements are Practice Points rather than graded recommendations.
  • Some sections incorporate prior KDIGO recommendations rather than a wholly de novo 2024 review.

  • Access to cystatin C, measured GFR, genetics, kidney dietitians, finerenone, GLP-1 RA, and SGLT2i varies globally.

Practice and implementation takeaways

Evaluation

Use both eGFR and albuminuria; confirm chronicity; use cystatin C/eGFRcr-cys when creatinine is insufficient for a consequential decision.

Risk

Build kidney-failure risk into referral and KRT-preparation workflows rather than relying on eGFR alone.

Disease modification

Layer proven therapies: BP/RAS inhibition where indicated, SGLT2i broadly, and selected finerenone/GLP-1 RA in appropriate T2D/CKD.

Medication safety

Make temporary holds explicit, document restart plans, and avoid reflexive permanent discontinuation after acute illness or surgery.

Hyperkalemia

Use mitigation strategies to preserve beneficial RASi/MRA therapy when clinically feasible.

Care models

Treat referral, symptoms, nutrition, education, transition, conservative care, and advance-care planning as part of CKD management—not merely end-stage planning.

Currency / external verification

Status as of August 2026: KDIGO states that the 2024 CKD guideline remains the current global standard, but on March 17, 2026 it initiated a focused update to Chapter 3, specifically newer therapies including SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1-based therapies, and nonsteroidal MRAs, including CKD without diabetes. Until that update is published, Chapter 3 should be used with an explicit recency check for fast-moving pharmacotherapy decisions.
