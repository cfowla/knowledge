One-page synthesis

ACNS0332 tested two treatment-intensification strategies in high-risk medulloblastoma. Carboplatin was added during craniospinal radiotherapy, and isotretinoin was added during and after maintenance chemotherapy. The primary clinical endpoint was event-free survival. The trial enrolled 294 patients and analyzed 261 after central review. Overall 5-year EFS was 62.9% and OS was 73.4%. The overall randomized carboplatin comparison did not show a statistically significant EFS difference: 66.4% with carboplatin versus 59.2% without, P=.11. Isotretinoin randomization stopped early for futility.

The main positive finding came from retrospective molecular classification. In group 3 medulloblastoma, 5-year EFS was 73.2% with carboplatin versus 53.7% without, P=.047. Metastatic group 3 results were similar in direction. The paper therefore argues for carboplatin during radiotherapy in high-risk group 3 disease and against extending that intensification to WNT, SHH, or group 4 disease. That conclusion is plausible but rests on a subgroup analysis that was not a randomization stratum. Hematologic toxicity during induction was clearly higher with carboplatin. The trial is important for molecularly informed treatment selection, but the group 3 efficacy claim is less secure than a prespecified molecular-stratified primary comparison would be.

Best-supported claim. The overall trial shows no significant EFS benefit from carboplatin across all high-risk medulloblastoma. A signal of benefit is concentrated in group 3, with a 19.5 percentage-point 5-year EFS difference.
Main uncertainty. Molecular subgroup was not used to stratify randomization. The group 3 P value is nominal and close to .05, so chance imbalance and multiplicity remain material concerns.

Section synthesis

Background and rationale

High-risk medulloblastoma was defined clinically by metastasis, diffuse anaplasia, or substantial residual disease. The treatment rationale combined prior carboplatin radiosensitization data with preclinical and clinical retinoid evidence. The study later incorporated molecular subgroup analysis as medulloblastoma classification changed during the long enrollment period.

Methods

Patients received 36 Gy craniospinal radiotherapy with a posterior fossa boost and weekly vincristine. Carboplatin-assigned patients received 35 mg/m2 before radiotherapy for 30 doses. Maintenance used cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, and vincristine for six cycles. Isotretinoin-assigned patients received 80 mg/m2 twice daily on days 15 through 28 for 12 cycles. Event-free survival was the primary clinical endpoint. Molecular subgroup classification used retrospective DNA methylation profiling, with whole-exome sequencing when tissue permitted.

The final April 11, 2018 protocol confirms the factorial treatment structure and notes the January 27, 2015 closure of isotretinoin arms. The trial used independent data and safety monitoring. Central pathology and radiology review was retrospective for this analysis, which removed 24 additional patients after enrollment.

Results

Among 261 analyzed patients, 5-year EFS was 62.9% and OS was 73.4%. Carboplatin produced 5-year EFS of 66.4% versus 59.2% without carboplatin, P=.11. Five-year OS was 77.6% versus 68.8%, P=.28. Isotretinoin showed nearly identical 5-year EFS at arm closure, 68.6% versus 67.8%.

Molecular classification was available for 231 patients. Group 3 showed the clearest carboplatin signal, with 5-year EFS 73.2% versus 53.7%, P=.047. In metastatic group 3, 5-year EFS was 64.8% versus 40.3%, P=.045, and OS 77.4% versus 51.2%, P=.046. Survival also varied across molecular subgroups independent of carboplatin assignment.

Safety and neurocognition

Carboplatin increased grade 3 or higher hematologic toxicity during induction. Neutrophil decrease rose from 30.0% to 57.9%, platelet decrease from 4.3% to 31.0%, anemia from 7.9% to 20.7%, and febrile neutropenia from 2.1% to 12.4%. Neurocognitive testing involved 116 participants and had fewer observations over time. The only statistically significant carboplatin comparison in the supplement was better global executive functioning at the second time point, a finding that should be treated cautiously because of sample size and multiple endpoints.

Discussion, limitations, and conclusion

The authors interpret the group 3 result as support for adding carboplatin during radiotherapy in high-risk group 3 medulloblastoma and for avoiding that intensification in other molecular subgroups. They also argue for rapid integrated molecular diagnosis. The paper explicitly recognizes increased toxicity and the need for better therapies.

The strongest limitation is the molecular subgroup analysis. Randomization was not stratified by subgroup, so subgroup balance occurred by chance. Very few patients had incomplete resection as the sole high-risk criterion. Neurocognitive data came from a small co-enrolled subset. Retrospective central review also exposed clinically important local staging and diagnosis discrepancies.

Practice and implementation takeaways

  • Do not summarize this trial as a positive overall carboplatin trial. The overall randomized EFS comparison was not significant.
  • When reusing the group 3 result, keep the subgroup design limitation beside the effect estimate.
  • Record the hematologic toxicity cost whenever the group 3 benefit is cited.
  • Use the molecular classification and protocol supplements when reproducing eligibility, regimen, or subgroup definitions.
  • Do not use this 2021 article alone as current treatment guidance. Verify current pediatric neuro-oncology protocols and guidelines before clinical use.
