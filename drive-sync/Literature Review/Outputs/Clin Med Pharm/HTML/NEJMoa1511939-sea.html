One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. SPRINT provides high-strength randomized evidence that, among selected high-risk adults without diabetes or prior stroke, an intensive SBP target below 120 mm Hg reduced the primary cardiovascular composite and all-cause mortality compared with a target below 140 mm Hg, at the cost of more hypotension, syncope, electrolyte abnormalities, and acute kidney injury.

The trial randomized 9361 participants at 102 sites. At 1 year, mean SBP was 121.4 versus 136.2 mm Hg. The primary composite occurred at 1.65%/year versus 2.19%/year (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64-0.89; P<0.001). All-cause mortality was also lower (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.60-0.90; P=0.003), as were cardiovascular death and heart failure. The reported NNT over 3.26 years was 61 for the primary outcome and 90 for all-cause death.

The benefit is not a free-standing mandate for every patient with hypertension. The trial excluded diabetes, prior stroke, adults under 50, and institutionalized older adults; treatment was open-label; BP was obtained using a standardized research protocol; and the intervention stopped early. Intensive treatment also produced more AKI/renal failure, hypotension, syncope, and electrolyte abnormalities, while injurious falls were not increased overall.

Methods and intervention architecture

Design: randomized, controlled, open-label, 102-site trial with blinded clinical-outcome adjudication. Randomization was stratified by site. The primary endpoint was time to first myocardial infarction, acute coronary syndrome, stroke, heart failure, or cardiovascular death. The planned sample size provided 88.7% power to detect a 20% primary-outcome effect at an assumed 2.2%/year standard-group event rate.

BP strategy: office BP used the mean of three seated measurements after 5 minutes of rest using an automated Omron 907 system. The supplementary intensive algorithm used active monthly titration until SBP <120 mm Hg or a clinical decision not to intensify further; the standard algorithm targeted <140 mm Hg and incorporated medication step-down for sufficiently low SBP. Protocol-defined group-sequential monitoring allowed early stopping; the efficacy boundary was crossed in 2015.

Main caution: a target number cannot be separated from the measurement protocol, patient selection, monitoring intensity, and adverse-event surveillance used to generate the evidence.

Internal validity: strong randomization, multicenter enrollment, blinded endpoint adjudication, prespecified outcomes, and large sample size. Open-label treatment can affect non-adjudicated behaviors and care intensity, but the major endpoints were clinically important and adjudicated. Early stopping for benefit can magnify treatment-effect estimates and reduces long-term exposure/safety information.

External validity: useful for high-risk adults resembling SPRINT participants, including many older adults and patients with CKD, but not directly established for diabetes, prior stroke, younger adults, nursing-home/assisted-living residents, or populations managed with materially different BP measurement and follow-up conditions.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use the strategy, not just the number
SPRINT's evidence couples the target with standardized measurement, active titration, frequent follow-up, and safety monitoring.
Benefit-harm discussion is mandatory
Cardiovascular and mortality benefits coexist with increased hypotension, syncope, electrolyte abnormalities, and AKI.
Renal signals need context
No clear benefit on the prespecified CKD composite was shown during the trial; incident eGFR decline among those without CKD was more frequent under intensive treatment.
Do not overgeneralize
Diabetes and prior stroke were excluded, and the trial does not validate the same target under every routine-office measurement workflow.
