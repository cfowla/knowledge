One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The review is useful for a quick empagliflozin HF-outcome synthesis, especially the consistent HHF direction. It is not strong enough to anchor a formulary-equivalence decision or replace the cited primary trials.

The review pooled four trials with estimable hazard ratios and used EMPA-TROPISM only in sensitivity analyses because its empagliflozin arm had zero events. Cardiovascular mortality pooled to HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78-0.96, but heterogeneity was high at I-squared 73%. The authors themselves state that EMPA-REG OUTCOME largely drove this mortality signal. HHF pooled to HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.64-0.77, with I-squared 0%, a more consistent result across the four main trials.

The paper has material methods and reporting limits. It does not report a review registration, full reproducible search strategies beyond PubMed, meta-analysis software, study weights, the size of the continuity correction, or a formal publication-bias analysis. Figures 4 and 5 also print EMPA-TROPISM trial rows with the exact pooled sensitivity estimates, even though the Results text does not provide those trial-level continuity-corrected HRs. That figure-level inconsistency lowers confidence in the sensitivity-analysis presentation.

Methods

The authors searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Google Scholar through March 2025 and manually screened references. Two teams of three reviewers screened records and extracted data. Two reviewers used RoB 2.0. Eligible studies were adult RCTs of empagliflozin reporting cardiovascular mortality or HHF. The source states PRISMA 2020 compliance.

Hazard ratios were pooled by inverse variance. The paper selected fixed effects for I-squared below 25%, random effects for 25% to 50%, and DerSimonian-Laird random effects above 50%. It assessed heterogeneity with Cochran Q and I-squared. EMPA-TROPISM entered sensitivity analyses with a continuity correction because of zero events in the treatment arm. The amount of that correction is not reported.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

Directly addresses empagliflozin cardiovascular death and heart-failure hospitalization, useful background for SGLT2 evidence review.

Evidence basis. Five RCTs spanning HFrEF, HFpEF, type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular disease, and post-MI populations.

Main limit. It does not compare empagliflozin with dapagliflozin and does not test inpatient formulary interchange.

What would change it. A direct head-to-head or class-effect synthesis with explicit formulary outcomes would raise the score.

Novelty 4/10

The review mainly repackages established landmark empagliflozin trials.

Evidence basis. The pooled analysis combines EMPA-REG OUTCOME, EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPEROR-Preserved, EMPACT-MI, and EMPA-TROPISM.

Main limit. No new patient-level analysis, subgroup model, or novel endpoint framework is reported.

What would change it. A prospective individual-patient-data meta-analysis or new comparative question would raise the score.

Method strength 4/10

Multiple databases and duplicate screening/extraction are strengths, but important reproducibility and synthesis details are missing.

Evidence basis. Search through March 2025, two screening teams, standardized extraction, RoB 2.0, inverse-variance pooling.

Main limit. No protocol registration is reported; non-PubMed strategies, software, study weights, publication-bias method, continuity-correction amount, and influence analysis are not provided. Model choice switches by observed I-squared.

What would change it. A registered protocol, reproducible searches, prespecified model, small-k robust random-effects method, influence analysis, and complete computation details would raise the score.

Evidence strength 5/10

The HHF signal is consistent, but the cardiovascular-mortality result is heterogeneous and largely driven by EMPA-REG OUTCOME.

Evidence basis. HHF pooled HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.64-0.77, I-squared 0%; CV mortality pooled HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78-0.96, I-squared 73%.

Main limit. Only four trials contribute estimable HRs to the primary meta-analyses, populations differ substantially, and Figures 4-5 contain trial-row labels that duplicate pooled estimates.

What would change it. A cleaner preregistered synthesis with validated study-level extraction and robust heterogeneity analysis would raise the score.

External validity 6/10

The trials span several clinically important cardiovascular populations, but pooling them does not make one effect estimate equally applicable to each setting.

Evidence basis. HFrEF, HFpEF, established ASCVD with diabetes, non-diabetic HFrEF, and post-MI populations are represented.

Main limit. Clinical heterogeneity is large, patient-level subgroup data are unavailable, and the post-MI population differs from chronic HF populations.

What would change it. Prespecified phenotype-specific pooling and patient-level subgroup analyses would raise the score.

Implementation value 5/10

The review supports the direction and magnitude of empagliflozin HHF benefit but should not drive formulary interchange decisions by itself.

Evidence basis. The pooled HHF estimate is precise and directionally consistent across the four main trials.

Main limit. No direct dapagliflozin comparison, dose-conversion question, inpatient initiation pathway, safety synthesis, or implementation analysis is included.

What would change it. Direct comparative effectiveness or class-effect evidence tied to formulary decisions would raise the score.

Systematic-review appraisal

The source reports four databases, citation searching, duplicate screening and extraction, and RoB 2.0. It does not report a registration or protocol identifier, complete reproducible strategies for all databases, a GRADE assessment, a formal small-study or publication-bias analysis, or enough computation detail to reproduce the forest plots. With only four estimable trials in each primary pool, heterogeneity estimates and random-effects inference are fragile. The source's threshold-based model switching adds another avoidable analytic choice.

Clinical heterogeneity matters. EMPA-REG OUTCOME enrolled type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular disease, EMPEROR trials enrolled chronic HF phenotypes, and EMPACT-MI enrolled post-MI patients without prior HF. A pooled HHF estimate can summarize direction, but it does not erase those population differences. The mortality pool is more difficult to interpret because I-squared is 73% and the treatment effect varies from 0.62 to 1.03.

Source-integrity findings

  • Figures 4 and 5 duplicate pooled estimates on the EMPA-TROPISM trial row.
  • The Discussion cites reference 9 for EMPA-TROPISM remodeling and biomarker findings, while the bibliography identifies EMPA-TROPISM as reference 11.
  • The Discussion makes favorable safety claims, but the Conclusions state that safety outcomes were not included in the review. Safety is therefore background discussion, not a synthesized review result.

Implementation and practice takeaways

  • Use the paper as a compact secondary summary of empagliflozin's direction of effect on HHF. The pooled HHF estimate is the clearest result in the paper.
  • Treat the pooled cardiovascular-mortality estimate cautiously. The source reports high heterogeneity and says EMPA-REG OUTCOME largely drove the effect.
  • Do not use this review alone to infer empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin equivalence, inpatient interchangeability, dose conversion, or comparative safety. Those questions are outside this paper's design.
  • For any decision that depends on one trial's exact estimate, read the cited primary publication. Trial-specific atoms in this package are explicitly tagged as secondary reports.
