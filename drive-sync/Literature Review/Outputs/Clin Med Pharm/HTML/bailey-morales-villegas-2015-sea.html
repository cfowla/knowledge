One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used to document long-term glycaemic durability, body-weight effects, and characteristic genital/urinary infection signals of dapagliflozin monotherapy. It should not be used alone to choose between dapagliflozin and another SGLT2 inhibitor or to infer heart-failure/kidney clinical-outcome superiority.

The trial followed treatment-naive adults with type 2 diabetes for 102 weeks after randomization to placebo or dapagliflozin 2.5, 5, or 10 mg once daily. After week 24, the control arm received blinded metformin 500 mg/day while dapagliflozin arms continued active treatment with metformin-matching placebo. At week 102, dapagliflozin 5 mg and 10 mg produced larger HbA1c reductions than control (−0.53 and −0.44 percentage points, respectively); fasting plasma glucose differences were −0.69 and −1.12 mmol/L. Only 10 mg showed a statistically nominal between-group body-weight difference (−2.60 kg). Dapagliflozin 5 mg also increased the estimated proportion achieving HbA1c <7% (33.2% vs 18.5%).

Safety was broadly consistent with the SGLT2 mechanism: overall adverse-event and serious-event rates did not show a clear dose-related excess, and major hypoglycaemia did not occur. Genital infection events were more frequent with dapagliflozin (9.4–15.7% for 5–10 mg vs 1.3% control), and UTI-suggestive events were 6.2–12.5% across dapagliflozin doses vs 4.0% control. Renal function remained broadly stable; uric acid decreased and haematocrit increased with dapagliflozin. Interpretation of durability is constrained by substantial discontinuation/rescue, post-rescue efficacy exclusion, and nominal post hoc week-102 testing.

Practice boundary. This is an older surrogate-endpoint trial. It predates the modern cardiorenal-outcomes evidence base and contains no direct empagliflozin comparison. Its strongest current use is safety/efficacy context, not formulary class-member selection.

Section condensation

Abstract

The authors framed dapagliflozin as an insulin-independent glucose-lowering option and evaluated persistence of monotherapy effects through 102 weeks. The abstract highlights statistically nominal week-102 advantages for HbA1c with 5 and 10 mg, fasting glucose with 5 and 10 mg, and body weight with 10 mg, while noting low hypoglycaemia and increased genital/UTI events.

Introduction

The rationale centers on progressive type 2 diabetes, limits of therapies that promote weight gain or hypoglycaemia, and renal SGLT2 inhibition as an insulin-independent mechanism. The paper positions long-term durability and safety as the key extension questions after the initial 24-week efficacy phase.

Patients and methods

Adults aged 18–77 years with treatment-naive type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled by diet/exercise were randomized in a blinded parallel design. The reported main cohort had HbA1c 7.0–10.0% and morning dosing. After week 24, the placebo group received low-dose metformin 500 mg/day; open-label rescue metformin 500–2000 mg/day could be added for worsening glycaemia.

Continuous efficacy endpoints used repeated-measures mixed models and excluded data after rescue without imputation, assuming missing-at-random data. Safety was descriptive and retained post-rescue observations. The study was powered for the week-24 primary analysis rather than the extension; week-102 P values are explicitly post hoc and nominal.

Results

Of 274 randomized main-cohort participants, 167 completed 102 weeks. Baseline HbA1c averaged about 7.8–8.0%, and most participants were overweight or obese. At week 102, HbA1c and fasting glucose remained lower in the 5 mg and 10 mg groups than in control; body weight was significantly lower only for 10 mg in the nominal between-group test. Dapagliflozin produced sustained, dose-dependent glucosuria.

Overall adverse-event rates ranged 67.2–80.0% and serious-event rates 1.4–9.2%, without a clear treatment-related serious-event signal. Hypoglycaemia remained uncommon with no major episodes. Genital and urinary infection events were more frequent in dapagliflozin groups, particularly genital events among women. Laboratory patterns included stable eGFR, lower uric acid, and higher haematocrit.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the findings as evidence that dapagliflozin monotherapy can maintain glycaemic and weight effects for two years without increasing hypoglycaemia. They acknowledge that rescue therapy and discontinuation complicate efficacy interpretation because including rescued observations confounds treatment effect while excluding them progressively reduces usable data.

The extension is best understood as supportive durability evidence rather than a definitive long-term comparative trial. The changed comparator, high attrition/rescue burden, nominal post hoc testing, predominantly White early-disease cohort, and surrogate outcomes materially limit stronger practice claims.

Relevance

Evidence basis: 102-week randomized dapagliflozin exposure with glycaemic, weight, and safety endpoints.

Main limiter: Older surrogate-focused trial with no active SGLT2 comparator.

Would change: Direct comparative or clinical-outcome evidence would raise the score.

5/10

Novelty

The main added value is durability of a previously established glucose-lowering effect through 102 weeks.

Evidence basis: Long-term blinded extension beyond the 24-week primary phase.

Main limiter: Mechanism and short-term efficacy were already established.

Would change: New patient-centered or cardiorenal endpoints would raise novelty.

6/10

Method strength

Randomization and blinding are strengths, but long-term inference is weakened by rescue, attrition, and a changed comparator.

Evidence basis: Randomized double-blind multicenter design; repeated-measures model.

Main limiter: 35–57% discontinued or received rescue and week-102 tests were nominal post hoc.

Would change: Lower attrition, fixed comparator, and prespecified powered extension testing would raise the score.

6/10

Evidence strength

The trial supports durable glycaemic and weight effects at selected doses but not hard clinical outcomes.

Evidence basis: Effect sizes and confidence intervals are reported for HbA1c, FPG, weight, and safety.

Main limiter: Post-rescue efficacy data were excluded and multiplicity was not controlled.

Would change: Complete follow-up and prespecified multiplicity-controlled long-term endpoints would raise the score.

5/10

External validity

Applicability is strongest to treatment-naive adults with early type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis: Multinational 85-center enrollment.

Main limiter: Predominantly White cohort, early disease, and selected HbA1c range.

Would change: More diverse populations and broader disease severity would raise the score.

5/10

Implementation value

Useful for contextualizing long-term glycaemic durability and genital/UTI safety, but insufficient alone for formulary preference.

Evidence basis: Dose-specific efficacy and safety through 102 weeks.

Main limiter: No head-to-head empagliflozin comparison and no contemporary HF/CKD outcome framework.

Would change: Direct comparative effectiveness and cardiorenal outcome evidence would raise the score.

Principal threats to inference: rescue/discontinuation, post-rescue exclusion under MAR assumptions, comparator change after week 24, nominal post hoc P values without multiplicity control, surrogate outcomes, and limited demographic diversity.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use

Long-term dapagliflozin glycaemic durability, weight trajectory, hypoglycaemia context, genital/UTI event rates, and laboratory patterns.

Do not use

Do not infer comparative superiority versus empagliflozin or modern HF/CKD benefit from this study.

Formulary role

Treat as supporting class-member safety/efficacy background. Pair with direct comparative evidence and contemporary cardiorenal outcome trials/guidelines for formulary decisions.

Read next

Prioritize direct dapagliflozin–empagliflozin comparisons and outcome trials relevant to heart failure, kidney disease, and inpatient use.
