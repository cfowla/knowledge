One-page synthesis

Source-derived finding

Core thesis: after more than one year of uninterrupted every-2-month cabotegravir/rilpivirine, substantial pharmacokinetic variability persists. Across 50 participants and 150 paired trough sampling visits, interindividual variability was high for both drugs, whereas intraindividual variability was moderate; repeat levels within the same person were therefore not perfectly stable even after the expected time to steady state.

Independent appraisal

The study is most useful as a warning against overinterpreting a single trough result. Its repeated-sampling design directly demonstrates within-person variability, but the small single-site cohort, standardized injection technique, absence of a comparator, uncertain pharmacodynamic thresholds, and lack of virologic failures prevent this study from establishing how many levels are needed, what action threshold should be used, or whether TDM-driven changes improve outcomes.

High-value takeaway: The paper supports a repeat-measurement interpretation principle more strongly than it supports any particular concentration cutoff.

Section condensation

Abstract

The authors prospectively sampled trough concentrations before three consecutive bimonthly injections in 50 virologically suppressed participants after >12 months of continuous LAI cabotegravir/rilpivirine. They defined variability using %CV and ICC and evaluated concentrations below 4×PA-IC90 thresholds of 664 ng/mL for cabotegravir and 48 ng/mL for rilpivirine.

Interindividual CV was high (58% cabotegravir; 52% rilpivirine), while intraindividual CV was moderate (24% and 23%) with ICC 0.82 and 0.72, respectively. Low-threshold troughs occurred in 8% of cabotegravir samples and 16% of rilpivirine samples, yet the paper reports maintained virologic suppression. The abstract concludes that one trough should not drive clinical decisions.

Introduction

The rationale is therapeutic drug monitoring uncertainty: prior cohorts showed wide between-person and within-person variability during the first year, and clinicians have used proposed efficacy targets to interpret low concentrations. The authors identify an evidence gap after the first year, noting that cabotegravir and rilpivirine reach steady state only after approximately 44 and 48 weeks, respectively.

The study therefore asks whether pharmacokinetic variability remains clinically relevant once long-term dosing has continued beyond that period.

Methods

This was a prospective observational PK cohort at the UNMC Specialty Care Center. Eligible participants were virologically suppressed and had ≥12 months of uninterrupted LAI cabotegravir/rilpivirine without missed/out-of-window doses; known interacting concomitant medications were excluded. Four clinical nurses administered all injections, and people with BMI >30 kg/m² received a 2-inch needle. Trough plasma was collected immediately before three consecutive injections and quantified by validated LC-MS/MS in a CLIA-certified laboratory; LLOQs were 20 ng/mL for cabotegravir and 5 ng/mL for rilpivirine.

Primary outcomes were inter- and intraindividual %CV. The authors classified %CV <20% as low, 20%–30% as moderate, and >30% as high. ICC assessed within-person agreement (poor <0.5, moderate 0.5–0.75, good >0.75). Secondary outcomes used 4×PA-IC90 thresholds. Fisher exact and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests evaluated exploratory associations; SAS 9.4 was used and P<0.05 defined statistical significance. IRB approval was EP-633-23.

Results

Fifty participants contributed 150 trough samples. At enrollment, median prior continuous LAI use was 65 weeks (IQR 56–82); the cohort was 80% male, median age 45 years (IQR 33–56), and median BMI 28 kg/m² (IQR 26–33). Geometric mean trough concentrations were 1384 ng/mL for cabotegravir and 83 ng/mL for rilpivirine. Interindividual CVs were 58% and 52%; intraindividual CVs were 24% and 23%, with ICCs of 0.82 and 0.72.

Twelve cabotegravir and 24 rilpivirine troughs were below 4×PA-IC90. Table 1 stratified participants by whether any study trough was low (18 vs 32 participants); albumin was the only displayed characteristic meeting the prespecified P<0.05 threshold (median 4.4 vs 4.6 g/dL; P=0.017). The main text reports 8 participants (17%) with at least one detectable HIV-RNA value >20 copies/mL, with 13 values ranging 23–75 copies/mL. Only one participant had both drug concentrations below their thresholds during detectable viremia, and all 8 subsequently re-suppressed without regimen change.

Discussion

The authors interpret the persistence of high between-person variability and moderate within-person variability as directly relevant to TDM. They emphasize uncertainty around clinically meaningful thresholds: the in-vitro 4×PA-IC90 cutoffs differ from phase 3 first-trough-quartile upper limits (cabotegravir 1120 ng/mL; rilpivirine 32 ng/mL), so a low concentration cannot be equated automatically with treatment failure.

The paper concludes that decisions should likely use more than one TDM level and specifically cautions against discontinuing LAI cabotegravir/rilpivirine because of a single low trough. The authors call for further PK/PD work to define exposure-response relationships more reliably.

Limitations

Reported limitations include a single clinical site and predominantly male cohort; controlled injections by four experienced nurses that may not represent wider practice; measurement only at trough rather than across the dosing interval; assessment of total plasma rather than intracellular concentrations; and incomplete evaluation of covariates such as pharmacogenetics and body composition beyond BMI.

Additional appraisal
The absence of virologic failures is reassuring descriptively but also means the study cannot estimate sensitivity/specificity of any threshold for failure. Exploratory Table 1 testing is small and apparently unadjusted for multiplicity, so the albumin P=0.017 signal should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than a validated predictor.

Table appraisal

Only albumin met the study’s P<0.05 criterion. With 18 participants in the low-trough group, multiple exploratory comparisons, and no reported multivariable adjustment in this analysis, this should not be interpreted as a validated clinical predictor.

Main strengths

  • Prospective repeated within-person sampling after the first year of continuous therapy.
  • Validated LC-MS/MS assay with explicit LLOQs and pre-dose sampling.
  • Standardized injection technique reduces one source of procedural noise when estimating biologic/PK variability.
  • Preserves both between-person and within-person views of variability and provides ICC alongside %CV.
  • Links concentration observations to contemporaneous virologic follow-up, while appropriately acknowledging threshold uncertainty.

Main limitations

  • Small, single-site cohort with 80% male participants and controlled administration by four experienced nurses.
  • Only three trough observations per person; no full dosing-interval PK profile or intracellular concentration assessment.
  • No sustained virologic failures, preventing derivation or validation of a failure-predictive therapeutic threshold.
  • Exploratory association testing is underpowered and not presented as a multivariable predictor model.
  • Supplementary Table S1 was not part of the user-specified source set, limiting full demographic/covariate review.
  • The main text reports 8 participants as 17% without clarifying the denominator; this was not silently recalculated.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this paper forUnderstanding that post-year-1 CAB/RPV trough values can vary meaningfully both between and within patients; contextualizing an isolated low TDM result.
Do not use this paper forDefining a universal “therapeutic” cutoff, proving a low trough causes virologic failure, or specifying an evidence-based discontinuation threshold.
Monitoring implicationIf TDM is obtained, the study supports interpreting a single result cautiously and considering repeat measurement rather than assuming one trough represents stable exposure.
Clinical conditions that matterVirologic history/status, injection timing/technique, time at steady state, interacting medications, and broader PK context remain necessary to interpret concentrations.
What should not be overclaimed: The observation that all participants remained or returned to suppression does not prove that low troughs are harmless. The cohort was already virologically suppressed at initiation of the observed period, failure events were absent, and true subtherapeutic exposure-response thresholds remain unresolved.

Bottom line: Maybe practice-informing, not practice-defining. The study provides credible descriptive evidence against making high-stakes decisions from a single trough concentration, but it does not establish a validated TDM target or management algorithm.

Confidence in appraisal: High for the main-text methods/results and moderate-high for practice translation. The primary article was fully available and visually reconciled; the stated Supplementary Table S1 was not supplied and was intentionally not retrieved.
