Scope: This is observational comparative-effectiveness evidence. It can support hypothesis generation about within-class differences, but it does not establish that empagliflozin causally outperforms dapagliflozin.

Synthesis

Core thesis: In propensity-score-matched TriNetX patients with heart failure, initiation of empagliflozin was associated with a modestly lower 1-year rate of all-cause mortality or hospitalization than initiation of dapagliflozin. The difference was driven by hospitalization, not a demonstrated mortality difference.

The Results text, main baseline table, and Supplement analyses use 11,007 patients per matched arm. The primary composite occurred in 3,545 empagliflozin patients (32.2%) and 3,828 dapagliflozin patients (34.8%), HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.86 to 0.94), log-rank P<.001. Hospitalization showed the same HR of 0.90 (95% CI 0.86 to 0.94). Mortality was 6.3% versus 6.9%, HR 0.91 (95% CI 0.82 to 1.00), and the authors reported no mortality difference.

Calculated, not source-reported: the crude difference between the reported primary event percentages is 2.6 percentage points. Because this is an observational time-to-event analysis with censoring, that calculation should not be treated as a causal absolute risk reduction or converted into an NNT.

HFrEF and HFpEF subgroup analyses were directionally similar, with HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.87 to 0.97) and HR 0.91 (95% CI 0.84 to 0.98), respectively. Their code definitions overlap through ICD-10 I50.4x, so the two subgroup analyses should not be treated as disjoint phenotypes.

Source-integrity issue: the corrected article still contains a matched-denominator conflict. The abstract and Figure 1 report 11,077 patients per group, while the Results text, baseline table, Supplement analyses, and the reported event percentages are consistent with 11,007 per group. This appraisal preserves the conflict and uses 11,007 only when describing analyses that explicitly use that denominator.

Methods

Cohort and exposure

TriNetX supplied deidentified electronic medical record data. Eligible patients had heart failure coded I50x, had not previously received an SGLT2 inhibitor, and newly started empagliflozin or dapagliflozin. Study day 0 was the initiation date. The authors used an intention-to-treat-like assignment: subsequent changes in medication did not alter the initial exposure group.

Confounding control

Propensity scores were generated with logistic regression using demographic factors, cardiac and diabetes diagnoses, medication use, GFR, hemoglobin A1c, natriuretic peptides, LVEF, and hospitalization history. Supplement eTable 1 supplies the coding and curated-variable definitions. Patients were matched 1:1 by greedy nearest-neighbor matching without replacement. Absolute SMD below 0.1 defined similarity.

Several continuous laboratory variables were converted to categorical indicators. A missing measurement generated zeros for all category indicators, while multiple measurements could place one patient into multiple categories. This choice retains patients in the model but makes the laboratory covariates indicators of observed historical ranges rather than a single baseline value.

Outcome analysis

Kaplan-Meier methods and log-rank tests generated time-to-event comparisons, with HRs and 95% CIs. Patients were censored the day after the last fact in their TriNetX record. E-values were used for sensitivity to unmeasured confounding. Secondary analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted and were explicitly designated hypothesis generating.

Subgroups

HFrEF used I50.2x or I50.4x. HFpEF used I50.3x or I50.4x. Because I50.4x appears in both definitions, the subgroup labels are not mutually exclusive by construction.

Figures, tables, and supplement

Baseline balance

The main table shows substantial prematching channeling on some therapies. Dapagliflozin patients had more angiotensin II inhibitor use (63.9% versus 55.4%; SMD 0.174) and sacubitril use (45.2% versus 32.9%; SMD 0.254). After matching, every displayed SMD is below 0.1, with the largest difference reported in the Results text as ACE-inhibitor use, SMD 0.013.

Survival curves

Figure 2 and Supplement eFigure 2 show small but persistent separation favoring empagliflozin for the composite and hospitalization. Supplement eFigure 3 shows near-overlap for mortality, consistent with the non-significant mortality interpretation. Falling numbers at risk by 360 days reinforce that the reported percentages should not be read as simple complete-cohort risks.

Internal validity

The strongest feature is the measured-covariate balance after matching. The main table and Supplement eFigure 1 support that the two matched groups were similar on the variables the study could measure. The weakness is that balance does not extend to unmeasured clinical severity or treatment-selection factors. NYHA class is the authors' explicit example. The E-value of 1.36 is not large, so a moderate unmeasured treatment-outcome association could materially weaken the observed HR of 0.90.

The initial treatment choice may reflect channeling that is only partly captured by available covariates. Prematching ARNI and angiotensin II inhibitor differences show that prescribing patterns differed before matching. Adherence could not be assessed, and later drug changes did not alter exposure classification.

TriNetX could not provide cluster-robust standard errors for matched pairs or practice clustering. This makes the precision estimates less secure than they would be under an analysis that fully accounted for correlated observations. The platform also could not identify exact heart-failure onset, so the authors could not quantify immortal-time bias.

Outcome validity and interpretation

The primary composite is patient-important but broad. The association is clearly driven by all-cause hospitalization, for which cause-specific information was unavailable. This leaves open whether the observed difference reflects heart-failure events, non-heart-failure admissions, residual disease severity, care patterns, or other factors.

Mortality does not show a demonstrated difference. The HR point estimate is similar to the composite estimate, but its 95% CI reaches 1.00 and the source reports no between-group difference. The correct interpretation is not that mortality effects are equivalent, but that this study did not establish a mortality difference.

HbA1c results apply only to the 38.0% and 40.2% of matched patients with at least one measured value during follow-up. That selected subset limits conclusions about glycemic equivalence. Safety ascertainment is also narrow because only UTI and DKA were defined.

Reporting integrity

The 11,077 versus 11,007 denominator conflict matters because it appears in a corrected article and affects the basic analytic sample description. The event percentages align with 11,007 per arm, but the abstract and Figure 1 still show 11,077. The safest approach is to cite the specific denominator source each time rather than collapse the conflict into one silently corrected value.

The immortal-time-bias reference-number mismatch is minor for the scientific result but is another source-quality defect worth preserving in a traceable appraisal.

Practice translation

What this supports: empagliflozin was associated with fewer all-cause hospitalization-or-death events than dapagliflozin in this matched real-world cohort, with consistent direction in the coded HFrEF and HFpEF subgroup analyses.

What this does not support: using this study alone to prefer empagliflozin over dapagliflozin for heart failure, infer a causal 10% relative benefit, infer mortality superiority, or generalize the limited UTI/DKA analysis to overall safety.

For formulary or therapeutic-interchange decisions, this study is best treated as a signal that merits randomized head-to-head confirmation. The authors' own caution against using it to select empagliflozin over dapagliflozin in quadruple therapy is appropriate.
