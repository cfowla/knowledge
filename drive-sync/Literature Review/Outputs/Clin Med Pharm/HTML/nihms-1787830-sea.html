One-page synthesis

Clinical question. In adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn’s disease (CDAI 220–450), how do infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, and risankizumab—alone or, where studied, with azathioprine—compare for induction and maintenance of clinical remission, and for maintenance-trial serious adverse events and infections?

Core finding. Among 2,931 biologic-naïve participants across 15 induction RCTs, infliximab plus azathioprine ranked highest for remission and had a direct advantage over infliximab alone (OR 1.65, 95% CI 1.07–2.54). In the network, infliximab, infliximab plus azathioprine, adalimumab, and ustekinumab were each favored over certolizumab pegol with moderate-confidence estimates. Among 2,479 biologic-experienced participants across 10 induction RCTs, risankizumab was favored over vedolizumab (OR 2.10, 95% CI 1.12–3.92; moderate confidence); adalimumab also ranked highly, but its estimate was strongly conditioned by selective GAIN eligibility.

Maintenance and safety. Across 15 maintenance RCTs (3,786 participants), all evaluated therapies beat placebo for maintenance remission, but no active agent was clearly superior to another. The apparent SAE advantage of infliximab plus azathioprine versus infliximab alone (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.33–0.97) is difficult to interpret because SAE definitions included worsening Crohn’s disease. Adalimumab had more infections than placebo (OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.17–2.01), while infliximab, infliximab plus azathioprine, and ustekinumab had fewer infections than adalimumab in the network.

Why the conclusions are conditional. The network is limited by sparse head-to-head trials, changing trial populations over two decades, selective prior-biologic eligibility in some studies, mixed maintenance designs, and reliance on CDAI remission rather than consistently defined corticosteroid-free or endoscopic outcomes. These limitations are especially important when interpreting SUCRA ranks as treatment hierarchy rather than as a probability-based summary of this particular evidence network.

Section condensation

Introduction

The review was motivated by rapid expansion of advanced therapies and the lack of sufficient direct comparisons to determine biologic sequence. The authors explicitly separated patients receiving a first biologic from those already exposed to biologic therapy because prior failure may mark more refractory disease and alters expected response.

Methods

MEDLINE, EMBASE, CENTRAL, recent conference proceedings, trial registries/unpublished sources, and expert contacts were searched through June 3, 2021 without language restriction. Eligible phase II/III RCTs enrolled adults with CDAI 220–450 and evaluated the specified biologic classes for induction (≥14 days) or maintenance (≥22 weeks). Two investigators independently screened and extracted data; risk of bias used the Cochrane tool. The review was not registered.

Induction remission (CDAI <150) was primary, response (CDAI decrease ≥100) secondary. Maintenance remission was primary for maintenance trials; SAEs and infections were analyzed for maintenance safety. Pairwise analyses used DerSimonian-Laird random effects; the NMA used a frequentist multivariate consistency-model random-effects meta-regression. SUCRA summarized rankings and GRADE rated certainty. Intention-to-treat denominators treated missing efficacy data as nonresponse.

Results — biologic-naïve induction

Fifteen RCTs (n=2,931) informed this network. Direct analyses showed large remission effects versus placebo for infliximab (OR 6.55, 95% CI 2.31–18.55) and adalimumab (OR 5.31, 2.99–9.43). Ustekinumab was not superior to adalimumab in SEAVUE (OR 1.11, 0.74–1.65), and adalimumab plus azathioprine was not superior to adalimumab alone (OR 0.57, 0.29–1.10). Infliximab plus azathioprine was superior to infliximab alone (OR 1.65, 1.07–2.54).

Network estimates favored infliximab (OR 4.53), infliximab plus azathioprine (7.49), adalimumab (3.01), and ustekinumab (2.63) over certolizumab pegol, each with moderate confidence. Infliximab plus azathioprine versus vedolizumab was OR 3.76 (1.01–14.03), but confidence was low. SUCRA ranked infliximab plus azathioprine 0.96, infliximab 0.81, adalimumab 0.67, ustekinumab 0.58, risankizumab 0.49, vedolizumab 0.45, and certolizumab 0.15.

Results — previous biologic exposure

Ten RCTs (n=2,479) informed this network. Direct estimates versus placebo favored ustekinumab (OR 2.55, 1.39–4.69), risankizumab (2.64, 1.89–3.68), and adalimumab after infliximab loss of response/intolerance (3.55, 1.82–6.93). Vedolizumab improved clinical response but not remission. Prior-treatment eligibility varied sharply: MOTIVATE included heavily pretreated patients, whereas GAIN excluded primary infliximab nonresponders.

Network estimates favored risankizumab over vedolizumab (OR 2.10, 1.12–3.92; moderate confidence) and adalimumab over vedolizumab (2.82, 1.20–6.62; low confidence). SUCRA ranked adalimumab 0.92, risankizumab 0.77, ustekinumab 0.53, and vedolizumab 0.23; the review repeatedly cautioned that adalimumab’s ranking was influenced by GAIN selection.

Results — maintenance and safety

Fifteen maintenance RCTs (n=3,786) were included. All active therapies were superior to placebo, but no active agent clearly outperformed another. Infliximab plus azathioprine (SUCRA 0.87), adalimumab (0.78), and infliximab (0.63) ranked highest; excluding treat-through studies produced similar rankings.

For safety, infliximab plus azathioprine had fewer SAEs than infliximab alone (OR 0.56, 0.33–0.97), but SAE definitions included worsening disease. Adalimumab increased infections versus placebo (OR 1.54, 1.17–2.01). Compared with adalimumab, infection odds were lower with infliximab (0.54, 0.33–0.90), infliximab plus azathioprine (0.46, 0.24–0.89), and ustekinumab (0.69, 0.50–0.97).

Discussion and conclusion

The authors place infliximab and adalimumab centrally in first-line biologic treatment and view IL23-pathway blockade as attractive after TNF-antagonist exposure. They frame network estimates as one input alongside safety, physician experience, cost/resource constraints, and patient values. The article also argues that mechanism-switching after TNF failure may be more effective than anti-integrin therapy, while acknowledging the absence of direct head-to-head evidence in that population.

The central limitations are few direct comparisons, inconsistent contemporary treat-to-target outcomes, population and design heterogeneity across eras, lack of individual-patient data, mixed maintenance designs, and no prospective review registration. The review’s strongest use is comparative evidence mapping rather than a definitive active-treatment hierarchy.

Validity and evidence interpretation

The review’s internal methods are strong for an aggregate-data NMA: duplicate review, explicit RCT eligibility, random effects, direct plus indirect analysis, SUCRA, risk-of-bias assessment, inconsistency checks, sensitivity analyses, and GRADE. The main inferential threat is transitivity rather than statistical heterogeneity alone. Biologic-exposed trials differed in number and type of prior failures, older and newer trials enrolled different disease severities/durations, and some active comparisons are built from second-order indirect loops.

SUCRA should therefore be read as a ranking summary conditional on this network, not as proof of clinically meaningful superiority. The authors generally respect that boundary by pairing rankings with GRADE and direct-comparison caveats. Safety rankings are less robust than efficacy rankings because RCTs are underpowered for rare harms and SAE definitions included disease worsening.

Practice translation

The source supports a historical evidence-sequencing framework: TNF antagonists, particularly infliximab plus azathioprine, performed strongly in biologic-naïve patients; risankizumab and ustekinumab were credible options after prior biologic/TNF exposure. Any current clinical decision requires separate review of contemporary guidelines, labels, newer head-to-head evidence, safety factors, patient-specific contraindications, and preferences.
