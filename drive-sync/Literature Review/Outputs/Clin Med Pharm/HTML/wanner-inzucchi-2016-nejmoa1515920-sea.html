One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. This is a pivotal renal-outcomes analysis from EMPA-REG OUTCOME, with unusually strong provenance because the supplied packet includes the article, full supplementary appendix, trial protocol, and publication statistical analysis plan.

The analysis compared pooled empagliflozin 10 mg/25 mg with placebo in a high-cardiovascular-risk type 2 diabetes population. Incident or worsening nephropathy occurred in 12.7% versus 18.8% (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.53–0.70). Progression to macroalbuminuria, doubling of serum creatinine with eGFR ≤45, and renal-replacement therapy also favored empagliflozin, whereas incident albuminuria among patients with normal albumin excretion at baseline did not differ significantly.

The renal-function trajectory is internally coherent with a hemodynamic effect: an early eGFR dip after treatment initiation, a markedly slower chronic decline from week 4 onward, and partial reversal after treatment discontinuation. Interpretation should remain bounded by the secondary renal endpoint hierarchy, absence of a priori microvascular power calculations, no multiplicity correction, low event counts for hard renal endpoints, selected high-CV-risk population, and sponsor involvement in data analysis.

Design and methods

Trial design and population

Patients with type 2 diabetes, established cardiovascular disease, and baseline MDRD eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m² were randomized to empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or placebo once daily on top of standard care. A total of 7020 treated patients from 590 sites in 42 countries formed the treated set; more than 99% had established cardiovascular disease.

At baseline, 17.8% had eGFR 45–59 and 7.7% had eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m²; 28.7% had microalbuminuria and 11.0% macroalbuminuria. ACE inhibitor or ARB use was 80.7%. Median treatment and observation durations were 2.6 and 3.1 years, respectively.

Renal endpoints and analysis

The prespecified renal composite “incident or worsening nephropathy” included macroalbuminuria progression, doubling of serum creatinine with eGFR ≤45, initiation of renal-replacement therapy, or renal death. Incident albuminuria was analyzed among those normoalbuminuric at baseline. The article also reports a post hoc harder renal composite of creatinine doubling, renal-replacement therapy, or renal death.

Time-to-event analyses used adjusted Cox models. Longitudinal CKD-EPI eGFR used MMRM and slope models. The analysis used nominal alpha 0.05 without multiplicity correction, and no formal power calculations were performed a priori for microvascular outcomes. Protocol Revision 4 and the Publication SAP substantiate prespecification of core renal endpoints and planned Cox/Kaplan–Meier/MMRM analyses.

Results

Renal events

Incident or worsening nephropathy occurred in 525/4124 (12.7%) empagliflozin-treated patients versus 388/2061 (18.8%) placebo patients (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.53–0.70; P<0.001). Progression to macroalbuminuria was 459/4091 versus 330/2033 (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.54–0.72), and doubling of serum creatinine with eGFR ≤45 was 70/4645 versus 60/2323 (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.39–0.79).

Renal-replacement therapy was initiated in 13/4687 versus 14/2333 (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.21–0.97; P=0.04). The post hoc hard renal composite favored empagliflozin (81/4645 vs 71/2323; HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.40–0.75). Incident albuminuria did not differ significantly (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.87–1.04; P=0.25).

eGFR trajectory

During the first 4 weeks, eGFR fell acutely with empagliflozin (weekly slopes −0.62 for 10 mg and −0.82 for 25 mg) but was essentially stable with placebo (+0.01). From week 4 through the last on-treatment measurement, annual decline was only −0.19 mL/min/1.73 m²/year for each empagliflozin dose versus −1.67 with placebo (P<0.001 for both comparisons).

After treatment stopped, eGFR increased over follow-up in both empagliflozin groups while remaining slightly negative with placebo. The adjusted mean difference from placebo in baseline-to-follow-up change was +4.7 mL/min/1.73 m² for both empagliflozin doses (95% CI 4.0–5.5; P<0.001).

Safety

Among participants with baseline eGFR ≤59, serious adverse events occurred in 45.5% with empagliflozin versus 52.9% with placebo; acute renal failure in 11.2% versus 14.3%; acute kidney injury in 2.1% versus 3.6%; and hyperkalemia in 3.9% versus 6.9%. Genital infection was more frequent with empagliflozin (5.3% vs 1.6%).

Patterns were broadly similar in the eGFR ≥60 stratum: genital infection remained more frequent with empagliflozin, while acute renal failure, acute kidney injury, and hyperkalemia were numerically lower. The source therefore does not show a generalized renal-safety penalty from empagliflozin in the impaired-kidney-function subgroup studied.

Source claims vs appraisal

Source-supported: Empagliflozin lowered the rate of the prespecified new/worsening-nephropathy composite and produced a slower chronic eGFR decline in the studied high-CV-risk type 2 diabetes population.
Do not overextend: This publication does not prove that all SGLT2 inhibitors are interchangeable, does not directly compare empagliflozin with dapagliflozin, and does not itself answer current inpatient formulary policy.
Appraisal constraint: The renal analysis was secondary, was not formally powered a priori for microvascular outcomes, used nominal testing without multiplicity correction, and includes explicitly post hoc analyses.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance — 10/10Directly addresses empagliflozin renal outcomes in type 2 diabetes and is central to SGLT2 cardiorenal evidence.

Evidence basis: Large prespecified renal analysis embedded in EMPA-REG OUTCOME.

Principal limiting factor: It is drug-specific and does not establish dapagliflozin equivalence or an unrestricted class effect.

Would change score: Direct head-to-head or class-comparative renal evidence would broaden formulary translation.

Novelty — 9/10At publication, this was pivotal evidence that an SGLT2 inhibitor altered clinically relevant renal outcomes beyond glycemic effects.

Evidence basis: Prespecified renal event analyses plus eGFR trajectory and post-treatment reversal.

Principal limiting factor: Some renal components were surrogate or low-frequency, and later evidence is outside this source packet.

Would change score: Independent replication across broader kidney-risk populations would strengthen novelty-to-generality.

Method strength — 8/10Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled parent trial with prespecified renal endpoints and detailed protocol/SAP provenance.

Evidence basis: 7020 treated participants, centralized labs, adjusted Cox models, MMRM, protocol and publication SAP.

Principal limiting factor: No a priori microvascular power calculation; nominal alpha without multiplicity correction; some renal analyses were post hoc.

Would change score: A renal-primary trial with prespecified multiplicity control would raise the score.

Evidence strength — 8/10The main renal composite and several clinically important components favored empagliflozin with reported uncertainty.

Evidence basis: Incident/worsening nephropathy HR 0.61; creatinine-doubling HR 0.56; RRT HR 0.45; coherent chronic eGFR slope pattern.

Principal limiting factor: Composite benefit was heavily influenced by macroalbuminuria; hard-event counts were small and one hard composite was post hoc.

Would change score: More hard renal events and renal-primary powering would raise confidence.

External validity — 6/10The multinational design helps transportability, but enrollment was deliberately restricted to high cardiovascular risk with eGFR ≥30.

Evidence basis: 590 sites in 42 countries; >99% established cardiovascular disease.

Principal limiting factor: Lower-risk diabetes, eGFR <30, and Black participants were insufficiently represented for strong direct generalization.

Would change score: Broader-risk and more diverse enrollment would raise the score.

Implementation value — 8/10The paper clearly establishes the renal outcome signal and eGFR trajectory needed for evidence synthesis and formulary reasoning.

Evidence basis: Dose arms, renal definitions, safety stratification, longitudinal eGFR, protocol/SAP detail.

Principal limiting factor: It does not answer interchangeability between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin or current inpatient-use policy by itself.

Would change score: Comparative and contemporary practice evidence is needed for those decisions.

Bottom line: Read first. High confidence in the appraisal because the supplied packet includes the primary article, full appendix, trial protocol, and Publication SAP, allowing the renal claims to be checked against their endpoint and analysis provenance.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Preserve for evidence historyThe article documents the renal signal that emerged within EMPA-REG OUTCOME and the eGFR pattern that became central to interpreting SGLT2 kidney effects.
