One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first for an empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin formulary evidence review. The study gives a direct, large, clinically interpretable comparison and finds no material cardiovascular difference. Its causal strength remains below a randomized head-to-head trial.

The authors emulated a pragmatic target trial in Danish registries for adults with treated type 2 diabetes who initiated empagliflozin or dapagliflozin from 2014 through 2020. Stabilized inverse probability treatment weighting addressed 57 measured confounders. The primary intention-to-treat analysis used competing-risk cumulative incidence methods and followed participants for up to six years. A per-protocol analysis, prespecified subgroup work, three-year analyses, weight truncation, and an alternate cardiovascular-death definition tested robustness.

The primary result was a comparative null. Six-year MACE risk was 10.0% in both groups, with a risk difference of 0.0 percentage points, 95% CI -0.9 to 1.0, and risk ratio 1.00, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.11. Myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, heart failure, cardiovascular death, and all-cause death were also similar. Results stayed similar in ASCVD and heart-failure strata, at three years, after weight truncation, and in the per-protocol analysis. Nominal age, civil-status, and employment subgroup differences are weak evidence because of multiplicity and the authors explicitly treat them as exploratory.

The practical result is narrow but useful. This study gives no cardiovascular-effectiveness reason to prefer empagliflozin over dapagliflozin or the reverse for the studied population. A formulary decision still needs evidence on current labeled indications, safety, kidney outcomes, inpatient handling, cost, access, and patient-specific factors.

Section condensation

Background and clinical question

Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin both have proven cardiovascular benefits versus placebo in selected populations, but the article identifies a lack of direct randomized cardiovascular comparison. The clinical question is whether intraclass differences translate into different cardiovascular outcomes in routine care.

Methods

The study used Denmark's linked national registries to emulate a target trial. Adults with treated type 2 diabetes, age 18 years or older, with no prior or current SGLT2 inhibitor use were eligible. Insulin monotherapy alone did not qualify. Follow-up ended at the relevant outcome, emigration, death, six years, or December 31, 2021.

Stabilized treatment weights used 57 potential confounders. Missing laboratory and socioeconomic data were multiply imputed with 10 imputations. Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence estimates incorporated competing events. The per-protocol analysis added inverse probability censoring weights with time-updated covariates. Bootstrap resampling used 500 samples. The supplement provides the target-trial specification, code lists, weight distributions, three-year results, truncated-weight results, cohort flow, balance plots, and dose-specific curves.

Population

The eligible cohort contained 57,276 initiators. Baseline age, sex, diabetes duration, hemoglobin A1c, eGFR, and lipid values were similar. Before weighting, empagliflozin users had somewhat more atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and insulin use, while dapagliflozin users more often used DPP-4 inhibitors. Measured covariates were well balanced after weighting.

Median follow-up was 3.2 years. Treatment discontinuation was common, 40% with empagliflozin and 45% with dapagliflozin. Switching to another SGLT2 inhibitor occurred in 1.2% and 4.5%, respectively.

Primary and secondary results

Six-year MACE risk was 10.0% in each group. The adjusted risk difference was 0.0 percentage points, 95% CI -0.9 to 1.0, and the risk ratio was 1.00, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.11. Figure 1 also shows no material difference for MI, ischemic stroke, heart failure, cardiovascular death, or all-cause death.

Three-year MACE risk was 5.2% versus 5.0%, RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.94 to 1.11. Truncating weights at 10 produced a six-year RR of 1.00, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.10. The alternate cardiovascular-death definition produced RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.77 to 1.13. Per-protocol MACE risk was 9.1% versus 8.8%, RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80 to 1.32.

Subgroups

Results remained compatible with no material treatment difference among participants with or without ASCVD and with or without heart failure. Age strata, civil status, and employment produced nominal differences, including RR 0.77 for age under 55 and RR 1.25 for age 70 or older. The authors caution that these could be chance findings from the large number of analyses.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the findings as support for similar cardiovascular effectiveness between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin and as compatible with an intraclass effect for these two agents. They stop short of claiming a class effect for every SGLT2 inhibitor.

The main limitation is residual confounding from nonrandom treatment assignment. Calendar-time prescribing could have shifted higher-risk patients toward empagliflozin after EMPA-REG OUTCOME. Cardiovascular-death classification may be imperfect because of the low autopsy rate. Multiplicity weakens isolated subgroup findings.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 9/10

Direct head-to-head cardiovascular comparison of the two SGLT2 inhibitors most relevant to a formulary choice.

Evidence basis. Large nationwide T2D cohort and clinically important cardiovascular endpoints.

Limiting factor. It does not compare safety, inpatient use, renal indications, acquisition cost, or all reasons for choosing one agent.

What would change it. A broader comparative-effectiveness package covering those domains would raise this score.

Novelty: 8/10

A direct empagliflozin-dapagliflozin target-trial emulation addresses a question not answered by placebo-controlled outcome trials.

Evidence basis. The study reports six-year comparative absolute and relative cardiovascular risks.

Limiting factor. It remains observational and follows prior indirect and cohort comparisons.

What would change it. A randomized head-to-head trial would add substantially more novelty.

Method strength: 8/10

The target-trial design, national linkage, 57-confounder weighting, multiple imputation, competing-risk methods, and sensitivity analyses are strong for observational research.

Evidence basis. Measured balance after weighting and consistent ITT, per-protocol, three-year, and truncated-weight analyses.

Limiting factor. Treatment was not randomized, so residual confounding remains possible.

What would change it. Random assignment or a credible negative-control framework would strengthen causal inference.

Evidence strength: 7/10

The primary null estimate is precise enough to exclude a large six-year relative difference in MACE, and secondary results point in the same direction.

Evidence basis. MACE RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.91-1.11; RD 0.0 percentage points, 95% CI -0.9 to 1.0.

Limiting factor. Several subgroup estimates are imprecise, and multiplicity produced nominal subgroup differences that the authors treat as exploratory.

What would change it. Replication in other health systems or a randomized comparison would raise confidence.

External validity: 7/10

The cohort covers more than 57,000 routine-care initiators with broad T2D characteristics.

Evidence basis. Nationwide Danish registries provide complete follow-up and real-world prescribing.

Limiting factor. The single-country setting, 2014-2020 treatment era, and local prescribing patterns may not generalize to current practice elsewhere.

What would change it. Replication in diverse countries and a more recent indication era would raise this score.

Implementation value: 8/10

The study supports avoiding a cardiovascular-effectiveness preference between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin when choosing between them for treated T2D.

Evidence basis. Six-year MACE and component outcomes were similar, including ASCVD and HF strata.

Limiting factor. It cannot by itself establish broad therapeutic interchangeability because comparative safety, renal outcomes, current indications, inpatient holding rules, and economics were not studied.

What would change it. A current multi-domain formulary review would make the result more actionable.

Main threat to validity. Residual confounding can still produce a comparative null or a modest bias despite excellent measured balance. The design materially reduces, but cannot remove, that problem.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use this for

A direct cardiovascular-effectiveness comparison when asking whether empagliflozin or dapagliflozin should be favored solely for six-year cardiovascular outcomes in treated T2D.

Do not use this for

A stand-alone declaration that the drugs are fully interchangeable across safety, kidney disease, heart-failure indications, perioperative/inpatient use, dose equivalence, or cost.

Formulary implication

The result removes a strong cardiovascular-effectiveness argument for keeping both agents solely because one is expected to outperform the other on MACE. Other formulary domains still need separate evidence.
