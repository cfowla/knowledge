One-page synthesis

The analysis included 6262 DELIVER participants; mean age was 72 years, 56% were male, mean eGFR was 61 mL/min/1.73 m², and 49% had eGFR below 60. For cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure, dapagliflozin versus placebo produced hazard ratios of 0.84 (95% CI 0.70–1.00), 0.68 (0.54–0.87), and 0.93 (0.76–1.14) across baseline eGFR strata ≥60, 45–<60, and <45 mL/min/1.73 m², respectively. The eGFR-category interaction P value was .16 and the continuous-eGFR interaction P value was .45.

Dapagliflozin produced an expected acute eGFR decrease by month 1, with a between-group difference of −3.3 mL/min/1.73 m², then slowed chronic decline from month 1 onward by 1.4 mL/min/1.73 m²/year. The post hoc kidney composite occurred in 79/3131 dapagliflozin and 73/3131 placebo participants, HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.79–1.49). The eGFR trajectory is therefore better supported than a hard kidney-event benefit in this analysis.

Section condensation

Introduction / background

The prespecified analysis addresses whether baseline kidney function modifies dapagliflozin’s cardiovascular benefit in HF with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction and characterizes kidney-function trajectories during treatment. The clinical issue is important because CKD is common in this HF population and an early SGLT2-associated eGFR decline can be misinterpreted without separating acute from chronic slope.

Methods

DELIVER enrolled adults aged at least 40 years with symptomatic heart failure, LVEF greater than 40%, elevated NT-proBNP, and structural heart disease; notable exclusions included eGFR below 25 mL/min/1.73 m² and type 1 diabetes. Participants were randomized 1:1 to dapagliflozin 10 mg daily or matching placebo. Cox interaction models assessed treatment effects across kidney-function strata, and repeated-measures mixed models estimated acute, chronic, and total eGFR slopes from measurements at baseline and months 1, 4, 12, 24, and 36.

Results

Cardiovascular treatment effects did not show statistically significant heterogeneity by baseline eGFR category or continuous eGFR. Dapagliflozin caused an early eGFR decrease but subsequently slowed chronic eGFR decline. The kidney composite was sparse and did not demonstrate benefit, with HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.79–1.49).

Discussion / conclusion

The authors interpret the findings as supporting dapagliflozin’s cardiovascular benefit across the kidney-function range represented in DELIVER and as showing a favorable longer-term eGFR trajectory after the initial treatment-related decline. The hard kidney-event analysis is substantially less informative than the cardiovascular and slope analyses because of low event counts.

Limitations

The sustained kidney composite was too infrequent for meaningful interpretation, leading to use of a non-sustained post hoc definition. Median follow-up was 2.3 years. Urine albumin excretion was not measured. Analyses used a two-sided alpha of .05 without multiplicity correction. Advanced CKD below eGFR 25 was excluded. These limitations constrain hard kidney-outcome and selected subgroup inferences.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance — 9/10

Rationale: Direct fit for SGLT2 inhibitor, HFmrEF/HFpEF, and kidney-function questions.

Evidence basis: The analysis directly evaluates treatment effects across eGFR strata and longitudinal eGFR change in 6262 randomized DELIVER participants.

Principal limiting factor: It is a prespecified secondary analysis rather than a kidney-outcome trial.

Would raise/lower: Dedicated kidney endpoints with greater event accrual would raise relevance for nephroprotection; a narrower or nonclinical population would lower it.

Novelty — 7/10

Rationale: Adds a useful kidney-function analysis to an established parent trial.

Evidence basis: It combines treatment-effect interaction analyses with acute, chronic, and total eGFR slopes.

Principal limiting factor: Dapagliflozin’s parent-trial cardiovascular efficacy was already established.

Would raise/lower: Definitive new kidney-event evidence or novel mechanistic measurements would raise the score; purely repetitive subgroup reporting would lower it.

Method strength — 8/10

Rationale: Randomized parent trial, prespecified kidney analyses, and repeated renal measurements provide a strong analytic base.

Evidence basis: Randomized dapagliflozin-placebo allocation, prespecified eGFR interaction analyses, and repeated-measures slope modeling.

Principal limiting factor: The kidney composite definition became post hoc because sustained events were too sparse, and multiplicity was not corrected.

Would raise/lower: Prespecified adequately powered hard kidney endpoints with multiplicity control would raise the score; more post hoc endpoint changes would lower it.

Evidence strength — 8/10

Rationale: Cardiovascular interaction and eGFR-slope findings are well supported, but hard kidney-event inference is weak.

Evidence basis: Interaction P values of .16 and .45 and quantified acute/chronic eGFR changes are supported by the randomized dataset; the kidney composite HR 1.08 has a wide 95% CI of 0.79–1.49.

Principal limiting factor: Only 152 post hoc kidney-composite events occurred across both groups.

Would raise/lower: More kidney events with a prespecified sustained endpoint and narrower uncertainty would raise the score; unstable subgroup estimates would lower it.

External validity — 7/10

Rationale: The international HF population included substantial CKD representation but excludes important advanced-CKD and albuminuria-defined groups.

Evidence basis: Forty-nine percent had eGFR below 60, but eGFR below 25 was excluded and urine albumin excretion was not measured.

Principal limiting factor: Results cannot be directly extended to eGFR below 25 or stratified by albuminuria.

Would raise/lower: Inclusion of advanced CKD and albuminuria-based analyses would raise the score; a more selected cohort would lower it.

Implementation value — 9/10

Rationale: The findings are highly useful for interpreting dapagliflozin treatment across moderate kidney dysfunction and distinguishing the early eGFR dip from chronic trajectory.

Evidence basis: Treatment-effect estimates across eGFR strata plus the −3.3 acute and +1.4/year chronic between-group eGFR differences provide directly interpretable clinical context.

Principal limiting factor: The paper does not establish a hard renal indication from kidney-event outcomes.

Would raise/lower: Stronger kidney-event evidence and more granular monitoring thresholds would raise the score; inconsistent slope findings would lower it.

Overall appraisal: Cardiovascular interaction findings are credible within the enrolled eGFR range because treatment was randomized and the kidney-function analysis was prespecified. The chronic eGFR slope result is clinically interpretable. The analysis does not establish reduction in end-stage kidney disease or kidney death.

Implementation / practice takeaways

  • Do not interpret the early dapagliflozin-associated eGFR decline in isolation; the trial separates that acute change from a slower chronic decline thereafter.
  • The cardiovascular benefit did not show significant heterogeneity across the kidney-function range enrolled in DELIVER.
  • Do not use this analysis alone to claim a reduction in hard kidney outcomes; those events were sparse and the reported composite was post hoc.
  • Do not extrapolate directly to eGFR below 25 mL/min/1.73 m² or to albuminuria-defined subgroups from this analysis.
