One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Management of anemia in CKD should be individualized around the cause of anemia, iron status, symptoms, transfusion risk, ESA/HIF-PHI safety, transplant candidacy, and patient preferences rather than a single hemoglobin threshold.

The 2026 update expands the 2012 framework across diagnosis, iron therapy, ESAs, HIF-PHIs, and transfusion. It introduces pathophysiology-oriented iron terminology—systemic iron deficiency and iron-restricted erythropoiesis—and gives concrete operational thresholds for iron initiation, ESA initiation/maintenance, monitoring, HIF-PHI stopping rules, and transfusion avoidance. The strongest implementation signal is not one universal target: it is repeated use of patient-specific risk/benefit assessment and shared decision-making.

The formal recommendation set is unusually cautious: all 8 graded recommendations use very-low-certainty (D) evidence; 7 are Level 2 suggestions and one (ESA maintenance Hb below 11.5 g/dl) is Level 1. This does not make the guideline methodologically weak. The evidence-review process is extensive, but many clinically central questions have sparse hard-outcome data, imprecision, heterogeneous trial designs, or indirectness. The 49 Practice Points therefore carry much of the day-to-day implementation detail and should be recognized as consensus guidance rather than RCT-derived recommendations.

Iron
Initiate in CKD G5HD at ferritin ≤500 μg/L and TSAT ≤30%; prefer i.v. iron in HD. In non-HD/PD, initiation depends on ferritin/TSAT combinations; oral vs i.v. route is individualized.
ESA
Use ESA rather than HIF-PHI as first-line after correctable causes are addressed. In dialysis, consider initiation around Hb 9–10 g/dl. In adults on ESA, target Hb below 11.5 g/dl.
HIF-PHI
Alternative role is constrained by long-term safety uncertainty. Avoid in selected high-risk groups; monitor closely; discontinue after 3–4 months without response.
Transfusion
Base chronic transfusion on symptoms/signs rather than an arbitrary Hb threshold and minimize exposure in transplant candidates because of allosensitization.

Guideline development and evidence base

KDIGO commissioned the Johns Hopkins University Evidence-based Practice Center as the Evidence Review Team. The ERT developed registered review protocols, searched the literature, extracted data with confirmation by a second reviewer, used the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool with independent dual assessment, synthesized evidence with random-effects meta-analysis, and graded certainty using GRADE. The Work Group wrote recommendations and Practice Points and graded recommendation strength.

The evidence program screened 11,094 citations and included 261 RCTs from 293 reports. The systematic reviews addressed iron dosing, HIF-PHI versus ESA, ESA therapy/targets, and HIF-PHI versus placebo/other HIF-PHIs. The data supplement provides search strategies, development/reporting checklists, 49 SoF tables focused on treatment comparisons (S4–S52), and four PRISMA diagrams. Public review occurred in November 2024; the supplement states that future updating is intended but no fixed timeframe is provided.

Evidence-method caveat. Methodological rigor at the guideline level does not remove limitations in the underlying evidence. The guideline itself repeatedly grades critical treatment evidence as very low certainty, particularly for iron hard outcomes and HIF-PHI safety comparisons.

Chapter 1 — Diagnosis and evaluation

The chapter separates anemia diagnosis from iron-state characterization and emphasizes looking beyond “renal anemia” when the initial workup is unrevealing. Screening includes CBC, reticulocytes, ferritin, and TSAT. Figure 5 supplies minimum Hb testing intervals (annual in G3, twice yearly in G4, every 3 months in G5/G5D), while Figure 6 expands evaluation to hemolysis, inflammation, nutritional deficiency, liver/thyroid/parathyroid disease, monoclonal proteins, and occult blood loss.

The terminology update is clinically useful: “systemic iron deficiency” describes depletion of body iron stores, whereas “iron-restricted erythropoiesis” describes inadequate circulating iron availability despite preserved stores. Severe iron deficiency or unexplained microcytosis should prompt consideration of blood-loss evaluation rather than automatic attribution to CKD.

Practice Point 1.2.1
In people with chronic kidney disease (CKD), test for anemia at referral, regularly during followup, and when anemia is suspected based on symptoms (Figure 5). Test for anemia with the following set: complete blood count, reticulocytes (reticulocyte production index), ferritin, and transferrin saturation (TSAT) (Figure 6).
Source: S18
Practice Point 1.2.2
In people with anemia and CKD in whom the initial tests do not reveal the cause, consider an expanded panel to identify potential underlying causes as warranted based on the clinical scenario:; • Blood smear review; • Haptoglobin; • Lactate dehydrogenase; • C-reactive protein; • Vitamin B12; • Folate; • Liver function tests; • Serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation, serum free light chains, and urinary Bence-Jones protein; • Thyroid-stimulating hormone; • Parathyroid hormone; • Fecal occult blood test.
Source: S20
Practice Point 1.2.3
In people with anemia and CKD who have ferritin <45 ng/ml (<45 μg/l) or microcytic anemia (mean corpuscular volume <80 fl) in the absence of measured ferritin or known genetic cause, and where the cause of iron deficiency is uncertain, consider clinical evaluation for blood loss. Referral to gastroenterologists/gynecologists/urologists may be appropriate to identify the cause.
Source: S20

Chapter 2 — Iron therapy

For CKD G5HD, KDIGO suggests initiating iron at ferritin ≤500 μg/L and TSAT ≤30% and prefers i.v. iron when starting therapy. A proactive i.v. strategy is favored in HD; routine iron can reasonably be withheld when ferritin exceeds 700 μg/L or TSAT is ≥40%. For people not receiving HD, initiation thresholds are lower and route selection is explicitly preference-, severity-, tolerability-, availability-, and cost-sensitive.

The pivotal quantitative anchor is PIVOTAL: 2,141 participants with CKD G5HD were randomized to proactive i.v. iron sucrose (400 mg monthly unless ferritin >700 μg/L or TSAT ≥40%) versus reactive 0–400 mg dosing triggered by ferritin <200 μg/L or TSAT <20%. After a median 2.1 years, the cardiovascular composite reported by KDIGO was lower with proactive therapy (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.64–1.00); adverse events were similar (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.95–1.08), and transfusion/ESA requirements were lower. KDIGO nevertheless rates the overall iron evidence in CKD G5HD as very low certainty and cautions against treating the PIVOTAL stopping limits as proven optimal targets.

Recommendation 2.1
In people with anemia and CKD G5 receiving hemodialysis (CKD G5HD), we suggest initiating iron therapy if ferritin ≤500 ng/ml (≤500 μg/l) and TSAT ≤30% (2D).

Chapter 3 — ESAs, HIF-PHIs, and hyporesponsiveness

After correction of reversible causes, KDIGO suggests ESA rather than HIF-PHI as first-line treatment. For dialysis, ESA initiation is suggested at Hb ≤9–10 g/dl. For non-dialysis CKD, kidney transplant recipients, and children, initiation is individualized around symptoms, transfusion avoidance, and treatment harms. In adults receiving ESA, the single Level 1 recommendation is to maintain Hb below 11.5 g/dl.

The harm signal from higher ESA targets remains central. TREAT found no benefit on its primary composite outcomes with darbepoetin targeting about 13 g/dl but increased stroke risk; CHOIR found a higher hazard for death/MI/heart-failure hospitalization/stroke with a 13.5 versus 11.3 g/dl target. HIF-PHIs generally correct Hb and are often noninferior to ESAs for erythropoietic efficacy, but KDIGO reports very-low-certainty critical-outcome evidence for HIF-PHI versus ESA in both dialysis and non-dialysis CKD, with lingering concern about MACE, thrombosis, malignancy, and uncertain long-term safety. The guideline therefore adds explicit high-risk exclusions, monitoring, event-triggered suspension, and 3–4-month stopping rules for nonresponse.

Practice Point 3.1.1
In people with anemia and CKD (whether receiving dialysis or not), the decision to use erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) or hypoxia-inducible factor–prolyl hydroxylase inhibitors (HIF-PHIs) to raise Hb should be made through a shared decision-making process, considering each individual’s symptoms, potential for harm from red blood cell (RBC) transfusions, and potential risk of adverse events (e.g., stroke, cardiovascular event, and cancer).

Practice Point 3.1.2
In people with anemia and CKD, address all correctable causes of anemia, including iron deficiency, prior to the initiation of treatment with an ESA or a HIF-PHI (Figure 8).

In people with anemia and CKD G5D or CKD not receiving dialysis with initial or subsequent ESA hyporesponsiveness, identify and treat the underlying causes of ESA hyporesponsiveness, if possible.

Practice Point 3.7.2

In people with anemia and CKD, if a decision is made to use HIF-PHI for the treatment of ESA hyporesponsiveness, use the lowest dose that alleviates anemia-related symptoms or reduces the risk of requiring an RBC transfusion.

In people with CKD, anemia, and ESA hyporesponsiveness, if a desired erythropoietic response has not been achieved after 3–4 months of initiating HIF-PHIs, discontinue treatment.

Chapter 4 — RBC transfusion

Transfusion is framed as a backup/urgent modality within a comprehensive anemia strategy rather than a routine Hb-driven treatment. In chronic anemia, KDIGO says the trigger should be symptoms and signs rather than an arbitrary Hb threshold. In acute hemorrhage, unstable coronary disease, or when rapid preoperative correction is required, urgency can outweigh longer-term risks.

Transplant candidacy changes the balance because allosensitization can delay or compromise transplantation. Historical prospective data cited by KDIGO found HLA antibodies in 28% after transfusion exposure, with higher sensitization among previously pregnant/transplanted patients. More broadly, general-population RCT evidence summarized in Chapter 4 supports restrictive transfusion strategies (often 7–8 g/dl) over liberal 9–10 g/dl thresholds without worse mortality or major morbidity, but KDIGO explicitly notes the paucity of CKD-specific randomized transfusion-threshold evidence.

Practice Point 4.1
In people with anemia and CKD, use RBC transfusion as part of a comprehensive treatment strategy, carefully weighing risks and benefits in a shared decision-making process.
Source: S28
Practice Point 4.2
In people with anemia and CKD eligible for organ transplantation, avoid, when possible, RBC transfusions to minimize the risk of allosensitization.
Source: S28
Practice Point 4.3
In people with CKD and chronic anemia, consider that the benefits of RBC transfusion may outweigh its harms in people in whom; • ESA or HIF-PHI therapy is ineffective (e.g., those with hemoglobinopathies, bone marrow failure, or ESA or HIF-PHI resistance); • ESA or HIF-PHI therapy may be harmful (e.g., those with previous or current malignancy or previous stroke).
Source: S28
Practice Point 4.4
In people with anemia and CKD, base the decision to transfuse on symptoms and signs caused by anemia rather than an arbitrary Hb threshold.
Source: S28
Practice Point 4.5
In people with CKD and acute anemia, consider RBC transfusion when the benefits outweigh the risks, including; • When rapid correction of anemia is required to stabilize the patient’s condition (e.g., acute hemorrhage or unstable coronary artery disease); • When rapid preoperative Hb correction is required.
Source: S28
Practice Point 4.6
Consider implementing strategies at the individual, organizational, and public health policy levels to reduce RBC transfusions in people with CKD (Table 11).
Source: S28

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is the central 2026 global reference for anemia management in CKD and is highly implementation-ready, but recommendation-specific certainty must be preserved.
10/10 Relevance

Rationale: Directly governs anemia evaluation and treatment across CKD populations.

Evidence: Diagnosis, iron, ESA/HIF-PHI, transfusion, dialysis, transplant, and pediatric algorithms.

Limit: Not a drug label or local protocol.

Would change: Lower only if the use case excludes CKD anemia.

8/10 Novelty

Rationale: Major update to 2012 with new HIF-PHI content and revised iron terminology/operational thresholds.

Evidence: New therapy class, 2024 evidence cutoff, revised algorithms.

Limit: Many principles build on established ESA/iron evidence.

Would change: Higher with stronger trial evidence establishing new patient-centered outcome targets.

9/10 Method strength

Rationale: Independent ERT, registered systematic reviews, dual review, Cochrane RoB, meta-analysis, GRADE, public review.

Evidence: 11,094 screened citations; 261 RCTs/293 reports; detailed SoF supplement.

Limit: Some synthesis uses conventional DerSimonian–Laird random effects and the source cannot overcome gaps in trial design/reporting.

Would change: Higher with more external replication of guideline evidence judgments and uniformly robust trial data.

6/10 Evidence strength

Rationale: Strong evidence-review process but low certainty for the actual management questions.

Evidence: All 8 graded recommendations are certainty D; multiple SoF tables cite serious risk of bias/imprecision.

Limit: Sparse hard outcomes, heterogeneous Hb/iron strategies, limited long-term HIF-PHI safety.

Would change: Higher with adequately powered, long-term RCTs focused on mortality, MACE, QoL, transfusion, and safety.

8/10 External validity

Rationale: Global, multi-population scope spanning adults, children, dialysis modalities, and transplant recipients.

Evidence: Population-specific recommendations and five algorithms.

Limit: Trial representation, resource availability, iron practices, and HIF-PHI approvals vary substantially by region.

Would change: Higher with more geographically representative trials and pediatric/transplant evidence.

10/10 Implementation value

Rationale: Concrete thresholds, monitoring intervals, dosing tables, reaction algorithms, and stopping rules.

Evidence: 57 formal guidance statements, 16 tables, 14 figures, 5 population algorithms.

Limit: Local formulary, labeling, reimbursement, dialysis workflow, and lab standards still require adaptation.

Would change: Lower only where local policy/regulatory constraints override specific therapies.

Main strengths

  • Broad scope with explicit population distinctions rather than a single anemia pathway.
  • Transparent separation of graded recommendations from consensus Practice Points.
  • Detailed supplement makes search, risk-of-bias, certainty, and meta-analytic evidence auditable.
  • High operational specificity for iron/ESA/HIF-PHI monitoring and transfusion stewardship.

Main limitations

  • Every graded recommendation is based on very-low-certainty evidence, despite differing recommendation strength.
  • HIF-PHI long-term safety and comparative patient-centered outcomes remain uncertain.
  • Pediatric, transplant, PD, and ESA-hyporesponsive populations have especially thin direct evidence.
  • Many Practice Points are expert guidance, not systematic-review-derived treatment effects.

Practice and implementation takeaways

Screening/workup

Build CBC + reticulocytes + ferritin + TSAT into CKD anemia workflows, with stage-based minimum monitoring and an expanded nonrenal workup when unexplained.

Iron

Separate initiation thresholds from withholding limits; do not convert PIVOTAL stopping limits into an assumed universal “target.”

ESA safety

Use the lowest effective dose, avoid rapid Hb rise, monitor 2–4 weeks after changes, and maintain adult Hb below 11.5 g/dl.

HIF-PHI governance

Make indication, country-specific labeling, high-risk exclusions, drug interactions, thyroid monitoring for roxadustat, event-triggered holds, and nonresponse stopping rules explicit.

Transfusion stewardship

Use symptoms/clinical urgency rather than a fixed chronic threshold; explicitly flag transplant candidacy and prior sensitizing events.

CDS modeling

Store recommendation strength/certainty separately from Practice Point status; preserve population, dialysis modality, treatment class, thresholds, units, monitoring interval, exceptions, and provenance.
