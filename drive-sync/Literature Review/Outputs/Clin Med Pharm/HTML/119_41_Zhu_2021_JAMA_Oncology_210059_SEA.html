One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In chemotherapy-refractory, IDH1-mutant advanced cholangiocarcinoma, ivosidenib produced a numerically favorable final overall-survival result despite extensive placebo-to-ivosidenib crossover, with supportive safety and selected quality-of-life findings. The cleanest randomized OS estimate was HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.56-1.12; one-sided P=.09), while the stronger HR 0.49 result depended on a prespecified RPSFT crossover-adjustment model.

The trial randomized 187 patients 2:1 to ivosidenib or placebo. By the final May 31, 2020 cutoff, 43 of 61 placebo-assigned patients (about 70%) had crossed over. Median OS was 10.3 vs 7.5 months in the ITT analysis; 12-month survival was 43% vs 36%. The RPSFT model reconstructed a 5.1-month placebo median and produced an adjusted HR of 0.49. The protocol is important for interpretation because it explicitly anticipated that crossover would prevent direct measurement of the true survival effect and left the final OS analysis with only 64% power under an assumed HR of 0.67.

Safety was generally manageable in this heavily pretreated population. Nausea was common, grade ≥3 ascites occurred in both arms, QT prolongation was more frequent with ivosidenib, and no treatment-related deaths were reported. QOL physical functioning favored ivosidenib at cycles 2 and 3, but these analyses were limited by substantial attrition, very small placebo samples at later time points, and no multiplicity adjustment for the supplemental QOL tests.

Best useUnderstand the randomized evidence base for ivosidenib in previously treated IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma, especially the contrast between ITT OS and crossover-adjusted OS.

Section condensation

Introduction

The article frames cholangiocarcinoma as an aggressive cancer with limited outcomes after standard chemotherapy and highlights IDH1 mutations as a targetable molecular subset. It recaps the earlier ClarIDHy PFS result (IRC HR 0.37) and positions this report as the final OS, updated safety, comutation, and QOL analysis.

The distinction matters: PFS was the protocol primary endpoint and had already met significance; this paper's central new contribution is final OS under substantial crossover, not a new primary PFS analysis.

Methods

Adults with centrally confirmed IDH1-mutant nonresectable/metastatic cholangiocarcinoma, ECOG 0-1, adequate organ function, and progression after 1-2 prior regimens were randomized 2:1 to ivosidenib 500 mg daily or matched placebo. Randomization used block size 6 and stratification by one vs two prior advanced-disease regimens. Placebo crossover to open-label ivosidenib after radiographic progression was built into the design.

OS was a key secondary endpoint tested only after significant PFS within a fixed-sequence strategy. The primary OS analysis was ITT; a prespecified RPSFT model adjusted for switching under a common-treatment-effect assumption. Supplement 2 specifies QOL mixed-effects repeated-measures models and states that QOL P values were not multiplicity-adjusted.

Results — population and efficacy

Of 231 screened patients, 187 were randomized (126 ivosidenib; 61 placebo). Ninety-three percent had metastatic disease, 47% had received two prior lines, and R132C was the dominant IDH1 variant. Forty-three placebo patients crossed over. With 150 deaths, median OS was 10.3 vs 7.5 months and HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.56-1.12; one-sided P=.09). Twelve-month survival was 43% vs 36%.

RPSFT adjustment yielded a modeled placebo median OS of 5.1 months and HR 0.49 (95% CI 0.34-0.70; one-sided P<.001). The model is a supportive analysis rather than a replacement for the randomized ITT contrast; the protocol itself anticipated substantial survival-estimation difficulty because crossover was allowed.

Results — safety

Nausea was the most common all-grade TEAE before crossover (42% vs 29%). Grade ≥3 ascites occurred in 9% vs 7%; anemia in 7% vs 0%; bilirubin increase in 6% vs 2%; and hyponatremia in 6% vs 10%. Serious TEAEs occurred in 34% vs 24%, with serious events judged treatment-related in 3 ivosidenib patients (2%).

QT prolongation was reported in 10% vs 3%. Dose reductions for TEAEs were uncommon (4% vs 0%), discontinuation due to TEAEs occurred in 7% vs 8%, and no treatment-related deaths were reported.

Results — quality of life and biomarkers

Physical functioning favored ivosidenib at cycle 2 day 1 (difference 11.0 points, 95% CI 4.23-17.73; P=.002) and cycle 3 day 1 (12.3, 95% CI 3.85-20.78; P=.004). QLQ-C30 pain also favored ivosidenib at cycle 2 (difference -10.4, 95% CI -20.18 to -0.52; P=.04). These nominal comparisons are tempered by shrinking sample sizes and no multiplicity adjustment.

PIK3CA, KRAS, BRAF, and FGFR2 were the most frequent baseline comutations. No single-gene baseline comutation showed a significant association with OS, PFS, or treatment duration; the lack of on-study biopsies limited resistance-mechanism analysis.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the combined efficacy, tolerability, and QOL results as evidence of clinical benefit and emphasize the therapeutic value of a noncytotoxic targeted option. They also compare the 10.3-month median OS favorably with historical second-line data.

The principal internal-validity issue for final OS is treatment switching: 70% of placebo-assigned patients crossed over. Additional limitations are sparse later QOL data, nominal unadjusted QOL P values, small subgroups, and no serial tissue biopsies for resistance analysis.

Conclusion

The source concludes that ivosidenib is well tolerated and clinically beneficial in advanced IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma. A more conservative evidence interpretation is that randomized disease-control evidence and safety are strong, while the final OS effect is directionally favorable in ITT and more pronounced only after a model-based crossover adjustment.

Critical appraisal point. The article's ITT OS estimate and RPSFT-adjusted estimate should not be collapsed into one effect. Randomization directly supports the ITT comparison; the RPSFT result addresses a counterfactual no-switching scenario and requires additional modeling assumptions. The protocol explicitly states that crossover was expected to make the true survival effect difficult to measure and that the trial would not be powered to detect a meaningful survival effect under some assumptions.
QOL caution. The later QOL findings are clinically interesting but fragile: analyzable sample sizes fell sharply, placebo n was only 21 at cycle 2 and 9 at cycle 3 for QLQ-C30 model estimates, and Supplement 2 states that P values were not adjusted for multiplicity.

Confidence in appraisal: High for source-internal interpretation because the primary article, trial protocol, and eMethods/eResults were all available. Lower for present-day practice translation because no external/current verification was requested.
