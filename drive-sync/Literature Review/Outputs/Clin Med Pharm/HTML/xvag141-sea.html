One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Modern HFrEF care emerged through successive randomized-trial demonstrations of benefit from RAAS blockade/ARNI, evidence-based beta-blockers, MRAs, and SGLT2 inhibitors. Above 40% LVEF, evidence is more heterogeneous; SGLT2 inhibitors have the most consistent outcome benefit across the spectrum, with finerenone and obesity-targeted incretin therapies adding phenotype-specific evidence.

The review’s strongest feature is its compact trial map: five large tables and five figures connect drug classes to landmark trial populations, sample sizes, primary endpoints, and effect estimates. The source also separates foundational therapy from adjunctive/selected-use therapies such as intravenous iron, vericiguat, ivabradine, digitalis glycosides, and incretin-based agents.

The main limitation is methodological rather than factual: trial inclusion is curated rather than systematic, and evidence spans decades of changing background therapy, LVEF thresholds, and eligibility criteria. Direct comparison of effect sizes across classes or eras is therefore inappropriate. The authors explicitly identify real-world implementation and under-representation as unresolved problems.

Section condensation

Introduction

The review frames heart-failure pharmacotherapy as a transition from symptom-focused care to disease-modifying treatment supported by landmark randomized trials. Its explicit purpose is to assemble large trials that materially influenced international guidelines or clinical practice, organized by drug class and by reduced versus preserved LVEF when possible. The authors use a broad reduced-EF spectrum below 50% for historical trial synthesis while acknowledging contemporary HFrEF (≤40%) and HFmrEF (41%–49%) definitions.

The central interpretive caveat is that trial populations, background therapy, design, and LVEF thresholds changed substantially over time. The review therefore functions best as a historical/evidence map rather than as a basis for direct cross-trial ranking.

Diuretics

Loop diuretics are presented as indispensable for rapid relief of congestion and dyspnoea but not as classic disease-modifying mortality-reducing therapy. Their adoption preceded modern outcome-trial standards; observational associations between higher diuretic exposure and mortality are explicitly treated as potentially confounded by disease severity.

Practice implication within the source: use diuretics for euvolaemia and symptom control while recognizing electrolyte, neurohormonal, and renal risks and the absence of definitive mortality benefit.

Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors

CONSENSUS and SOLVD established survival benefit in symptomatic HFrEF, while SAVE, AIRE, and TRACE extended RAAS inhibition to post-MI LV dysfunction/HF. ATLAS supported dose optimization by reducing HF-related events despite no significant mortality difference between high- and low-dose lisinopril.

The authors conclude that ACE inhibitors reduce mortality, hospitalization, and progression across HFrEF. PEP-CHF was underpowered and neutral for its primary endpoint, so the source does not treat ACE inhibition as established disease-modifying therapy for HFpEF.

Angiotensin receptor blockers

ELITE II did not show losartan superiority over captopril; Val-HeFT reduced a morbidity/mortality composite mainly through fewer HF hospitalizations, and the CHARM program established benefit in ACE-inhibitor-intolerant HFrEF and incremental benefit when added to ACE inhibition. HEAAL supports higher losartan dosing in ACE-inhibitor-intolerant HFrEF.

VALIANT and OPTIMAAL reinforced ACE inhibition as the reference post-MI strategy, while CHARM-Preserved and I-PRESERVE were neutral for the main HFpEF outcomes. The review therefore positions ARBs mainly as alternatives when ACE inhibitors are not tolerated.

Angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors

PARADIGM-HF is treated as the pivotal trial establishing sacubitril/valsartan as a major HFrEF disease-modifying therapy (HR 0.80 for cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization versus enalapril). PIONEER-HF and TRANSITION support early initiation around hospitalization, although the former emphasized NT-proBNP and exploratory clinical events.

PARAGON-HF did not meet statistical significance for the overall HFpEF primary endpoint (rate ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.75–1.01; P=.06). The review therefore regards ARNI as foundational in HFrEF but more selective in HFpEF/HFmrEF.

Beta-adrenergic receptor antagonists

MERIT-HF, CIBIS-II, COPERNICUS, the US carvedilol program, and SENIORS underpin beta-blocker use in HFrEF; the review cites meta-analyses suggesting roughly 30%–35% relative all-cause mortality reduction with evidence-based agents. Bucindolol in BEST was neutral, emphasizing that benefit is not automatically a class effect for every beta-blocker.

For HFmrEF, evidence is largely subgroup/meta-analytic and appears rhythm dependent, with weaker benefit in atrial fibrillation. For HFpEF, large definitive mortality trials are absent; small trials and observational data do not establish a consistent HF-specific prognostic benefit.

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists

RALES, EPHESUS, and EMPHASIS-HF established substantial benefit with steroidal MRAs across severe HFrEF, post-MI LV dysfunction/HF, and milder symptomatic HFrEF. TOPCAT was neutral for its overall primary endpoint and showed important geographic heterogeneity.

FINEARTS-HF extended the class story with finerenone in LVEF ≥40%, reducing total worsening-HF events plus cardiovascular death (rate ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.95).

Sodium–glucose co-transporter-2 inhibitors

DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced showed similar relative reductions in cardiovascular-death/HF-event composites in HFrEF (HR 0.74 and 0.75). EMPEROR-Preserved and DELIVER extended benefit above 40% LVEF (HR 0.79 and 0.82), with consistency irrespective of diabetes status.

The review identifies SGLT2 inhibitors as the only pharmacological class with consistent benefit across the full LVEF spectrum. In HFpEF/HFmrEF, benefit was driven mainly by fewer HF events rather than a statistically significant cardiovascular-mortality reduction.

Intravenous iron

AFFIRM-AHF and IRONMAN support lower recurrent HF hospitalization/cardiovascular-event burdens with intravenous ferric carboxymaltose or ferric derisomaltose in iron-deficient patients with reduced or mildly reduced EF, especially in COVID-19 sensitivity analyses. The review notes that other large trials were neutral and that contemporary meta-analyses favor recurrent-HF-event reduction.

The source states that sufficiently powered randomized outcome data for HFpEF are not available.

Vericiguat

VICTORIA enrolled recently worsened HFrEF and reduced cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.82–0.98), whereas VICTOR in stable ambulatory HFrEF was neutral (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.83–1.04). The source emphasizes differences in baseline risk and recent worsening as clinically meaningful context.

A pooled individual-participant analysis is reported as statistically favorable (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.85–0.98; P=.0088), but VICTOR’s individual mortality findings are treated as exploratory. No phase III HFpEF RCT is reported.

Ivabradine

SHIFT showed an 18% relative reduction in the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening-HF hospitalization in symptomatic HFrEF patients in sinus rhythm with elevated heart rate on background therapy (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.75–0.90).

The review notes guideline and European regulatory heart-rate thresholds and reports that a small HFpEF trial lowering heart rate did not improve outcomes.

Digitalis glycosides

DIG found no all-cause mortality benefit with digoxin (risk ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.91–1.07) but reduced HF and overall hospitalizations. An ancillary preserved-EF analysis was neutral for major clinical outcomes.

DIGIT-HF is presented as contemporary evidence that digitoxin reduced all-cause death or worsening-HF hospitalization in symptomatic HFrEF (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.69–0.98).

Incretin-based therapies

The review distinguishes a strong obesity/HFpEF signal from uncertain HFrEF effects. STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM improved functional capacity and weight; their pooled hierarchical clinical analysis favored semaglutide (win ratio 1.65, 95% CI 1.42–1.91). SUMMIT reported fewer cardiovascular-death/worsening-HF events with tirzepatide (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.40–0.95).

In contrast, FIGHT was neutral for its primary hierarchical endpoint and LIVE reported more serious cardiac events with liraglutide (10% vs 3%; P=.04). The authors conclude that the most robust HF evidence currently pertains to obesity-associated HFpEF.

Diversity of care and implementation schemes

The review shifts from efficacy to delivery: inequitable access to GDMT, rapid multidrug initiation/uptitration, and under-representation of women, older adults, socioeconomically disadvantaged patients, and patients with major comorbidity are emphasized as current problems. STRONG-HF and related analyses are used to support aggressive implementation after acute HF.

The authors also identify uncertainty around therapy during intolerance and advanced mechanical circulatory support as areas without clear guideline-level answers.

Conclusions

The final synthesis is that decades of RCTs created complementary disease-modifying therapy for HFrEF, with major survival and morbidity gains from targeting neurohormonal and cardiorenal pathways. The evidence in HFmrEF/HFpEF is less uniform, although SGLT2 inhibitors show the broadest consistent trial benefit.

The authors prioritize closing the guideline-to-practice gap, improving trial representation, developing additional therapies, and advancing precision medicine.

Declarations, data availability, and funding

The article reports extensive author-industry relationships, including consulting, honoraria, travel support, advisory roles, and research relationships across multiple pharmaceutical and device companies. Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: a high-yield map of landmark HF pharmacotherapy trials and how they accumulated into modern GDMT. Do not use it as the sole authority for current recommendation class/level, dosing, contraindications, sequencing, or monitoring.
Relevance — 10/10
Rationale: Directly maps landmark HF pharmacotherapy trials to contemporary GDMT across LVEF phenotypes.
Evidence basis: Broad class coverage, trial tables, and implementation discussion.
Limiting factor: It is not a guideline and cannot supply current recommendation wording by itself.
What would raise/lower: A formal linkage of each trial to current guideline class/level would further increase utility.
Novelty — 5/10
Rationale: The value is synthesis and organization rather than generation of new efficacy evidence.
Evidence basis: Cross-class historical timeline and clinically oriented consolidation.
Limiting factor: No original systematic evidence synthesis or new trial data.
What would raise/lower: A prespecified systematic-review method or original quantitative synthesis would raise novelty.
Method strength — 5/10
Rationale: The review uses explicit pragmatic selection criteria but does not report a systematic search, dual selection, or formal risk-of-bias process.
Evidence basis: Trial selection criterion is stated; key RCTs are tabulated with outcomes and estimates.
Limiting factor: Selection is author-curated and may omit influential or contradictory evidence.
What would raise/lower: A reproducible search/selection protocol and formal evidence-quality assessment would raise the score.
Evidence strength — 7/10
Rationale: Many summarized claims rest on large RCTs, but the review itself is a secondary narrative synthesis and some sections rely on subgroup/post-hoc/meta-analytic evidence.
Evidence basis: Tables preserve sample sizes, effect estimates, and uncertainty for landmark trials.
Limiting factor: Secondary reporting, heterogeneous eras/background therapy, and selective evidence prevent treating all class conclusions as equally secure.
What would raise/lower: Primary-paper verification plus systematic grading of each claim would raise confidence.
External validity — 7/10
Rationale: The source spans HFrEF through HFpEF and multiple drug classes, but trial populations and treatment eras differ substantially.
Evidence basis: Broad phenotype coverage and explicit discussion of under-represented populations.
Limiting factor: Women, older adults, socioeconomic groups, and high-comorbidity patients remain under-represented; trial eligibility varies.
What would raise/lower: More pragmatic/representative trials and stratified implementation evidence would improve generalizability.
Implementation value — 9/10
Rationale: The class-by-class trial map is highly usable for understanding why modern HF pharmacotherapy is structured as it is.
Evidence basis: Tables, timelines, and implementation discussion make trial-to-practice translation efficient.
Limiting factor: Exact prescribing, sequencing, contraindication, monitoring, and guideline-grade details are not comprehensive.
What would raise/lower: Pairing the review with current guideline/label algorithms would make it directly operational.
Main strengths. Broad landmark-trial coverage; preserved effect sizes and uncertainty; useful phenotype separation; explicit acknowledgement of changing trial eras and implementation gaps; unusually current inclusion of 2024–2025 studies such as FINEARTS-HF, VICTOR, DIGIT-HF, and SUMMIT.
Main limitations. Narrative selection without a reproducible systematic search or formal risk-of-bias framework; heterogeneous endpoint definitions and background therapy; some conclusions depend on post-hoc/subgroup/meta-analytic evidence; no direct head-to-head basis for cross-class effect-size ranking.

Confidence in appraisal: High for describing the article and its internal evidence map; moderate for any practice implication that would require verification against current guidelines or primary trial publications.

Practice translation

Clinical questionWhich landmark randomized trials shaped drug therapy for HF across LVEF phenotypes, and what did they show for mortality and HF events?
HFrEFThe review supports the modern multi-pillar concept: ARNI/RAAS blockade, evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor, with class-specific evidence from major RCTs.
HFmrEF/HFpEFSGLT2 inhibitors provide the most consistent cross-spectrum outcome signal; finerenone and obesity-targeted incretin therapies add newer phenotype-specific evidence, while several older RAAS/beta-blocker trials were neutral or less convincing.
AdjunctsIntravenous iron, vericiguat, ivabradine, digitalis glycosides, and incretin-based therapies are context-dependent rather than interchangeable with foundational HFrEF therapy.
Safety/monitoringThe review flags hyperkalaemia/renal dysfunction with combined RAAS inhibition, heart-rate/rhythm dependence for beta-blockers and ivabradine, and adverse cardiac-event signals with liraglutide in HFrEF.
ImplementationClosing the gap between evidence and actual initiation/uptitration is treated as a major next frontier; representation and access remain constraints.
What should not be overclaimed. A trial table is not a guideline algorithm. Neutral overall HFpEF trials may contain subgroup signals, and positive composites may be driven by HF hospitalization rather than mortality. Historical trials also occurred on very different background therapy from current practice.
