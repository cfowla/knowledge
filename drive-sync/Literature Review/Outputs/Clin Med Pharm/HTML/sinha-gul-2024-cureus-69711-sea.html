One-page synthesis

Question. Does dapagliflozin differ from empagliflozin for major cardiovascular outcomes in adults with type 2 diabetes?

Source answer. Across 12 included study entries, the authors report no significant head-to-head difference for MACE, cardiovascular mortality, myocardial infarction, or hospitalization for heart failure. They report one comparative signal favoring dapagliflozin for atrial fibrillation: empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin RR 1.127 (95% CI 1.006-1.263). Both drugs are reported to reduce hospitalization for heart failure versus placebo: dapagliflozin RR 0.70 (0.59-0.84) and empagliflozin RR 0.71 (0.59-0.86).

What is most reusable. The main comparative message is class-level similarity for the major cardiovascular endpoints evaluated, with a narrow AF signal favoring dapagliflozin. The source is also useful as a map to direct comparative observational studies and major SGLT2 trials.

What weakens it. The paper mixes RCT and observational evidence, contains inconsistent design counts and evidence-grading logic, cannot assess publication bias, has only four AF studies, and may include overlapping DECLARE-TIMI 58 publications as separate study entries. These issues materially limit causal and comparative inference.

Section condensation

Introduction and background

The paper frames T2DM as a high cardiovascular-risk state and positions dapagliflozin and empagliflozin as SGLT2 inhibitors with established cardiovascular benefits. The stated gap is limited direct comparative evidence between the two agents, especially for atrial fibrillation and adverse cardiovascular outcomes.

The clinical premise is relevant, but much of the introduction cites class effects and prior trials rather than evidence that the two drugs differ from one another.

Methods

The authors report PRISMA-guided searches of Scopus, Embase, PubMed, and Web of Science from inception through 5 August 2024. Adults with T2DM, with or without HF, were eligible; dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and placebo comparisons from RCTs or observational studies were permitted. Two reviewers handled search/selection and data extraction, with consensus or a third reviewer for disagreements.

RCTs were assessed using the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool and observational studies with the Newcastle-Ottawa scale. RRs and 95% CIs were analyzed in RStudio; NMA used a frequentist random-effects framework. GRADE was used for direct/network evidence. The paper does not adequately demonstrate key network assumptions such as transitivity across its heterogeneous designs and populations.

Results

The PRISMA diagram reports 1,164 records, 178 duplicates removed, 986 screened, 31 full-text reports assessed, and 12 studies included. The source's design accounting is inconsistent: Results says 7 observational/5 RCTs, Table 1 shows 6/6, and Petrie 2020 appears in both the RCT and observational quality tables.

The reported head-to-head estimates show no clear dapagliflozin-empagliflozin difference for MACE, cardiovascular mortality, MI, or HF hospitalization. AF is the exception, with empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin RR 1.127 (1.006-1.263). HHF is the strongest placebo-connected result for both drugs.

Discussion, limitations, and conclusion

The authors interpret the overall cardiovascular efficacy of the two drugs as broadly comparable, while suggesting dapagliflozin may be preferable when AF risk is a particular concern. They propose mechanistic explanations but acknowledge that the reason for the AF difference is unknown.

Stated limitations include predominance of observational evidence, only four AF studies, inability to assess publication bias, and inability to perform subgroup analyses. The conclusion calls for direct head-to-head and longer-term studies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Do not use for practice as a standalone source.
Best use: hypothesis-generating comparative synthesis and source map. Do not use for: establishing dapagliflozin-empagliflozin therapeutic interchangeability or treating the AF signal as definitive superiority.

Source-integrity and appraisal concerns

  1. Results prose states seven observational studies and five RCTs, while Table 1 labels six observational studies and six RCTs. Petrie et al. 2020 appears in both Table 2 (RCT risk of bias) and Table 3 (observational quality), producing overlapping design classification.
  2. Methods states funnel plots were used to assess small-study effects, while Limitations and Table 6 state publication bias could not be assessed because fewer than 10 studies contributed to each outcome.
  3. Hospitalization-for-heart-failure prose reports empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin RR 1.02 (95% CI 0.84-1.24), whereas Figure 6 and Table 4 report the upper bound as 1.23.
  4. Table 6 certainty labels are internally difficult to reconcile with its own domain judgments: some 'High' entries include serious inconsistency and/or imprecision, while some 'Low' entries list no serious inconsistency, indirectness, or imprecision.
  5. Table 1 lists Wiviott 2019 and Zelniker 2020 as separate included entries with the same dapagliflozin/placebo arm sizes and 50.4-month follow-up; the Zelniker citation explicitly describes a DECLARE-TIMI 58 analysis, creating a source-level risk of overlapping trial populations if the two entries were pooled independently.

Relevance

Rationale: Directly addresses dapagliflozin-versus-empagliflozin cardiovascular outcomes in T2DM.

Evidence basis: Five cardiovascular endpoints plus head-to-head and placebo-connected network comparisons.

Principal limiting factor: The evidence base mixes randomized and observational designs and includes possible overlapping trial populations.

What would raise/lower it: A rigorous head-to-head comparative synthesis with deduplicated populations would raise the score.

6/10

Novelty

Rationale: The direct comparison and atrial-fibrillation signal add a useful comparative angle.

Evidence basis: Network estimates and SUCRA rankings compare both drugs rather than only class versus placebo.

Principal limiting factor: Most underlying evidence predates the review and several comparisons remain indirect or imprecise.

What would raise/lower it: New direct randomized or carefully emulated comparative evidence would raise the score.

4/10

Method strength

Rationale: The review uses PRISMA, risk-of-bias tools, GRADE, and a frequentist random-effects NMA, but key design/reporting problems weaken confidence.

Evidence basis: Search/selection methods, Cochrane RoB, NOS, GRADE, inconsistency checks, and RStudio methods are reported.

Principal limiting factor: RCT and observational evidence are mixed; design counts conflict; transitivity/network assumptions are not adequately demonstrated; potential overlapping DECLARE publications are not clearly handled.

What would raise/lower it: Explicit network geometry, overlap deduplication, transitivity assessment, protocol details, and sensitivity analyses by study design would raise the score.

4/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: The central finding of similar major cardiovascular outcomes is plausible within the paper, but the comparative precision and evidence-quality reporting are limited.

Evidence basis: Most head-to-head CIs cross 1; AF is based on four studies; HHF versus placebo is the clearest consistent effect in the reported network.

Principal limiting factor: The GRADE table is internally difficult to reconcile and publication bias could not be assessed.

What would raise/lower it: More direct comparative data and internally coherent certainty grading would raise the score.

6/10

External validity

Rationale: Large observational cohorts broaden real-world representation, but the pooled population is heterogeneous.

Evidence basis: Included studies span T2DM with and without HF and follow-up from 4 to 50.4 months.

Principal limiting factor: Clinical settings, baseline cardiovascular risk, HF phenotype, dosing, and design differ substantially across studies.

What would raise/lower it: Stratified analyses by population and design with consistent endpoint definitions would raise the score.

5/10

Implementation value

Rationale: The review can inform hypothesis generation and source discovery, but should not determine formulary interchangeability by itself.

Evidence basis: It suggests no material difference in MACE, CV mortality, MI, or HHF between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin and reports an AF signal favoring dapagliflozin.

Principal limiting factor: Methodological inconsistencies and possible population overlap make the AF superiority signal too fragile for standalone practice decisions.

What would raise/lower it: Confirmation in high-quality direct comparative evidence would raise implementation value.

Confidence in appraisal: high. The main limitations are visible within the supplied full text, tables, figures, and references and do not depend on external evidence.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What the paper reasonably supports. Within this synthesis, dapagliflozin and empagliflozin look broadly similar for MACE, CV mortality, MI, and HHF; both have favorable HHF estimates versus placebo.
What not to overclaim. The AF result is statistically narrow, based on four studies, and sits within a review with mixed study designs, internal reporting inconsistencies, and possible population overlap.
Formulary translation. This paper can be cited as secondary evidence against a large cardiovascular efficacy separation between dapagliflozin and empagliflozin, but it is not sufficient by itself to justify or reject formulary interchange. The primary comparative studies and stronger class-effect evidence should carry more weight.
