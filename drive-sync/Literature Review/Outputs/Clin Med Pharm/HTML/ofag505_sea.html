One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In this real-world cohort, infection was very common during bispecific-antibody treatment for multiple myeloma: 352 episodes occurred at 3.20 per course-year, 81.6% of courses had at least one infection, and 45.9% had at least one grade ≥3 infection. Respiratory disease dominated, viral etiologies were most frequent, and infection burden was greatest in the first 6 months.

Risk was associated with cumulative immunosuppression and treatment complexity. Main-text multivariable results linked previous BsAb exposure, prior allogeneic HSCT, and ICANS to overall infection; anti-BCMA therapy carried higher overall infection risk than anti-GPRC5D therapy. IVIg was associated with lower overall, viral, and severe-infection risk. Poorer ECOG status, prior anti-CD38 therapy, grade 4 neutropenia, and ICANS were among predictors of severe infection.

A stewardship-relevant observation was the low rate of microbiologically documented infection during the first CRS episode (5/73, 6.8%) despite empiric antibiotics in 82.2% of first CRS episodes. This supports early reassessment/de-escalation as a hypothesis for practice, but the study is not a randomized stewardship trial and severe CRS/ICANS requires a different risk posture.

Important source limitation. The manuscript refers to Supplementary Methods, Figure S1, and Tables S1-S9, but those materials were not present in the specified source folder. Main-text values summarizing those analyses were retained; supplementary-only detail was not reconstructed.

Section-by-section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames infection as a major complication of BsAbs in MM and reports 92 patients, 98 courses, and 352 infection episodes. Respiratory and digestive infections predominate; viral etiology is most common. It highlights higher risk with anti-BCMA versus anti-GPRC5D, prior BsAb exposure, allogeneic HSCT, and ICANS, with IVIg protective.

The conclusion emphasizes cumulative immunosuppression and CRS/ICANS-related immunosuppression while noting that concurrent infection was uncommon during first CRS episodes.

Introduction

The paper describes BsAbs as T-cell engagers targeting BCMA, GPRC5D, or FcRH5 and outlines why infection remains likely despite targeted antimyeloma activity: underlying MM, prior therapies, treatment-induced hypogammaglobulinemia, immunosuppression used for CRS/ICANS, and possible T-cell exhaustion.

The stated gap is a lack of granular real-world characterization of incidence, microbiology, clinical syndromes, temporal patterns, and risk factors across BsAb use.

Materials and Methods

All adults receiving BsAbs at one Madrid center from January 2020 through May 2025 were included. Patients came from a prospective registry, with missing variables added retrospectively from EHRs. Follow-up began at BsAb initiation and ended one month after discontinuation or at a new treatment line. Proven/probable bacterial and respiratory viral infections were defined; cystitis and asymptomatic bacteriuria were excluded.

All patients received PJP prophylaxis and nearly all received acyclovir; severe HGG triggered monthly IVIg 0.4 g/kg. Infection rates were expressed per course-year. Recurrent-event Cox models used a two-stage covariate-selection strategy and treated IVIg as time-dependent; a Fine-Gray death-competing-risk model served as sensitivity analysis.

Results

Median time at risk was 316 days. Infection incidence was 3.20/course-year (95% CI 2.88-3.55); 81.6% of courses experienced infection. Respiratory syndromes accounted for 71.3% and digestive syndromes 11.9%. Microbiological confirmation occurred in 42.0%; viral, bacterial, and fungal etiologies accounted for 62.8%, 33.2%, and 2.8% of episodes, respectively. Severe infection affected 45.9% of courses. Six of 13 deaths were infection-attributable.

Main-text adjusted analyses associated previous BsAb exposure (aHR 1.75), prior allogeneic HSCT (aHR 1.59), and ICANS (aHR 3.23) with overall infection. IVIg was protective for overall (aHR 0.68), viral (0.59), and severe infection (0.42). Anti-BCMA carried higher overall infection risk than anti-GPRC5D (aHR 1.41). First-CRS episodes received empiric antibiotics frequently, but concurrent microbiologically documented infection was only 6.8%.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the infection burden as consistent with prior BsAb experience, with early treatment months being highest risk. They argue that viral respiratory infections dominate, bacterial infections reflect humoral immune deficits and comorbidity, and opportunistic infection was comparatively uncommon under universal PJP prophylaxis. They emphasize cumulative immunosuppression, disease/functional status, ICANS, cytopenias, and prior therapies as risk determinants.

The paper proposes IVIg as an important infection-prevention measure and suggests antimicrobial stewardship during early uncomplicated CRS, while cautioning that severe CRS/ICANS warrants closer surveillance. The authors explicitly limit comparative conclusions because the cohort was single-center, observational, heterogeneous, COVID-era, and censored after BsAb discontinuation/new therapy.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. High clinical relevance and useful real-world signals for infection prevention, surveillance, IVIg, and antimicrobial stewardship around BsAbs; evidence is informative but observational and not sufficient for causal agent ranking or protocol mandates.
Relevance9/10

Directly addresses infections, prophylaxis, IVIg, CRS/ICANS, and stewardship in a high-risk hematologic population. Limiting factor: narrow disease/therapy context. Higher with multicenter practice implementation data.

Novelty7/10

Adds detailed real-world incidence, recurrent-event risk modeling, temporal patterns, and first-CRS infection data. Limited because BsAb infection risk is already recognized. Higher with prospective standardized surveillance and external replication.

Method strength6/10

Complete-center cohort, recurrent-event Cox modeling, time-dependent IVIg, and competing-risk sensitivity analysis are strengths. Observational single-center design, stepwise covariate selection, heterogeneous regimens, and partially retrospective data constrain causal inference.

Evidence strength6/10

Large event count relative to cohort size and effect estimates with CIs support descriptive and associative conclusions. Several important models/definitions reside in missing supplement, some CIs are wide, and residual confounding is plausible.

External validity5/10

Real-world clinical setting helps applicability, but one Spanish center, local prophylaxis/epidemiology, COVID-era observation, and heterogeneous therapies limit transportability.

Implementation value8/10

Useful for identifying surveillance priorities, HGG/IVIg strategy, infection-risk discussion, and early CRS antibiotic reassessment. Lower for direct drug selection or mandatory prophylaxis policy because comparative causality is not established.

PECO / clinical question

Population: Adults with multiple myeloma treated with bispecific antibodies at a single tertiary center.
Exposure: BsAb therapy; class, prior therapies, cytopenias, IVIg, CRS/ICANS and their treatments.
Comparator: Observational contrasts; anti-GPRC5D served as comparator for the anti-BCMA class analysis.
Outcomes: Overall, viral, bacterial, and severe infections; temporal incidence; microbiology; infection during first CRS; mortality.

Main strengths

  • Systematic event capture from treatment initiation with 352 infection episodes.
  • Recurrent-event analysis rather than only first-event analysis.
  • Time-dependent modeling of IVIg and competing-risk sensitivity analysis.
  • Clinically useful detail on syndrome distribution, severe infection, CRS/ICANS, and antimicrobial use.

Main limitations

  • Single-center observational cohort; no randomized comparator.
  • Heterogeneous BsAb agents/combinations and baseline patient profiles.
  • Incomplete EHR data for cytopenia duration; vaccination status not collected.
  • Cystitis excluded; follow-up censored after discontinuation/new line, potentially missing late infection.
  • COVID-era epidemiology may alter viral burden and generalizability.
  • Supplementary methods and regression tables were unavailable for this review.
  • Minor source-level denominator inconsistencies are present (for example, bacteremia is written as 14/358 despite 352 total episodes; Table 1 lists PJP prophylaxis as 100 [100.0%] despite 98 courses). These were not silently corrected.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Monitor early and longitudinallyRisk was highest in the first 6 months, but remained substantial later. Respiratory viral and bacterial infections were the main burden.
Track humoral immunityHGG was common and worsened during therapy. IVIg was associated with lower overall, viral, and severe infection risk; this supports attention to IgG and replacement criteria, while not proving causality.
Escalate vigilance with ICANS / cytopeniaICANS and grade 4 neutropenia were strong severe-infection signals. Patients receiving intensified immunosuppression merit closer reassessment for occult infection.
CRS stewardship opportunityEmpiric antibiotics were common during first CRS, but concurrent documented infection was uncommon. Early microbiology-driven reassessment and de-escalation is a reasonable implementation target if local protocols and patient severity allow.
Do not over-rank BsAb agentsThe anti-BCMA versus anti-GPRC5D association is hypothesis-generating. Heterogeneous patients/regimens preclude firm intrinsic class or agent causal comparisons.
Do not infer omitted supplementSupplementary definitions and full regression tables were absent, so downstream use should preserve that provenance gap.
Do not use this study alone to: mandate a specific BsAb based on infection risk; define a universal antifungal/CMV prophylaxis policy; or replace local protocols for CRS/sepsis differentiation.
