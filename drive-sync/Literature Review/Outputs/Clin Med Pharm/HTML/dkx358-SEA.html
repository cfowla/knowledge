One-page synthesis

Core thesis: The report documents microbiologic clearance of post-neurosurgical carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae meningitis during ceftazidime/avibactam plus intraventricular gentamicin, with EVD exchange and serial CSF follow-up.

An elderly patient with subarachnoid and intraventricular hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, EVD placement, and suboccipital craniectomy later developed MDR K. pneumoniae in BAL and then EVD cultures. The BAL and CSF isolates had matching susceptibility profiles and were CARBA NP positive. After a brief earlier ceftazidime/avibactam course and a switch to doxycycline, definitive meningitis treatment restarted ceftazidime/avibactam 2.5 g IV every 8 hours and added intraventricular gentamicin 8 mg daily; the EVD was exchanged the next day.

Repeat cultures became negative. Ceftazidime/avibactam was given for 21 days and intraventricular gentamicin for 15 days. Serial CSF measurements were followed through therapy; the cell index peaked at 49.2 on treatment day 5 and was 0.0 by day 23. The EVD was removed after therapy and a ventriculoperitoneal shunt placed. The patient was discharged on hospital day 74 to long-term care, moving spontaneously and responding to voice commands. The authors explicitly state that the treatment effect cannot be divided between ceftazidime/avibactam and intraventricular gentamicin and that additional clinical and pharmacokinetic studies are needed.

Interpretive boundary: This is hypothesis-generating evidence. The paper documents a successful course but does not demonstrate ceftazidime/avibactam monotherapy efficacy, human CSF exposure targets, or an optimal treatment duration.

Section condensation

Unheaded narrative — rationale and case onset

The authors frame CRE CNS infection as difficult because therapeutic options are limited and CNS penetration of many agents is inadequate or poorly characterized. Their stated purpose is to report a post-neurosurgical carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae meningitis case successfully treated with ceftazidime/avibactam in combination with intraventricular gentamicin.

The patient presented with acute headache and loss of consciousness, diffuse SAH/IVH, obstructive hydrocephalus, EVD placement, and later suboccipital craniectomy. A suspected aspiration pneumonia prompted piperacillin/tazobactam plus vancomycin. Persistent fever and leukocytosis on hospital day 23 led to BAL culture growth of K. pneumoniae.

Unheaded narrative — antimicrobial course and microbiology

Piperacillin/tazobactam was changed to ceftazidime/avibactam 2.5 g IV every 8 hours plus nebulized polymyxin E. On day 26, after MDR susceptibility results, ceftazidime/avibactam was changed to doxycycline. Recurrent fever triggered EVD cultures on day 31; these grew MDR K. pneumoniae with susceptibilities matching the BAL isolate. Both isolates were identified as carbapenemase producers by CARBA NP.

Ceftazidime/avibactam was restarted, intraventricular gentamicin 8 mg daily was added, and the EVD was exchanged the next day. All repeat cultures were negative. Treatment stopped after 21 days of ceftazidime/avibactam and 15 days of intraventricular gentamicin; the EVD was then removed and a VPS placed.

Unheaded narrative — outcome, interpretation, and limitations

Neurologic status remained largely unchanged during hospitalization. On day 74, the patient was discharged to long-term care, moving spontaneously and responding to voice commands. The authors contextualize the case as the third documented CRE meningitis and second reported ceftazidime/avibactam-treated case, and cite prior experimental/clinical literature to support biologic plausibility for CNS use.

The key limitation is explicit: the contribution of ceftazidime/avibactam cannot be separated from intraventricular gentamicin. EVD exchange also occurred during definitive therapy. The report has no human ceftazidime/avibactam CSF concentration data, and the optimal duration is uncertain. The authors conclude that the successful course justifies additional clinical and pharmacokinetic investigation rather than definitive practice claims.

Funding and transparency

The study was carried out as part of routine work. Transparency declarations state that there were no conflicts to declare.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used as a detailed historical rescue-treatment case and a source of concrete regimen/monitoring data, not as evidence that establishes ceftazidime/avibactam efficacy or dosing for meningitis.

Relevance 7/10

Rationale: Directly relevant to CRE CNS pharmacotherapy and rescue-treatment reasoning.

Evidence basis: Detailed treatment timeline, susceptibility profile, serial CSF data, and microbiologic outcome.

Principal limit: One historical single case with no contemporary practice verification.

Would raise/lower: A larger CNS CRE series or prospective PK/clinical data would raise this score.

Novelty 7/10

Rationale: At publication, the authors frame it as the third documented CRE meningitis case and second treated with ceftazidime/avibactam.

Evidence basis: The source explicitly situates the case against two prior CRE meningitis reports.

Principal limit: Novelty is case-based rather than methodologic and is time-dependent.

Would raise/lower: Confirmation that the case materially changed later CNS CRE practice would raise it; many subsequent similar cases would lower it.

Method strength 3/10

Rationale: The report is descriptive and uncontrolled, with major co-intervention and source-control confounding.

Evidence basis: Combination ceftazidime/avibactam plus intraventricular gentamicin, EVD exchange, serial cultures, and CSF analyses are reported.

Principal limit: No comparator, no prespecified protocol, no PK sampling, and no causal isolation of components.

Would raise/lower: Prospective standardized data with drug concentrations and a comparator would raise the score.

Evidence strength 3/10

Rationale: Microbiologic clearance is documented, but the case cannot establish which intervention caused it or define efficacy.

Evidence basis: Repeat cultures became negative; CSF indices were followed through therapy; the authors explicitly acknowledge attribution uncertainty.

Principal limit: Single-patient evidence, concurrent intraventricular gentamicin, EVD exchange, and absent ceftazidime/avibactam CSF PK.

Would raise/lower: Replicated outcomes with PK/PD linkage and controlled comparative evidence would raise the score.

External validity 2/10

Rationale: Generalizability is very limited beyond a similar post-neurosurgical CRE meningitis scenario.

Evidence basis: One elderly patient with SAH/IVH, EVD, craniectomy, and MDR K. pneumoniae.

Principal limit: Sparse patient detail, no organism carbapenemase genotype, no ceftazidime/avibactam MIC, and highly specific neurosurgical context.

Would raise/lower: Multi-center CNS infection data across organisms, resistance mechanisms, and patient types would raise the score.

Implementation value 4/10

Rationale: The paper provides a concrete regimen and monitoring trajectory but not enough evidence to define standard dosing or duration.

Evidence basis: Ceftazidime/avibactam 2.5 g IV q8h for 21 days, intraventricular gentamicin 8 mg daily for 15 days, EVD exchange, serial CSF analyses.

Principal limit: No human CNS PK, no dose-adjustment context, and no basis for separating drug effect from source control/co-therapy.

Would raise/lower: Validated human CSF PK targets, dosing guidance, and comparative outcome data would raise the score.

Validity summary

Population: one elderly post-neurosurgical patient with EVD-associated CRE K. pneumoniae meningitis. Intervention: ceftazidime/avibactam 2.5 g IV every 8 hours plus intraventricular gentamicin 8 mg daily, accompanied by EVD exchange. Comparator: none. Outcomes: culture clearance, serial CSF parameters, treatment duration, device management, and discharge status.

Principal threats to inference: uncontrolled single case; simultaneous active agents; EVD exchange/source control; no human ceftazidime/avibactam CSF PK; no ceftazidime/avibactam MIC; no carbapenemase genotype; limited patient covariates and long-term outcome data.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this source can support
A documented precedent for using systemic ceftazidime/avibactam with intraventricular gentamicin and device exchange in a difficult post-neurosurgical CRE meningitis case.
What it cannot support
Causal attribution to ceftazidime/avibactam, monotherapy efficacy, a universal 21-day duration, or a human CNS PK target.
High-value details to retain
Ceftazidime/avibactam 2.5 g IV q8h × 21 days; intraventricular gentamicin 8 mg daily × 15 days; EVD exchange; serial CSF trend; gentamicin MIC 1 mg/L; ceftazidime/avibactam susceptible by disc diffusion.
Read-next logic
Prior CRE meningitis cases, the cited rabbit CNS PK study, intraventricular aminoglycoside literature, and guidance on healthcare-associated ventriculitis/meningitis are the most useful reference-chain targets.
