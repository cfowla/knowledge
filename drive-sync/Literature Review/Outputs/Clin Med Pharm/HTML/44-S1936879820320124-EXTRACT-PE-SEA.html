One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In a prospective 119-patient, 22-site single-arm study of predominantly intermediate-risk/submassive PE, Indigo aspiration thrombectomy was followed by a substantial 48-hour reduction in RV/LV ratio and a low observed 48-hour major adverse event rate, with very little intraprocedural thrombolytic use.

What the source found. Mean RV/LV ratio fell from 1.47 to 1.04; mean reduction was 0.43 (95% CI 0.38-0.47; p<0.0001), corresponding to 27.3% mean reduction. The modified ITT estimate was similar at 0.42. The 48-hour composite major adverse event rate was 1.7% (2/119; 95% CI 0.0-4.0); major bleeding was 1.7%, device-related death 0.8%, device-related pulmonary vascular injury 0.8%, and device-related cardiac injury 0%. Median device insertion-to-removal time was 37 minutes (IQR 23.5-60.0). Intraprocedural tPA occurred in 2/119 (1.7%); 94.1% received no tPA through 48 hours.

How to interpret it. Measurement credibility is improved by independent core-lab CTA analysis and Clinical Events Committee adjudication. However, the principal efficacy outcome is a physiologic surrogate, follow-up is 30 days, and the absence of a comparator means the trial cannot determine whether the device improves patient-centered outcomes or is better than alternative management strategies. The result is best treated as prospective device-performance evidence and hypothesis-supporting comparative context, not definitive treatment-selection evidence.

Methods

Population and eligibility

Adults with acute symptomatic PE for 14 days or less, CTA-confirmed PE, systolic BP at least 90 mmHg, and RV/LV ratio greater than 0.9 were eligible. The supplement lists 22 exclusion criteria, including recent tPA, hemodynamic instability requiring inotropes, severe/chronic pulmonary hypertension, high oxygen requirements, hematologic/renal/coagulation thresholds, HIT, anticoagulation contraindication, recent major trauma or cardiopulmonary surgery, active chemotherapy, short life expectancy, pregnancy, intracardiac thrombus, ECMO, and concurrent investigational-study participation.

Intervention and oversight

The Indigo system used an 8-F aspiration catheter with tubing, pump, and separator. Operators advanced the catheter proximal to thrombus and used sustained aspiration, optionally using the separator to clear the catheter and repeating aspiration across pulmonary vessels. Independent core-lab imaging supplied the primary RV/LV measurements; a Clinical Events Committee adjudicated safety events, and an independent DSMB reviewed aggregate safety data.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary efficacy endpoint was baseline-to-48-hour RV/LV ratio change; success required the lower 95% CI bound for mean change to exceed 0.2. The primary safety endpoint was 48-hour major adverse events (device-related death, major bleeding, or device-related serious adverse event); success required the upper 95% CI bound to remain below 40%. Analyses used ITT and a modified ITT population excluding adjunctive clot-reduction treatments/thrombolytics through 48 hours. The supplement states the planned sample was up to 150 to obtain 100 evaluable treated patients.

Results

Baseline

Mean age was 59.8 ± 15.0 years; 44.5% were women. Elevated troponin was present in 70.6% (84/119), elevated BNP in 61.0% of those measured (50/82), and mean baseline RV/LV ratio was 1.47 ± 0.30. The study enrolled 118 patients classified as submassive PE and 1 as massive PE at enrollment.

Procedure

Conscious sedation was used in 97.5%; femoral access in 85.7%; the Indigo system accessed the embolus in 118/119 (99.2%). Median device insertion-to-removal time was 37.0 minutes (IQR 23.5-60.0). Intraprocedural tPA was used in 2 patients (1.7%), and 112/119 (94.1%) had no tPA through 48 hours. Estimated blood loss was under 400 mL in 73.1%; no transfusions were attributed to aspiration blood loss itself.

Efficacy

The ITT mean RV/LV ratio fell from 1.47 to 1.04 at 48 hours. Mean reduction was 0.43 (95% CI 0.38-0.47; 27.3%; p<0.0001); the mITT mean reduction was 0.42 (95% CI 0.37-0.46; 26.9%; p<0.0001). Systolic PA pressure decreased 4.3 mmHg immediately after aspiration and 4.7 mmHg after all procedural treatment, and CT obstruction index decreased by 11.3% at 48 hours (p<0.0001).

Safety

The primary 48-hour composite major adverse event rate was 1.7% (2/119; 95% CI 0.0-4.0; p<0.0001), with 3 qualifying events in 2 patients. Major bleeding occurred in 2/119, device-related death in 1/119, device-related serious adverse events in 1/119, and device-related cardiac injury in none. One patient developed hemoptysis from distal vessel perforation, then access-site bleeding and ventricular tachycardia and died about 11 hours after venous puncture; a second patient had major groin-access bleeding treated with 2 units of packed red cells.

At 30 days, all-cause mortality was 2.5% (3/119), device-related serious adverse events 1.7% (2/119), and symptomatic recurrent PE 0%. No intracranial bleeding was reported. Two deaths were attributed to progression of pre-existing disease (cancer and pre-existing ischemic stroke).

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon / skim deeply. Strong as a prospective device-performance and safety characterization study; insufficient alone to select aspiration thrombectomy over alternative treatment strategies.

Relevance - 8.0/10

Directly informs evidence for catheter-based management of intermediate-risk PE.

Evidence basis: Prospective multicenter PE intervention study with detailed device, efficacy, and safety endpoints.

Principal limit: Device-specific and not a pharmacotherapy comparison.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with head-to-head randomized clinical-outcome data.

Novelty - 7.0/10

At publication, it provided the first prospective trial of the Indigo aspiration system for acute PE.

Evidence basis: Authors describe EXTRACT-PE as the first prospective Indigo PE trial.

Principal limit: Single-arm device evidence and physiologic endpoints limit incremental certainty.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with a novel comparative design or patient-centered endpoint.

Method strength - 6.0/10

Prospective multicenter design, core-lab imaging, CEC adjudication, and DSMB oversight strengthen measurement quality.

Evidence basis: 119 patients across 22 sites; independent imaging and safety adjudication.

Principal limit: No randomization or comparator; operator discretion; short follow-up.

Would raise/lower: Would rise substantially with randomization, prespecified comparator, and longer follow-up.

Evidence strength - 5.5/10

The trial demonstrates within-group physiologic improvement and a low observed short-term event rate, but causal comparative benefit is not established.

Evidence basis: RV/LV reduction 0.43 (95% CI 0.38-0.47); 48-h MAE 1.7% (2/119).

Principal limit: Primary efficacy endpoint is a surrogate and there is no control arm.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with powered clinical outcomes and direct comparative estimates.

External validity - 5.0/10

Multicenter enrollment helps, but restrictive eligibility and near-exclusive intermediate-risk population narrow generalization.

Evidence basis: 22 U.S. sites; 118/119 were submassive PE.

Principal limit: Excludes several high-risk/comorbid groups and cannot generalize to higher-risk PE.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with broader pragmatic enrollment and external replication.

Implementation value - 6.0/10

Provides detailed eligibility, procedural technique, timing, and adverse-event characterization useful for protocol review.

Evidence basis: Supplement gives full eligibility; procedure time median 37 min; safety events described.

Principal limit: Does not establish when aspiration should replace anticoagulation, thrombolysis, or other catheter strategies.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with comparative selection criteria, standardized stopping rules, and patient-centered benefits.

Internal validity

Prospective design, multicenter enrollment, independent imaging core lab, Clinical Events Committee adjudication, and DSMB oversight reduce some measurement and adjudication bias.
The single-arm design leaves substantial confounding by natural history, concurrent anticoagulation/supportive care, patient selection, and regression toward the mean. The efficacy endpoint is a surrogate, and the trial is not powered for long-term clinical events.

External validity

Generalization is strongest to hemodynamically stable adults with acute CTA-confirmed PE and RV dilation who resemble the trial's restrictive eligibility criteria. The authors explicitly state that findings cannot be generalized to higher-risk PE. Operator discretion in aspiration duration also limits reproducibility.

Industry context

The trial was funded by Penumbra. Several authors reported Penumbra research, speaking, consulting, or stock relationships, alongside relationships with other companies. This does not invalidate the findings, but it increases the importance of independent replication and randomized comparison.

Practice / implementation takeaways

What this supports

Indigo aspiration thrombectomy can produce rapid reduction in RV/LV ratio with a low observed short-term adjudicated major adverse event rate in a selected intermediate-risk PE cohort.

What it does not establish

It does not establish superiority to anticoagulation, thrombolysis, catheter-directed thrombolysis, or another thrombectomy system, nor a mortality or quality-of-life benefit.

Operational detail worth retaining

Median device time 37 minutes; 8-F system; 99.2% technical access; active monitoring of aspirated blood volume is advised by the authors.

Best next evidence

Randomized comparative trials with standardized selection/stopping criteria, longer follow-up, and patient-centered outcomes.
