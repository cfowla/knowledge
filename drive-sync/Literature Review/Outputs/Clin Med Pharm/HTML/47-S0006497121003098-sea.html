One-page synthesis

OAsIs tested a biologically motivated combination of BTK inhibition (ibrutinib), anti-CD20 therapy (obinutuzumab), and BCL2 inhibition (venetoclax). The dose-finding component enrolled relapsed MCL patients and tested venetoclax 400, 600, and 800 mg/day on top of fixed ibrutinib/obinutuzumab. No DLT was observed, the formal MTD was not reached, and the DSMC selected 400 mg/day for expansion. A subsequent untreated cohort used the fixed 400 mg/day dose after a venetoclax ramp-up. The protocol was amended during development to reduce the venetoclax starting dose and strengthen TLS prevention after TLS was reported in the external AIM study.

Activity signals were substantial but come from small, heterogeneous, nonrandomized cohorts. At cycle 6, complete response by Lugano criteria was 67% in relapsed triple-therapy cohort B (16/24) and 86.7% in untreated cohort C (13/15). Among MRD-evaluable patients, peripheral-blood MRD negativity after cycle 3 was 71.4% in cohort B (10/14) and 100% in cohort C (12/12). Cohort B 1-year PFS was 74.5% and 1-year OS 87.5%; the abstract reports 2-year PFS 69.5% and OS 68.6%. Safety was dominated by hematologic toxicity in relapsed patients: grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 75% of cohort B, with grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia and neutropenia prominent. The untreated cohort had fewer high-grade AEs (53%).

Key interpretive limit: Cohort A is not a randomized control. The authors explicitly state that the study was not designed or powered to isolate the incremental benefit or toxicity of adding venetoclax.

Methods and protocol details

Study structure

Cohort A first established the safety of ibrutinib plus obinutuzumab in 9 relapsed patients. Cohort B then enrolled relapsed/refractory patients for venetoclax dose finding on top of the same backbone; three planned dose levels were 400, 600, and 800 mg/day. Cohort C enrolled previously untreated patients using the selected fixed venetoclax dose. DLT assessment occurred in cycle 1 for cohort A and cycle 2 for cohorts B/C.

Regimen and TLS mitigation

Ibrutinib was dosed at 560 mg/day. Obinutuzumab was given 1000 mg IV on cycle 1 days 1/2, 8, and 15, then continued on scheduled cycle-day dosing and maintenance. Venetoclax was introduced after initial therapy and ramped 20, 50, 100, and 200 mg/day before escalation to 400 mg/day and, during dose finding, 600 or 800 mg/day. Supplement S4 stratified TLS risk by node diameter and absolute lymphocyte count, recommended allopurinol, risk-adapted rasburicase, oral/IV hydration, and chemistry monitoring at baseline, 8 h, and 24 h after initiation/dose changes.

Eligibility and measurement

Key eligibility included adult/adolescent age thresholds by country, relapsed/refractory disease after at least one prior therapy for cohorts A/B, untreated histologically confirmed MCL for cohort C, stage II-IV disease needing treatment, ECOG 0-2, ANC ≥1000/mm³, platelets ≥75,000/mm³ (or ≥50,000/mm³ with marrow involvement), and adequate renal/hepatic function. Major exclusions included warfarin/vitamin K antagonists, strong CYP3A inhibitors, major organ dysfunction, active serious infection, and prior allograft under the final protocol.

Clinical response used Cheson 1999 CT criteria and Lugano PET criteria. MRD was assessed in blood and marrow by ASO-qPCR targeting clonal IgH or t(11;14), designed for 10⁻⁵ sensitivity; investigators were blinded to MRD results. TP53 mutation used targeted sequencing; 17p deletion used array CGH when tumor infiltration was sufficient.

Statistics

MTD was defined as a dose associated with DLT in 33% of cohort B during cycle 2. A modified continual reassessment method was planned if DLT occurred, with three patients per dose level and an exponential model. The planned total sample was 48 (9/24/15 across A/B/C). Safety and efficacy analyses included all patients receiving at least one dose; PFS/OS used Kaplan-Meier estimation.

Results

Dose finding and safety

No DLT occurred in any cohort; the CRM was therefore not used. The formal MTD was not reached at 800 mg/day. The DSMC selected venetoclax 400 mg/day for expansion, with the Discussion citing lack of higher response at 600-800 mg/day, more transfusion needs at higher doses, and reduced pill burden as reasons. During cycles 1-6, grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 8/9 (89%) cohort A, 18/24 (75%) cohort B, and 8/15 (53%) cohort C. Hematologic toxicity was concentrated in relapsed patients, especially thrombocytopenia and neutropenia. One grade 3 biological TLS occurred in cohort A and one grade 1 biological TLS in cohort C.

Efficacy

Cohort A had 7/9 complete responses at cycle 6. In cohort B, 20/24 (84%) responded after two cycles, including 9 CR/CRu; at cycle 6, 16/24 (67%) were in CR/CRu by Cheson and 16/24 (67%) had CR by Lugano. Cohort C had 12/15 (80%) CR/CRu by Cheson and 13/15 (86.7%) CR by Lugano at cycle 6.

With median follow-up 17 months in cohort B, 1-year PFS was 74.5% (95% CI 58.8-94.5) and 1-year OS 87.5% (95% CI 75.2-100). The abstract additionally reports 2-year PFS 69.5% (95% CI 52.9-91.4) and 2-year OS 68.6% (95% CI 49.5-95.1). Cohort C median follow-up was 14 months; 1-year PFS was 93.3% (95% CI 81.5-100) and 1-year OS 100%.

MRD and high-risk features

MRD was evaluable in 32/48 patients. After cycle 3, 26/32 (81%) were MRD negative in peripheral blood: 4/6 (66.7%) cohort A, 10/14 (71.4%) cohort B, and 12/12 (100%) cohort C. All who were MRD negative at cycle 3 remained negative at cycle 6 when tested in blood and marrow except one cohort A patient. Among six patients MRD positive in blood at cycle 3, two became negative in both blood and marrow by cycle 6. Four patients remained persistently MRD positive at cycle 6.

IGHV status did not show a clear MRD-response separation (7/8 mutated vs 20/25 unmutated were MRD negative by cycle 3). Eleven patients with TP53 alterations were among the 26 cycle-3 MRD-negative patients; however, the sample was too small for strong high-risk subgroup inference. In cohort B, 2/4 blastoid-variant patients reached CR at cycle 6.

Strengths

  • Prospective multicenter design with prespecified dose-finding logic and DSMC oversight.
  • Detailed supplemental protocol: eligibility, dosing, TLS risk mitigation, AE tables, adherence, amendments.
  • Molecular depth of response measured with blinded MRD testing.
  • Explicit reporting of denominator limitations and lack of power for cohort comparison.

Limitations

  • Small nonrandomized cohorts; cohort A is not a valid causal comparator for cohort B.
  • MRD analyses exclude one-third of the enrolled population.
  • Shorter follow-up in triple-therapy cohorts than cohort A; several estimates are immature.
  • Subgroup claims for TP53/blastoid disease are based on very small numbers.
  • Protocol evolved materially during the study in response to TLS and dose-selection information.

Bottom line

The study demonstrates feasibility and deep response signals for the triplet and supports the 400 mg venetoclax expansion dose under the studied protocol. It does not establish that adding venetoclax improves outcomes over ibrutinib-obinutuzumab, nor does it establish a current standard regimen.

Currency / external verification

External regulatory verification: In the United States, the accelerated approval indication for ibrutinib in adults with MCL after at least one prior therapy was voluntarily removed in May 2023. FDA lists the indication as withdrawn, and the 2023 labeling revision removed the MCL indication. This postdates OAsIs and materially reduces direct U.S. practice applicability of the regimen as published.
