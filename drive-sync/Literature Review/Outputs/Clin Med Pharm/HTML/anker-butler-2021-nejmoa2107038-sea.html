One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Empagliflozin reduced the primary composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization in symptomatic HF with LVEF >40% (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69–0.90), with the effect driven mainly by fewer HF hospitalizations rather than cardiovascular mortality.

The trial randomized 5,988 patients across 622 centers in 23 countries. The primary composite occurred in 13.8% with empagliflozin versus 17.1% with placebo (6.9 vs 8.7 events/100 patient-years; HR 0.79; p<0.001). The reported NNT over the median trial period was 31 (95% CI 20–69). First HF hospitalization was lower (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.60–0.83). Cardiovascular mortality was not separately reduced with a confidence interval excluding no effect (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.76–1.09).

Both key secondary outcomes passed the prespecified hierarchy: total first and recurrent HF hospitalizations were lower (407 vs 541; HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.61–0.88; p<0.001), and annual eGFR decline was slower (−1.25 vs −2.62 mL/min/1.73 m²/year; between-group difference 1.36, 95% CI 1.06–1.66; p<0.001). KCCQ, the renal composite, total all-cause hospitalizations, new diabetes, and mortality analyses were outside the confirmatory hierarchy and should remain nonconfirmatory.

Interpretive boundary: the composite endpoint must not be summarized as a mortality benefit. All-cause mortality was 14.1% versus 14.3% (HR 1.00, 95% CI 0.87–1.15). The LVEF ≥60% subgroup favored empagliflozin numerically but had a 95% CI crossing 1 (HR 0.87, 0.69–1.10); no subgroup-interaction conclusion should be inferred from that estimate alone.

Safety: In the safety population, serious adverse events were 47.9% with empagliflozin and 51.6% with placebo. Hypotension (10.4% vs 8.6%), urinary tract infection (9.9% vs 8.1%), and genital infection (2.2% vs 0.7%) were more frequent with empagliflozin; acute renal failure was 12.1% vs 12.8%, ketoacidosis 0.1% vs 0.2%, and lower-limb amputation events 0.5% vs 0.8%.

Section-by-section condensation

Background and rationale

The paper frames HF with preserved EF as an area with limited effective pharmacologic options and notes prior SGLT2 benefit in diabetes and HFrEF, while evidence in preserved EF remained uncertain. EMPEROR-Preserved was designed to test whether empagliflozin could reduce major HF outcomes in this phenotype.

Design and oversight

The study was randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, event-driven, and multinational. Ethics approval and written consent were required. The executive committee included sponsor representatives; the sponsor handled data collection/storage. Countervailing safeguards included an independent DMC, blinded clinical-event adjudication, independent replication of analyses, and independent academic interpretation.

Population and treatment

Eligibility required chronic symptomatic HF, LVEF >40%, elevated NT-proBNP, and either structural heart disease or recent HF hospitalization. Randomization was stratified by region, diabetes, eGFR threshold, and LVEF threshold. Treatment was empagliflozin 10 mg once daily or placebo, both added to usual therapy; clinicians could modify concomitant treatment after randomization.

Baseline balance was close: mean age ≈72 years; ≈45% women; ≈49% diabetes; mean eGFR 60.6; about half eGFR <60; mean LVEF 54.3%; and approximately one third each in LVEF 40–<50, 50–<60, and ≥60% strata.

Endpoints and statistical plan

The primary endpoint was time to first adjudicated CV death or HF hospitalization. The first key secondary endpoint was total first and recurrent HF hospitalizations; the second was eGFR slope. A stepwise hierarchy preserved type I error. The primary Cox model used alpha 0.0497 after one interim analysis; recurrent HF hospitalization used a joint frailty model; eGFR slope used an on-treatment random-coefficient model with alpha 0.001.

No imputation was used. Outcomes outside the hierarchy were not multiplicity-adjusted. The trial targeted 841 primary events for 90% power to detect HR 0.80; enrollment was expanded based on blinded event-rate monitoring without changing the target event count. The interim analysis did not trigger early termination.

Results

The primary endpoint favored empagliflozin: 415/2,997 (13.8%) vs 511/2,991 (17.1%), HR 0.79 (0.69–0.90), p<0.001. NNT was 31. First HF hospitalization favored empagliflozin, while CV death did not show a separately definitive effect. Recurrent HF hospitalization and eGFR slope both met the confirmatory hierarchy.

Other analyses showed a small KCCQ difference at 52 weeks (1.32 points, 95% CI 0.45–2.19), no definitive renal-composite effect (HR 0.95, 0.73–1.24), no all-cause mortality effect (HR 1.00, 0.87–1.15), and modest changes in weight, blood pressure, uric acid, hematocrit, and NT-proBNP. These nonhierarchical findings should not be elevated to confirmatory status.

Safety

Serious adverse events were numerically lower with empagliflozin, but selected adverse events including hypotension, urinary tract infection, and genital infection were more frequent. Ketoacidosis was rare; all reported ketoacidosis events occurred in participants with diabetes at baseline. Safety reporting covered events through 7 days after treatment discontinuation, with amputations followed through trial end.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the result as a clinically meaningful reduction in major HF outcomes, predominantly hospitalization, with benefit seen with or without diabetes. They explicitly state that treatment did not appear to affect cardiovascular or other-cause deaths in this trial.

Appraisal should preserve the ≈23% study-drug discontinuation rate, the composite-endpoint composition, the nonconfirmatory status of outcomes outside the hierarchy, and the selected trial population. The study’s “preserved EF” entry criterion (>40%) includes patients in a mildly reduced EF range, so the label should not be silently narrowed to a contemporary ≥50% HFpEF-only population.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: foundational randomized evidence for empagliflozin in HF with LVEF >40%, especially hospitalization and renal-function trajectory. Do not use as a direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin equivalence trial or as proof of a cardiovascular-mortality benefit.

Main strengths

  • Large, multinational, event-driven randomized trial with clinically consequential HF endpoints.
  • Blinded adjudication, prespecified multiplicity control, independent DMC, and independent statistical verification.
  • Complete supplied source packet includes final protocol and revised TSAP, allowing analysis-plan reconciliation.

Main limitations

  • Primary composite benefit is mainly hospitalization-related; cardiovascular and all-cause mortality were not separately reduced with confidence intervals excluding no effect.
  • About 23% stopped study drug for non-death reasons; the authors note possible attenuation toward the null.
  • Outcomes outside the hierarchy cannot be treated as confirmatory because multiplicity adjustment was not applied.
  • The trial population is selected by natriuretic-peptide and structural/recent-event criteria and excludes eGFR <20/dialysis and recent SGLT2 exposure.
  • The LVEF >40% entry definition spans both mildly reduced and conventionally preserved ranges.
