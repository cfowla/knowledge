One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The review found no clear agent-level differences for all-cause mortality, MI, stroke, or discontinuation. Empagliflozin was the only agent with a statistically significant cardiovascular-mortality reduction versus placebo (RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.69–0.93) and serious-adverse-event reduction (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.76–0.99). HF hospitalization was lower for dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, and sotagliflozin; canagliflozin crossed the null.

The review is useful because it assembles five SGLT2/1-2 agents into one comparative network and preserves clinically important mortality, hospitalization, MI, stroke, and safety outcomes. The strongest practical signal is class-level HF hospitalization benefit with agent-level variation in precision rather than convincing direct evidence of one drug’s superiority.

The main constraint is network geometry: the supplied CINeMA tables show zero direct studies for the drug-versus-drug comparisons. Comparative rankings therefore depend on indirect evidence through placebo/common comparators. Several source-internal reporting conflicts further lower confidence in fine-grained ranking: search cutoff, study/patient totals, RoB tool labeling, and certainty ratings are not internally consistent.

Bottom line: useful for comparative context and formulary discussion; insufficient as a stand-alone basis to prefer empagliflozin or sotagliflozin over another evidence-supported SGLT2 inhibitor.

Methods and review architecture

Search and selection

Full-text Methods report PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase through 8 April 2024, ClinicalTrials.gov, and reference-list checking without language or publication-period limits. Eligible studies were RCTs comparing an SGLT2 inhibitor with placebo or another SGLT2 inhibitor in HF. Two reviewers screened records/full texts and a third adjudicated conflicts.

Figure 1 reports 2,219 records identified, 142 duplicates removed, 2,077 screened, 28 full texts assessed, and 16 RCTs included. The supplied PRISMA checklist maps the review’s reporting locations, while Supplementary Table S1 supplies the database-specific search strings and counts.

Extraction, bias, certainty, and synthesis

Two authors independently extracted baseline and outcome data. Primary outcomes were all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, MI, and HF hospitalization; serious adverse events and discontinuation were secondary. Dichotomous outcomes were analyzed with frequentist random-effects NMA in R using netmeta/netrank and expressed as RR with 95% CI.

The review reports a Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment and CINeMA certainty evaluation. However, the Methods label the risk-of-bias tool “ROB-1” while citing RoB 2 and listing its five domains. In addition, the main-text description of moderate/low CINeMA confidence does not match the supplied S2–S8 tables, which label every listed comparison “High” despite multiple major concerns.

Authors’ interpretation

The authors emphasize empagliflozin for cardiovascular mortality and serious adverse events and sotagliflozin for HF hospitalization, and state that these findings should help guide drug selection. They also acknowledge the absence of head-to-head trials and call for direct comparative RCTs.

Authors’ limitations

The review excluded observational studies, could not evaluate all marketed SGLT2 inhibitors, lacked cardiorenal-syndrome data, and had unequal agent representation (empagliflozin six RCTs, dapagliflozin four, fewer for other agents).

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as comparative context for SGLT2 formulary/HF discussions. Do not use it alone to assert head-to-head superiority or to replace current guideline and direct-trial review.
9/10
Relevance

Directly addresses comparative SGLT2 inhibitor efficacy/safety in HF and is highly relevant to agent-selection questions.

Evidence basis: Large HF-focused network with clinically central outcomes.

Principal limit: The review does not establish direct head-to-head superiority.

Would change score: Direct comparative trials or a cleaner high-certainty network would raise the score.

6/10
Novelty

The agent-level NMA adds comparative framing beyond class-vs-placebo meta-analysis, but prior SGLT2 NMAs already existed.

Evidence basis: Five agents are placed in a single network.

Principal limit: Most cross-drug comparisons are indirect and prior NMAs are cited.

Would change score: New direct comparative RCTs or substantially improved network evidence would raise novelty.

5/10
Method strength

Random-effects frequentist NMA, PRISMA-NMA reporting, registration, RoB assessment, and CINeMA are strengths, but reporting inconsistencies and sparse direct evidence weaken confidence.

Evidence basis: 16 RCTs; prespecified methods; risk-of-bias and CINeMA processes.

Principal limit: No direct drug-vs-drug studies in supplied CINeMA tables; unclear RoB tool labeling; conflicting search cutoff and study totals.

Would change score: Transparent transitivity/directness diagnostics and internally consistent reporting would raise the score.

5/10
Evidence strength

The evidence base is large and RCT-derived, but the comparative drug ranking rests mostly on indirect estimates with imprecision/incoherence concerns.

Evidence basis: 80,666 participants reported in Results; clinically important outcomes.

Principal limit: Cross-agent estimates are indirect; certainty reporting conflicts between main text and supplement.

Would change score: Direct head-to-head trials and reconciled certainty judgments would raise the score.

6/10
External validity

The network spans several major HF and cardiorenal SGLT2 programs, but trial populations and agent representation are heterogeneous.

Evidence basis: Broad multicenter RCT evidence across several agents.

Principal limit: Unequal agent representation and heterogeneous underlying trial populations limit clean transport of relative rankings.

Would change score: Balanced HF-specific trials across agents and phenotypes would raise the score.

5/10
Implementation value

Useful for hypothesis generation and formulary discussion, but not sufficient alone to choose one agent over another.

Evidence basis: Provides agent-specific RR estimates for CV mortality, HHF, and serious AEs.

Principal limit: No direct head-to-head evidence and source inconsistencies make strong agent-ranking decisions fragile.

Would change score: Concordant direct comparative evidence and guideline/real-world integration would raise implementation value.

Critical source-consistency issues

  • Search date: abstract says through March 2023; Methods says through 8 April 2024.
  • Study/population total: abstract/Results report 16 RCTs and 80,666 participants; Discussion says 12 trials and 69,024 participants.
  • Risk-of-bias tool: Methods says ROB-1 but cites/describes RoB 2.
  • CINeMA: main text describes many comparisons as moderate/low confidence; supplied S2–S8 tables label all listed comparisons High while simultaneously documenting major concerns.
  • References: printed reference 22 appears to concatenate an incomplete Nassif et al. citation after the Wiviott citation.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
Use the paper to understand the comparative network, magnitude of HF-hospitalization effects, and where agent-specific signals emerge.
Do not overclaim
Do not convert placebo-anchored indirect estimates into proof that empagliflozin or sotagliflozin is clinically superior to dapagliflozin.
Formulary implication
The findings are compatible with a strong class effect on HF hospitalization, but agent choice should be triangulated with direct pivotal trials, current guidelines, renal indications, safety, cost/access, and local operational constraints.
Read next
Prioritize direct pivotal HF RCTs and any true head-to-head or high-quality comparative-effectiveness evidence before changing agent preference.
