One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In previously treated, biomarker-enriched CLL, acalabrutinib maintained noninferior progression-free survival compared with ibrutinib while reducing several cardiovascular toxicities, most notably atrial fibrillation/flutter and hypertension.

Among 533 randomized patients, median IRC-assessed PFS was 38.4 months in both groups; HR 1.00 (95% CI 0.79-1.27), satisfying the prespecified HR noninferiority margin of 1.429. The Kaplan-Meier curves crossed at about 33 months, violating proportional hazards; the source addressed this with an RMST sensitivity analysis showing a 1.1-month difference over 55 months (95% CI -2.17 to 4.36), also supporting noninferiority.

The clearest comparative safety signal was any-grade atrial fibrillation/flutter: 9.4% with acalabrutinib versus 16.0% with ibrutinib (risk difference -6.6 percentage points, 95% CI -12.2 to -0.9; P=.02). Hypertension events were also lower (9.4% vs 23.2%), and cumulative-incidence HRs favored acalabrutinib for atrial fibrillation/flutter (0.52, 95% CI 0.32-0.86) and hypertension (0.34, 95% CI 0.21-0.54). Grade ≥3 infection rates were similar (30.8% vs 30.0%), and once this gate-kept superiority test failed, later secondary-endpoint P values were descriptive.

Methods

Clinical question and PICO

Population: adults with previously treated CLL requiring therapy, ECOG ≤2, and centrally confirmed del(17p) and/or del(11q). Key exclusions included significant cardiovascular disease, warfarin/equivalent vitamin K antagonist use, prior BTK or BCL-2 inhibitor treatment, and proton-pump inhibitor requirement.

Intervention: acalabrutinib 100 mg orally twice daily until progression or unacceptable toxicity. Comparator: ibrutinib 420 mg orally once daily on the same duration rule. Primary outcome: blinded IRC-assessed PFS. Key secondary outcomes: any-grade atrial fibrillation, grade ≥3 infection, Richter transformation, and OS.

Randomization, masking, and analysis

Randomization was 1:1 and stratified by del(17p), ECOG 2 versus ≤1, and 1-3 versus ≥4 prior therapies; crossover was prohibited. Treatment was open label, but the IRC adjudicating progression and response was blinded. Efficacy used the intention-to-treat population; safety used treated patients.

The primary HR noninferiority margin was 1.429 (30%); 500 planned patients provided 80% power at one-sided alpha .025. A gate-keeping sequence controlled family-wise error: after PFS noninferiority, superiority was tested for atrial fibrillation, grade ≥3 infections, Richter transformation, then OS. Failure at a step made later P values descriptive.

Results

Population

808 patients were screened and 533 randomized at 124 centers in 15 countries (268 acalabrutinib; 265 ibrutinib). Median age was 66 years; 45.2% had del(17p), 64.2% had del(11q), and median prior therapies was two.

Safety

Median exposure was 38.3 months with acalabrutinib versus 35.5 months with ibrutinib. Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 68.8% versus 74.9%; adverse-event discontinuation occurred in 14.7% versus 21.3%. The source reports fewer total cardiac events (24.1% vs 30.0%), hypertension events (9.4% vs 23.2%), and any-grade atrial fibrillation/flutter (9.4% vs 16.0%) with acalabrutinib.

Signals were not uniformly favorable: fungal opportunistic infection was reported in 3.8% with acalabrutinib and 1.9% with ibrutinib. Grade ≥3 infections overall were nearly identical (30.8% vs 30.0%), Richter transformation was 3.8% versus 4.9%, major bleeding 4.5% versus 5.3%, and treatment-emergent AE deaths 6.4% versus 9.5%.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: comparative efficacy/tolerability evidence when evaluating acalabrutinib versus ibrutinib in previously treated CLL. Do not use this 2021 article alone to define current 2026 treatment sequencing or guideline-preferred therapy.
9/10

Relevance

Direct head-to-head comparison of two covalent BTK inhibitors in previously treated high-risk CLL.

Evidence basis: Randomized phase III design; efficacy and toxicity endpoints directly compare acalabrutinib with ibrutinib.

Principal limiting factor: Population is restricted to del(17p)/del(11q) previously treated CLL.

What would raise/lower it: Broader contemporary comparator data and current guideline integration would strengthen practice relevance.

9/10

Novelty

First randomized phase III direct comparison of acalabrutinib and ibrutinib in CLL.

Evidence basis: The study directly tests similar efficacy with improved tolerability rather than comparing each drug with chemoimmunotherapy.

Principal limiting factor: Novelty is relative to 2021 and does not address later BTKi options.

What would raise/lower it: Comparison with newer agents and longer-term follow-up would lower uncertainty about enduring novelty.

8/10

Method strength

Randomization, prespecified noninferiority margin, blinded IRC, multiplicity control, and sensitivity analysis support internal validity.

Evidence basis: 533 patients; multicenter phase III trial; gate-kept endpoints; ITT efficacy; blinded IRC.

Principal limiting factor: Open-label treatment; PFS proportional-hazards assumption was violated.

What would raise/lower it: Blinded treatment and a primary analysis prespecified around nonproportional hazards would raise confidence.

8/10

Evidence strength

The primary noninferiority result is supported by both HR and RMST analyses, and the atrial-fibrillation endpoint met the multiplicity-controlled sequence.

Evidence basis: PFS HR 1.00 (95% CI 0.79-1.27); RMST difference 1.1 months (95% CI -2.17 to 4.36); AF/flutter 9.4% vs 16.0%, P=.02.

Principal limiting factor: Later secondary endpoints became descriptive after grade ≥3 infection superiority was not established; some safety comparisons are unadjusted.

What would raise/lower it: Longer follow-up with mature OS and independently replicated comparative safety would raise the score.

6/10

External validity

Results are applicable to previously treated CLL with del(17p) and/or del(11q), but direct extrapolation beyond that enrichment is limited.

Evidence basis: 124 centers across 15 countries improve geographic breadth.

Principal limiting factor: Key cardiovascular exclusions and biomarker-enriched eligibility narrow generalizability; treatment-naive patients were not studied.

What would raise/lower it: Pragmatic comparative trials across broader CLL populations would raise the score.

8/10

Implementation value

The trial provides actionable comparative toxicity and efficacy information when choosing between these two agents.

Evidence basis: Dosing, discontinuation rates, AF/hypertension/bleeding profiles, and exposure duration are explicitly reported.

Principal limiting factor: The article alone does not establish current treatment sequencing, present-day guideline preference, or label changes.

What would raise/lower it: Current guideline/label verification and newer comparator evidence would raise implementation value.

Main strengths

  • Randomized direct comparison rather than indirect cross-trial comparison.
  • Blinded independent review for PFS/response despite open-label treatment.
  • Prespecified noninferiority margin and multiplicity-controlled secondary sequence.
  • The source acknowledges the proportional-hazards violation and supplies an RMST sensitivity analysis.
  • Long enough follow-up (~41 months) to characterize cumulative cardiovascular toxicity better than short-duration trials.

Main limitations

  • Open-label treatment can influence subjective adverse-event reporting and discontinuation behavior.
  • Biomarker-enriched, previously treated population and cardiovascular exclusions limit external validity.
  • PFS curves crossed; the headline Cox HR is not sufficient by itself to characterize the time-varying treatment relationship.
  • After grade ≥3 infection superiority failed, Richter transformation and OS inferential testing was not formally confirmatory under the gate-keeping plan.
  • Data Supplement and protocol were not available in the supplied Drive folder, limiting independent inspection of additional methods and safety details.
  • Several authors reported industry relationships and some were employees of Acerta/AstraZeneca; medical writing was funded by AstraZeneca. This does not invalidate the results but increases the importance of prespecified methods and blinded outcome review.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this source supports

For patients resembling the trial population, acalabrutinib preserved PFS relative to ibrutinib while reducing atrial fibrillation/flutter, hypertension, and several treatment-limiting adverse effects.

What it does not establish

It does not establish superiority for PFS or OS, does not study treatment-naive patients directly, and does not compare against noncovalent BTK inhibitors or later-generation regimens.

Clinical monitoring implication

Cardiovascular history, blood pressure, arrhythmia risk, bleeding risk, and chronic-treatment tolerability are central differentiators when the choice is specifically between these two agents.

Current-practice boundary

Before applying this as a 2026 treatment-selection rule, verify current guidelines, labels, resistance data, sequencing evidence, and newer head-to-head trials.
