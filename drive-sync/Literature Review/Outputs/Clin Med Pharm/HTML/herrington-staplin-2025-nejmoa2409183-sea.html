One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The extended EMPA-KIDNEY follow-up shows that benefits attributable to randomized empagliflozin exposure did not disappear immediately after study-drug withdrawal, but the relative effect attenuated and was concentrated in roughly the first 6–12 months off treatment.

Across the active and post-trial periods, kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death occurred in 26.2% versus 30.3% (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.72–0.87). During the post-trial period alone, after trial drug had stopped, the primary HR was 0.87 (0.76–0.99). The post-trial effect was strongest in the first year (HR 0.76, 0.60–0.96), weaker in the second year (0.90, 0.75–1.07), and imprecise thereafter. End-stage kidney disease remained lower across combined follow-up (9.0% vs 11.3%; HR 0.74, 0.64–0.87), with a post-trial HR of 0.80 (0.66–0.98).

The most important interpretive constraint is treatment crossover after the trial: open-label SGLT2-inhibitor use became common and similar in both groups (about 43% vs 40%). This dilutes the randomized contrast and makes post-trial comparisons according to actual SGLT2 use strongly confounded. The paper therefore supports the durability and importance of sustained SGLT2 therapy in CKD; it does not establish empagliflozin–dapagliflozin equivalence or justify a molecule-specific formulary switch by itself.

Formulary boundary: this is an empagliflozin-versus-placebo randomized lineage study. Dapagliflozin was not the randomized comparator. Use this source as class/durability evidence, not as direct interchangeability evidence.

Section condensation

Background and objective

EMPA-KIDNEY had already shown cardiorenal benefit with empagliflozin in a broad CKD population. This report asked whether benefit attributable to the original randomized assignment persisted after the trial drug stopped.

Methods

Participants had eGFR 20 to <45 mL/min/1.73 m², or eGFR 45 to <90 with UACR ≥200 mg/g. The active trial randomized empagliflozin 10 mg daily or placebo. PTFU was optional at 185/241 centers in seven countries; trial drug was not supplied post-trial, and local clinicians could start open-label SGLT2 inhibitors.

The prespecified primary analysis retained intention-to-treat comparison by original randomized group across the entire follow-up. Cox models adjusted for minimization variables; Kaplan–Meier methods quantified event probabilities and absolute benefits. Local creatinine replaced central sampling post-trial, and hospitalization outcomes were intentionally not collected.

Results

Of 6609 randomized participants, 4891 entered PTFU. The PTFU cohort was clinically broad: mean age 63 years, 34% women, 57% without diabetes, mean eGFR 36.9, and 49% with UACR ≤300 mg/g. Vital-status ascertainment was highly complete. Open-label SGLT2 use converged after trial completion.

The combined primary HR was 0.79 (0.72–0.87); post-trial-only HR was 0.87 (0.76–0.99). Kidney progression, ESKD, and death/ESKD favored prior empagliflozin assignment over the entire period. Cardiovascular death was lower (HR 0.75, 0.59–0.95), whereas noncardiovascular death was not materially different (HR 0.97, 0.79–1.20). Last-measurement eGFR was 0.8 mL/min/1.73 m² higher (0.1–1.4).

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the early post-trial separation as a finite carryover effect, strongest during the first 6 months and apparent for up to about 12 months. Acute eGFR rebound after withdrawal could contribute to eGFR-based endpoints but cannot explain the continuing ESKD signal.

Key limitations are the optional PTFU subset, exclusion of Japan, local rather than central creatinine, absence of hospitalization outcomes, and substantial nonrandomized post-trial SGLT2 initiation. Participants who did not start an SGLT2 inhibitor were prognostically different, precluding credible naive comparisons based on actual post-trial drug use.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Directly informs durability and continuity of SGLT2 kidney protection in CKD; it does not compare empagliflozin with dapagliflozin.

Evidence basis
Large CKD trial plus prospective post-trial follow-up.
Principal limiting factor
No direct molecule-to-molecule comparison.
Would raise the score
A prespecified head-to-head comparative trial or validated causal comparison of individual SGLT2 inhibitors.

Novelty 8/10

Adds randomized-assignment carryover evidence after trial-drug withdrawal, beyond the original EMPA-KIDNEY treatment-period report.

Evidence basis
Two additional years of post-trial observation with original allocation preserved.
Principal limiting factor
Post-trial exposure became nonrandomized and converged between groups.
Would raise the score
Longer controlled treatment-withdrawal data or replicated carryover evidence.

Method strength 8/10

Original randomization, prespecified post-trial DAP, maintained blinding, adjudication, and high follow-up completeness support inference about original assignment.

Evidence basis
6609 randomized; 4891 post-trial; prespecified Cox/ITT analyses.
Principal limiting factor
Optional PTFU subset, open-label SGLT2 use, local creatinine, and no hospitalization capture.
Would raise the score
Mandatory near-complete follow-up with randomized continuation/withdrawal and standardized central measures.

Evidence strength 8/10

The combined primary effect is precise and the post-trial primary HR remains below 1, but benefit attenuates and later-year intervals cross 1.

Evidence basis
Primary HR 0.79 combined; 0.87 post-trial; first-year 0.76, second-year 0.90.
Principal limiting factor
Crossover and time-varying attenuation limit claims about persistence beyond about 12 months.
Would raise the score
Replicated withdrawal analyses with sustained off-treatment contrast and longer follow-up.

External validity 8/10

Broad CKD etiologies, wide kidney-function/albuminuria spectrum, and majority without diabetes improve applicability.

Evidence basis
57% without diabetes; broad CKD causes; seven countries in PTFU.
Principal limiting factor
Japan excluded from PTFU and only 74% of randomized participants entered it.
Would raise the score
Comparable post-trial retention across all countries and additional geographic diversity.

Implementation value 8/10

Supports avoiding unnecessary SGLT2 discontinuation and favors long-term therapy when indicated, but cannot justify a molecule-specific formulary interchange by itself.

Evidence basis
Residual benefit up to about 12 months and authors’ long-term-treatment interpretation.
Principal limiting factor
No direct empagliflozin–dapagliflozin effectiveness or safety comparison.
Would raise the score
Direct class-member comparative effectiveness with clinical kidney/HF outcomes.

Bottom line

Best use: durable kidney/cardiorenal-effect evidence supporting continued SGLT2 therapy in eligible CKD patients and helping explain why stopping therapy may forfeit future benefit.

Do not use for: claiming direct therapeutic equivalence of empagliflozin and dapagliflozin, estimating molecule-specific inpatient heart-failure effectiveness, or inferring causal effects of post-trial open-label SGLT2 initiation.

Confidence in appraisal: high for the source’s randomized-assignment findings and major limitations; moderate for the exact biological mechanism of carryover, which remains uncertain.

Implementation / practice takeaways

  • When an SGLT2 inhibitor is indicated for CKD, the trial supports sustained treatment rather than planned discontinuation after short exposure.
  • Residual benefit after withdrawal is finite and attenuated; the data do not support a long “drug holiday” strategy.
  • For a dapagliflozin-only inpatient formulary argument, pair this source with direct dapagliflozin CKD/HF trials and molecule-level comparative-effectiveness evidence.
  • Do not compare PTFU initiators versus noninitiators as though treatment were randomized; the paper documents major prognostic imbalance.
  • Interpret post-trial eGFR trajectories with acute-dip reversal and open-label crossover in mind.
