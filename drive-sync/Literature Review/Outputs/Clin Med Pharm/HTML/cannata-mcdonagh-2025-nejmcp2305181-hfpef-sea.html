One-page synthesis

The article treats HFpEF as a heterogeneous syndrome rather than a single disease. It uses LVEF at least 50 percent as the contemporary definition, but repeatedly notes that diagnosis requires more than ejection fraction. The practical diagnostic approach combines symptoms and signs, echocardiographic structure and filling-pressure measures, natriuretic peptides, and exclusion of common mimics. Figure 1 is the most reusable diagnostic object because it supplies concrete thresholds for left ventricular mass, left atrial volume, E/e-prime, pulmonary pressure, tricuspid regurgitation velocity, NT-proBNP, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure, and left ventricular end-diastolic pressure.

The treatment synthesis is strongest for congestion relief and SGLT2 inhibitors. The review reports empagliflozin and dapagliflozin trial hazard ratios of 0.79 and 0.82, and a pooled hazard ratio of 0.80 for cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization. It also stresses that the composite benefit is driven mainly by fewer heart failure hospitalizations, not a clear cardiovascular mortality reduction. Finerenone and obesity-directed incretin therapy broaden the therapeutic options. Semaglutide and tirzepatide data are presented as clinically meaningful for obesity-related HFpEF. By contrast, older RAS inhibitor, ARNI, MRA, and beta-blocker evidence is more mixed, often neutral overall or supported by subgroup signals.

Verdict: Read first. It is a high-value clinical synthesis and a useful map of HFpEF diagnosis, treatment, and research gaps. Use it as an orientation and implementation reference, not as the final authority for 2026 practice. An updated ACC HFpEF Expert Consensus Decision Pathway was published in July 2026 after the article's printed guideline table.

Section condensation

The clinical problem

The review defines HFpEF at LVEF at least 50 percent and reports that it accounts for up to half of hospitalized heart failure. It describes a rising prevalence, incidence of 7 to 18 per 1000 person-years, and substantial post-hospitalization mortality. The phenotype is commonly older, female, and burdened by obesity, diabetes, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, and other conditions. These comorbidities are not peripheral. They shape symptoms, prognosis, diagnostic ambiguity, and treatment targets.

A cited MAGGIC analysis reports lower adjusted mortality than HFrEF, HR 0.68, 95 percent CI 0.64 to 0.71. That relative comparison should not obscure the high absolute risk after hospitalization. The authors also separate true HFpEF from treatable phenocopies such as amyloid and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

Diagnosis

The authors require symptoms or signs, LVEF at least 50 percent without prior reduced EF, and objective structural or filling-pressure abnormalities. They recommend ruling out pulmonary disease, ischemia, valvular disease, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, Fabry disease, and amyloidosis. NT-proBNP at 125 pg/mL is described as highly sensitive but poorly specific in a cited diagnostic study. Obesity can suppress natriuretic peptide concentrations while renal impairment and atrial fibrillation can raise them.

Diagnostic scores can help, but the review says they lack robust validation and leave uncertainty in about 30 percent of patients. When noninvasive testing is inconclusive, the authors support exercise or resting hemodynamics, diastolic stress testing, and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging when a mimic is suspected.

Treatment goals and older neurohormonal therapies

The treatment goals are symptom relief, better quality of life, and fewer hospitalizations. The review states that no available medical therapy has shown a significant mortality reduction in HFpEF. It emphasizes decongestion and management of hypertension, obesity, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, ischemic disease, and sleep apnea.

RAS inhibitor and ARNI results are mixed. CHARM-Preserved, I-PRESERVE, and PEP-CHF were neutral for their primary composites, though some hospitalization signals favored treatment. PARAGON-HF was borderline overall with subgroup signals in women and lower EF. PARALLAX reduced NT-proBNP without improving 6-minute walk distance. MRA evidence includes neutral TOPCAT, physiological but not symptomatic benefit in Aldo-DHF, and positive FINEARTS-HF evidence for finerenone. The review does not support routine beta-blocker use for HFpEF itself in the absence of another indication.

SGLT2 inhibitors and incretin therapy

Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin have the most consistent randomized HFpEF evidence in the review. EMPEROR-Preserved HR was 0.79, 95 percent CI 0.69 to 0.90. DELIVER HR was 0.82, 95 percent CI 0.73 to 0.92. The pooled composite HR was 0.80, 95 percent CI 0.73 to 0.87. Hospitalization reduction was stronger, HR 0.74, while cardiovascular death did not reach conventional significance, HR 0.88, P=0.052.

For obesity-related HFpEF, STEP-HFpEF reports better KCCQ score, weight, walk distance, and C-reactive protein with semaglutide. SUMMIT reports a tirzepatide HR of 0.62 for cardiovascular death or worsening HF plus a 6.9-point KCCQ advantage. The authors consider these results clinically relevant in obesity-related HFpEF but identify non-obese HFpEF as an unanswered question.

Devices, guidelines, and uncertainty

CardioMEMS studies support hemodynamic monitoring in selected chronic heart failure patients, while interatrial shunt trials are less reassuring. REDUCE LAP-HF II did not improve its composite endpoint. RELIEVE-HF had a neutral win ratio and a possible harm signal in preserved EF patients.

The guideline section explains that all surveyed international guidance supports diuretics and that the 2023 ESC focused update gives SGLT2 inhibitors Class I. Older U.S. and Japanese guidance gives Class IIb recommendations to selected ARNI or MRA use. The authors identify persistent uncertainty in classification, diagnostic criteria, phenotype-specific therapy, non-obese use of incretin therapies, and mortality reduction.

Authors' case recommendations

For the vignette, the authors recommend intravenous diuresis, then oral loop diuretic after euvolemia, plus an SGLT2 inhibitor. They would intensify treatment of resistant hypertension and obesity, add spironolactone if blood pressure stays above 130 over 80 mm Hg despite ramipril and amlodipine, consider a GLP-1 receptor agonist for obesity, and assess for sleep-disordered breathing.

The long-term plan is lifestyle management, surveillance for progression, and active treatment of comorbidities. These are author recommendations within a narrative review, not graded guideline recommendations.

Purpose: Provide a pragmatic HFpEF diagnostic pathway.
Inputs: Symptoms and signs, LVEF, ECG, echocardiography, natriuretic peptides, clinical mimics.
Key structural thresholds: LV mass index >=95 g/m2 in women or >=115 g/m2 in men; relative wall thickness >0.42; LA volume index >34 mL/m2 in sinus rhythm or >40 mL/m2 in AF.

Natriuretic peptide thresholds: NT-proBNP >=125 pg/mL in sinus rhythm or >365 pg/mL in AF.
Invasive thresholds: PCWP >=15 mm Hg at rest or >=25 mm Hg with exercise, PCWP:CO slope >=2 mm Hg/L/min, or LVEDP >=16 mm Hg.
Failure mode: Obesity can lower natriuretic peptides, while renal impairment and AF can raise them. Mimics must be excluded.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 9/10

Directly addresses HFpEF diagnosis and treatment with practical thresholds, doses, trial effect estimates, and guideline comparisons.

Evidence basis. A broad HFpEF Clinical Practice review with diagnostic and treatment sections.

Limiting factor. It is secondary synthesis rather than a primary or systematic evidence review.

What would change the score. Would rise with explicit evidence-selection methods and current guideline integration.

Novelty: 6/10

At publication, it combined mature HFpEF management with newer finerenone and incretin evidence in one clinical review.

Evidence basis. FINEARTS-HF, STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT, SGLT2, device, and diagnostic material appear together.

Limiting factor. Most component evidence was already published elsewhere.

What would change the score. Would rise with new analysis, a new diagnostic framework, or original comparative synthesis.

Method strength: 5/10

The article is a narrative Clinical Practice review with clear structure but no reproducible search, study-selection method, or formal risk-of-bias process.

Evidence basis. The Journal format states a case, evidence review, guideline review, and author recommendations.

Limiting factor. No systematic review method or evidence-grading procedure is reported.

What would change the score. Would rise with protocolized searching, selection, appraisal, and evidence grading.

Evidence strength: 7/10

Many central claims cite large randomized trials and formal guidelines, but this source itself is secondary and sometimes relies on subgroup signals or mixed LVEF populations.

Evidence basis. Pivotal SGLT2 trials, MRA trials, incretin trials, diagnostic consensus, and guideline documents are cited.

Limiting factor. Trial inclusion thresholds often extend below LVEF 50 percent, and one printed confidence interval is internally impossible.

What would change the score. Would rise with primary-source reconciliation and explicit certainty grading for each recommendation.

External validity: 8/10

The review covers common HFpEF phenotypes and comorbidities and uses international guidance, but HFpEF heterogeneity and trial LVEF thresholds limit exact transfer to every phenotype.

Evidence basis. Broad clinical syndrome, several comorbid phenotypes, and multiple international guidelines are discussed.

Limiting factor. Some evidence comes from LVEF greater than 40 or 45 percent cohorts rather than strict HFpEF.

What would change the score. Would rise with phenotype-specific evidence and more direct evidence in strict LVEF at least 50 percent populations.

Implementation value: 9/10

The diagnostic thresholds, treatment classes, doses, guideline table, and case recommendations are directly usable for clinical review and workflow design.

Evidence basis. Figure 1, Table 1, trial summaries, and the conclusion provide concrete decisions.

Limiting factor. The guideline table predates the July 2026 ACC HFpEF pathway and should not be treated as the current final authority.

What would change the score. Would rise with explicit integration of the 2026 ACC pathway and current drug labeling.

Main strengths

  • Concrete diagnostic thresholds and a usable diagnostic flow.
  • Quantitative treatment effects for major HFpEF drug classes.
  • Direct comparison of several international guidelines and dosing details.
  • Good separation of established hospitalization benefit from the absence of a demonstrated mortality benefit.
  • Useful research-horizon appendix with 36 ongoing or recently completed studies.

Main limitations

  • Narrative review design with no reported systematic search, study-selection process, risk-of-bias assessment, or formal evidence certainty grading.
  • Some trial evidence comes from mildly reduced EF or LVEF above 40 or 45 percent cohorts, so strict HFpEF inference is imperfect.
  • Some recommendations rely on subgroup findings after neutral primary trial outcomes.
  • The printed PARAGLIDE-HF confidence interval is internally impossible.
  • The guideline table is no longer fully current after publication of the July 2026 ACC HFpEF Expert Consensus Decision Pathway.

Source-integrity finding. The selected PDF prints a PARAGLIDE-HF NT-proBNP geometric mean ratio of 0.85 with 95 percent CI 0.73 to 0.10. The interval cannot be correct as printed because its upper bound is below the point estimate and lower bound. The ATOM set preserves the source rather than guessing the intended number.

Practice translation

Use now

Use the diagnostic thresholds, congestion strategy, SGLT2 hospitalization evidence, comorbidity framework, and obesity-directed therapy summary as a concise clinical reference.

Do not use alone

Do not use the article as the sole authority for 2026 guideline class, drug labeling, renal dosing, contraindications, or phenotype-specific treatment decisions.

Read next

Prioritize primary EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER, FINEARTS-HF, STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT, major diagnostic consensus sources, and the 2026 ACC HFpEF Expert Consensus Decision Pathway.
