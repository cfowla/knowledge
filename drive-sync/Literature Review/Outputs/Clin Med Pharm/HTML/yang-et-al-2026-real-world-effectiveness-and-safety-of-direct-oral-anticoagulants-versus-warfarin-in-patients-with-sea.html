One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first for AF-MS anticoagulation evidence; do not treat as definitive practice-changing randomized evidence.

Core thesis. In a matched Taiwan claims cohort of 1034 DOAC users and 1034 warfarin users with atrial fibrillation and moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis, DOAC use was associated with more ischemic stroke at 1 year (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.05-1.41; RD +4.97 percentage points) and more composite stroke (RR 1.23, 95% CI 1.07-1.42), while myocardial infarction was lower (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.37-0.99). At 5 years, ischemic stroke and composite stroke no longer differed statistically, whereas systemic embolism was lower with DOACs (RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.31-0.88). Major bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage, gastrointestinal bleeding, other bleeding, and all-cause death did not differ at either horizon.

Why it matters. The design directly targets a common evidence gap because moderate-to-severe MS was excluded from landmark DOAC trials. The methods are stronger than a simple claims comparison: investigators emulated target trials, incorporated patients with prior warfarin exposure, matched contemporaneously on time-conditional propensity scores, trimmed non-overlap, and modeled absolute and relative risks. The central uncertainty is whether residual differences in MS severity, warfarin quality (INR), treatment adherence, dose, and clinical decision-making explain some of the observed short-term signal.

Methods condensation

Clinical question and target trial

Adults with AF accompanying moderate-to-severe MS diagnosed from 2011 through 2021 were eligible if they had no prior DOAC use before AF-MS diagnosis and no baseline valve replacement. Prior warfarin use was allowed. Strategies were initiation of a DOAC (with warfarin discontinuation if applicable) versus initiation or continuation of warfarin. The causal contrast was the observational analogue of an intention-to-treat effect.

Effectiveness outcomes included ischemic stroke (main), systemic embolism, ischemic stroke/systemic embolism composite, composite stroke, MI, and all-cause death. The main safety outcome was major bleeding (intracranial plus gastrointestinal bleeding), with component and other critical-site bleeding also assessed at 1 and 5 years.

Emulation, matching, and statistics

Using NHIRD, DOAC initiators without prior warfarin were matched to warfarin initiators; switchers with prior warfarin were matched to warfarin users with similar prior exposure duration (±1 month) and calendar period (±2 months). Each time-based exposure set contained one DOAC initiator and multiple candidate warfarin users. Matching was 1:1 on nearest time-conditional propensity score without replacement, with non-overlap trimming at the first and 99th PS percentiles.

Balance was assessed by standardized mean differences. Cumulative risks, absolute RDs, and RRs at 1 and 5 years were estimated using pooled logistic regression with treatment, linear/quadratic follow-up time, and baseline covariates; uncertainty used a 300-sample nonparametric bootstrap. Additional analyses stratified prior warfarin exposure and DOAC type.

Results condensation

After matching, each group contained 1034 patients. Mean age was about 68 years, roughly 32% were male, mean CHA2DS2-VASc was 4.7, and all reported baseline SMDs were <0.1. DOAC use was 37.9% rivaroxaban, 24.5% dabigatran, 22.0% apixaban, and 15.6% edoxaban. Median follow-up was 34 months in both groups.

The most important finding is horizon-dependent: the 1-year signal favored warfarin for ischemic and composite stroke, while the 5-year estimates for those outcomes crossed the null. Conversely, systemic embolism favored DOACs at 5 years and MI favored DOACs at 1 year. Bleeding and death estimates were compatible with no between-group difference. In additional analyses, the short-term stroke signal was concentrated in DOAC initiators with prior warfarin use; rivaroxaban showed higher ischemic/composite stroke risk at 1 and 5 years, while non-rivaroxaban DOACs were not reported to increase ischemic stroke. Numerical subgroup estimates reside in supplement-only tables not supplied here.

Discussion and limitations

The authors position the study against inconsistent prior evidence: rivaroxaban performed worse than vitamin K antagonist therapy in INVICTUS, whereas an earlier Korean observational study had favored DOACs. They argue their design better handles prior warfarin use and measured confounding than simpler active-comparator new-user approaches in this setting. They also interpret the rivaroxaban-specific result as aligned with INVICTUS and call for trials of other DOACs.

Load-bearing limitations: modest matched sample; no granular MS-severity data; no INR values or time-in-therapeutic-range data; no reliable treatment adherence or clinical-decision data; no per-protocol analysis; no dose-specific analysis. These gaps leave important residual confounding and exposure-misclassification pathways open.

Model inference: the disappearance of the ischemic-stroke difference at 5 years despite a significant 1-year signal should not be interpreted as proof that long-term treatments are equivalent. In an observational ITT analogue, later treatment changes, adherence differences, competing risks, and residual confounding can dilute or reshape long-horizon estimates.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses anticoagulant choice in AF with moderate-to-severe MS, a high-risk clinical population.

Evidence basis: Matched real-world AF-MS cohort; clinically meaningful thromboembolic and bleeding outcomes.

Limiting factor: Observational evidence cannot establish treatment equivalence to a randomized trial.

Would raise/lower: A large pragmatic RCT across contemporary Asian practice would raise confidence.

Novelty 8/10

Rationale: Applies target-trial emulation with prevalent new-user methods to a population largely excluded from landmark DOAC trials.

Evidence basis: Time-conditional propensity-score matching and inclusion of prior warfarin users.

Limiting factor: The treatment comparison itself is not novel; the main contribution is design and population.

Would raise/lower: Independent replication with granular echocardiographic and INR data would increase novelty and impact.

Method strength 7/10

Rationale: The causal framework, active comparator, contemporaneous matching, trimming, measured-confounder adjustment, and bootstrap uncertainty are strong for claims data.

Evidence basis: 1:1 time-conditional PS matching; all reported SMDs <0.1; pooled logistic modeling; 300-sample bootstrap.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding, no MS-severity/INR data, no adherence data, no per-protocol analysis, and no dose analysis.

Would raise/lower: Prospective randomization or validated clinical covariates for MS severity, INR quality, adherence, and dose would raise the score.

Evidence strength 6/10

Rationale: The 1-year stroke signal is clinically important and statistically supported, but attenuation at 5 years and subgroup dependence reduce certainty.

Evidence basis: At 1 year ischemic stroke RR 1.22 (95% CI 1.05-1.41), RD +4.97 points; at 5 years RR 1.08 (0.95-1.21).

Limiting factor: Observational design and treatment changes/adherence during long follow-up are not captured by the ITT analogue.

Would raise/lower: Consistent randomized results, especially for non-rivaroxaban DOACs, would raise confidence.

External validity 6/10

Rationale: Population-wide Taiwan data improve representativeness for Taiwanese/Chinese practice.

Evidence basis: NHIRD covers >99% of Taiwan; matched cohort includes prior and new warfarin users.

Limiting factor: Generalizability outside Asian populations, and to patients with well-characterized MS severity, is uncertain.

Would raise/lower: Multi-country replication with echocardiographic characterization would raise the score.

Implementation value 7/10

Rationale: The study is useful for risk framing and supports caution with rivaroxaban, but it is not sufficient alone to justify an off-label treatment strategy.

Evidence basis: Outcome-specific 1- and 5-year estimates and DOAC-type analyses are clinically interpretable.

Limiting factor: No dosing analysis, no INR control, and supplement-only subgroup numbers were unavailable in the supplied source.

Would raise/lower: Randomized head-to-head data for individual DOACs, dosing, and INR-quality strata would increase actionability.

Bottom line: High relevance and useful methodology. The study strengthens concern about DOAC use—particularly rivaroxaban—in AF with moderate-to-severe MS, but its observational limitations and unavailable granular INR/MS-severity information prevent it from resolving the treatment question for individual non-rivaroxaban DOACs.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
Use the paper to inform risk discussions, literature review, and evaluation of off-label DOAC exposure in AF-MS, especially rivaroxaban.
Do not overclaim
Do not interpret the non-significant 5-year stroke estimate as equivalence, and do not generalize the non-rivaroxaban qualitative finding into proven safety or effectiveness.
Monitor in future evidence
Randomized results by specific DOAC, anticoagulant dose, MS severity, INR quality/TTR, adherence, and prior warfarin exposure.
Reproducibility
Statistical code is available from the corresponding author; patient-level data require formal Taiwan Ministry access and are not public.
