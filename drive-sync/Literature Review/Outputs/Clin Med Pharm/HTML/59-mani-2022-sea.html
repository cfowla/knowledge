One-page synthesis

MANI was created to fill a longitudinal evidence gap in mild and moderate asthma. The paper describes a planned Italian network that will recruit at least 20,000 adults from 200 pulmonology and allergology centers, follow routine clinical care, and observe disease severity, control, exacerbations, treatment patterns, patient-reported outcomes, inflammatory features, exposures, and costs. The planned follow-up extends for 10 years after the last participant is enrolled.

The paper's strongest contribution is operational. It specifies who will be enrolled, what will be measured, how often follow-up questionnaires will be issued, how data will enter the REDCap case report form, and the broad statistical approach. Its main limitation is equally clear. This is a protocol-style article without cohort results. The authors also acknowledge that observational design and recruitment from Italian secondary care limit causal interpretation and generalizability.

Verdict: Skim deeply. Keep this source for MANI design, outcome definitions, registry provenance, and future linkage to completed MANI analyses. Do not use it as an efficacy or comparative-effectiveness result.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames mild and moderate asthma as common but poorly characterized longitudinally. MANI is presented as a large real-world observational effort intended to track epidemiological evolution and a broad set of patient, treatment, inflammatory, environmental, and lifestyle outcomes.

The abstract's "results and conclusion" language is prospective rather than empirical. It says the study will add knowledge. No enrolled-cohort result is reported.

Introduction

The introduction argues that existing asthma natural-history evidence often comes from severe or difficult-to-treat populations or from data sources that lack reliable diagnoses, lung function, symptoms, and biomarkers. It also reviews treatment and phenotyping literature to motivate a dedicated mild/moderate asthma network.

Those epidemiologic and treatment statements are background claims tied to cited literature. They are not MANI-generated evidence and were therefore excluded from the primary-study atom set.

Methods

The source describes MANI as cluster-based, real-world, cross-sectional, prospective, and observational. It plans a non-probability sample of consecutive adults age 18 to 80 with specialist-diagnosed mild-to-moderate asthma. Recruitment spans 200 Italian pulmonology and allergy centers. Severe asthma, selected pulmonary diseases or infections, and immunological disorders requiring immunosuppressants or continuous oral steroids are excluded.

Baseline data include history, asthma features, comorbidity, available spirometry and imaging, questionnaires, self-rated control, and laboratory testing for allergy and airway inflammation. Follow-up questionnaires are planned at least every 6 to 12 months. The statistical plan uses descriptive summaries, incidence with 95% confidence intervals, and Cox proportional hazards regression. The sample-size statement assumes at least 20,000 participants to detect an estimated 8% incidence for the primary outcome with beta error 0.1 and alpha error 0.05.

Discussion

The authors expect heterogeneity in risk factors and progression and plan to use sociodemographic, clinical, and patient-reported data to identify clusters. They suggest that cluster-specific analyses may focus on individual aggravating factors during exacerbations and progression.

The paper explicitly acknowledges two constraints: observational design and recruitment in Italian secondary care. The discussion remains a statement of expected value because no prospective outcome data are presented.

Disclosures, ethics, and data access

The article reports no conflicts of interest. It describes the study as nonprofit and supported by the Italian Respiratory Society and the Italian Society of Allergology, Asthma and Clinical Immunology. It states that eligible participants provide informed consent and that the protocol follows listed ethics and legal frameworks.

The paper states that study datasets will be available from the steering committee upon reasonable request. It also describes REDCap-based data collection and center-level access controls. These are reported design features, not independently audited security or governance findings.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 6/10

Useful for understanding a national mild/moderate asthma registry and its planned longitudinal outcomes.

Evidence basis. The source defines a large real-world cohort, clinically relevant outcomes, and a 10-year follow-up plan.

Limiting factor. It contains no completed cohort results.

What would change the score. Completed longitudinal MANI analyses with patient-centered outcomes would raise this score.

Novelty: 5/10

The national network focus on mild and moderate asthma is useful, but the paper mainly describes a registry design.

Evidence basis. The source extends the Italian network model from severe asthma to a planned mild/moderate cohort.

Limiting factor. No comparative empirical result or validated new measurement method is reported.

What would change the score. Evidence that the network yields uniquely informative phenotypes or outcomes would raise this score.

Method strength: 5/10

The plan is prospective, multicenter, large, and explicit about outcomes and analysis, but sampling is non-probability and observational.

Evidence basis. The article specifies 200 centers, at least 20,000 participants, eligibility criteria, repeated follow-up, and a statistical plan.

Limiting factor. Secondary-care recruitment and lack of randomization limit causal inference and representativeness.

What would change the score. Probability-based recruitment, prespecified missing-data methods, and stronger confounding control would raise this score.

Evidence strength: 3/10

The article strongly documents what MANI intends to do, but it provides no participant outcome data.

Evidence basis. The paper reports the protocol, planned measurements, and sample-size assumptions.

Limiting factor. There are no observed effects, rates, confidence intervals from enrolled patients, or validation results.

What would change the score. Published prospective MANI outcome analyses would raise this score.

External validity: 5/10

A 200-center Italian network could capture broad secondary-care practice, but the sampling frame does not represent all asthma care settings.

Evidence basis. Centers span pulmonology and allergology sites across Italy and recruit consecutive referred patients.

Limiting factor. The authors themselves identify secondary-care recruitment in Italy as a generalizability limit.

What would change the score. Primary-care participation, probability sampling, and replication outside Italy would raise this score.

Implementation value: 6/10

The paper gives enough detail to understand the registry plan and outcome domains, but not enough to reproduce every operational decision.

Evidence basis. It names REDCap, follow-up intervals, data domains, planned statistics, eligibility criteria, and governance elements.

Limiting factor. No full data dictionary, analytic code, missing-data plan, or completed workflow performance is provided.

What would change the score. A public protocol, data dictionary, statistical analysis plan, and operating metrics would raise this score.

Clinical validity

Population. Adults age 18 to 80 with specialist-diagnosed mild-to-moderate asthma seen in participating Italian pulmonology or allergy clinics.

Exposure or intervention. No study-assigned treatment. MANI observes routine care, treatment patterns, environmental factors, behavior, and other clinical exposures.

Comparator. No single prespecified comparator is defined in this design article. Some secondary analyses are planned across treatment groups and schedules.

Outcomes. Disease-severity evolution is primary. Secondary domains include control, exacerbations, medication use, patient-reported measures, inflammatory patterns, exposures, digital care, and costs.

Validity constraints. Non-probability secondary-care sampling can create selection bias. Observational treatment comparisons are vulnerable to confounding. The article does not specify every later analytic detail, including a full missing-data strategy or complete confounder plan.
