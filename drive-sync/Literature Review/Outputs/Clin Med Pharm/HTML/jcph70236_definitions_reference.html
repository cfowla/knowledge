Definitions in paper context

Definitions are paraphrased for use while reading this paper. The “context” column explains how the term functions in the article rather than giving a stand-alone textbook entry.

TermWorking definitionUse in this paperReading note
CYP3A5 A cytochrome P450 enzyme that contributes to rivaroxaban metabolism; activity varies substantially by genotype. The review highlights rs776746/CYP3A5*3 as a reduced- or non-function variant that may increase rivaroxaban concentrations and minor bleeding risk, although some clinical studies showed modest or nonsignificant effects after adjustment. Interpret with renal function and interacting drugs; CYP3A5 genotype alone is not a complete exposure predictor.
CYP3A4 A major hepatic and intestinal cytochrome P450 enzyme involved in oxidative metabolism of rivaroxaban. The paper identifies CYP3A4 polymorphisms such as rs2242480, rs2246709, and rs3735451 as associated in some studies with higher rivaroxaban concentrations, prolonged PT, or minor bleeding. The review also emphasizes drug interactions with CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers as a major practical modifier.
ABCB1 A gene encoding P-glycoprotein, an efflux transporter that can limit absorption, distribution, and tissue exposure of substrate drugs. ABCB1 variants were among the most studied rivaroxaban pharmacogenomic factors, with reported associations for rs1045642, rs4148738, rs1128503, and rs2032582, but findings were often inconsistent across studies. ABCB1 is clinically relevant partly because P-gp inhibitors can increase DOAC exposure.
SUSD3 Sushi domain-containing protein 3, a gene/locus not classically part of rivaroxaban metabolism or transport. The review lists SUSD3 rs76292544 as an emerging genetic signal associated with bleeding risk during rivaroxaban or DOAC therapy. Mechanism and reproducibility remain preliminary; treat as a hypothesis-generating bleeding-risk locus.
CYPJ2J Likely a typographical rendering of CYP2J2, a cytochrome P450 enzyme expressed in heart, liver, and intestine. The article discusses CYP2J2, not “CYPJ2J,” as a contributor to rivaroxaban metabolism alongside CYP3A4/5; CYP2J2 variants were not strongly associated with clinical outcomes in the reviewed evidence. Use CYP2J2 for searching and interpretation.
dual mode of elimination Rivaroxaban elimination through two major routes: metabolic degradation and renal excretion of unchanged active drug. The paper states that about two-thirds of rivaroxaban is inactivated by metabolism and about one-third is excreted unchanged in urine. This explains why hepatic metabolism, transporter effects, renal function, and drug interactions can all influence exposure.
single nucleotide polymorphism A common single-base DNA variant that may alter gene expression, protein function, or serve as a marker linked to a functional variant. The review uses SNPs to organize evidence linking variants in CYP enzymes, transporters, and emerging genes with rivaroxaban pharmacokinetics, toxicity, or efficacy. An SNP association does not prove mechanism unless supported by functional or replicated clinical data.
CES1 Carboxylesterase 1, a hydrolytic enzyme important for some drug metabolism pathways. In this review, CES1 is mentioned mainly as a comparator pharmacogenomic gene affecting dabigatran levels, not as a central rivaroxaban gene. Do not over-apply CES1 to rivaroxaban from this paper; its role is contextual within broader DOAC pharmacogenomics.
sulfotransferases Phase II conjugation enzymes that add sulfate groups to substrates, altering metabolism and elimination. The article mentions sulfotransferases in relation to apixaban plasma levels and metabolism, not as a main rivaroxaban pathway. Included to contrast rivaroxaban-focused CYP/transporter genetics with other DOAC pharmacogenomic pathways.
P-glycoprotein An ATP-dependent efflux transporter encoded by ABCB1 that pumps drugs across cell membranes. The paper links P-glycoprotein to DOAC pharmacokinetics and notes that ABCB1/P-gp variants or inhibitors may affect rivaroxaban exposure and bleeding risk. P-gp is both a pharmacogenomic and drug-interaction concept.
minimal allele frequency In the paper’s phrasing, this refers to allele-frequency information used to decide whether a variant is common enough to analyze; the standard term is usually minor allele frequency (MAF). The authors used SNPedia MAF/GMAF information and excluded many SNPs with MAF below 1% unless they strengthened a conclusion. Low MAF can make a study underpowered because few participants carry the variant allele.
ABCG2 A gene encoding the breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP), an efflux transporter involved in drug absorption, distribution, and excretion. The review describes ABCG2 variants such as rs2231142, rs3114018, and rs2622604 as potential modifiers of rivaroxaban exposure or bleeding risk, with mixed study results. ABCG2 is a transporter signal, not a metabolic enzyme signal.
POR Cytochrome P450 oxidoreductase, an electron-transfer partner required for normal function of microsomal CYP enzymes. POR rs1057868 appears in the review’s table of SNPs significantly correlated with rivaroxaban pharmacokinetics, but the narrative detail is limited. A POR variant could influence CYP-mediated metabolism indirectly rather than by transporting rivaroxaban itself.
APOB Apolipoprotein B, a major structural protein in atherogenic lipoproteins and lipid transport. The review reports APOB rs13306198 as an emerging variant associated with higher bleeding risk during rivaroxaban or other DOAC therapy. This is not a canonical rivaroxaban ADME gene; interpret as an emerging outcome association needing validation.
BRD3 Bromodomain-containing protein 3, a chromatin-associated regulatory protein. The article lists BRD3 rs467387 in a table under efficacy-related SNPs, but provides minimal mechanistic discussion. Treat as a preliminary pharmacogenomic signal rather than a validated rivaroxaban pathway.
CYP2C19 A cytochrome P450 enzyme best known clinically for activating clopidogrel and metabolizing multiple drugs. The review states CYP2C19 does not directly affect rivaroxaban concentrations, but variants may affect bleeding outcomes when rivaroxaban is co-administered with clopidogrel because platelet reactivity changes. Relevant mainly in combination antithrombotic therapy, not rivaroxaban monotherapy exposure prediction.

Interpretation notes

Core versus emerging genes. CYP3A4/5, ABCB1/P-gp, and ABCG2/BCRP are the most mechanistically plausible rivaroxaban PK genes in this review. SUSD3, APOB, BRD3, and several other loci are better treated as emerging outcome signals.
Genetics must be read alongside renal function and interactions. The review repeatedly frames pharmacogenomics as additive to clinical factors such as renal impairment, age, antiplatelet therapy, and CYP3A4/P-gp interacting drugs.
Typo handling. The requested term “CYPJ2J” does not match the paper’s gene notation. The relevant term in the article is CYP2J2.
