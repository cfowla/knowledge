One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In this single-center retrospective pediatric asthma cohort, montelukast initiation was followed by a substantial rate of parent-reported neuropsychiatric reactions severe enough to prompt drug cessation, and the matched comparison suggested a markedly higher cessation risk than with low-dose ICS monotherapy.

The main cohort included 106 children aged 1-17 years who had initiated montelukast as monotherapy or adjunct therapy. Seventeen stopped montelukast because of parent-reported neuropsychiatric adverse drug reactions (ADRs), for an incidence of 16% (95% CI 10%-26%). Restricting events to those adjudicated as definitely or probably related yielded 13 events and an incidence of 12% (95% CI 7%-21%); none were classified as definitely related. Conservative denominator-expansion sensitivity analyses reduced the parent-reported incidence to 14% and 8% but did not erase the signal.

Twenty-five children (24%) reported at least one neuropsychiatric ADR, totaling 57 reactions. Irritability, aggressiveness, and sleep disturbance were most frequent. Median onset was 7 days (IQR 2-14), and median resolution after cessation was 2 days (IQR 0-3.5). No suicidal ideation was reported in this cohort. In the 84:84 matched cohort, 12 montelukast-treated children versus one ICS-treated child stopped therapy for a parent-reported neuropsychiatric ADR, producing a relative risk of 12.0 (95% CI 1.6-90.2). The effect remained large when restricted to adjudicated probable events (RR 9.0, 95% CI 1.2-69.5) and in unmatched post hoc analyses.

Load-bearing caution. The primary endpoint is not the incidence of all neuropsychiatric symptoms. It is neuropsychiatric ADRs that led parents to stop the drug. The 16% estimate therefore reflects event occurrence plus recognition, attribution, and tolerance. The retrospective interview occurred a median of about 3 years after drug initiation, and only 106 of 223 prescribed montelukast patients completed the interview.

Methods

Design and population

Children came from the Sainte-Justine University Health Centre asthma clinic and the Pediatric Asthma Database and Biobank. The retrospective window covered February 2011 through April 2016. Eligibility required age 1-17 years, physician-confirmed asthma, and initiation of maintenance montelukast as the only newly added medication. Children denying intake of the medication of interest were excluded.

The main exposure included montelukast monotherapy or adjunct therapy to ICS or ICS/LABA. In the nested matched cohort, montelukast monotherapy or adjunct-to-ICS initiators were matched 1:1, within ±90 days of initiation, to children started on ICS monotherapy at ≤250 micrograms/day HFA-beclomethasone equivalent.

Ascertainment and adjudication

A trained research assistant used a non-leading standardized parent interview. The interview first asked about asthma-drug cessation and adverse reactions, then prompted with a neuropsychiatric ADR list. Reported events were characterized for symptom type, onset, dose changes/cessation, dechallenge, and rechallenge.

Potential neuropsychiatric ADRs were coded and sent to a six-member adjudication committee. Two blinded adjudicators independently assessed severity, evolution, and causality using the Naranjo score while alternately assuming the drug was montelukast or ICS; disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Outcomes and statistics

The primary outcome was the incidence of parent-reported neuropsychiatric ADRs leading to montelukast cessation. Secondary outcomes included all neuropsychiatric ADRs, timing, resolution, severity, causality, candidate host/treatment determinants, and relative incidence versus ICS in the matched cohort.

The primary incidence used Poisson 95% confidence intervals. Sensitivity analyses restricted events by adjudicated causality and expanded denominators under conservative nonparticipant assumptions. Modified multivariable logistic regression estimated relative risk in the matched comparison; exploratory determinant models included clinical, co-treatment, dose, CYP2C8*3, and SLCO2B1 variables.

Results

Patient flow and cohort

Of 1010 database patients, 223 had a montelukast-initiation prescription. After 117 nonparticipants (not reached, declined, or did not recall intake), 106 eligible children completed the interview. The cohort had a median age of 5 years (IQR 3-8), 58% were male, 62% Caucasian, and 83% had persistent asthma. Montelukast was prescribed as monotherapy in 43%, adjunct to ICS in 43%, and adjunct to ICS/LABA in 14%. Among 104 with dose recorded, 51% received 4 mg, 45% 5 mg, and 4% 10 mg.

Reaction phenotype and timing

Twenty-five children (24%) reported at least one neuropsychiatric ADR, with 57 reactions in total. The most frequently reported categories were irritability (9 children), aggressiveness (8), and general sleep disturbance (7). Median onset was 7 days after initiation (IQR 2-14), and symptoms resolved a median of 2 days after cessation (IQR 0-3.5). No suicidal ideation was reported. The adjudication table classified reactions as very mild or mild; none required an antidote/medical intervention, were life-threatening, or required hospitalization.

Discussion, limitations & generalisability

Authors' interpretation

The authors interpreted the 16% drug-cessation incidence and the 12-fold matched relative risk as evidence of a meaningful real-world neuropsychiatric safety signal that differed from earlier trial reports. They proposed possible explanations including a true real-world effect, interaction with other asthma controllers, greater recognition after safety warnings, and lower family tolerance for behavior changes.

They also emphasized the early timing of most reactions and rapid improvement after cessation. Exploratory analyses suggested associations with ICS/LABA co-treatment and, under a broader event definition, non-Caucasian ethnicity; the authors acknowledged that small numbers limited firm conclusions about determinants.

Internal validity limits. Selection bias is plausible because 117 of 223 prescribed montelukast patients did not participate, and nonparticipants differed in ethnicity. Interviews were retrospective, with about a 3-year median delay from initiation to interview. Parent reports may be influenced by label awareness and attribution. Matching and multivariable models cannot remove unmeasured confounding by indication, asthma burden, adherence, prior exposure, co-medication, or family behavior/tolerance.
Causality boundary. Naranjo adjudication supported "probable" or "possible" relationships but produced no "definite" cases. Importantly, adjudicators gave the same causality assessment whether they were told to assume the culprit drug was montelukast or ICS. That feature reduces confidence that the Naranjo process discriminated drug-specific causality in this design.
Generalisability. Results principally apply to children 1-17 years seen in a tertiary pediatric asthma setting, many with substantial prior asthma morbidity and neurobehavioral comorbidity. The study itself states that findings may not generalize to other or older populations.

Evaluation & appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High clinical relevance and historically important real-world safety signal, but moderate evidentiary strength. Use it as supporting evidence and methodological context, not as a standalone causal incidence estimate or current prescribing rule.

Bottom-line appraisal

The study is stronger as a safety-signal and hypothesis-generating comparative cohort than as a precise estimator of causal risk. The large relative risks are directionally compelling, but the confidence intervals are extremely wide, the event count is small, participation is incomplete, and the primary outcome depends on retrospective parent report and treatment cessation. Its greatest durable value is documenting real-world pediatric event patterns, early timing, dechallenge response, and the discrepancy between trial-era safety reporting and postmarketing experience.

Practice translation

Use from the sourceDiscuss the possibility of behavior, mood, and sleep changes when montelukast is started; the source observed many reported events within the first 2 weeks.
Do not inferDo not treat 16% as a universal causal incidence, the RR of 12 as a precise effect size, or the exploratory ethnicity/ICS-LABA associations as validated patient-level predictors.
Monitoring signalRapid improvement after cessation was common in the cohort, but the study was not designed to define a standardized management algorithm.
Current-practice boundaryCurrent prescribing decisions should use the present product label, regulatory communications, and contemporary guidelines rather than this 2017 paper alone.
External currency verification - not source-derived. On March 4, 2020, the U.S. FDA required a Boxed Warning for serious neuropsychiatric events with montelukast. FDA advises counseling patients/caregivers about mental health effects, considering risks and benefits for asthma, and reserving montelukast for allergic rhinitis when alternatives are ineffective or not tolerated. This later action does not retroactively invalidate the article's 2017 correction: at the time of the original publication, the existing FDA warning was not yet a Boxed Warning.
