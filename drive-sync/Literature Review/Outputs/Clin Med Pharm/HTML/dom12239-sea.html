One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In a six-RCT primary network connected through sulphonylureas, dapagliflozin produced HbA1c lowering similar to DPP-4 inhibitors, TZDs and sulphonylureas at about 1 year, with more favorable weight than DPP-4 inhibitors and sulphonylureas and substantially lower hypoglycaemia odds than sulphonylureas.

The systematic review screened 4270 abstracts (2882 after deduplication), found eight eligible studies and used six in the primary NMA; the two GLP-1 trials were excluded from the base case because their intensive 600-kcal-deficit lifestyle programs were considered inconsistent with the other trials. All primary-network trials used a sulphonylurea active comparator, so most dapagliflozin comparisons were indirect rather than head-to-head.

The random-effects NMA estimated dapagliflozin HbA1c differences of -0.08 percentage points versus DPP-4 inhibitors, -0.02 versus TZDs and 0.00 versus sulphonylureas, with all 95% credible intervals spanning no difference. Weight favored dapagliflozin by -2.74 kg versus DPP-4 inhibitors and -4.67 kg versus sulphonylureas. Hypoglycaemia odds were much lower versus sulphonylureas (OR 0.06), but comparative estimates versus DPP-4 inhibitors and TZDs were imprecise. Systolic blood pressure was not meta-analysed.

Main interpretive constraint. The network is sparse and star-shaped, class-level pooling is assumed, hypoglycaemia definitions differ, and evidence coverage ends in 2012. The paper is therefore supportive historical evidence, not a current equivalence study.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper frames second-line therapy after metformin as a tradeoff between glycaemic efficacy and treatment burdens such as hypoglycaemia and weight gain. Because dapagliflozin lacked head-to-head trials against most relevant classes, the authors used NMA to estimate relative 1-year effects while retaining randomization within the component RCTs.

The key methodological premise is comparability of relative treatment effects across the network. This transitivity/exchangeability assumption becomes the central validity constraint because all paths run through sulphonylureas.

Methods — systematic review

Searches covered EMBASE, MEDLINE, CENTRAL, conference proceedings, trial registries, references and manufacturer-held dapagliflozin reports, with an October 2012 update and no language restriction. Two reviewers independently filtered studies and extracted data. Eligible RCTs lasted 52 weeks ±6 weeks and enrolled adults with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy.

Outcomes were HbA1c, body weight, systolic blood pressure and hypoglycaemia. Missing variance could be imputed from the same drug class when feasible. Appendix S1 supplies detailed database strategies, improving search reproducibility.

Methods — network meta-analysis

Licensed agents and doses were pooled within classes and sulphonylureas served as the common comparator. HbA1c and weight used normal likelihoods; hypoglycaemia used odds ratios. Both fixed and random effects were presented, with random effects selected a priori. Baseline HbA1c was examined as a network-level covariate; its coefficient was not significant.

Two GLP-1 studies were removed post hoc from the base case because they included an intensive diet/exercise program. Model selection considered DIC, covariate significance and posterior between-study variance.

Results

Eight studies met review criteria; six formed the primary network (four DPP-4, one TZD, one dapagliflozin), all linked through sulphonylureas. HbA1c effects were similar across classes. Dapagliflozin showed favorable weight differences versus DPP-4 inhibitors and sulphonylureas and markedly lower hypoglycaemia odds versus sulphonylureas. TZD weight could not be estimated due to missing variance; SBP was too sparsely reported for NMA.

The updated search added a linagliptin-versus-sulphonylurea trial whose 52-week HbA1c, weight and hypoglycaemia direction/magnitude were broadly consistent with NMA outputs. Appendix S2 translated relative effects to absolute estimates.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret dapagliflozin as providing similar glycaemic control with weight loss and less hypoglycaemia than sulphonylureas. They emphasize clinical relevance of avoiding weight gain and hypoglycaemia, while noting that other adverse-event categories were not suitable for NMA.

They also acknowledge heterogeneous trial characteristics, sparse pairwise evidence, untestable unknown confounding and the dependence of NMA validity on trial comparability. Industry funding and substantial author-industry relationships are disclosed.

Detailed EMBASE/Ovid MEDLINE search strings, diabetes/drug terms and randomized-trial filters. Included because it materially supports reproducibility.

Appendix S2 — Absolute estimates

Using the sulphonylurea anchors, estimated absolute HbA1c changes were -0.69% for dapagliflozin, -0.67% for TZDs and -0.61% for DPP-4 inhibitors. Estimated absolute weight changes were -3.36 kg for dapagliflozin and -0.61 kg for DPP-4 inhibitors, with SU anchored at +1.31 kg. The GLP-1 sensitivity weight estimate was extreme and strongly influenced by the intensive lifestyle studies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used as historical indirect-comparison evidence for dapagliflozin’s 1-year glycaemic, weight and hypoglycaemia profile. Do not use it alone to make a current inpatient formulary-equivalence decision.

Strengths

  • Explicit eligibility criteria and broad multi-source search with update.
  • Independent dual screening/data extraction.
  • Bayesian NMA reports uncertainty and both fixed/random effects.
  • Baseline-HbA1c meta-regression and sensitivity analysis expose key assumptions.
  • Appendices preserve search reproducibility and absolute-effect translation.

Limitations

  • Indirect star network through sulphonylureas; no direct dapagliflozin-vs-DPP-4/TZD trial evidence.
  • Only six RCTs in the primary network and one to four RCTs per pairwise comparison.
  • Class-level pooling, heterogeneous hypoglycaemia definitions, and missing variance/SBP data.
  • Post-hoc removal of two GLP-1 lifestyle trials highlights sensitivity to design differences.
  • Industry funding and author conflicts increase the importance of independent corroboration.
  • Search end date October 2012 makes the paper obsolete for modern cardiovascular/renal outcomes evidence.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Useful signal
Supports a historical view that dapagliflozin’s glucose lowering was broadly comparable to common second-line classes while weight and hypoglycaemia differentiated therapies.
Do not overclaim
The paper does not show direct dapagliflozin–empagliflozin equivalence, inpatient interchangeability, cardiovascular equivalence, renal equivalence, or class-wide safety equivalence.
For formulary rationale
Treat as background evidence only. Contemporary outcomes trials, head-to-head comparative effectiveness, labels and current guideline statements should carry more weight.
