One-page synthesis

Core thesis: The author accepts all-cause mortality as an objective and robust benchmark but argues that using it as the sole primary endpoint in HABP/VABP trials can miss clinically meaningful antibiotic effects because deaths may arise from comorbid illness and because mortality does not capture outcomes such as ventilator-free days or ICU length of stay.

The letter is motivated by a reported discordance in the Nguyen et al. meta-analysis: newer antibiotics were reported to improve microbiological eradication against carbapenem-resistant gram-negative bacilli (risk ratio 1.50) without reducing 28-day all-cause mortality. The author treats this discordance as a reason to reconsider whether a mortality-only endpoint adequately isolates treatment benefit in a population with substantial competing causes of death.

The proposed alternative is not to abandon mortality, but to refine endpoint design. The author suggests composite outcomes such as survival without organ failure or time to clinical stability while explicitly acknowledging that adjudicating infection-related death is difficult. The argument is conceptually relevant to trial design, but the letter provides no new empirical analysis showing that the proposed alternatives are more sensitive, unbiased, statistically efficient, or better aligned with regulatory decision-making.

Do not overread the paper: It does not demonstrate that all-cause mortality is an invalid endpoint, that composite endpoints are superior, or that newer antibiotics improve patient-centered outcomes despite unchanged mortality. Those are hypotheses or design arguments, not results generated by this correspondence.

Section condensation

Opening and cited meta-analysis

The author highlights a reported mismatch between microbiological and mortality outcomes in a recent HABP/VABP meta-analysis: microbiological eradication favored newer antibiotics in carbapenem-resistant gram-negative bacilli (RR 1.50), whereas 28-day all-cause mortality was not reduced. The letter uses this contrast to frame a trial-endpoint question rather than to reanalyze the meta-analysis.

Comorbidity and background mortality

The first argument is that HABP/VABP populations often have substantial comorbidity. The author reasons that deaths within 28 days may result from conditions such as heart failure, malignancy, or thromboembolism rather than the index pneumonia, creating background mortality that can obscure an antibiotic-specific effect.

This is a plausible causal argument, but the correspondence does not quantify competing causes of death, estimate attributable mortality, or show how much statistical power or treatment-effect dilution occurs under all-cause mortality.

Patient-centered outcomes

The second argument is that all-cause mortality fails to capture outcomes meaningful to clinicians and families, specifically ventilator-free days and ICU length of stay. Earlier extubation is offered as an example of clinical value that could occur without a mortality difference.

The source does not present HABP/VABP trial data demonstrating improvement in these outcomes with newer antibiotics; the point is conceptual and endpoint-oriented.

Alternative endpoint framework

The author acknowledges the difficulty of adjudicating infection-related deaths and therefore does not simply substitute cause-specific mortality. Instead, the letter proposes considering composite endpoints such as survival without organ failure or time to clinical stability to balance objectivity and clinical relevance.

The correspondence does not specify component definitions, hierarchy or weighting, time windows, handling of competing events, missing data, or a statistical estimand. Those omissions limit direct implementation in a trial protocol.

Conclusion and disclosures

The concluding position is deliberately moderate: all-cause mortality remains a robust benchmark, but its suitability as the sole primary endpoint deserves reconsideration. The author states that no data were created or analyzed, no funding was received, and no conflicts of interest were reported.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Use it forInterpreting HABP/VABP trials or meta-analyses where microbiological improvement does not translate into mortality benefit; prompting explicit discussion of what the primary endpoint is intended to capture.
Do not use it forChoosing an antibiotic, claiming newer agents improve patient-centered outcomes, rejecting mortality endpoints outright, or adopting a composite endpoint without formal specification and validation.
