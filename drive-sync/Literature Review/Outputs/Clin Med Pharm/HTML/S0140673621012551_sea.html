One-page synthesis

Core result: Among pcJIA participants who responded to 18 weeks of open-label tofacitinib, continuing tofacitinib reduced randomized-withdrawal flare risk versus switching to placebo: 29% vs 53% by week 44; HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.27-0.79).

The trial enrolled 225 children/adolescents at 64 centers in 14 countries. The primary efficacy analysis focused on 184 pcJIA participants; 142 responders were randomized after the 18-week run-in. The responder-enriched withdrawal design is efficient for demonstrating maintenance of effect, but it answers a narrower question than a conventional parallel-group trial: whether responders maintain control if therapy is continued, not the absolute response probability for all treatment-naive or treatment-eligible patients.

Key secondary efficacy findings supported the primary result. At week 44, JIA/ACR30/50/70 rates were 71%/67%/54% with tofacitinib versus 47%/47%/37% with placebo, and CHAQ-DI change favored continued treatment (-0.09 vs +0.03; p=0.0292). Inactive disease and remission were less frequent. Safety over the trial was broadly similar across randomized groups for common events, but the sample and follow-up were insufficient for uncommon or long-latency risks.

Methods and design

Study design and participants

Part 1 was an 18-week open-label tofacitinib run-in. Eligible patients were 2 to <18 years with defined JIA categories; pcJIA required at least five active joints and inadequate response to at least one DMARD. Patients with active systemic features, persistent oligoarthritis, undifferentiated JIA, or recent active uveitis were excluded.

Patients achieving at least JIA/ACR30 at week 18 entered part 2 and were randomized 1:1 to continue tofacitinib or switch to placebo for 26 weeks or until flare. Randomization was stratified by JIA category and baseline CRP; patients, investigators, and sponsor were masked in part 2.

Intervention and concomitant therapy

Patients weighing at least 40 kg received oral tofacitinib 5 mg twice daily. Lower-weight patients received 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, or 4 mg twice daily by weight band to approximate adult exposure; oral solution was used in lower-weight participants. Stable NSAIDs, methotrexate, and low-dose oral glucocorticoids were permitted.

Endpoints and statistics

The primary endpoint was pcJIA flare rate by week 44. Flare required at least 30% worsening in at least three of six JIA/ACR core variables with no more than one improving at least 30%. Key secondary endpoints were ACR30/50/70 response and CHAQ-DI change at week 44. The trial used ITT efficacy analyses, a gatekeeping multiplicity strategy, Kaplan-Meier/log-rank methods for time to flare, mixed models for continuous outcomes, and a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

Participants discontinuing part 2 for reasons other than remission were counted as flared and as nonresponders thereafter. This conservative rule protects the primary endpoint from attrition bias but makes later observed-case continuous plots less directly comparable with the binary estimands.

Results

Enrollment and baseline

Of 286 screened, 225 were enrolled: 184 pcJIA, 20 PsA, and 21 ERA. Median age was 13 years; 75% were female; 65% received concomitant methotrexate. Among pcJIA participants, 142/184 (77%) were randomized in part 2.

Efficacy

Primary flare by week 44 was 21/72 (29%) with continued tofacitinib and 37/70 (53%) with placebo, p=0.0031; HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.27-0.79). The sensitivity analysis estimated a -25 percentage-point difference (95% CI -40 to -10; p=0.0013). Median time to flare was 155 days with placebo and not estimable with tofacitinib.

Week-44 ACR30/50/70 were 71%/67%/54% versus 47%/47%/37%; inactive disease was 26% versus 17%; clinical remission was 4% in each group. JADAS minimum disease activity was 47% versus 33%, and JADAS inactive disease 18% versus 10%. Exploratory PsA/ERA comparisons were based on very small randomized samples.

Safety

In part 2, any AE occurred in 77% of tofacitinib and 74% of placebo participants; SAEs in 1% and 2%, respectively. During all tofacitinib exposure, 48% had infections/infestations, 2% serious infections, 1% herpes zoster, and 1% adjudicated hepatic events. No deaths were reported.

The article reports no gastrointestinal perforation, interstitial lung disease, MACE, macrophage activation syndrome, malignancy, opportunistic infection, thrombosis, or tuberculosis. These zero counts should not be interpreted as exclusion of risk because exposure time and sample size were limited.

Figure 2 — Primary and secondary efficacy in randomized pcJIA

Primary result: flare by week 44 occurred in 21/72 (29%) continuing tofacitinib versus 37/70 (53%) placebo; HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.27-0.79). The sensitivity risk difference was -25 percentage points (95% CI -40 to -10). Time-to-flare analysis gave the same HR 0.46; placebo median time to flare was 155 days while the tofacitinib median was not estimable because 71% remained flare-free.

Secondary pattern: response curves separated after withdrawal, especially for JIA/ACR30 and JIA/ACR70; inactive-disease rates were less clearly separated.

Figure 3 — Mean JADAS over time

Mean JADAS fell rapidly during open-label part 1 and remained low with continued tofacitinib in part 2. At week 44, observed mean JADAS was approximately 3.6 with tofacitinib and 3.7 with placebo among participants still observed; the shrinking observed-case denominators make this figure less robust for between-group inference than the prespecified binary endpoints.

Table 1 — Baseline characteristics

Among 225 enrolled participants, median age was 13 years, 75% were female, median disease duration was 2.5 years, and 147 (65%) received methotrexate. The pcJIA cohort was dominated by RF-negative polyarthritis (104/225 overall) and was predominantly White (196/225, 87%).

Table 2 — Treatment-emergent adverse events

In part 2, any adverse event occurred in 68/88 (77%) tofacitinib versus 63/85 (74%) placebo; serious adverse events occurred in 1/88 (1%) versus 2/85 (2%). Across the entire tofacitinib exposure period, 107/225 (48%) had infections/infestations and 4/225 (2%) had serious infections. Permanent discontinuation due to adverse events in part 2 was 18% versus 34%.

Selected AESIs were absent during the trial, including deaths, MACE, malignancy, thrombosis, opportunistic infection, and tuberculosis. The trial duration and sample size are inadequate to establish rare-event risk.

Table 3 — Laboratory values

Median creatine kinase, hematologic indices, AST/ALT, and lipid values were broadly similar between randomized groups at week 44. The article reports no Hy's law cases; laboratory interpretation is limited by different observed denominators at later time points.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first for pivotal pcJIA efficacy evidence; do not use alone for long-term safety or current prescribing decisions.

Strengths

Prospective phase 3 design; blinded randomized withdrawal; central/independent confirmation of key efficacy assessments; explicit endpoint definitions; ITT analysis; multiplicity control; protocol and SAP available; clinically interpretable effect magnitude.

Limitations and uncertainty

The design enriches for initial responders and assesses maintenance indirectly through flare after withdrawal. The randomized sample is modest; later continuous outcomes have shrinking observed denominators; PsA/ERA comparisons are underpowered; the cohort was predominantly White. The 44-week exposure is too short to characterize rare or long-term adverse events. Sponsor involvement and financial relationships require transparent interpretation but do not invalidate the prespecified randomized result.

Bottom line

The source provides strong trial-level evidence that continuing tofacitinib maintains disease control better than withdrawal to placebo among pcJIA patients who first respond during an 18-week run-in. It is substantially less informative about treatment initiation versus an active comparator, nonresponder management, rare harms, and long-term comparative effectiveness.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
As pivotal efficacy evidence and as a source for endpoint definitions, withdrawal-trial design, pediatric weight-based dosing, and response magnitudes.
Do not overclaim
Do not infer that 71% of all eligible pcJIA patients will respond to starting therapy; the week-44 randomized cohort was selected for prior response.
Safety boundary
Common-event safety is described; rare-event absence is not proof of absence. Use current labeling/guidance and longer-term data for contemporary safety decisions.
