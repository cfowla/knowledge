One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. High clinical-pharmacy relevance and a useful efficacy-versus-exposure signal, but not practice-changing evidence on its own.

The study built the first oral ciprofloxacin popPK model reported specifically for adults aged at least 75 years, using 42 concentrations from 15 hospitalized patients. A one-compartment model with first-order absorption and elimination best fit the data. Mean apparent clearance was 14.3 L/h and apparent volume 247 L; interindividual variability was large (75.2% for CL/F and 36.0% for V/F). The absorption rate constant could not be identified from the sparse sampling and was fixed at 2.5 h-1. No covariate was retained in the final model.

Monte Carlo simulation exposed a clinically important tension. At the 0.5 mg/L P. aeruginosa breakpoint, PTA for AUC0-24/MIC >=125 was only 18.7%, 56.6%, and 76.5% for 250, 500, and 750 mg q12h. An exploratory 1250 mg q12h regimen reached 92.7%, but this exceeded the observed dose range and was not presented as a recommendation. Meanwhile, simulated 750 mg q12h yielded mean AUC0-24 124.3 mg*h/L and median 103.5 mg*h/L, above the paper's cited proposed 100 mg*h/L upper exposure limit for many simulated patients. The authors therefore frame early TDM as a potentially useful strategy, while acknowledging that larger outcome-linked studies are required.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper frames ciprofloxacin as one of few orally available antipseudomonal options and uses AUC0-24/MIC >=125 as the efficacy target. It notes that higher exposure may create toxicity concerns, particularly in older adults with renal and physiologic variability. Oral ciprofloxacin popPK is much less studied than IV dosing, and no prior model specifically guided oral dosing in older adults.

The primary aims were to develop an oral popPK model in this population and use Monte Carlo simulation to test whether standard oral regimens achieve the target for P. aeruginosa. The paper also cites a proposed AUC0-24 upper safety limit of 100 mg*h/L, while explicitly noting that this limit is not clinically validated.

Methods

This prospective pilot enrolled patients >=75 years who had received oral ciprofloxacin within 48 h of sampling. Blood was obtained at assumed steady state after 48 h using peak, mid-interval, and trough sampling. Total concentrations were measured with a validated UHPLC-diode array assay. Model development in NONMEM evaluated one- and two-compartment structures, exponential IIV, multiple residual-error models, SAEM estimation, and weakly informative priors because of sparse data. Covariates explored were age, sex, weight, serum creatinine, Cockcroft-Gault CrCl, CKD-EPI renal function, and CRP.

Model assessment used convergence/precision criteria, GOF diagnostics, a 1000-simulation pcVPC, and 1000-replicate bootstrap. Monte Carlo simulation generated 1000 virtual patients for standard q12h regimens across MIC 0.125-2 mg/L; the optimal regimen was defined as the lowest achieving >=90% PTA. D-optimality was also used to evaluate more informative sampling times for a future study.

Results

Fifteen patients contributed 42 concentrations. Median age was 82 years, weight 75 kg, and Cockcroft-Gault CrCl 46 mL/min; 80% had P. aeruginosa. A one-compartment model was selected. CL/F was 14.3 L/h, V/F 247 L, IIV 75.2% and 36.0%, and proportional residual variability 16.1%. Ka was fixed at 2.5 h-1. Weight on V/F reduced OFV but made little difference to IIV and was not retained.

At MIC 0.5 mg/L, PTA was 18.7%, 56.6%, and 76.5% for 250, 500, and 750 mg q12h; 1250 mg q12h reached 92.7%, and no regimen achieved the target above MIC 0.5 mg/L. At MIC 0.25 mg/L, 750 mg q12h reached 96.0%. Simulated mean AUC0-24 rose from 41.4 to 82.8 to 124.3 mg*h/L across 250, 500, and 750 mg q12h. Sampling optimization proposed approximately 0.40, 2.05, and 12 h, materially improving expected Ka precision.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the model as showing inadequate standard-regimen PTA at the current 0.5 mg/L breakpoint despite substantial exposure, particularly with 750 mg q12h. They discuss a possible mismatch between susceptibility breakpoint and achievable oral target exposure in older adults and suggest a 0.25 mg/L cutoff may be more consistent with >=90% PTA. They explicitly caution that the simulated 1000-1250 mg q12h regimens are exploratory and should not be used as dosing recommendations.

Major limitations are the 15-patient sample, sparse sampling, fixed Ka, need for priors, extrapolation beyond observed doses, large IIV, no patient-isolate MICs, and no clinical outcome validation. The authors suggest early TDM may help manage exposure variability and target attainment, but call for larger prospective outcome-linked studies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance9/10

Directly relevant to oral antipseudomonal ciprofloxacin, renal-function-sensitive dosing, older adults, PK/PD, and TDM.

Evidence basis: older inpatient cohort with observed oral exposure plus dose/MIC simulations.

Limiting factor: modeled rather than clinical outcome evidence.

Would rise: direct outcome-linked TDM/dosing study.

Novelty8/10

First oral ciprofloxacin popPK model specifically in older adults according to the authors.

Evidence basis: dedicated >=75-year oral cohort and future-study optimal-design analysis.

Limiting factor: model built from only 15 patients.

Would rise: external validation plus prospectively tested dosing strategy.

Method strength5/10

Appropriate nonlinear mixed-effects workflow, diagnostic checks, pcVPC, bootstrap, and transparent use of priors.

Evidence basis: NONMEM SAEM, 1000 pcVPC simulations, 1000 bootstrap replicates.

Limiting factor: sparse sampling, fixed Ka, weakly informative priors, very small cohort.

Would rise: richer PK sampling and larger prospective dataset without dependence on fixed absorption parameters.

Evidence strength5/10

The PK and PTA findings are internally coherent but remain simulation-driven and not linked to patient outcomes.

Evidence basis: observed concentrations anchor the model; PTA values are model outputs.

Limiting factor: no isolate MICs or clinical outcomes; higher doses are extrapolated.

Would rise: measured MICs, clinical/microbiologic outcomes, and prospective validation.

External validity4/10

Applicability is limited to a narrow single-centre older inpatient population.

Evidence basis: median age 82; 80% P. aeruginosa.

Limiting factor: excluded ICU, CrCl <30, dialysis, and unstable renal function; N=15.

Would rise: multicentre cohorts spanning frailty, renal-function range, ICU/outpatient settings, and diverse infection syndromes.

Implementation value6/10

Useful for identifying an efficacy-exposure tension, selecting patients for closer exposure assessment, and designing a larger PK study.

Evidence basis: explicit PTA curves, simulated AUC distribution, and optimized sampling times.

Limiting factor: no validated bedside dosing algorithm or clinical TDM intervention.

Would rise: target-concentration protocol, outcome-linked TDM thresholds, and prospective implementation study.

Validity and practice translation

Do not use this study to justify doses above 750 mg q12h. The authors themselves state that 1000-1250 mg q12h simulations were exploratory and outside the observed dose range.

The strongest practice signal is not "give more ciprofloxacin," but that older adults may simultaneously have inadequate modeled PTA at a 0.5 mg/L MIC and high/variable exposure at standard high oral dosing. That tension supports individualized assessment and further study. The paper's TDM suggestion is plausible within its results but remains unvalidated as an outcome-improving strategy in this population.

Practice and implementation takeaways

What this supportsQuestioning the assumption that susceptibility at MIC 0.5 mg/L guarantees adequate oral ciprofloxacin PK/PD target attainment in very old adults.
What it does not supportRoutine escalation above labeled/observed regimens, breakpoint changes, or a definitive TDM target based on this pilot alone.
Useful bedside implicationWhen ciprofloxacin is being relied on for serious Pseudomonas infection in an older adult, MIC, renal function, exposure variability, toxicity risk, and feasibility of TDM deserve explicit consideration.
Research / build implicationA future CDS/TDM workflow would need isolate MIC, standardized AUC estimation, renal-function context, exposure safety criteria, and prospective outcome validation rather than dose-only rules.
