Synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The correspondence is useful for interpretive boundary-setting but is not a new clinical trial.

Three letters challenge possible explanations of DAPA-HF: serum-urate reduction, diuretic/volume effects, and protocol-permitted glucose-lowering medication changes or unblinding. The authors reply that serum urate was not measured; the early creatinine rise may reflect tubuloglomerular feedback rather than simple volume contraction; diuretic use changes were small; and only 5.4% of participants met the HbA1c/treatment criteria under which insulin or sulfonylurea reduction could be considered.

The reply is strongest where it clarifies what the protocol required and what the trial actually measured. It is weaker where it rules out mechanisms without direct mediation experiments. These statements should therefore remain interpretive follow-up rather than being converted into trial-generated causal evidence.

Letters and reply

Urate hypothesis

Borghi and Cicero proposed urate lowering as a mediator. The investigators state that urate was not measured and argue that available urate-lowering evidence makes this explanation unlikely.

Diuresis hypothesis

Peled and Dau proposed that greater diuresis might account for benefit. The authors point to the small early creatinine change, possible tubuloglomerular feedback, reversal with longer treatment, small changes in diuretic use, and lack of trials showing diuretic dose escalation reduces HF admissions or death.

Glucose-lowering treatment and blinding

Vieira and Mehra asked whether insulin/secretagogue reductions could confound the diabetic subgroup and affect blinding. The authors clarify that reduction was optional for a small eligible subgroup, changes were infrequent, benefit was also present without diabetes, and HbA1c separation was small.
