Source context

The article reports early institutional experience using cefazolin therapeutic drug monitoring in severe infections, most often MSSA bacteremia and endocarditis. The methods compare patient factors and cefazolin exposure categories, then use logistic regression to assess predictors of hypoalbuminemia among those with albumin measurements.

Best useClarifying how cefazolin exposure, protein binding, renal clearance, albumin, and infusion strategy interact in severe infections.
Do not use forDefinitive outcome-based cefazolin target validation or a universal dosing algorithm.
Evidence maturityObservational, hypothesis-generating, clinically relevant, but limited by sample size and total-only cefazolin concentrations.

Definitions in paper context

Definitions are paraphrased for use while reading this paper. The “context” column explains how the term functions in the article rather than giving a stand-alone textbook entry.

TermWorking definitionUse in this paperReading note
Cephalosporium acremonium A fungus historically associated with the discovery of cephalosporin antibiotics; the organism is now commonly referred to as Acremonium chrysogenum in updated taxonomy. The introduction uses it to trace cefazolin back to the original post-war discovery of cephalosporin-producing organisms isolated from Sardinian sewage. It is historical background, not a variable in the study or a microbiologic pathogen in the cohort.
cephalosporin C A naturally occurring beta-lactam compound isolated from Cephalosporium/Acremonium that became a chemical starting point for semisynthetic cephalosporin antibiotics. The paper states that isolation of cephalosporin C paved the way for cefazolin development. Its role is lineage/history: it explains cefazolin’s pharmacologic ancestry, not current dosing or susceptibility interpretation.
augmented renal clearance Renal drug elimination that is higher than expected, usually reflected by high creatinine clearance and faster clearance of renally eliminated drugs. The introduction cites prior cefazolin pharmacokinetic data linking augmented renal clearance and obesity with subtherapeutic cefazolin concentrations. In this cohort, patients with subtherapeutic levels had higher median creatinine clearance than others. The paper does not set a formal ARC threshold; it uses creatinine clearance patterns as a mechanistic clue for lower cefazolin exposure.
chi-squared test A statistical test that compares proportions across groups when categorical data have adequate expected cell counts. The methods used chi-squared testing, along with Fisher’s exact test, to compare categorical variables between cefazolin exposure groups or albumin groups. A significant p value indicates group imbalance/association, not causation.
Fisher’s exact test An exact test for categorical variables, preferred when sample size is small or expected cell counts are sparse. The paper reports it as an alternative to the chi-squared test for categorical comparisons in a 70-patient cohort with some low-frequency features such as vasopressor use or renal replacement therapy. Useful in this paper because several cells contain zero or few patients.
Wilcoxon rank-sum test A nonparametric test comparing the distribution of a continuous variable between two independent groups, often used when data are skewed. The paper used it for continuous variables reported as medians and IQRs, such as cefazolin concentrations, creatinine clearance, dose, and length of stay. It tests rank distributions rather than assuming normally distributed means.
stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis A regression approach for a binary outcome that includes multiple predictors, with variables added or retained based on predefined screening criteria. The authors used stepwise multivariable logistic regression to assess predictors of hypoalbuminemia, entering variables with p < 0.10 from bivariate screening; endocarditis and lower cefazolin concentration remained associated. Stepwise selection can be unstable in small samples; interpret associations as exploratory rather than definitive causal predictors.
bivariate model An initial analysis evaluating the relationship between one predictor and one outcome, or comparing one variable across two groups. The paper used bivariate results to screen variables for entry into the multivariable model. Bivariate associations may disappear after adjustment and can be confounded by disease severity, dosing strategy, renal function, or infection syndrome.

Interpretation notes

Most clinically relevant term cluster. Augmented renal clearance, hypoalbuminemia, protein binding, and infusion strategy form the practical pharmacokinetic frame for this paper: each can alter total cefazolin concentrations and the probability of falling below the authors’ target range.
Statistical-method terms are screening tools, not proof of mechanism. The chi-squared, Fisher’s exact, Wilcoxon, bivariate, and stepwise logistic methods identify associations in a small observational cohort. They do not validate the cefazolin therapeutic range or establish outcome causality.
