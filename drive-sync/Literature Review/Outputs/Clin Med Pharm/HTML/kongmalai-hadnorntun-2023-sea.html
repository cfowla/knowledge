One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. High formulary relevance, but active-agent ranking should remain hypothesis-generating because the network is largely indirect, clinically heterogeneous, and frequently very-low confidence.

Core thesis. Across HF-T2D randomized evidence, SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the composite of cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization, with the composite benefit driven primarily by fewer HF hospitalizations. The overall pairwise pooled RR for the composite was 0.77 (95% CI 0.73-0.81). For HF hospitalization, network estimates versus standard care were 0.51 for canagliflozin, 0.68 for sotagliflozin, 0.71 for empagliflozin, and 0.79 for dapagliflozin. The overall network ranked canagliflozin highest for the composite and HF hospitalization, but the HF-specific sensitivity analysis instead ranked sotagliflozin first, then empagliflozin, then dapagliflozin.

Why it matters. The review supports a class-level HF hospitalization benefit and provides a structured view of cross-agent evidence. It does not establish clinical interchangeability or superiority between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin by direct randomized comparison. Canagliflozin's apparent ranking advantage rests heavily on post-hoc CANVAS evidence, and the authors explicitly caution its interpretation.

Safety. The pooled serious-AE RR was 0.87 (95% CI 0.83-0.91). Network estimates for serious AEs were below 1 for canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, and empagliflozin. In contrast, canagliflozin had an increased any-AE estimate versus standard care (RR 1.50, 95% CI 1.01-2.23). Safety statements in the discussion are internally imprecise relative to the result tables.

Interpretive boundary: Use this paper to support class-effect context and identify where evidence differs by agent. Do not use its SUCRA ordering alone to select one SGLT2 inhibitor for a formulary.

Section-by-section condensation

Introduction

The paper frames HF as a major complication of T2D and notes that outcome trials established SGLT2 inhibitors as cardiovascular therapies beyond glucose lowering. The stated gap is comparative uncertainty among individual agents in patients who have both HF and T2D.

The review includes canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, and sotagliflozin and emphasizes receptor-selectivity differences as a possible explanation for heterogeneous cardiovascular effects.

Methods

PROSPERO CRD42022353754. PubMed, Embase, and CENTRAL were searched from inception to 15 Aug 2022 with a 23 Sep 2022 update and no language restriction. Eligible evidence was randomized and had to report HF-T2D outcomes for the composite CV-death/HFH endpoint or secondary mortality, hospitalization, and safety endpoints. RoB 2 was used for trial bias assessment.

Pairwise meta-analysis used RRs and 95% CIs; a two-stage NMA estimated active-agent and standard-care contrasts. SUCRA ranked treatments. Transitivity, comparison-adjusted funnel plots, sensitivity analyses, and CINeMA confidence assessment were prespecified or reported as robustness checks.

Results - evidence base

From 7,952 search records, 13 publications from 11 RCTs contributed 20,438 patients with T2D and HF. Evidence mixed HF-specific RCTs with HF subgroup/post-hoc analyses from CV outcome trials. RoB agreement was 72.73% (kappa 0.48); five RCTs were low risk and six had some concerns.

Table 1 shows clinically important variation in age, EF, follow-up, and background HF therapy. This variation later appears in the transitivity assessment and weakens confidence in active-agent ranking.

Composite CV death/HFH

Ten studies (n=20,191) contributed. Overall pooled SGLT2i RR was 0.77 (0.73-0.81). Dapagliflozin 0.80 (0.72-0.87), empagliflozin 0.79 (0.71-0.88), and sotagliflozin 0.74 (0.68-0.81) were significant in pairwise pooling. Network comparison suggested canagliflozin vs dapagliflozin RR 0.75 (0.59-0.97) and sotagliflozin vs dapagliflozin RR 0.88 (0.78-1.00).

SUCRA ranked canagliflozin 95.9%, sotagliflozin 77.4%, and empagliflozin 53.2%. This ranking is not equivalent to a high-certainty direct superiority claim.

HF hospitalization

Eight studies (n=12,391) contributed. Overall pooled RR was 0.71 (0.66-0.76). Network RRs versus standard care were 0.51 for canagliflozin, 0.68 for sotagliflozin, 0.71 for empagliflozin, and 0.79 for dapagliflozin; canagliflozin vs dapagliflozin was 0.64 (0.43-0.97).

SUCRA ranked canagliflozin 95.5%, sotagliflozin 66.0%, and empagliflozin 57.2%. The discussion interprets HF hospitalization benefit as broadly consistent with a class effect.

CV death and all-cause mortality

Nine studies (n=14,349) contributed CV death: overall RR 0.90 (0.81-0.99), but no individual active agent was significant versus standard care in the NMA. For all-cause mortality, eight studies (n=12,493) yielded overall RR 0.90 (0.82-0.99); dapagliflozin was significant in pairwise analysis at 0.84 (0.72-0.98).

The all-cause mortality text labels canagliflozin RR 0.76 (0.58-1.00) significant, whereas Table 3 does not mark that estimate significant and the abstract/discussion say only dapagliflozin reached statistical significance. This is retained as an unresolved source inconsistency.

Safety

Seven studies (n=12,303) reported serious AEs; pooled SGLT2i RR was 0.87 (0.83-0.91). The NMA reported lower SAE risk versus standard care for canagliflozin 0.81, dapagliflozin 0.87, and empagliflozin 0.89. Eight studies (n=12,377) reported any AE; pooled RR 1.07 (0.99-1.15) was not significant.

Canagliflozin had a significant any-AE estimate versus standard care of 1.50 (1.01-2.23) and the highest probability of any AEs. The discussion's statement that no significant safety associations were found conflicts with these result-level findings.

Sensitivity, transitivity, confidence

Restricting to HF-specific trials retained dapagliflozin RR 0.82 (0.74-0.92), empagliflozin 0.79 (0.71-0.89), and sotagliflozin 0.70 (0.62-0.78), with SUCRAs 44.0%, 58.4%, and 97.6%, respectively. Excluding the small CANONICAL study did not materially change conclusions.

Transitivity was imperfect: canagliflozin comparisons differed in age, sex, and EF, ertugliflozin comparisons were younger, and sotagliflozin comparisons had shorter follow-up in mortality networks. CINeMA identified very-low confidence for important estimates, with incoherence amplified by the absence of closed loops.

Discussion and conclusions

The authors interpret the composite/HFH benefit as supportive of an SGLT2 class effect, while proposing canagliflozin as a possible preferred agent in the full network. They also acknowledge that its efficacy estimate is primarily derived from a post-hoc CANVAS analysis and should be interpreted cautiously.

The conclusion itself contains the major restraint: the HF-specific sensitivity analysis changes the top ranking to sotagliflozin, followed by empagliflozin and dapagliflozin. This instability, plus very-low-confidence network comparisons, limits practice-level agent selection.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 9/10

Directly addresses comparative SGLT2 cardiovascular outcomes in HF-T2D.

Basis: Five-agent network and clinically relevant HF outcomes.

Limiting factor: Does not directly answer inpatient interchangeability or formulary logistics.

Would raise: Direct comparative evidence in the target formulary population.

Novelty: 7/10

Provides an agent-level network ranking in a focused HF-T2D population.

Basis: Broader five-agent evidence base and multiple cardiovascular endpoints.

Limiting factor: Ranking methods add information but not direct head-to-head proof.

Would raise: New direct randomized active-agent comparisons.

Method strength: 7/10

Search, RoB 2, NMA, transitivity checks, sensitivity analyses, and CINeMA are substantial strengths.

Basis: Registered systematic review with multiple robustness assessments.

Limiting factor: Aggregate data, mixed trial types, network without closed loops.

Would raise: IPD analysis and a better-connected network.

Evidence strength: 5/10

Class-level HF benefit is credible, but comparative superiority is low confidence.

Basis: Consistent versus-control reductions for composite/HFH.

Limiting factor: Very-low CINeMA confidence and indirect active-agent contrasts.

Would raise: Direct head-to-head trials or high-confidence closed-loop network estimates.

External validity: 6/10

Relevant to HF-T2D, but included populations and HF definitions differ materially.

Basis: Large total cohort spanning HFrEF/HFpEF and CVOT subgroups.

Limiting factor: Post-hoc CVOT subgroups, variable EF/follow-up/background therapy.

Would raise: Consistent prospectively defined HF cohorts resembling target practice.

Implementation value: 6/10

Useful for formulary evidence context but insufficient alone to choose a preferred drug.

Basis: Quantifies class effect and comparative uncertainty.

Limiting factor: SUCRA rankings are unstable and low certainty.

Would raise: Triangulation with direct comparative real-world data, labels, guidelines, acquisition cost, and inpatient workflow evidence.

Bottom line. Use now for class-effect context: yes. Use now to claim canagliflozin, empagliflozin, or dapagliflozin superiority/interchangeability: no. Any formulary decision should weight direct outcomes evidence, guideline/label indications, safety, renal thresholds, operational constraints, and cost rather than this SUCRA ranking alone.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Supports
SGLT2 inhibitors reduce HF hospitalization and composite CV death/HFH in HF-T2D as a class.
Does not establish
Direct empagliflozin-dapagliflozin equivalence or canagliflozin superiority.
High-value caveat
Agent ranking changes in the HF-specific sensitivity analysis and many NMA contrasts are very low confidence.
Formulary use
Keep as supporting evidence, not the governing comparative-efficacy source.
