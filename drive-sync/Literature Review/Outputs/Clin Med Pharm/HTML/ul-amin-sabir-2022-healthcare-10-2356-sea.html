One-page synthesis

Verdict: Do not use for practice as a stand-alone source.
Best use: historical early-acute-HF evidence map and reference finder. The review is clinically relevant, but central reporting errors and claim inflation make it a poor basis for independent treatment decisions.

The review pooled EMPULSE, EMPA-RESPONSE-AHF, and SOLOIST-WHF, totaling 1,831 participants. It reports lower heart-failure-event risk, fewer total heart failure events, lower risk of cardiovascular death or heart failure event, fewer readmissions, and a small KCCQ effect. The primary pooled all-cause mortality estimate was not significant at RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.49 to 1.09, p=0.12. Safety outcomes were individually non-significant.

The main problem is not simply small sample size. Several source statements do not agree with the forest plots. RR 0.66 is described as a 62% reduction rather than 34%. RR 0.82 is described as a 28% reduction rather than 18%. Hypoglycemia prose reports p=0.79 while Figure 11 gives overall p=0.15 and heterogeneity p=0.79. Figure 12 contains an intervention denominator inconsistent with Table 1. The conclusion then says SGLT2 inhibitors decrease all-cause mortality despite the non-significant pooled mortality estimate.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame acute heart failure as a high-risk hospitalization with frequent early readmission and mortality. They position SGLT2 inhibitors as a drug class with established chronic heart-failure and diabetes outcome evidence, then ask whether those benefits extend to patients hospitalized with acute heart failure.

The prespecified efficacy set includes all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, heart failure events, readmissions, and KCCQ symptoms. Serious adverse events are the stated secondary focus.

Methods

The review reports PRISMA 2020 adherence and PROSPERO registration. PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library were searched through 13 August 2022, with reference-list and ClinicalTrials.gov checks. Eligible studies were randomized adult acute-HF trials of SGLT2 inhibitors, with or without diabetes.

Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data with third-reviewer adjudication. Binary outcomes used risk ratios, KCCQ used standardized mean difference, random-effects models were applied, and heterogeneity used Q and I2. Funnel-plot assessment was omitted because fewer than 10 studies were available. RoB 2 was used for trial-level risk of bias.

Results

The review screened 1,985 records after removing 642 duplicates from 2,627 identified records. It assessed 529 full texts and included 3 trials. The pooled sample was 1,831, with 913 intervention participants, 33.7% women, and a large majority with diabetes. EMPULSE and EMPA-RESPONSE-AHF used empagliflozin; SOLOIST-WHF used sotagliflozin.

Key pooled estimates were all-cause mortality RR 0.73 (0.49 to 1.09), heart failure events RR 0.66 (0.58 to 0.75), total HF events RR 0.65 (0.46 to 0.91), cardiovascular death or HFE RR 0.82 (0.73 to 0.93), readmission RR 0.76 (0.60 to 0.98), and KCCQ SMD -0.27 (-0.40 to -0.14). Safety RRs had confidence intervals crossing 1. The article also catalogued 15 ongoing trials with planned enrollment of 17,503.

Discussion and clinical-practice recommendations

The discussion treats the pooled results as support for adding SGLT2 inhibitors to usual care after acute-HF hospitalization. It cites individual trials and related studies to strengthen that case and includes a clinical-practice section that favors initiation when heart failure predominates.

That practice language is stronger than the meta-analysis can support. The pooled evidence compares SGLT2 inhibitors mainly with placebo, not ACE inhibitors or other active heart-failure therapies. The review also mixes acute-HF settings, drugs, timepoints, and outcome definitions.

Limitations and conclusion

The authors acknowledge variable intervention duration, inconsistent HFE definitions, inconsistent follow-up and endpoint timing, small sample size, and a predominantly diabetic population. These are material limits, not minor caveats.

The conclusion goes further than the primary mortality analysis by stating that SGLT2 inhibitors decrease all-cause mortality. That statement is not supported by the pooled all-cause mortality CI and p value reported in Figure 3.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

Directly addresses SGLT2 use around acute-heart-failure hospitalization.

Evidence basis: Three acute-HF randomized trials and clinically relevant efficacy/safety endpoints.

Principal limit: The source is from 2022 and does not include later trials or guidance.

Would change with: A current systematic review with the completed ongoing-trial evidence would raise this score.

Novelty 6/10

It pooled an early, small acute-HF evidence base and mapped 15 ongoing trials.

Evidence basis: At publication, only three randomized trials were available for pooling.

Principal limit: The analysis is now historically dated and its methods are conventional.

Would change with: Evidence that the synthesis changed conclusions beyond the individual trials would raise this score.

Method strength 5/10

The review used PRISMA, prospective registration, duplicate screening, RoB 2, random-effects pooling, and sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: Methods section, PRISMA diagram, RoB 2 figure, and forest plots are explicit.

Principal limit: Only three heterogeneous trials were pooled; no certainty framework such as GRADE is reported; several numerical/reporting inconsistencies remain.

Would change with: More trials, prespecified harmonized outcomes, certainty grading, and corrected reporting would raise this score.

Evidence strength 4/10

Some pooled endpoints favor SGLT2 inhibition, but the evidence base is small and several central claims overstate or misstate the reported statistics.

Evidence basis: Readmission, HF-event, and composite estimates have CIs excluding 1; all-cause mortality does not.

Principal limit: Arithmetic errors in stated relative reductions, a hypoglycemia p-value conflict, a denominator conflict, and claim inflation weaken confidence.

Would change with: Reanalysis from primary trial data with corrected statistics and more completed trials would raise this score.

External validity 4/10

The pooled population spans acute and acutely decompensated HF but is heavily diabetic and underrepresents women.

Evidence basis: n=1,831; 33.7% female; roughly 83% to 84% had diabetes; interventions and follow-up varied.

Principal limit: The source itself notes limited sample size and poor generalizability to non-diabetic patients.

Would change with: Larger diverse acute-HF trials with consistent timepoints and more non-diabetic participants would raise this score.

Implementation value 3/10

Useful as a historical evidence map and reference finder, but unsafe as a stand-alone practice document.

Evidence basis: It identifies the three included trials and many ongoing studies.

Principal limit: The practice section goes beyond the analyzed placebo-controlled evidence and current clinical status was not verified in this source-only run.

Would change with: A current guideline-linked synthesis with corrected analyses and explicit initiation/monitoring boundaries would raise this score.

Source-integrity findings

  • Heart-failure-event prose says 62% reduction while RR 0.66 corresponds to 34%.
  • Cardiovascular death or HFE prose says 28% reduction while RR 0.82 corresponds to 18%.
  • Hypoglycemia prose gives p=0.79; Figure 11 gives overall-effect p=0.15 and heterogeneity-test p=0.79.
  • Figure 12 uses an SGLT2-group denominator inconsistent with Table 1 for EMPA-RESPONSE-AHF.
  • Table 1 calls EMPULSE a Netherlands study; the discussion describes a multicountry trial.
  • The conclusion claims lower all-cause mortality although the pooled mortality estimate is not significant.

PICO and validity

Population: adults hospitalized with de novo acute HF or acutely decompensated chronic HF, mostly with diabetes. Intervention: empagliflozin or sotagliflozin in the included trials. Comparator: placebo/control. Outcomes: mortality, HF events, readmission, KCCQ symptoms, and safety. Internal validity is limited more by the tiny evidence base, heterogeneous endpoints/timepoints, and reporting errors than by the authors' stated RoB 2 judgments.
