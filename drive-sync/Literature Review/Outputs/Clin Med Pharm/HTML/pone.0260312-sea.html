One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Across 13 prospective studies with 422 participants, furosemide plus albumin produced more short-term urine output and urinary sodium excretion than furosemide alone, but response was highly heterogeneous and the certainty of the diuretic evidence was low.
Urine output
Pooled mean difference +31.45 mL/hour (95% CI 19.30 to 43.59); I² 87%.
Urinary sodium
Pooled mean difference +1.76 mEq/hour (95% CI 0.83 to 2.69); I² 92%.
Potential modifiers
Stronger effects were reported with albumin <2.5 g/dL, albumin dose ≥30 g, earlier observation, and some renal-impairment strata.
Central limitation
The review does not establish a reliable patient-centered benefit such as edema resolution; most evidence is small, heterogeneous, and partly crossover.

The review is most useful as evidence that a transient pharmacodynamic signal may exist in selected hypoalbuminemic patients. It is weaker as a basis for a fixed treatment protocol because included regimens, diseases, baseline renal function, and outcome windows varied substantially. The authors’ own GRADE table rates the diuretic evidence as low certainty.

Section-by-section condensation

Introduction

The rationale is pharmacologic: furosemide is highly albumin-bound and reaches the tubular lumen through proximal-tubule organic-anion secretion. Hypoalbuminemia may reduce effective tubular delivery, providing a mechanism for albumin co-administration, but clinical trials had been inconsistent.

Methods

PubMed, EMBASE, Medline, and Cochrane were searched through October 18, 2020. Eligible studies were prospective adult comparisons of furosemide plus albumin versus furosemide alone reporting urine output or urinary sodium. Two investigators independently screened and extracted data; randomized studies were assessed with RoB 2.

Different collection windows were normalized to hourly rates. Parallel and crossover effects were synthesized with generic inverse variance under a random-effects model. For crossover studies, no carry-over/period effect was assumed and a within-person correlation of 0.5 was imputed. Sensitivity analyses excluded outliers, high-risk trials, or crossover trials; subgroup interaction p<0.1 was treated as a potential effect-modifier signal.

Results

Thirteen studies (422 participants; 9 crossover, 4 parallel) covered nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, unspecified hypoalbuminemia, and critical illness. Combination therapy increased urine output by 31.45 mL/hour and urinary sodium excretion by 1.76 mEq/hour, but heterogeneity was high. Four trials had high overall risk of bias.

Sensitivity analyses generally preserved direction of effect, although excluding crossover designs left only three parallel trials and the urine-output result became a trend. Subgroup analyses suggested larger effects with lower albumin, higher albumin dose, earlier observation, and some renal-impairment strata.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the pooled effects as support for possible benefit in selected patients, especially albumin <2.5 g/dL and higher albumin doses. They also emphasize that a rise in urine output does not establish edema resolution and that only one study reported body-weight reduction.

Major limitations are small total enrollment; major clinical and dosing heterogeneity; non-weight-standardized regimens; crossover carry-over uncertainty; unrecorded salt restriction, NSAID and probenecid exposure; an assumed 60-kg body weight for one subgroup cutoff; and only three parallel trials after crossover exclusion.

Conclusion

The authors conclude that albumin plus furosemide might improve diuresis and natriuresis versus furosemide alone, but high heterogeneity and limited sample size require further parallel randomized trials.

Verdict: Read soon. Useful for understanding the evidence architecture and plausible responders, but not sufficient as a stand-alone practice directive.

Directly addresses a recurring inpatient diuretic-resistance question.

Evidence basis: 13 prospective studies and clinically familiar intervention/comparator.

Principal limit: Outcomes are short-term surrogates and populations are heterogeneous.

Would raise score: Prospective patient-centered outcome trials in clearly defined hypoalbuminemic populations.

Novelty: 6/10

Updates earlier mixed evidence and explores clinically plausible effect modifiers.

Evidence basis: Subgroup analyses by albumin level, albumin dose, renal function and time.

Principal limit: Many subgroup thresholds are exploratory and the evidence base is old/small.

Would raise score: A larger individual-patient-data synthesis or new well-powered trials.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use
Use the paper to frame a conditional, uncertain evidence signal around hypoalbuminemia, dose, early response, and renal function.
Do not overclaim
Do not infer proven edema resolution, durable clinical benefit, or a universally optimal albumin/furosemide regimen from these pooled surrogate outcomes.
CDS modeling
If modeled, retain albumin level, renal function, albumin/furosemide dose, observation window, disease state, and evidence certainty as explicit context rather than a binary “use albumin” rule.
Research priority
Parallel randomized trials with standardized dosing, patient-centered outcomes, safety reporting, and prespecified effect-modifier analyses.
