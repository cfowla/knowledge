One-page synthesis

Core finding: In five HF-focused randomized trials, pooled serious adverse events were lower with SGLT2 inhibitors (OR 0.85, 95% CI 0.77–0.92). Genital infection (OR 2.97), UTI (OR 1.18), and hypotension (OR 1.17) were increased. Other prespecified safety outcomes did not show statistically significant differences in the main analysis.

The review was designed around safety rather than efficacy. It deliberately restricted the main evidence set to trials in which heart failure or recent HF hospitalization was part of the enrollment framework, yielding DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, SOLOIST-WHF, EMPEROR-Preserved, and PRESERVED-HF. About 80% of participants had LVEF below 50%, so the pooled evidence is not evenly distributed across EF phenotypes.

Two sensitivity analyses were directionally reassuring for the principal conclusion. Adding four small trials left the SAE estimate essentially unchanged (OR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78–0.93). Excluding the two HFpEF-focused trials strengthened the SAE estimate to OR 0.82 (95% CI 0.75–0.89), while UTI and hypotension no longer met the review's significance rule. Interpretation remains constrained by uneven safety-event reporting, rare-event imprecision, pharmacologic heterogeneity from sotagliflozin, lack of diabetes-stratified safety reporting, and absent formal risk-of-bias/certainty assessment in the supplied report.

Methods

Search and study selection

The review reports protocol registration under CRD 42022303987. EMBASE, MEDLINE/PubMed, and Cochrane CENTRAL/Ovid were searched through April 14, 2022. A librarian developed the PubMed strategy; the strategy was translated to other databases. No additional filters or limits were applied beyond the RCT filters. Appendix A provides the full search strings.

Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts in duplicate, with consensus resolution. The PRISMA flow reports 1,813 records identified, 424 duplicates removed, 1,389 records screened, 14 reports assessed, 9 excluded (7 wrong population, 1 wrong outcome, 1 wrong setting), and 5 included. The source's sentence describing exclusion of small trials and phase wording is grammatically ambiguous; this appraisal does not infer a phase-based exclusion beyond what can be supported.

Outcomes and analysis

The primary outcome was serious adverse events using a conventional serious-event definition. Exploratory outcomes were bone fracture, ketoacidosis, genital infection, UTI, volume depletion, VTE, amputation, hypoglycemia, AKI, hyperkalemia, hypotension, and symptomatic hypotension.

Effects were pooled as odds ratios with 95% CIs in RevMan 5.4.1 using a random-effects model. A CI containing 1 was treated as non-significant. Sensitivity analyses added four trials with fewer than 300 participants and separately excluded EMPEROR-Preserved and PRESERVED-HF.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as an HF-specific safety synthesis and a map of which SGLT2 adverse-event signals were supported by the major HF trials available through April 2022. Do not use it alone to set current prescribing, monitoring, or interchange policy.
8.5/10

Relevance

Rationale: Directly addresses SGLT2-inhibitor safety in heart failure using trials spanning reduced and preserved EF.

Evidence basis: Five HF-focused RCTs and clinically relevant safety outcomes.

Principal limiting factor: The evidence cutoff is April 2022 and the review is not a current-practice update.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with newer HF trials and patient-level safety data.

6.5/10

Novelty

Rationale: At publication, the review added HFpEF trial safety data to an HF-specific safety synthesis.

Evidence basis: Inclusion of EMPEROR-Preserved and PRESERVED-HF alongside HFrEF/worsening-HF trials.

Principal limiting factor: Several earlier SGLT2/HF meta-analyses already existed.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with new comparative or mechanistic safety information.

6.5/10

Method strength

Rationale: Search and duplicate screening were reasonably strong, but formal risk-of-bias/certainty methods are not reported.

Evidence basis: Three databases, librarian-developed strategy, registered protocol, duplicate screening, random-effects pooling.

Principal limiting factor: No reported trial-level risk-of-bias assessment, GRADE/certainty framework, or publication-bias analysis; eligibility wording is partly ambiguous.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with explicit RoB 2, certainty assessment, duplicate data extraction, and prespecified handling of sparse harms.

6.0/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: Pooled RCT safety data are useful, but many harms were sparsely and inconsistently reported and confidence intervals are wide for rare events.

Evidence basis: 15,987 participants in the main analysis; pooled ORs with 95% CIs and sensitivity analyses.

Principal limiting factor: Outcome availability varied by trial; several rare-event estimates are imprecise; Figure 7 has an internal duplication error.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with harmonized safety definitions, complete trial reporting, and patient-level data.

7.0/10

External validity

Rationale: The review includes HFrEF, HFpEF, and recent worsening HF populations, but the majority had LVEF <50%.

Evidence basis: Five large HF trials across multiple clinical phenotypes.

Principal limiting factor: About 80% had LVEF <50%; diabetes-stratified safety could not be examined; agents were not pharmacologically identical.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with broader HFpEF representation and stratified analyses by diabetes, renal function, age, and frailty.

6.5/10

Implementation value

Rationale: Useful for counseling about genital infection, UTI, and hypotension signals, but insufficient as a standalone prescribing standard.

Evidence basis: Clear pooled estimates for common safety concerns and sensitivity analyses.

Principal limiting factor: Does not provide patient-level absolute risk, NNH, monitoring algorithms, or current guideline integration.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with absolute risks, baseline-risk stratification, and contemporary practice guidance.

Strengths

  • HF-specific trial inclusion rather than extrapolation from diabetes-only populations.
  • Three-database search with librarian-developed strategy and complete search syntax.
  • Independent duplicate screening and protocol registration.
  • Quantitative pooling with confidence intervals and sensitivity analyses across trial-size and EF phenotype.

Limitations

  • No formal risk-of-bias assessment or evidence-certainty framework is reported in the supplied article/supplements.
  • Safety outcomes were not uniformly reported by all trials, so denominators differ substantially by outcome.
  • Rare-event estimates are imprecise, including ketoacidosis and VTE.
  • Sotagliflozin's dual SGLT1/2 activity introduces class heterogeneity.
  • No safety stratification by diabetes status was available.
  • About 80% of the pooled population had LVEF below 50%.
  • Appendix B contains multiple labeling/cross-reference inconsistencies, including the duplicated Figure 7 hyperkalemia panel.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Useful signal
The review supports paying particular attention to genital infection, UTI, and hypotension when counseling or monitoring SGLT2i-treated HF patients.
Do not overread nulls
Several outcomes have wide intervals or incomplete reporting; “not statistically significant” is not proof of no risk.
Class interpretation
Pooled results combine dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and sotagliflozin, so class-level conclusions should preserve pharmacologic and trial-population heterogeneity.
Current-use boundary
Use this source as background evidence, then reconcile with newer trials, labels, guidelines, and local formulary requirements before changing practice.
