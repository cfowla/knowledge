One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. DELIVER is a large, well-controlled outcomes trial that materially extends SGLT2-inhibitor evidence into HFmrEF/HFpEF and improved-EF populations.

Core finding. Over median 2.3 years, the primary composite occurred in 16.4% with dapagliflozin versus 19.5% with placebo (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.73–0.92; P<0.001). The effect was driven mainly by fewer worsening-HF events (HR 0.79, 0.69–0.91; HF hospitalization HR 0.77, 0.67–0.89). Cardiovascular death (HR 0.88, 0.74–1.05) and all-cause death (HR 0.94, 0.83–1.07) were not individually established. Total worsening-HF events plus CV deaths were also reduced (rate ratio 0.77, 0.67–0.89).

Symptoms and range of EF. KCCQ symptom analysis favored dapagliflozin (win ratio 1.11, 1.03–1.21), with a post-hoc survivor mean difference of 2.4 points. Prespecified effects were directionally consistent across EF categories, including LVEF ≥60% (HR 0.78, 0.62–0.98) and prior LVEF ≤40% that improved (HR 0.74, 0.56–0.97), but subgroup analyses were underpowered and should not be read as independent efficacy trials.

Most important caveats. The proportional-hazards assumption for the primary Cox model was violated (P=0.006), although robust and competing-risk analyses remained consistent. COVID-era restrictions altered the primary KCCQ analysis population. Black enrollment was low overall (about 2.5%; no African sites), and specific eligibility criteria constrain generalizability. The dual-primary framework and KCCQ analysis were amended in November 2020; multiplicity was explicitly controlled, but the chronology is methodologically load-bearing and deserves attention.

Methods

Design and participants

DELIVER randomized 6,263 adults with symptomatic chronic HF and LVEF >40% to dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily or matching placebo. Eligibility required structural heart disease and elevated NT-proBNP; patients could be outpatient or hospitalized after stabilization, and prior LVEF ≤40% was allowed if it improved above 40%. eGFR <25 mL/min/1.73 m², type 1 diabetes, hypotension, and several recent major cardiovascular events were among exclusions.

Randomization used balanced blocks stratified by type 2 diabetes. Follow-up was scheduled at day 30, day 120, then every 120 days. An independent clinical-events committee adjudicated primary events while blinded to treatment. The sponsor and an academic team performed analyses; the academic team had unrestricted database access.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary endpoint was first worsening HF (unplanned HF hospitalization or urgent visit requiring IV therapy) or CV death. The analysis was concurrent in the overall population and LVEF <60% population. Final alpha was 0.048 after interim monitoring, split as 0.024 and 0.038 across the two correlated primary tests. Efficacy was intention-to-treat; safety included all participants receiving ≥1 dose.

Cox/Kaplan–Meier methods were used for time-to-event endpoints; recurrent HF/CV-death events used the Lin proportional-rates method. KCCQ used a rank ANCOVA/win-ratio framework. COVID censoring, competing-risk analysis, and nonproportional-hazard checks were prespecified/supportive. A protocol amendment on Nov 4, 2020 introduced the dual-primary analyses; a Nov 6, 2020 SAP change restricted the primary KCCQ population to assessments planned/performed before Mar 11, 2020.

Safety

Serious adverse events occurred in 43.5% versus 45.5%, and study-drug discontinuation due to adverse events in 5.8% versus 5.8%. Renal serious/discontinuation events were 2.3% versus 2.5%; major hypoglycemia 0.2% versus 0.2%; definite/probable ketoacidosis 0.1% versus 0. No Fournier gangrene occurred. The trial was not designed to prove equivalence of uncommon harms, and small event counts remain imprecise.

Sensitivity analyses

COVID-censoring produced a primary HR of 0.81 (0.72–0.91); competing-risk analysis yielded subdistribution HR 0.82 (0.73–0.92). The primary proportional-hazards test was significant (P=0.006), but early curve separation followed by approximately parallel trajectories and model-free sensitivity analysis supported a persistent treatment effect rather than a reversal.

Evaluation and appraisal

10/10

Relevance

Directly practice-relevant HF outcomes evidence for dapagliflozin across LVEF >40%.

Basis: Large event-driven clinical-outcomes RCT.
Limit: Does not answer all patient-specific or current-guideline questions.
Would change with: Different question or population.
9/10

Novelty

At publication, materially closed an evidence gap across HFmrEF/HFpEF and included improved-EF and recently hospitalized patients.

Basis: Broad EF and enrollment design relative to preceding HF trials.
Limit: Builds on DAPA-HF and contemporaneous EMPEROR-Preserved rather than establishing a new drug class.
Would change with: Substantially redundant evidence would lower novelty.
9/10

Method strength

Randomized, double-blind, independently adjudicated, event-driven design with prespecified multiplicity control and sensitivity analyses.

Basis: 6,263 randomized; ITT; blinded adjudication; independent academic analysis.
Limit: Mid-trial 2020 endpoint-analysis amendments and primary-model nonproportionality require careful interpretation.
Would change with: Fully fixed analysis before recruitment or stronger time-varying-effect modeling would raise confidence.
9/10

Evidence strength

Primary HF-driven clinical outcome is precise and robust across major sensitivity analyses.

Basis: HR 0.82 (0.73–0.92); recurrent-event RR 0.77; consistent competing-risk/COVID analyses.
Limit: CV and all-cause mortality CIs cross 1; KCCQ effect size is modest.
Would change with: Independent replication with mortality power would raise the score.
8/10

External validity

20-country enrollment, inpatient/outpatient inclusion, diabetes/non-diabetes, kidney-function and EF spectrum improve applicability.

Basis: Broad eligibility and consistent major subgroups.
Limit: Specific exclusions, <5% Black enrollment overall, no African enrollment, and subgroup imprecision.
Would change with: Broader racial/geographic representation and pragmatic confirmation.
9/10

Implementation value

Provides a clear event-outcomes basis for considering SGLT2 inhibition in HFmrEF/HFpEF.

Basis: Clinically interpretable dose, endpoints, adverse-event framework, broad EF consistency.
Limit: This 2022 trial is not itself a current guideline or prescribing document.
Would change with: Current guideline/label integration and local formulary constraints.
Main strength: large randomized outcomes evidence with detailed protocol/supplement support and multiple robustness analyses.
Main limitation: interpret the composite as predominantly HF-event benefit; do not infer a proven standalone mortality reduction from DELIVER.
Amendment note: dual-primary and KCCQ analysis changes were introduced in November 2020. The source documents multiplicity control and blinding around interim analysis, but these changes remain important to audit when judging confirmatory strength.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now?
Use as high-quality outcomes evidence supporting dapagliflozin/SGLT2 inhibition in symptomatic HF with LVEF >40%, including improved EF, subject to current guidance and patient-specific eligibility.
Do not overclaim
DELIVER alone does not establish a statistically significant cardiovascular- or all-cause-mortality reduction.
Safety translation
Overall serious/discontinuation events were similar, but rare-event counts do not rule out uncommon harms.
Formulary relevance
The class-consistency context is useful, but DELIVER directly tests dapagliflozin versus placebo—not therapeutic interchangeability against another SGLT2 inhibitor.
