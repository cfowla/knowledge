One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The authors use a single alveolar-osteitis case with a mecA-positive, multidrug-resistant Staphylococcus epidermidis isolate to argue that empiric systemic antibiotics may be ineffective and that dentistry should account for antimicrobial resistance.

A 35-year-old woman presented with right mandibular pain after independently starting cefixime two days earlier without improvement. Examination of tooth 4.6 ultimately showed a fracture with pulpal involvement and purulent-bleeding drainage; the tooth was extracted after local antimicrobial and mechanical measures. A specimen collected from the pulpal chamber/root canals before extraction underwent culture, staphylococcal identification, mecA PCR, and disk-diffusion susceptibility testing.

Four days after extraction, the patient developed a clinical picture diagnosed as alveolar osteitis: persistent pain, absent alveolar clot, gingival inflammation, socket tenderness, and a palpable submandibular node. Local management included suture removal, saline/hydrogen-peroxide irrigation, topical antiseptic gel, and chlorhexidine. The isolate was characterized as methicillin-resistant S. epidermidis; cefixime was switched to azithromycin after the mecA result, but azithromycin was stopped after susceptibility testing showed resistance to erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. Seven days after AO management, inflammation/infection signs had resolved and a stable fibrin clot was present.

Load-bearing limitation. The microbiologic sample came from the pulpal chamber/root canals before extraction, not from the alveolar socket when AO was diagnosed four days later. The report therefore shows temporal association, not direct microbiologic proof that the MRSE isolate caused the postoperative AO.

Section condensation

Summary

The abstract frames empiric broad-spectrum antibiotic prescribing as an AMR concern and presents the case as an example in which systemic antibiotics may be insufficient for AO involving a multidrug-resistant organism. It identifies the key sequence: cefixime exposure, tooth extraction with purulent drainage, bacteriologic workup including mecA PCR and susceptibility testing, MR S. epidermidis with multidrug resistance, and clinical resolution after seven days.

The summary appropriately signals that antibiotic efficacy is controversial in AO, but its concluding implication exceeds what a single uncontrolled case can prove. The observed resolution after local management and discontinuation of an antibiotic to which the isolate was resistant is compatible with the authors' interpretation, but not specific for it.

Introduction

The introduction situates AO within dental antimicrobial stewardship, emphasizing AMR risks from empiric antibiotic use, the usual early post-extraction timing and clinical features of AO, and proposed mechanisms involving fibrinolysis, bacteria, and surgical trauma. It states that routine extraction antibiotics are usually unnecessary and that evidence for antibiotic treatment of established AO is controversial.

The section functions mainly as secondary-source background. Claims about incidence, prophylaxis, and etiology depend on cited reviews/trials and should not be treated as findings generated by this case report.

Case report — presentation and extraction

The patient had no reported systemic pathology and had self-started cefixime two days before presentation. Clinical and radiographic assessment localized pathology to tooth 4.6; cavity access revealed purulent-bleeding drainage and a fracture extending to the chamber floor/mesial root wall, leading to a diagnosis of cracked tooth syndrome with pulpitis and extraction.

Before extraction, a pulpal/root-canal specimen was collected for bacteriology. The procedure included preoperative biofilm removal and chlorhexidine rinse, extraction with root separation, socket debridement and saline irrigation, collagen sponge placement and suturing. Ibuprofen and chlorhexidine were prescribed and cefixime was continued. The source does not report cefixime dose, route, or frequency.

Microbiological analyses and clinical course

The specimen was cultured on multiple media under aerobic, anaerobic, and microaerobic conditions. Identification used Gram staining and API Staph biochemical testing; mecA was assessed by PCR. Disk diffusion tested ten antibiotics and was interpreted using CLSI breakpoints. Four days after extraction, persistent pain, absent clot, gingival inflammation and socket tenderness led to an AO diagnosis. Local therapy comprised suture removal and irrigation with saline/hydrogen peroxide, then topical antiseptic gel and chlorhexidine.

The isolate was characterized as MR S. epidermidis. After mecA confirmation, cefixime was changed to azithromycin pending susceptibility results. The isolate was later classified as multidrug-resistant and resistant to erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, so azithromycin was stopped. Seven days after AO management, signs and symptoms of inflammation/infection had resolved and a stable fibrin clot was present.

Discussion

The authors interpret the case through an AMR/stewardship lens: beta-lactams are unsuitable for methicillin-resistant staphylococci; mecA-positive strains may require combined phenotypic and genotypic characterization; and empiric antibiotic decisions should consider prior misuse and resistance risk. Because the organism was resistant to azithromycin yet the patient recovered, they suggest spontaneous resolution without antibiotic influence.

The report's strongest practice-level message is caution against assuming that systemic antibiotics are necessary or effective for AO. Its organism-specific causal claim is much weaker: the specimen was collected from the infected tooth before extraction, not from the AO socket at the time of postoperative disease, and there is no comparator or repeated sampling.

Validity: The clinical chronology and laboratory methods are reasonably documented for a case report, but causal inference is weak. The microbiologic sample site/time is a central limitation, there is no untreated or alternative-treatment comparator, and multiple local interventions coincided with recovery.

Practice translation: The case supports a stewardship principle: avoid assuming empiric systemic antibiotics will cover resistant staphylococci or improve AO, and use microbiology when clinically indicated. It does not support a specific antibiotic choice, routine mecA testing for all AO, or withdrawal of systemic therapy in patients with separate indications for antibiotics.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now: Reinforce dental antimicrobial stewardship; recognize that mecA-positive staphylococci are not addressed by beta-lactam assumptions; obtain culture/AST when an infection indication and clinical context justify it.
Do not overclaim: This case does not prove that MR S. epidermidis caused the AO, that systemic antibiotics are broadly ineffective for AO, or that the local regimen caused recovery.
