One-page synthesis

Core thesis: ASH shifts adult primary ITP management toward earlier combination therapy and an explicitly ranked second-line strategy. For initial therapy, rituximab + corticosteroids or a thrombopoietic agent + corticosteroids is conditionally suggested over corticosteroids alone or MMF + corticosteroids. After initial corticosteroids, a thrombopoietic agent is the only strong recommendation; rituximab is the next conditional option, with BTK inhibitor/MMF/SYK inhibitor as conditional alternatives when leading options are unsuitable.

The guideline's distinctive method is a GRADE Evidence-to-Decision framework for multiple comparisons. It gives sustained response off treatment (SRoT), health-related quality of life, bleeding, thrombosis, infection, and rescue therapy higher decision weight than platelet-count response alone. This is clinically important because the first-line combination recommendation is driven chiefly by greater SRoT at 6 and 12 months, less minor bleeding/rescue therapy, and better HRQoL dimensions rather than short-term platelet response.

The central caution is certainty. Many treatment comparisons are based on low/very-low certainty, indirect populations, single-arm studies, heterogeneous regimens, or imprecise confidence intervals. The guideline therefore contains only one strong recommendation: a thrombopoietic agent for adults needing additional treatment after corticosteroids. Cost, equity, access, pregnancy considerations, and patient preference are explicitly incorporated into treatment selection.

Methods and evidence development

Guideline development

ASH assembled a multidisciplinary panel including two patient representatives. The University of Oklahoma supported evidence synthesis and guideline methods. Conflicts were disclosed and managed under ASH policies; the source states no panelist met the prespecified recusal criteria for the prioritized questions. ASH wholly funded the guideline, while staff coordinated logistics but reportedly did not choose questions or determine recommendations.

For each question, the team used systematic reviews and GRADE EtD frameworks. Literature searches were updated July 19, 2025. The multiple-comparison implementation first compared each intervention with standard care and then compared interventions across the same EtD domains: benefits, harms, certainty, values, resource use, equity, acceptability, and feasibility.

Prioritized outcomes

Critical outcomes included SRoT at 6 and 12 months, HRQoL, major/minor bleeding, thrombosis, infection, GI symptoms, durable response at 6 and 12 months, time to response, and need for rescue therapy; corticosteroid exposure was additionally considered for initial therapy. The source explicitly values patient-important outcomes over platelet count as a surrogate.

Recommendation process and review

The panel developed recommendations in a 2-day in-person meeting followed by online deliberation, using consensus or voting when necessary; an 80% majority was required for a strong recommendation. Draft recommendations were posted for external review on December 12, 2025. Sixteen individuals/organizations commented; remarks changed, recommendations did not. ASH governance bodies approved the process and submission in May 2026, followed by Blood Advances peer review.

Recommendation 1 — Initial therapy

Conditional recommendation, low certainty: For adults with primary ITP requiring initial therapy, suggest either rituximab + corticosteroids (± IVIG) or a thrombopoietic agent + corticosteroids (± IVIG) rather than MMF + corticosteroids or corticosteroids alone. If rituximab and thrombopoietic agents are not available, suggest corticosteroids alone over MMF + corticosteroids (very-low certainty).

Key implementation remarks

  • A brief corticosteroid course (≤2 weeks) ± IVIG may be used when diagnostic uncertainty exists and platelet response would increase confidence in ITP.
  • Most studied corticosteroid regimen: dexamethasone 40 mg daily ×4 days for 1–3 cycles separated by 2–4 weeks; prednisolone 0.5–2 mg/kg/day is also described.
  • Limit total corticosteroid administration to ≤6 weeks to reduce toxicity.
  • Most studied rituximab regimen: 375 mg/m² weekly ×4; biosimilars are acceptable.
  • For thrombopoietic agents, any approved agent at a standard starting dose may be used; optimal duration is uncertain and guided by response, toxicity, and platelet stability. When responding on combined corticosteroid + TPA therapy, taper corticosteroids before the TPA.

Harms and tradeoffs

TPA combination evidence suggested possible thrombosis signal (29/290 vs 0/201; OR 11.49, 95% CI 0.76–174.32), but the estimate was extremely imprecise. Anti-CD20 increased infection in direct evidence (26% vs 13%; OR 2.50, 95% CI 1.01–6.21) and attenuates vaccine response for at least 6 months. The panel judged MMF's undesirable effects moderate, largely because HRQoL was worse despite better platelet response. Cost and inequity were major concerns for rituximab and TPAs.

Recommendation 2 — Additional treatment after initial corticosteroids

Recommendation 2a: Recommend a thrombopoietic agent (strong; moderate certainty) or suggest rituximab (conditional; low certainty). Rituximab may be preferable when avoiding chronic medication is highly valued or TPA therapy is contraindicated, inaccessible, or intolerable.
Recommendation 2b: If a patient is ineligible for or declines both a TPA and rituximab, suggest a BTK inhibitor, MMF, or a SYK inhibitor (conditional; very-low certainty). The evidence was too indirect to rank these three. Azathioprine was explicitly deprioritized because of a less favorable benefit-to-harm ratio, though low cost, global availability, and pregnancy use may matter in some settings.

Harms and access

For TPAs, infection and thrombosis estimates were imprecise; the panel judged overall undesirable effects small and primarily thrombotic. Anti-CD20 harms were driven mainly by infection. SYK-inhibitor burden was driven mainly by diarrhea; BTK-inhibitor burden included rescue therapy, infection, diarrhea, and bleeding. Costs were judged large for BTK/SYK inhibitors and TPAs, moderate for rituximab, and negligible for MMF/azathioprine. Equity concerns were greatest for expensive or unevenly available agents.

Good practice statements

Splenectomy timing

If feasible, delay splenectomy for at least 1 year after diagnosis because remission may occur.

Splenectomy safety

Provide appropriate immunizations at least 2 weeks before splenectomy when feasible; counsel about antibiotic prophylaxis and lifelong sepsis/thrombosis risk, teach prompt fever management, and follow at least annually for platelet count, counseling, and immunization adherence.

TPA switching

Consider switching for inadequate response, adverse effects, comorbidities, poor adherence, or patient preference.

Switch execution

Use shared decision-making, document the rationale, avoid treatment gaps, and monitor closely around the switch.

The source explicitly states that its good-practice statements were not based on a systematic review of available evidence, even though the panel considered the net benefit unequivocal.

Figure 1 — Interpretation of recommendation strength

Strong recommendations use “recommends” / “recommends against” and are intended to apply to most patients and most clinical situations. Conditional recommendations use “suggests” / “suggests against” and require more individualized decision-making around risks, values, preferences, and context; policy adoption should be more deliberative.

Figure 2 — Interpretation of GRADE certainty

The figure defines high, moderate, low, and very-low certainty as progressively decreasing confidence that the true effect lies on the specified side of a decision threshold or within the target range. The guideline's key practical implication is that Recommendation 2a for a thrombopoietic agent has moderate certainty, while most other recommendations are low or very low certainty.

Limitations, uncertainty, and research priorities

Evidence limitation: Low/very-low certainty dominates many comparisons. Direct head-to-head evidence is sparse; several interventions rely on indirect or single-arm data, and estimates are frequently imprecise.
  • Restricted scope: adults with primary ITP requiring initial or second-line therapy only.
  • Multiple-comparison cap: GRADE framework limited each question to ≤7 interventions; cyclosporine, danazol, dapsone, sirolimus, tacrolimus, and others were not evaluated.
  • Indirectness: studies with splenectomized or more refractory/heavily pretreated patients were often used when direct evidence was absent.
  • Supplement access: source references Supplements 2–8 for disclosure forms, search methods, risk-of-bias/evidence details, but those were absent from the retrieved file set.
  • Version: evaluated PDF carries a draft watermark.

Research priorities emphasized by the panel

  • Optimal TPA agent, dose, and duration for initial therapy.
  • Optimal rituximab dose/schedule for initial therapy.
  • RCTs comparing rituximab + corticosteroids directly with TPA + corticosteroids.
  • SRoT follow-up beyond 12 months and whether combination therapy changes long-term disease course rather than delaying relapse.
  • Higher-quality second-line evidence for azathioprine, BTK inhibitors, MMF, and SYK inhibitors.
  • Biomarkers/clinical predictors to guide therapy sequencing and evidence in pregnancy.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a practice-changing adult ITP guideline with high implementation value, but evidence certainty is heterogeneous and the evaluated file is a draft-marked manuscript. Best use: framework for adult primary ITP initial/second-line shared decision-making and local guideline adaptation after confirming final publication wording. Do not use it as a substitute for guidance on diagnosis, pregnancy, pediatrics, secondary ITP, refractory ITP, or critical bleeding.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Initial therapy

Do not treat corticosteroid monotherapy as the only default. The guideline conditionally shifts toward rituximab + corticosteroids or TPA + corticosteroids when the diagnosis and patient context support combination therapy.

After corticosteroids

TPA is the highest-ranked option and the only strong recommendation. Rituximab is a reasonable conditional alternative when avoiding chronic therapy matters or TPA is unsuitable.

Fallback therapies

BTK inhibitor, MMF, and SYK inhibitor are conditional fallbacks when TPA/rituximab are not used; the guideline does not claim a defensible rank order among them.

Azathioprine

Deprioritized on benefit-to-harm grounds, but may retain a role where affordability, access, or pregnancy considerations dominate.

Corticosteroid stewardship

Keep total corticosteroid exposure ≤6 weeks; a ≤2-week diagnostic trial can be reasonable when uncertainty exists before escalating to combination therapy.

Local adaptation

Formulary, insurance, pregnancy, infection/vaccine status, thrombosis risk, adherence, preference for chronic versus time-limited therapy, and access should be explicit decision variables.
