One-page synthesis

Core result: Initial triple oral therapy did not improve the prespecified 26-week primary endpoint compared with initial double oral therapy. PVR fell 54% with triple therapy and 52% with double therapy; the between-group ratio of geometric means was 0.96 (95% CI 0.86–1.07; P=0.42).

TRITON tested whether simultaneously targeting the endothelin, nitric oxide, and prostacyclin pathways from treatment initiation would outperform an endothelin-receptor-antagonist plus PDE5-inhibitor regimen in newly diagnosed PAH. Both groups had substantial within-group improvements in PVR, 6-minute walk distance, NT-proBNP, and several hemodynamic measures at week 26, but the randomized between-group comparisons did not show additional week-26 benefit from adding selexipag.

Longer-term analyses pointed in a potentially favorable direction for triple therapy: 16 versus 27 patients experienced a first disease-progression event (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.32–1.09), and 31 versus 67 total recurrent disease-progression events were reported. However, these findings are exploratory, not a rescue of the failed primary endpoint. The all-cause death comparison (2 versus 9 deaths; post hoc HR 0.23, 95% CI 0.05–1.04) is particularly uncertain because the trial was not designed or powered as a mortality trial.

Interpretive boundary: The source supports a robust conclusion of no demonstrated superiority for triple therapy on week-26 PVR. It supports only a hypothesis-generating signal—not definitive proof—of improved long-term clinical outcomes.

Methods and trial architecture

Design and governance

TRITON was a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3b study. Randomization was 1:1 and stratified by geographic region and baseline WHO functional class. The steering committee and sponsor collaborated on design and conduct oversight; Covance collected/managed data and Datamap performed analyses according to a prespecified statistical analysis plan. An independent data and safety monitoring committee monitored the study.

Population

Eligible patients were 18–75 years old, newly diagnosed with PAH within 6 months, treatment-naive, with right-heart-catheterization confirmation, PVR ≥6 WU, and 6MWD ≥50 m. Eligible etiologies included idiopathic, heritable, drug/toxin-induced, connective-tissue-disease-associated, HIV-associated, and repaired simple congenital systemic-to-pulmonary shunts. The protocol also excluded major competing cardiopulmonary phenotypes, including patients with multiple HFpEF risk factors or relevant lung disease criteria.

Interventions

Both groups received macitentan 10 mg once daily and tadalafil beginning at 20 mg once daily and increasing to 40 mg once daily at day 8 ±3. The triple group added selexipag beginning at 200 µg twice daily around day 15 ±3 with weekly 200-µg twice-daily increments up to the maximum tolerated dose (maximum 1,600 µg twice daily); the double group received matching placebo.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary endpoint was the week-26 change in PVR expressed as the ratio to baseline. The prespecified analysis used ANCOVA on log-transformed PVR with treatment, region, baseline functional class, and baseline log PVR. The trial planned approximately 238 patients for 90% power to detect a 20% treatment difference at two-sided α=0.05 and included a futility interim analysis. Secondary endpoints were hierarchically ordered, so failure of the primary endpoint made downstream comparisons exploratory rather than confirmatory.

For missing primary-endpoint data, prespecified rules included last observation carried forward and, when no postbaseline value existed, baseline carried forward. Supplementary sensitivity analyses using observed cases, alternative imputation, rescue-treatment handling, multiple imputation, and a modified analysis set were directionally consistent with a treatment ratio close to 1 and did not establish a clear triple-therapy advantage.

Baseline severity was substantial: mean PVR 12.0 WU, mean 6MWD 346 m, and 197/247 (79.8%) patients were WHO functional class III/IV. Women comprised 75.7% of the cohort, and the mean age was 51.9 years.

Disease progression and mortality

First disease-progression events occurred in 16/123 (13.0%) triple-therapy patients and 27/124 (21.8%) double-therapy patients, HR 0.59 (95% CI 0.32–1.09). Hospitalization for worsening PAH accounted for 10 versus 19 first events. Across recurrent events, 31 versus 67 total disease-progression events were reported, corresponding to a modelled annualized rate ratio of 0.39 (95% CI 0.15–1.00).

By the end of the main observation period, 2 patients assigned to triple therapy and 9 assigned to double therapy had died. The post hoc mortality HR was 0.23 (95% CI 0.05–1.04). Because these clinical-event analyses were not the powered primary comparison, the effect estimates should be read as signals with substantial statistical uncertainty.

Safety and tolerability

The as-treated safety set included 119 patients classified to triple therapy and 127 to double therapy. Any adverse event was reported in 100% versus 96.9%, and serious adverse events in 42.9% versus 31.5%. Discontinuation of double-blind treatment because of an adverse event was 16.0% versus 14.2%.

Independent evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first for the specific question of initial triple oral PAH therapy.
Best use: defining what randomized evidence does—and does not—show about adding initial selexipag to macitentan+tadalafil. Do not use this trial alone as proof that initial triple oral therapy improves long-term clinical outcomes.

Strengths

  • Direct randomized, double-blind comparison of clinically relevant treatment strategies.
  • Clear prespecified primary endpoint and statistical plan with multiple sensitivity analyses.
  • Detailed protocol, analysis-set, titration, discontinuation, and adverse-event data in the supplement.
  • Disease-progression events were systematically collected and adjudicated, allowing hypothesis generation beyond the hemodynamic endpoint.

Limitations and threats to interpretation

  • The primary endpoint was negative; favorable downstream clinical-event trends cannot reverse that confirmatory result.
  • The study was not event-driven or adequately powered to establish a disease-progression or mortality benefit; the reported hazard-ratio confidence intervals cross 1.
  • The selected treatment-naive population limits generalizability to broader PAH practice, especially older/comorbid or previously treated patients.
  • Prespecified imputation was necessary for some primary-endpoint data. Sensitivity analyses are reassuring about direction but do not produce a compelling treatment effect.
  • The sponsor funded the study and collaborated in design/oversight/analysis; multiple authors had sponsor employment or financial relationships. This does not invalidate the trial but increases the value of protocol/SAP concordance and transparent reporting.

Relevance

Direct randomized evidence on initial triple versus double oral PAH therapy.

Evidence basis: Treatment-naive newly diagnosed PAH; macitentan+tadalafil+selexipag versus macitentan+tadalafil+placebo.

Principal limit: The trial population is clinically selected and the primary comparison was negative.

Would change with: Higher if the decision specifically concerns initial oral combination strategy; lower for other PAH phenotypes or established therapy.

8/10

Novelty

The study directly tested an initial three-pathway oral regimen against an initial two-pathway oral regimen.

Evidence basis: The authors identify the absence of prior direct initial triple-versus-double oral randomized data as the motivating gap.

Principal limit: Prior uncontrolled triple-therapy and selexipag-on-background-therapy evidence already supported the biological premise.

Would change with: A clearly event-driven triple-versus-double study with long-term clinical outcomes would add more novelty and consequence.

8/10

Method strength

Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3b design with prespecified primary analysis and multiple sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: 1:1 randomization, stratification, blinded placebo, 247 randomized participants, ANCOVA-based primary analysis, protocol/SAP supplement.

Principal limit: Primary endpoint missingness used prespecified imputation; long-term analyses were not the trial's powered primary test and sponsor participated in design/analysis oversight.

Would change with: Stronger with an event-driven design powered for clinical progression/mortality and minimal reliance on imputation.

7/10

Evidence strength

The negative 26-week primary result is well supported; the apparent long-term benefit signal is substantially less certain.

Evidence basis: PVR ratio of geometric means 0.96 (95% CI 0.86–1.07; P=0.42); disease-progression HR 0.59 (95% CI 0.32–1.09).

Principal limit: The disease-progression and mortality findings are exploratory/post hoc or underpowered, with confidence intervals crossing the null.

Would change with: Higher with replicated, adequately powered clinical-event evidence; lower if sensitivity results materially diverged or follow-up were less complete.

6/10

External validity

The sample represents a defined, relatively severe newly diagnosed treatment-naive PAH population rather than the full PAH spectrum.

Evidence basis: Age 18–75; diagnosis within 6 months; PVR ≥6 WU; 79.8% WHO FC III/IV; explicit exclusions for prior PAH therapy and important comorbidity patterns.

Principal limit: Selection criteria, baseline severity, and exclusions constrain generalization to older, comorbid, less hemodynamically severe, or previously treated patients.

Would change with: Broader pragmatic enrollment and prespecified subgroup power would raise external validity.

7/10

Implementation value

The protocol provides reproducible dosing/titration and a detailed tolerability profile, but not proof that routine initial triple oral therapy is superior.

Evidence basis: Macitentan 10 mg daily, tadalafil titrated to 40 mg daily, selexipag initiated at 200 µg twice daily and titrated weekly to tolerated dose; detailed adverse-event tables.

Principal limit: No superiority on the primary endpoint and more prostacyclin-pathway adverse effects with triple therapy.

Would change with: Higher if an event-driven trial established net clinical benefit and implementation tradeoffs across risk strata.

Implementation and practice takeaways

What the study supportsBoth initial strategies produced marked 26-week hemodynamic and functional improvement. Adding selexipag did not demonstrate superior PVR reduction at week 26.
What remains unresolvedWhether initial triple oral therapy reduces clinically important long-term progression or mortality compared with initial double therapy.
Safety implicationTriple therapy adds a substantial burden of expected prostacyclin-pathway adverse effects, especially during titration.
Research next stepAn adequately powered, event-driven comparison focused on disease progression and long-term outcomes, with prespecified risk-stratum analyses.
Current-practice boundary: This source-only appraisal does not determine whether later guidelines or regulatory documents changed how the regimen should be used after 2021.
