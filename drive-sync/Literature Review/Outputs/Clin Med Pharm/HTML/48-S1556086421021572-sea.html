One-page synthesis

Core finding. Phase 1 identified 50 mg intrathecal pemetrexed as the recommended/maximum tolerated dose after 2 of 3 patients at 80 mg developed dose-limiting toxicity. In phase 2, the study reported an 84.6% clinical response rate (22/26 evaluable) and median overall survival of 9.0 months (95% CI 6.6–11.4), with myelosuppression the dominant serious toxicity signal.

The clinical signal is notable because the cohort was heavily pretreated: all patients had EGFR-mutant NSCLC with leptomeningeal disease after at least three treatment lines, 25/30 had received osimertinib, and many had poor baseline performance status. The regimen combined intrathecal pemetrexed with intrathecal dexamethasone and vitamin supplementation. The primary endpoint, however, was not a conventional radiographic objective-response endpoint; it was a composite clinical classification based on neurologic symptoms/signs and KPS, with its operational definitions supplied in Supplemental Table 1.

The evidence is hypothesis-generating rather than practice-defining. There was no concurrent control group, only 30 phase 2 patients, 4 patients died before the 4-week efficacy assessment, subgroup analyses were small, and several internal numerical inconsistencies occur between abstract, narrative text, tables, and Figure 3. The 50-mg dose and the high observed response rate are therefore useful as a prospective signal and regimen description, not as proof of superiority over other CNS-directed strategies.

Methods

Design and eligibility

Open-label, single-arm phase 1/2 trial (ChiCTR1800016615). Eligible patients were 18–75 years old with probable or confirmed EGFR-mutant NSCLC leptomeningeal metastases and multiline treatment failure (≥3). Exclusions included HIV, severe pemetrexed allergy, severe infection or major comorbidities, inability to comply with testing, and pregnancy. LM diagnosis combined clinical, CSF, and radiographic findings under EANO-ESMO criteria.

Dose escalation and treatment

Phase 1 used accelerated titration. Levels 1–4 were 15, 30, 50, and 80 mg intrathecal pemetrexed; Figure 1 also shows an unused 10-mg level 0. One patient was treated per level until a DLT or level 4, then the design reverted to 3+3. Prespecified DLTs were grade ≥3 neurotoxicity, grade ≥3 leukoencephalopathy, or grade 4 myelosuppression lasting >7 days. The 80-mg cohort produced 2 DLTs among 3 patients, establishing 50 mg as RD/MTD.

The phase 2 regimen was 50 mg intrathecal pemetrexed by lumbar puncture, with intrathecal dexamethasone 5 mg at each administration. The paper visually reports folic acid 400 μg orally once daily and vitamin B12 1000 μg intramuscularly 1–2 weeks before the first pemetrexed dose and every 9 weeks. Induction was on days 1 and 5 of week 1, followed by less frequent consolidation/maintenance dosing. The narrative and Figure 1 are not perfectly aligned on the exact transition from q3-week to monthly dosing, so the artifact does not silently reconcile them.

Response and statistics

Clinical response was assessed weekly. CR, obvious response, or PR had to persist for at least one week to count as effective; stable disease and PD were ineffective. Supplemental Table 1 required neurologic and KPS conditions to be satisfied synchronously. The planned phase 2 sample size was 27, based on a response-rate improvement from 40% to 80%, ~80% power, one-sided alpha 0.20, and 10% exclusion. OS used Kaplan-Meier methods; log-rank and Fisher tests were used for subgroup comparisons.

Results

Population

Thirty patients entered phase 2 (16 women, 14 men; mean age 54, range 31–70). Thirteen had ECOG ≤2 and 17 had ECOG >2. KPS was <60 in 18 patients. Twenty-five had previously received osimertinib, and 5 had prior intrathecal cytarabine or methotrexate. Initial CSF cytology was positive in 15/30, with six additional positives on repeat testing; MRI was positive in 19 and negative in 11.

Efficacy and survival

Four patients died within four weeks and could not be evaluated for the primary efficacy endpoint, leaving 26 evaluable patients. Response was reported in 22/26 (84.6%): CR 2 (7.7%), obvious response 13 (50.0%), PR 7 (26.9%), stable disease 3 (11.5%), and PD 1 (3.8%). Median OS for all 30 patients was 9.0 months (95% CI 6.6–11.4); 19/30 were dead at study end.

Performance status and response category were associated with survival: median OS was 12 months for KPS ≥60 versus 6 months for KPS <60 (p=0.0027), and Figure 3 shows 12 months for effective responders versus 4.5 months for ineffective patients (p<0.0001). Brain-metastasis subgroup reporting is internally inconsistent: the results narrative reports 8 vs 11 months (p=0.2058), while Figure 3C reports 8 vs 13 months (p=0.2080). Prior osimertinib and concurrent EGFR-TKI use did not show statistically significant response differences in the reported exploratory comparisons.

Exploratory CSF findings

KPS improved in 18/30 (60%). CSF CEA decreased in all 9 patients measured. Cytologic response occurred in 3/15 (20%) patients with initially positive CSF cytology. In 15 patients with paired gene testing, mutation detection was 93.3% in CSF versus 53.3% in plasma; T790M was found in plasma in two patients but in no CSF samples.

Safety

At 80 mg in phase 1, 2/3 patients had grade 4 myelosuppression, establishing the dose-limiting signal. At the 50-mg phase 2 dose, Table 3 reports 11/30 (36.7%) with myelosuppression: grade 2 in 6, grade 3 in 4, and grade 4 in 1. Other reported events were nausea (2 grade 1), vomiting (6 grade 1), limb pain (5 total; 2 grade 3), back pain (2), paralysis (2 grade 2), and headache (3; 1 grade 3). No ALT/AST toxicity, acute cerebral meningitis, or leukoencephalopathy was reported in Table 3.

Internal safety-reporting inconsistency. The abstract reports myelosuppression in 9/30 (30%), while Results/Table 3 report 11/30 (36.7%). Table 3 reports four grade-3 cases, while the Discussion states three. These values are preserved rather than reconciled.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon; do not use alone to establish practice. The study is valuable for prospective dose finding and for defining a 50-mg regimen, but its uncontrolled phase 2 design, nonstandard response endpoint, small sample, early unevaluable deaths, and internal reporting inconsistencies materially limit causal and comparative interpretation.
Relevance8/10
High relevance to CNS oncology pharmacotherapy and route-specific dosing; limited routine applicability.
Novelty8/10
Prospective phase 1/2 evaluation of intrathecal pemetrexed in a highly selected EGFR-mutant LM population.
Method strength5/10
Prospective dose escalation is useful, but phase 2 is single-arm, small, and uses a nonstandard response endpoint.
Evidence strength4/10
Signals are clinically interesting but uncontrolled, imprecise, and affected by endpoint/denominator and internal reporting inconsistencies.
External validity4/10
Single-country, EGFR-mutant, heavily pretreated population with poor performance status; wild-type disease was not studied.
Implementation value5/10
Provides a reproducible 50-mg intrathecal regimen and safety signal, but evidence is insufficient to establish comparative benefit or standard practice.

Strengths

  • Prospective phase 1/2 design with explicit DLT criteria and a prespecified dose-escalation scheme.
  • Clinically difficult, heavily pretreated leptomeningeal-metastasis population.
  • Operational response criteria are available in the supplement rather than left implicit.
  • Survival and toxicity are reported alongside the response endpoint.

Principal limitations

  • No randomized or contemporaneous comparator.
  • Only 30 phase 2 patients and 26 evaluable for the primary clinical-response endpoint.
  • Response endpoint is symptom/KPS based and not equivalent to conventional radiographic ORR.
  • Subgroup analyses are underpowered and exploratory.
  • Several source-internal inconsistencies affect safety, WBRT history, brain-metastasis survival, endpoint terminology, and Table 1 formatting.
  • Only EGFR-mutant disease was studied.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Best useProspective signal for intrathecal pemetrexed dose selection, regimen design, and safety monitoring in future studies or highly specialized clinical discussions.
Do not use forClaims of superiority, routine standard-of-care adoption, or precise comparative efficacy against osimertinib or other intrathecal agents.
Key safety focusMyelosuppression, including grade 4 toxicity at 80 mg and a grade 4 event at the 50-mg phase 2 dose.
Before clinical translationVerify current guidelines, regulatory status, institutional procedures, intrathecal preparation/administration standards, and contemporary alternatives independently.
