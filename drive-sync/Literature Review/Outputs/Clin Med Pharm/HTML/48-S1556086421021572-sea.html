One-page synthesis

Core finding. The dose-escalation portion identified 50 mg intrathecal pemetrexed as the recommended/maximum tolerated dose after 2 of 3 patients at 80 mg developed DLTs. In phase 2, the study reported an 84.6% clinical response rate (22/26 evaluable) and median overall survival of 9.0 months (95% CI 6.6–11.4), with myelosuppression the main serious toxicity signal.

The regimen used intrathecal pemetrexed with intrathecal dexamethasone, folic acid, and vitamin B12. Phase 2 dosing was twice in week 1 (days 1 and 5), then every 3 weeks as consolidation until progression, then every 4 weeks as maintenance. The response endpoint was not a standard radiographic ORR; it was a clinical composite requiring neurologic improvement plus KPS thresholds, defined in the supplement. Four patients died before 4 weeks and were excluded from the primary efficacy denominator. A simple all-enrolled denominator therefore yields 22/30 = 73.3%, a model calculation rather than the study's reported primary result.

The study is useful as early dose-finding and signal-generation evidence in a difficult population, but it is not comparative efficacy evidence. The small single-arm sample, nonstandard response endpoint, exclusion of early deaths from efficacy assessment, one-sided alpha 0.20 in the sample-size framework, exploratory subgroups, and multiple internal numerical inconsistencies materially limit confidence in the 84.6% response estimate and any causal survival interpretation.

Source-internal discrepancies requiring caution: abstract myelosuppression 9/30 (30%) versus Results/Table 3 11/30 (36.7%); grade 3 myelosuppression 4 in Results/Table 3 versus 3 in Discussion; previous WBRT 5 in Table 1 versus 8 in narrative; brain-metastasis subgroup OS 8 vs 11 months, p=0.2058 in text versus 8 vs 13 months, p=0.2080 in Figure 3C; Figure 1 labels the primary endpoint “ORR” while Methods define clinical response rate; Table 1 displays “Mean 9–70 / Range 28” for time to meningeal metastasis and is not silently repaired here.

Methods and trial architecture

Clinical question / PICO

Population: adults 18–75 years with probable or confirmed EGFR-mutant NSCLC leptomeningeal metastases and at least three prior treatment failures. Intervention: intrathecal pemetrexed dose escalation in phase 1; phase 2 used the recommended 50 mg dose with intrathecal dexamethasone 5 mg, plus folate/B12 support. Comparator: none. Outcomes: clinical response rate, overall survival, adverse events.

Dose finding and regimen

Phase 1 started at 15 mg, then 30 mg, 50 mg, and 80 mg using accelerated titration. DLTs were grade ≥3 neurotoxicity, grade ≥3 leukoencephalopathy, or grade 4 myelosuppression lasting >7 days. Dose level 4 (80 mg) produced 2 DLTs among 3 patients, leading to 50 mg as the reported RD/MTD. Phase 2 treatment continued until progression, unacceptable toxicity, or discontinuation.

Response assessment

The investigators explicitly rejected exclusive reliance on RANO LM response assessment and instead used criteria adapted from a prior clinical study: complete, obvious, or partial response counted as effective when sustained for at least 1 week; stable and progressive disease were ineffective. Supplemental Table 1 shows that each response category required both neurologic signs/symptoms and a KPS condition synchronously. This endpoint is clinically oriented but not equivalent to conventional radiographic objective response rate.

Statistics

The planned sample size was 27, aimed at detecting an increase in clinical response from 40% to 80% with approximately 80% power, a one-sided significance level of 0.20, and 10% exclusion allowance. Survival used Kaplan-Meier methods and subgroup comparisons used log-rank/Fisher tests. The relatively permissive design alpha, absence of a control arm, small subgroup sizes, and exclusion of four early deaths from the primary efficacy denominator are major interpretive constraints.

Results

Phase 1 dose escalation

DLTs were 0/1 at 15 mg, 0/1 at 30 mg, 0/1 at 50 mg, and 2/3 at 80 mg. The investigators therefore reported 50 mg as both RD and MTD. The 80 mg DLT signal was grade 4 myelosuppression.

Phase 2 population

Thirty patients were enrolled; 16 were women and 14 men. Most were heavily pretreated: 25/30 had prior osimertinib and all had EGFR mutations and prior TKIs. Seventeen had KPS <60. Four patients died within 4 weeks and were not evaluable for the reported efficacy endpoint.

Efficacy

Among 26 evaluable patients, 22 responded (84.6%): CR 2/26, obvious response 13/26, PR 7/26, stable disease 3/26, and PD 1/26. Median OS for all 30 patients was 9.0 months (95% CI 6.6–11.4). Clinical response status was associated with OS in Figure 3B (median 12 vs 4.5 months; p<0.0001), but this is a post-treatment responder analysis and should not be interpreted causally. KPS ≥60 was associated with longer median OS than KPS <60 (12 vs 6 months; p=0.0027).

Exploratory findings included CSF CEA reduction in all 9 tested patients, cytologic response in 3/15 initially positive patients (20%), and higher positive mutation detection in CSF than plasma (93.3% vs 53.3%) among 15 paired tests. T790M was detected in plasma of 2 patients but not in CSF. These are small, exploratory biomarker observations.

Safety

At 50 mg, most reported toxicities were grade 1–2, but myelosuppression remained clinically important. Table 3 reports myelosuppression in 11 patients: grade 2 in 6, grade 3 in 4, and grade 4 in 1. Grade 3 neurotoxicity-like events included limb pain in 2 and headache in 1. There were no Table 3 events for acute cerebral meningitis, leukoencephalopathy, or ALT/AST elevation. Internal count discrepancies are detailed above and prevent a single reconciled toxicity total from being asserted without qualification.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High-value early signal and dose-finding paper for intrathecal pemetrexed, but not sufficient as stand-alone practice-changing evidence.
Relevance — 8/10Directly addresses salvage pharmacotherapy for EGFR-mutant NSCLC leptomeningeal metastases.
Evidence basis: prospective phase 1/2 regimen and safety/response data.
Limiter: narrow population and investigational context.
Raise/lower: higher with comparative/current-practice integration; lower if question is general NSCLC without LM.
Novelty — 8/10The paper presents a prospective phase 1/2 intrathecal pemetrexed program and identifies a 50 mg RD/MTD.
Evidence basis: dose escalation plus phase 2 expansion.
Limiter: builds on earlier pilot/preclinical work.
Raise/lower: external replication would shift novelty toward maturity rather than novelty.

Limiter: single arm, small sample, permissive alpha, nonstandard response endpoint, early-death exclusion.

Evidence basis: all patients EGFR-mutant; 25/30 previously received osimertinib.
Limiter: no EGFR-wild-type cohort and limited sample diversity.
Raise/lower: multicenter international replication would raise it.
Implementation value — 6/10The dose, schedule, supplementation, DLTs, and monitoring are operationally specific.
Evidence basis: detailed intrathecal regimen and AE table.
Limiter: investigational evidence and uncertain optimal treatment duration/interval.
Raise/lower: mature comparative trials, guideline/label alignment, and standardized administration guidance would raise it.

Validity and bias

  • Selection/generalizability: rare, highly selected, heavily pretreated EGFR-mutant cohort.
  • Performance/detection bias: open-label and primary response depends on clinical examination plus KPS.
  • Attrition/analysis population: 4/30 early deaths excluded from primary efficacy assessment; calculated all-enrolled responder proportion is 73.3%, not 84.6%.
  • Confounding: no control arm; concurrent EGFR TKI in some patients and prior therapies vary substantially.
  • Subgroups: exploratory, small, not powered for definitive inference.
  • Reporting integrity: multiple internally inconsistent counts/values across abstract, text, tables, and figures reduce confidence in exact secondary estimates.

Practice translation

This source supports considering 50 mg intrathecal pemetrexed as an investigational dose for further study in this specific population. It does not establish superiority over other intrathecal agents, systemic EGFR-directed strategies, radiation approaches, or supportive care, and it does not by itself justify routine clinical adoption. Current guideline, regulatory, institutional, and pharmacy-preparation requirements were not externally verified in this source-only appraisal.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use nowUse as phase 1/2 dose/safety signal and as a source for the 50 mg investigational regimen architecture.
Do not overclaimDo not treat 84.6% response as a controlled efficacy estimate; 4 early deaths were excluded and the endpoint was nonstandard.
Safety focusMyelosuppression is the dose-limiting signal; counts are internally inconsistent, so cite the exact source location when using a number.
Read nextPrior phase 1 intrathecal pemetrexed work, the rat PK/safety study, EANO-ESMO LM guidance, RANO response papers, BLOOM osimertinib, and comparative intrathecal-therapy literature.
