One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In young, low-cardiorenal-risk Korean adults with T2DM who remained on therapy for at least 365 days, empagliflozin and dapagliflozin had broadly similar major cardiovascular and renal outcomes. Empagliflozin was associated with lower all-cause-hospitalization and kidney-event hazards than DPP-4 inhibitors, but the observational design and persistent-user selection limit causal interpretation.

The study began with 497,024 adults with T2DM and formed final eligible cohorts of 17,050 EMPA, 29,434 DAPA, and 55,854 DPP-4i users. Separate 1:1 propensity-score matches created two clinical comparisons with 17,050 patients in each arm. Baseline covariates were reported as balanced after matching (all SMD <0.10). The matched population was approximately 40 years old, about 70% male, with ASCVD prevalence around 4% and CKD under 1%.

Within class, EMPA versus DAPA yielded aHR 0.96 (95% CI 0.92–0.99) for hospitalization and 0.60 (0.44–0.82) for hypoglycemia, while ASCVD, heart failure, kidney events, DKA, UTI, microvascular complications, and diabetic foot were not statistically different. The authors explicitly caution that the small hospitalization and hypoglycemia differences should not be interpreted as pharmacologic superiority. Versus DPP-4i, EMPA had lower hospitalization (aHR 0.83, 0.80–0.87) and kidney-event hazards (0.76, 0.66–0.88), higher UTI (1.14, 1.08–1.21) and microvascular-complication hazards (1.10, 1.05–1.17), with hypoglycemia and DKA findings labeled exploratory because early events may be selected out.

The 1-year laboratory analysis used only ~18% of matched patients with eligible follow-up screening. EMPA had greater fasting-glucose and BMI reductions than DAPA (ΔΔ −4.06 mg/dL and −0.17 kg/m²) and substantially greater reductions than DPP-4i (−15.80 mg/dL and −0.74 kg/m²); eGFR change did not differ significantly in either comparison.

Interpretation boundary: Eligibility required future persistence (≥365 days and PDC ≥80%) while follow-up was anchored at index day 0. The paper itself notes that discontinuers and acute events causing non-persistence may be underrepresented. This is especially important for safety outcomes.

Design and methods

Population and exposure

T2DM required at least two outpatient T2DM claims during 2013–2024 plus a health-screening record; type 1 diabetes was excluded. A new-user restriction excluded prior EMPA/DAPA/DPP-4i before January 1, 2016. Inclusion required age ≥19 years, ≥365 days of index-drug coverage, PDC ≥80%, baseline laboratory measurements within 90 days before index, and complete fasting glucose/eGFR/BMI. One-year laboratory analyses further required a measurement at 365 ±60 days.

Index was first study-drug prescription. Follow-up was on treatment until discontinuation, switch, death, or December 31, 2024. The main analytical vulnerability is that future persistence determined eligibility even though outcome time began at index, creating a persistent-user cohort.

Confounding control and statistics

Propensity scores were estimated separately for EMPA–DAPA and EMPA–DPP-4i using baseline covariates. Matching was 1:1 nearest neighbor without replacement, greedy, with a 0.2-SD caliper on the logit propensity score; SMD <0.10 defined adequate balance. Cox models estimated HRs/95% CIs and then additionally adjusted baseline covariates as a doubly robust approach.

Laboratory endpoints used linear mixed models with group, time, and group×time fixed effects, compound-symmetry covariance, and Kenward–Roger degrees of freedom. No formal correction for multiple comparisons was applied; p values are nominal.

Limitations and source consistency

Residual confounding

HbA1c, albuminuria, measured blood pressure, smoking, alcohol, diabetes severity, prescriber preference, lifestyle, and treatment intensification were unavailable or not prespecified.

Persistent-user selection

≥365-day coverage/PDC eligibility can underrepresent early discontinuation and acute safety events.

Laboratory selection

Only ~18% had eligible 1-year screening measurements.

Generalizability

Young, mostly male, health-screened, low-cardiorenal-risk Korean population.

Endpoint heterogeneity

Claims-based kidney and microvascular composites can be misclassified and surveillance-sensitive.

Multiplicity

No formal correction; isolated p values are nominal.

Four source-integrity flags were retained:
  1. Main text says all-cause hospitalization; Supplementary Table S1 says diabetes-related hospitalization based on primary E11.x–E14.x diagnosis.
  2. Figure 2 comparison labels are reversed relative to Table 2/prose direction while displaying the same HRs.
  3. Methods use January 1, 2016 for new-user exposure classification; Figure 1 prints “since 2016.05.”
  4. Main-text kidney-event definition includes dialysis and broader acute/chronic kidney disease; Supplement S1 gives N17/N18/N19 and describes incident AKI or advanced CKD without a dialysis procedure code.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as supportive real-world evidence for broad within-class similarity, not as proof that empagliflozin is intrinsically superior to dapagliflozin or as a stand-alone formulary-switch justification.
Relevance9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses within-class EMPA–DAPA and between-class EMPA–DPP-4i comparative effectiveness, including clinical and metabolic endpoints.

Evidence basis: Nationwide active-comparator analysis with formulary-relevant contrasts.

Limiting factor: Population and design limit causal interpretation.

Would change: Would rise with prospective randomized head-to-head evidence in a clinically representative population.

Novelty7/10

Rationale: Combines separate pairwise active-comparator cohorts with both time-to-event clinical outcomes and 1-year metabolic trajectories.

Evidence basis: Large NHIS dataset and direct within-class comparison.

Limiting factor: Related real-world and indirect comparative studies already exist.

Would change: Would rise with prespecified mechanistic or causal analyses that resolve agent-level heterogeneity.

Method strength6/10

Rationale: Separate propensity matching and covariate-adjusted Cox models are appropriate for observational comparative effectiveness, but persistent-user conditioning and unavailable confounders remain important.

Evidence basis: 1:1 matching, SMD balance checks, doubly adjusted Cox models, mixed models.

Limiting factor: ≥365-day eligibility can select persistent survivors; residual confounding and nominal multiplicity remain.

Would change: Would rise with a new-user active-comparator design that does not condition eligibility on future persistence, richer confounders, and multiplicity control.

Evidence strength5/10

Rationale: Associations are quantitatively reported with confidence intervals, but observational design, selection mechanisms, outcome-definition inconsistencies, and multiple testing reduce certainty.

Evidence basis: Matched n=34,100 per pairwise clinical cohort; effect estimates and CIs reported.

Limiting factor: No randomization; source inconsistencies affect HOS and figure direction; acute safety is explicitly exploratory.

Would change: Would rise with replicated results using transparent endpoint definitions and independent datasets or randomized evidence.

External validity4/10

Rationale: The matched cohort was unusually young, predominantly male, low-risk, Korean, health-screened, and restricted to persistent users.

Evidence basis: Nationwide claims coverage increases breadth within the selected Korean population.

Limiting factor: Mean age ~40, ~70% men, ASCVD ~4%, CKD <1%, follow-up lab subset ~18%.

Would change: Would rise with replication in older, higher-risk, multi-country and less selected populations.

Implementation value6/10

Rationale: Useful as supportive real-world evidence for class similarity, but not sufficient to justify pharmacologic superiority or a formulary switch by itself.

Evidence basis: Direct EMPA–DAPA comparison and authors explicitly reject superiority interpretation.

Limiting factor: Observational design and small within-class absolute differences.

Would change: Would rise with robust head-to-head randomized outcome evidence and clearer applicability to inpatient formulary decisions.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate. The main numerical results are well specified and the full supplement was available, but causal certainty is limited by observational selection/confounding and several source-internal definition/label inconsistencies.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this supports

For major cardiovascular and renal outcomes, the EMPA–DAPA point estimates were close to unity, consistent with class-level similarity in this selected cohort.

What this does not support

The study does not establish pharmacologic superiority of EMPA over DAPA. The authors themselves caution against that interpretation.

Formulary use

Useful as one supporting RWE layer when assessing whether a dapagliflozin-only formulary is likely to sacrifice major cardiorenal effectiveness, but randomized outcomes evidence, guideline indications, labeling, inpatient operational constraints, and acquisition cost remain separate evidence domains.

Safety use

Do not rely on the hypoglycemia/DKA comparisons as early-event safety estimates because the design preferentially includes persistent users.
