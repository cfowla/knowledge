One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In a contemporary TriNetX cohort of adults with transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) who received tafamidis or vutrisiran, baseline MRA use was not associated with lower two-year mortality, heart-failure hospitalisation, ventricular arrhythmia or the composite of death/HF hospitalisation after propensity-score matching. Hyperkalaemia was also not statistically higher among MRA users. The study therefore argues that incremental benefit from conventional mineralocorticoid receptor blockade may be limited once disease-modifying therapy is in place.

What the source reports. Among 4,598 ATTR-CM patients initiating disease-modifying therapy, exclusions left 649 MRA users and 2,455 non-users; 505 per group were matched. The matched primary composite was 138/505 (27.3%) versus 136/505 (26.9%), HR 1.04 (95% CI 0.82-1.32; p=0.73). Mortality HR was 1.00 (0.70-1.46), HF hospitalisation HR 1.09 (0.84-1.43), ventricular arrhythmia HR 1.07 (0.77-1.48), and hyperkalaemia HR 1.13 (0.89-1.44). The tafamidis-only sensitivity analysis and ATTR subtype analyses showed the same general null pattern.

Independent appraisal. The null matched associations are clinically interesting but not definitive. The design selects patients already demonstrating MRA exposure/tolerance before disease-modifying therapy, relies on baseline exposure classification despite substantial post-index crossover, lacks granular cardiac phenotype and individual amyloid staging, and cannot eliminate confounding by severity or treatment selection. Confidence intervals remain compatible with clinically meaningful benefit or harm for several outcomes. Several internal reporting inconsistencies also reduce confidence in numerical polish, although the central matched point estimates are consistent across the text, figures and main endpoint table.

Best use. Contemporary hypothesis-generating evidence that routine incremental benefit from MRAs in disease-modifying-therapy-treated ATTR-CM should not be assumed. Do not use as: proof that MRAs are ineffective, a basis to withdraw an otherwise indicated/tolerated MRA, or evidence specific to vutrisiran-treated patients.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames the unresolved question as whether MRAs add clinical benefit to modern ATTR-CM disease-modifying therapy. It reports a retrospective TriNetX cohort with 1:1 propensity matching and identifies 505 matched users and 505 non-users from a broader cohort of 4,598 treated patients.

The abstract reports no significant difference for the composite of death/HF hospitalisation (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.82-1.32), mortality (HR 1.00, 0.70-1.46), HF hospitalisation (HR 1.09, 0.84-1.43), ventricular arrhythmia (HR 1.07, 0.77-1.48), or hyperkalaemia (HR 1.13, 0.89-1.44), and concludes that incremental benefit appears limited.

Introduction

The introduction situates ATTR-CM as a restrictive cardiomyopathy in which tafamidis and gene-silencing therapies have improved prognosis but residual morbidity and mortality remain. It notes that conventional HF therapies are uncertain because ATTR-CM patients were excluded from major HF trials and because restrictive physiology/dysautonomia can impair tolerance of neurohormonal blockade.

The specific evidence gap is that prior registry data associated MRA use with lower mortality, but very few patients in that registry received ATTR-specific disease-modifying therapy. The study therefore tests whether the earlier MRA association persists in a contemporary disease-modifying-therapy-treated population.

Methods

TriNetX US data were used to identify adults receiving first tafamidis or vutrisiran within one year after incident HF. MRA users required at least two spironolactone/eplerenone prescriptions within the prior year; non-users had none. The study excluded major MRA contraindications and very high NT-proBNP, then used 1:1 greedy nearest-neighbour propensity matching with a 0.1 pooled-SD caliper across demographics, comorbidities, medications, BMI, EF, eGFR and NT-proBNP.

Outcomes were evaluated over two years with Cox hazard ratios and Kaplan-Meier/log-rank analyses. HF hospitalisation was operationalised as intravenous-diuretic administration; hyperkalaemia was potassium ≥5.5 mEq/L. Baseline treatment group assignment was retained under an ITT framework. ATTRwt/ATTRv analyses used ICD-10-CM coding, and a tafamidis-only sensitivity analysis addressed the small vutrisiran subgroup.

Results

From 4,598 ATTR-CM patients on disease-modifying therapy, 1,543 were excluded, leaving 649 MRA users and 2,455 non-users; propensity matching produced 505 per arm. The narrative describes MRA users as clinically sicker before matching. After matching, the authors state that baseline variables were balanced, although Table 1 contains at least one SMD above the prespecified 0.10 balance threshold (troponin-I SMD 0.117). During follow-up, 114/505 baseline non-users (22.6%) later had an MRA prescription but remained in the non-user group.

Before matching, MRA exposure was associated with worse composite outcome (HR 1.53, 1.28-1.82) and HF hospitalisation (HR 1.80, 1.48-2.20), consistent with confounding by severity. After matching, all principal HRs clustered near 1 with confidence intervals crossing 1. The tafamidis-only sensitivity analysis and ATTRwt/ATTRv subgroup analyses were directionally similar, but subtype samples were much smaller.

Discussion

The authors interpret the findings as suggesting that contemporary prognosis may be driven more by disease stage and ATTR-specific therapy than by conventional neurohormonal blockade. They contrast their result with prior amyloidosis registry findings and propose that earlier-era diagnostic timing, healthcare-access confounding and sparse disease-modifying therapy in earlier cohorts may explain the discrepancy.

The discussion also notes that the current cohort had relatively more female and Black patients than major registries, raising the possibility of more variant disease, while acknowledging that subtype classification was incomplete. The authors view the absence of Kaplan-Meier divergence and consistency across sensitivity/subgroup analyses as supporting limited incremental MRA benefit, but they explicitly call for prospective confirmation.

Limitations

Major internal-validity limitations are retrospective design, residual confounding by HF/amyloid severity, possible selection bias during eligibility and matching, absent granular echocardiographic phenotype, incomplete genetic/subtype ascertainment, inability to incorporate individual Gillmore stage, and possible competing-risk bias for non-fatal endpoints.

Exposure measurement is also important: treatment was fixed at baseline, while adherence, treatment duration, discontinuation and outside prescriptions were unavailable. Post-index initiation among baseline non-users and discontinuation among users could bias effects toward the null. Generalisability is further limited by prevalent-user/tolerance selection, predominance of tafamidis, sparse vutrisiran exposure, and a network dominated by large academic centres.

Conclusion

The authors conclude that MRA use offered limited incremental clinical benefit in this contemporary disease-modifying-therapy-treated ATTR-CM cohort. They caution that extrapolation to vutrisiran is uncertain because most patients received tafamidis and recommend prospective trials of MRA efficacy.

Source reconciliation and QA findings

  • Pre-match baseline values: several percentages in the narrative Results differ from Table 1. The appraisal preserves the higher-level finding (MRA users appeared sicker before matching) and does not silently select one conflicting percentage set.
  • Balance threshold: methods define SMD <0.10 as balanced; Table 1 reports troponin-I SMD 0.117 after matching, so “well balanced” is not literally true for every displayed covariate.
  • Tafamidis-only sensitivity: narrative Results gives composite HR 1.04 and mortality HR 1.05, whereas Table 2 reports 1.05 and 1.06, respectively, with identical confidence intervals/p values. Table 2 values are used for the structured endpoint extraction and the discrepancy is retained here.
  • Primary Table 2 headings: HR and p-value column labels appear transposed in the upper subtable; the data pattern and narrative make the intended mapping clear.
  • Subtype counts: the Results reports ATTRwt n=2,116, ATTRv n=939 and unknown n=2,847 in a 4,598-patient cohort. Those counts exceed the cohort total when summed, implying overlap or a reporting/definition issue not resolvable without the supplemental query details.
  • Disease-modifying therapy counts: tafamidis n=4,386 plus vutrisiran n=377 also exceeds 4,598; the article does not state that these are mutually exclusive, so overlap is possible and is not treated as an error.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. The paper directly addresses a clinically relevant contemporary evidence gap and is useful for calibrating expectations about conventional HF therapy in ATTR-CM, but the observational prevalent-user design and exposure misclassification prevent practice-defining conclusions.

Directly evaluates adjunctive pharmacotherapy in disease-modifying-therapy-treated ATTR-CM.

Evidence basis: clinically meaningful mortality/HF/safety endpoints in a modern cohort.

Limiting factor: question applies chiefly to tafamidis-era care.

Would rise: broader representation of current disease-modifying therapies.

8/10
Novelty

Targets whether prior MRA registry associations persist after ATTR-specific therapy became routine.

Evidence basis: very high DMT exposure compared with earlier conventional-HF-therapy registries.

Limiting factor: retrospective database design.

Would rise: prospective comparative data.

6/10
Method strength

Large real-world source, explicit exclusions, 1:1 PS matching, falsification endpoint, subgroup and sensitivity analyses.

Evidence basis: balanced major covariates and consistent null analyses.

Limiting factor: residual confounding, prevalent-user selection, incomplete phenotype/stage, baseline-only exposure.

Would rise: new-user active-comparator design, time-varying exposure, richer phenotype or randomisation.

5/10
Evidence strength

Point estimates are consistently near 1, but the design cannot establish absence of benefit and several CIs allow meaningful effects.

Evidence basis: 505-per-arm matched analysis plus tafamidis sensitivity and subtype analyses.

Limiting factor: non-randomised confounding and 22.6% post-index MRA initiation in baseline non-users.

Would rise: prospective randomised or robust quasi-experimental confirmation.

6/10
External validity

Large US multi-organisation cohort with more female and Black representation than some registries.

Evidence basis: TriNetX network across US regions.

Limiting factor: largely academic centres, tolerance-selected MRA users, predominant tafamidis, sparse vutrisiran, incomplete subtype classification.

Would rise: external replication in independent registries and broader therapy mix.

5/10
Implementation value

Useful for avoiding overconfidence that MRAs necessarily add outcome benefit in modern ATTR-CM.

Evidence basis: null matched outcomes and no clear safety signal among selected patients.

Limiting factor: no dosing/titration/adherence detail and inadequate causal certainty for treatment withdrawal or guideline change.

Would rise: prospective efficacy/safety trial with treatment protocols and tolerability outcomes.

Principal safety/appraisal caution. Hyperkalaemia HR 1.13 (0.89-1.44) should not be read as proof of equal safety. The cohort excluded patients with baseline hyperkalaemia, eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² and ESRD/dialysis, and MRA users had prior prescriptions intended to establish tolerance. This selection narrows the safety question to relatively MRA-eligible/tolerant patients.

Implementation / practice takeaways

What this supportsUse the paper as contemporary observational evidence that additional outcome benefit from MRAs is uncertain in ATTR-CM already treated with disease-modifying therapy.
What this does not supportDo not infer equivalence, no causal benefit, or a reason to stop an MRA that has another indication or is clinically required.
Safety interpretationNo statistically significant hyperkalaemia increase was observed, but selection excluded major renal/potassium contraindications and favoured prior MRA tolerance.
Evidence gapA prospective study should use clear ATTR subtype/stage, new-user exposure, time-varying adherence/discontinuation, granular cardiac phenotype, and adequate representation of tafamidis and gene-silencing therapies.
