One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. This is directly relevant supportive evidence for an empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin formulary question, but its strongest defensible conclusion is no detected clinically large difference in the primary composite within the authors’ observational design, not causal proof that the drugs are universally interchangeable.

The authors used Korean NHIS claims, mortality, and health-screening data to identify 135,559 new users of empagliflozin or dapagliflozin without established ASCVD, prior heart failure, or advanced kidney disease. After 1:1 propensity-score matching, 48,351 patients remained in each group. During median 4.4 years of follow-up, the primary composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke, heart-failure hospitalization, or ESRD occurred at 5.150 versus 5.311 events per 1,000 person-years; empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.89–1.09; P=0.72). The authors’ predefined equivalence interval was 0.86–1.16, and the primary CI fell within it.

Secondary outcome hazard ratios were not statistically different, but their confidence intervals were materially wider for cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, and ESRD. Seven alternative sensitivity analyses yielded primary-outcome HRs from 0.96 to 0.99. These consistency checks strengthen robustness to the modeled analytic choices but cannot remove confounding from unmeasured HbA1c, diabetes duration, albuminuria, LVEF, adherence, prescribing rationale, formulary access, or other channeling factors. The study is therefore useful as large-scale real-world support for formulary flexibility, while direct randomized evidence remains the more appropriate standard for a causal equivalence claim.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper frames SGLT2 inhibitors as foundational cardiorenal therapy but identifies a direct-comparison gap between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin in primary prevention. Prior evidence is largely agent-specific outcome trials, indirect network comparisons, or higher-risk populations with established disease.

The prespecified aim was a direct real-world comparison of cardiovascular and renal outcomes in T2DM patients without established cardiovascular or advanced renal disease.

Methods

The Korean NHIS cohort used a new-user design from January 2014 through December 2021. T2DM required ICD-10 E11–E14 plus at least one inpatient or two outpatient records. Major exclusions included dual exposure to both drugs, prior ASCVD, prior heart failure, advanced kidney disease, and missing recent national health-screening data. The primary composite was cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, HF hospitalization, or ESRD; MACE and each component were secondary outcomes.

Confounding control used 1:1 nearest-neighbor propensity-score matching without replacement with a 0.05-SD logit caliper and balance criterion |SMD|<0.1. Cox models used an intention-to-treat framework with dapagliflozin as reference. Sensitivity analyses included Fine-Gray competing risk, index-year adjustment, alternative calipers, IPTW, stabilized IPTW, and multivariable Cox. The authors defined primary-outcome therapeutic equivalence as a 95% HR CI fully contained within 0.86–1.16.

Results

The unmatched cohort included 48,373 empagliflozin and 87,186 dapagliflozin users. Matching produced 48,351 per arm, with all listed covariates balanced at SMD<0.1. Median follow-up was 4.4 years (IQR 2.7–5.7). The primary outcome was HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.89–1.09; P=0.72), with incidence 5.150 versus 5.311 per 1,000 person-years.

MACE HR 0.94 (0.85–1.05); CV death 1.10 (0.82–1.47); MI 1.04 (0.83–1.307); stroke 0.90 (0.79–1.02); HF hospitalization 1.35 (0.94–1.93); ESRD 1.27 (0.93–1.74). Sensitivity estimates for the primary endpoint remained close to unity, HR 0.96–0.99 across the seven alternate approaches.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the matched and sensitivity analyses as supporting similar cardiorenal effectiveness and clinical interchangeability in primary prevention, and argue that agent choice may therefore reasonably incorporate tolerability, cost, access, and formulary constraints.

That interpretation should be bounded by the study design. A source-defined observational equivalence margin does not eliminate residual confounding, and the wider secondary-endpoint CIs do not establish equivalence for those individual outcomes. The paper itself acknowledges the absence of head-to-head randomized evidence.

Limitations, funding, and availability

Key source-stated limitations are non-randomized allocation; missing diabetes duration, HbA1c, UACR, LVEF, lifestyle and adherence data; unknown prescribing rationale; channeling bias from preferences/formulary/access; incomplete capture of obesity GLP-1 RA use; claims-based event and cohort misclassification; no quantitative albuminuria; no empagliflozin dose analysis; and uncertain generalizability beyond Korea.

Yuhan Corporation funded the study, while the authors state the sponsor had no role in design, conduct, analysis, interpretation, manuscript preparation, or submission. Authors declared no competing interests. NHIS data are request-access through NHISS. The protocol was approved by Ulsan University Hospital IRB 2023-08-028.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 10/10

Rationale: Directly addresses empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin comparative cardiorenal effectiveness in a population relevant to formulary interchangeability.

Evidence basis: Nationwide active-comparator cohort; primary-prevention T2DM; matched n=96,702.

Limiting factor: Does not address inpatient switching or acute-hospital initiation directly.

Would change score: Direct pragmatic randomized evidence or inpatient-specific comparative data would raise implementation certainty.

Novelty 7/10

Rationale: Adds a large primary-prevention head-to-head real-world comparison where randomized direct evidence is absent.

Evidence basis: Korean NHIS new-user cohort with formal source-defined equivalence analysis.

Limiting factor: Other observational and indirect comparative studies already exist.

Would change score: A randomized direct comparison would materially increase novelty.

Method strength 7/10

Rationale: New-user design, extensive propensity-score methods, balance checks, competing-risk analysis, and multiple sensitivity analyses strengthen internal consistency.

Evidence basis: 1:1 matching, SMD <0.1, Cox ITT, Fine-Gray, IPTW/stabilized IPTW, alternative calipers.

Limiting factor: Non-randomized treatment allocation and important unmeasured clinical variables remain.

Would change score: Randomization, negative controls, richer clinical covariates, and adherence/as-treated analyses would raise the score.

Evidence strength 6/10

Rationale: The primary estimate is precise enough to exclude large differences within the authors’ chosen margin, but observational equivalence remains vulnerable to residual confounding.

Evidence basis: Primary HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.89–1.09); sensitivity estimates 0.96–0.99.

Limiting factor: The 0.86–1.16 margin is source-defined, and secondary endpoints have wider CIs without separate equivalence testing.

Would change score: Head-to-head randomized outcomes with prespecified clinically justified equivalence margins would raise the score.

External validity 6/10

Rationale: The nationwide dataset is broad within Korea but the selected screened primary-prevention cohort may not generalize to other countries, ethnic groups, or higher-risk cardiorenal populations.

Evidence basis: Entire Korean NHIS plus health-screening data.

Limiting factor: Korean population; exclusion of established ASCVD/HF/advanced kidney disease; required recent screening data.

Would change score: Replication in diverse health systems and higher-risk populations would raise the score.

Implementation value 8/10

Rationale: Useful supportive evidence for formulary flexibility when choosing between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin, especially when cost/access drive selection.

Evidence basis: No detected primary or secondary outcome difference; source discusses formulary restrictions and access.

Limiting factor: Does not prove causal pharmacologic equivalence or address acute inpatient conversion logistics.

Would change score: Pragmatic randomized comparative data and inpatient-specific evidence would raise the score.

Principal interpretive constraint: The primary CI lies inside the authors’ equivalence interval, but propensity-score methods can only balance measured covariates. The design therefore supports comparative effectiveness under measured adjustment, not definitive causal therapeutic equivalence.
Best evidence contribution: Very large matched primary-prevention cohort, clinically relevant composite outcome, and unusually broad sensitivity analysis all point to a stable near-null primary comparative estimate.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate. Whole-source article/visual coverage is complete for the supplied packet, but the underlying causal inference remains observational.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this for
Supportive real-world evidence that choosing empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin in lower-risk T2DM is unlikely to create a large difference in the composite cardiorenal outcome measured here.
Do not use this for
Claiming universal pharmacologic interchangeability, dose equivalence, acute inpatient switching safety, or proven equivalence of each secondary endpoint.
Formulary translation
Reasonable as one comparative-evidence component when cost, access, contracting, or formulary simplicity favors one agent, provided agent-specific indications, dosing, contraindications, and patient context are addressed elsewhere.
Read next
Direct comparative observational cohorts/meta-analyses in references 21–28, plus eventual head-to-head pragmatic randomized evidence when available.
