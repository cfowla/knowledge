One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Broad, rapid PCR screening became operationally feasible when the hospital moved to a high-throughput open-channel platform; combined with aggressive infection-prevention measures, this preceded termination of a 29-patient C. auris colonization outbreak without clinical infections.

The hospital began with culture and external testing, then added low-throughput PCR and ultimately house-wide high-throughput PCR. Testing scaled to 11,638 total assays across the three primary in-house methods, while median collection-to-result turnaround fell from 82.1 hours for culture to 37.6 hours for low-throughput PCR and 20.3 hours for high-throughput PCR. Across the outbreak, 5,563 unique patients were tested and 29 colonized patients were identified; none developed associated clinical C. auris infection.

The strongest operational signal is the combination of faster test turnaround, universal admission/weekly screening, enhanced room/equipment decontamination, Contact Plus precautions, and movement restrictions. Environmental/room pathways were plausible: 50% of secondary transmission was attributed to rooms previously occupied by colonized patients, and hospital-acquired cases had more bed moves than a contemporaneous comparison sample. Genomic sequencing of five outbreak isolates showed a tight Clade I cluster (4-9 SNPs), supporting recent clonal spread. The major constraint is causal identification: multiple interventions changed together, the outbreak had no contemporaneous control, and PCR-only detections sometimes lacked culture confirmation/viability data.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame C. auris as a persistent healthcare-transmission problem for which culture-based screening can be too slow for operational containment. They position molecular detection as a way to shorten turnaround but emphasize that capacity becomes a limiting factor when surveillance expands beyond targeted contacts.

The report's purpose is to describe Washington State's early hospital outbreak experience and the real-time escalation from culture to low-throughput and then high-throughput PCR, together with evolving IPC measures.

Materials and Methods

Harborview Medical Center is described as a 500-bed public, Level I trauma/burn hospital with predominantly double-occupancy rooms and several congregate care areas. Colonization was any positive culture/PCR from skin, nares, respiratory, or urinary sites without clinical infection. Culture used chromogenic agar/MALDI-TOF; PCR used ITS2-targeting laboratory-developed tests on BD MAX and cobas 8800 open channels. Available isolates underwent whole-genome sequencing.

IPC included Contact Plus precautions, EHR-supported contact tracing, enhanced equipment cleaning, dual terminal cleaning and UV-C, ATP verification, and later movement restrictions. The packet does not include the supplementary methodological detail cited for culture/PCR validation, sequencing/bioinformatics, or some IPC procedures.

Results — outbreak detection and testing escalation

The first hospital-onset colonization triggered unit closure, decontamination, and contact screening. Culture produced 1,181 tests with median turnaround 82.1 h; low-throughput PCR added 1,539 tests with median turnaround 37.6 h. Continued transmission and the operational burden of contact tracing led to house-wide screening and high-throughput PCR by week 10.

From day 67 through day 163, 8,918 high-throughput assays were performed, median turnaround was 20.3 h, and 13 new colonized individuals were found. Across six months, 5,563 unique patients were tested and 29 colonized patients were identified; no associated clinical infections occurred. Enhanced decontamination was followed by no further transmission from known colonized patients, but several measures changed concurrently.

Results — transmission patterns and genomics

Room occupancy and movement emerged as important epidemiologic signals. Hospital-acquired cases averaged 2.72 inpatient bed moves before first positivity versus 1.17 in a random sample of 30 contemporaneous patients (p=0.007). Half of secondary transmission was attributed to rooms previously occupied by colonized patients.

Five outbreak isolates were Clade I and clustered within 4-9 SNPs. A pre-outbreak Clade I isolate was about 200 SNPs away, arguing against a common source with that historical isolate. All tested isolates were fluconazole resistant above 64 µg/mL, with resistance-associated mutations differing by clade.

Discussion and limitations

The authors argue that rapid, high-capacity PCR and rigorous IPC were synergistic and necessary for containment. They also report substantial implementation cost: $310,000 in reagents and $351,000 including labor, with low-throughput PCR having the highest per-test cost.

Key limitations are reduced culture availability for sequencing, uncertain viability of many PCR-only positives, absence of environmental cultures, uncertain transportability of the amount of screening required, and inability to separate the effects of interventions introduced together. The report therefore supports an implementation strategy and biologically coherent outbreak narrative more strongly than a causal estimate of any one intervention.

Validity

Strengths include complete outbreak chronology, large testing volume, platform-level operational data, room/bed-space investigation, and whole-genome sequencing. The main threats are before/after confounding, changing surveillance intensity (which changes ascertainment), lack of a contemporaneous control, and multi-component intervention bundling. The absence of symptomatic infection is clinically favorable but cannot establish what fraction of detected colonization would otherwise have progressed.

What would raise confidence

Access to the supplementary assay/sequence material, externally replicated outbreak series using a prespecified containment protocol, quasi-experimental comparison across units/hospitals, and cost-effectiveness analysis with avoided transmissions and downstream clinical outcomes.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
For outbreak-planning concepts: surveillance capacity, turnaround-time targets, EHR order-set integration, room/equipment decontamination, and movement controls.
Do not overclaim
Do not infer that universal PCR alone caused containment, or that the same testing volume is required at every hospital.
Monitor
Testing capacity, collection-to-result time, compliance, room/bed-space exposures, culture recovery for genomics, and full program cost.
