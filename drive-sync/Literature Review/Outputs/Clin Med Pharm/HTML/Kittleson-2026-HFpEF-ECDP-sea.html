Management of Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction: 2026 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway

Summary, evaluation, and appraisal | DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.06.018 | 2026 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway

Bottom line

This 2026 ACC pathway is a high-implementation-value update for HFpEF. Its clearest practice changes are a more explicit diagnostic workflow, systematic exclusion of mimics, use of SGLT2 inhibitors plus a nonsteroidal MRA as the core of optimal medical therapy, phenotype-directed incretin therapy for obesity, and a cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic approach to comorbidity management.

The evidence base is not uniform. SGLT2 inhibitor and finerenone recommendations rest on large randomized trials. Incretin therapy has strong symptom, functional, and weight-loss evidence but fewer hard HF events. ARNI advice depends on subgroup signals. Several AF, blood-pressure, revascularization, and device statements rely on observational, post hoc, or mixed evidence. The document itself is expert consensus, not a formally graded clinical practice guideline.

How the pathway was developed

ACC describes ECDPs as focused consensus documents for questions where clinicians need practical guidance and existing evidence or guidelines do not fully answer the problem. The committee reviews key studies, guidelines, and consensus statements, develops the decision process iteratively, resolves disagreement by committee consensus, then undergoes formal peer review, public comment, and ACC approval.

This is an important appraisal boundary. The pathway is not a comprehensive systematic review and does not apply a recommendation class and level-of-evidence system to each statement. It explicitly preserves clinician judgment and shared decision-making.

Diagnosis

No single confirmatory test

The document starts from a practical problem: HFpEF has no pathognomonic echocardiographic finding, and natriuretic peptides can be normal, especially with obesity. The pathway therefore combines pretest probability, clinical scoring, echocardiography, natriuretic peptides, and active exclusion of alternative causes.

Diagnostic-score comparison

ScoreBasisBest use described by the pathwayMain limitation
H2FPEFSix accessible variables; derived and validated against invasive exercise hemodynamics.General or specialist cardiology, inpatient or outpatient settings.May not fully separate obesity-related symptoms from HFpEF.
HFA-PEFFExpert-consensus algorithm with pretest assessment, echo and natriuretic-peptide scoring, functional testing, and etiologic assessment.Specialist cardiology.More complex. Stress echo and invasive hemodynamics are often impractical.
HFpEF-ABAAge, BMI, and atrial fibrillation.Primary care and EMR population screening.Needs further validation, an online calculator, and has no established action thresholds.

The source describes H2FPEF as more sensitive and HFA-PEFF as more specific. It proposes H2FPEF screening followed by HFA-PEFF confirmation as one practical strategy. A low score does not end the workup when symptoms and signs create high pretest probability.

Natriuretic peptides. Normal values alone do not exclude HFpEF. The document notes lower values with HFpEF and obesity and higher values with CKD, and it cites patients with invasively elevated filling pressures despite values below usual HF thresholds.

Sex-specific findings

The pathway summarizes differences in risk factors, concentric remodeling, biomarkers, and exercise hemodynamics in women and men. Women have more dyspnea and worse quality of life, lower peak oxygen consumption, and different adiposity and vascular patterns. The committee is explicit that these differences do not yet support a defined sex-specific HFpEF treatment strategy.

Nonpharmacologic treatment and devices

Exercise and weight loss

Three to four months of exercise training generally improves peak oxygen consumption, functional capacity, and quality of life. The source does not claim a reduction in HF hospitalization or death. Longer follow-up is inconsistent, and adherence is a major constraint. The document calls the absence of U.S. cardiac-rehabilitation coverage for HFpEF a care gap.

Caloric restriction and aerobic exercise can produce additive short-term gains in obesity-related HFpEF. The durability problem is substantial. In one follow-up, weight regained after the supervised intervention was predominantly fat, and prior functional gains diminished. The pathway carefully separates planned weight reduction from unintentional loss due to frailty or cachexia.

Pulmonary artery pressure monitoring

The evidence is mixed but potentially useful in selected patients. CHAMPION showed a large hospitalization reduction in the preserved-EF subgroup, but the pathway notes a contact-bias concern. GUIDE-HF was neutral overall, with a pre-COVID signal. MONITOR-HF improved quality of life and hospitalization. The committee suggests considering monitoring for patients with prior HF hospitalization and persistent NYHA class III symptoms despite optimal therapy, labile volume, cardiorenal syndrome, or difficult attribution of dyspnea.

Other devices

Splanchnic nerve ablation and interatrial shunting have not shown a clear overall benefit. Atrial shunting may increase cardiovascular events, although selected responder analyses remain hypothesis-generating. Pacing results conflict. Exercise-triggered rate-adaptive pacing did not improve peak oxygen consumption or quality of life, while a personalized higher resting-rate strategy in patients with existing pacemakers improved several surrogate and patient-centered measures.

Comorbidities and the CKM framework

The 2026 update treats HFpEF as part of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic disease rather than as an isolated cardiac disorder. That framing is clinically useful because many therapies have overlapping benefits.

Appraisal

Clinical relevance: very highThe document addresses current HFpEF diagnosis and therapy and is structured for point-of-care use.
Evidence recency: highIt incorporates evidence through mid-2026, including finerenone and incretin data unavailable to the 2023 ECDP.
Method transparency: moderateThe ECDP development process, peer review, public comment, and RWI governance are described, but the document is not a systematic review with recommendation-specific evidence grading.
Evidence strength: mixed by topicSGLT2 and finerenone evidence is strong. ARNI subgroup advice, some AF and blood-pressure conclusions, and several device strategies are less secure.
Implementation value: very highThe diagnostic scores, mimic workup, dose table, cautions, and treatment algorithms translate directly into clinical workflow.
Generalizability: moderate to highThe pathway is broad but mainly ambulatory and U.S.-oriented. Access, reimbursement, invasive testing, and specialty resources vary.

Where confidence is highest

  • Do not diagnose HFpEF from preserved EF alone. Use symptoms, objective HF evidence, probability scoring, and a deliberate search for mimics.
  • SGLT2 inhibitors reduce HF events across preserved or mildly reduced EF populations and improve health status.
  • Finerenone adds randomized outcome evidence for LVEF at least 40% and is a major reason the 2026 pathway changed.
  • Obesity is central to HFpEF phenotype and management, not a peripheral comorbidity.

Where the source asks the reader to tolerate uncertainty

  • HFpEF-ABA action thresholds are not established.
  • Sex differences are real, but sex-specific treatment is not defined.
  • Incretin therapy has convincing symptom, function, and weight effects, while hard HF event certainty is still developing.
  • Sacubitril-valsartan advice depends on subgroup findings rather than an overall positive PARAGON-HF primary endpoint.
  • AF rhythm-control, HFpEF blood-pressure range, and revascularization statements include observational or secondary-analysis evidence.
  • Most device therapies remain selective or investigational rather than routine.

Source-integrity and version findings

Figure numbering mismatch. Supplemental Table S1 refers to a new Figure 9 for the CKM benefit matrix. The supplied main proof contains Figures 1 through 8 and labels that matrix as Figure 8.
Proof pagination. The citation line in the supplied main PDF still uses placeholder volume and page fields. This appraisal does not invent final bibliographic pagination.
GLP-1 terminology. Some comorbidity paragraphs use the phrase "GLP-1 receptor antagonists," while the treatment section describes GLP-1 receptor agonist or incretin-based therapy and Figure 7 uses GLP-1RA. This internal inconsistency is flagged rather than corrected from outside sources.

Practice-ready pathway summary

  1. Identify compatible symptoms and signs. Confirm that the universal HF definition is met rather than relying on EF alone.
  2. Estimate probability with a diagnostic score appropriate to the setting. Do not use a normal natriuretic peptide value as a stand-alone rule-out test.
  3. Actively evaluate pulmonary, kidney, high-output, obesity or frailty causes and distinct cardiac diseases before calling the syndrome HFpEF.
  4. Start optimal medical therapy around an SGLT2 inhibitor plus nonsteroidal MRA when not contraindicated. Add diuretics for congestion and phenotype-directed therapy for obesity, lower-range preserved EF, hypertension, or other indications.
  5. Treat CAD, AF, hypertension, CKD, diabetes, obesity, and sleep-related or other comorbidities as part of the HFpEF disease burden.
  6. Use exercise and sustained weight-management strategies. Reserve invasive monitoring or device approaches for selected patients and experienced programs.
