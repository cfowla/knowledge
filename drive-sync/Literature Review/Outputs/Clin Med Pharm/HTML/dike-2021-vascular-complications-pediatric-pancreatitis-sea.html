Scope warning: This descriptive case series can characterize observed frequency and case patterns in the INSPPIRE ARP/CP cohort. It does not establish a preferred imaging modality, prove risk factors, or establish the efficacy or safety of anticoagulation for pediatric pancreatitis-associated thrombosis.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Peripancreatic vascular complications were uncommon in this pediatric ARP/CP registry: 5 of 410 children with recorded imaging (1.2%) had a vascular event. Four had venous thrombosis and one had a left gastric artery pseudoaneurysm. Four of the five events occurred during moderately severe or severe acute pancreatitis, and the splenic vein was the most frequently involved vessel.

Treatment signal. Two venous-thrombosis patients received enoxaparin and one recanalized; among two venous-thrombosis patients described in the abstract as receiving no anticoagulant, one recanalized. No bleeding or systemic adverse effects were reported in the two enoxaparin-treated children. This is too small a denominator to support comparative treatment conclusions. The pseudoaneurysm was treated with coiling.

Complication pattern. None of the five cases required ICU care or had pancreatic necrosis; one had organ failure and three had pseudocysts. One child developed new diabetes five years later, while another had preexisting diabetes and EPI. All five cases were female, but the paper treats this as an unexplained observation rather than an established risk factor.

Internal source-integrity issue. The source alternates between “vascular complications” and “PSMVT” when describing the 1.2% frequency. Because one of five events was an arterial pseudoaneurysm, 1.2% corresponds to all vascular complications, not strictly to the four venous thromboses. The abstract and Results/Table 2 also differ in how the non-anticoagulated denominator is expressed. These discrepancies are retained rather than harmonized.

Section condensation

Introduction

The manuscript frames portosplenomesenteric venous thrombosis and pseudoaneurysm as recognized complications of pancreatitis in adults, often in more severe disease, while pediatric evidence consisted largely of isolated reports. It highlights uncertainty about anticoagulation and the absence of a defined pediatric incidence, natural history, or risk-factor profile.

The study objective was therefore descriptive: use INSPPIRE to estimate frequency and characterize risk factors and outcomes in children with ARP or CP who developed peripancreatic vascular complications.

Methods

Children aged 19 years or younger with ARP or CP were enrolled at participating INSPPIRE centers. Vascular complications were identified from imaging reviewed by pediatric radiologists at each site. Standardized patient and physician questionnaires were entered in REDCap from March 2012 through February 2017.

Because organ-failure duration was not collected, moderately severe and severe acute pancreatitis could not be separated and were combined. Recanalization was the primary outcome. Secondary outcomes included ICU stay, pancreatic necrosis or pseudocyst, organ failure, EPI, and diabetes. Baseline ultrasound results were not collected, creating an ascertainment limitation.

Results

The registry contained 441 children (58% ARP, 42% CP). At least one imaging study was recorded in 410. Five children had a vascular complication (1.2%): four venous thromboses and one left gastric artery pseudoaneurysm. Four events occurred during moderately severe or severe acute pancreatitis and one during a mild episode. All five cases were female.

Two thrombosis patients received enoxaparin and one recanalized; the abstract reports one recanalization among two venous-thrombosis patients without anticoagulation. No anticoagulant-associated bleeding or systemic effects were observed. Across the five cases there were no ICU stays or pancreatic necrosis, one organ failure, three pseudocysts, one later new-onset diabetes case, and one child with preexisting diabetes/EPI.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the observed events as rare and linked to greater pancreatitis severity, with splenic-vein involvement most common. Female sex, PRSS1 variants, and hypertriglyceridemia are presented as signals requiring further study rather than proven risk factors. The authors also discuss uncertainty about whether CT, MRI, or Doppler ultrasonography is preferable for vascular-complication detection.

Key limitations are retrospective data collection, incomplete genetic testing, inability to distinguish moderately severe from severe AP, exclusion of children with a single AP episode without CP, incomplete baseline ultrasound ascertainment, and only five vascular events. These limitations are especially consequential for the anticoagulation conclusion: the source supports only a small descriptive safety observation, not treatment efficacy.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used as a pediatric signal-generating case series and frequency estimate for ARP/CP, not as a treatment-guiding anticoagulation study.
7/10

Relevance

Directly relevant to pediatric pancreatitis vascular complications, but the clinical question is narrow.

Evidence basis: Multicenter pediatric ARP/CP cohort with explicit vascular-event outcomes.

Limiting factor: Niche population and rare event.

Would raise score: Broader pediatric acute-pancreatitis inclusion and a larger event count.

8/10

Novelty

The paper provides an early cohort-level estimate rather than another isolated pediatric case report.

Evidence basis: Authors report the first incidence estimate in a defined pediatric ARP/CP cohort.

Limiting factor: Only five events limit what the novelty can establish.

Would raise score: Independent replication with systematic vascular imaging.

4/10

Method strength

The registry provides a defined multicenter cohort, but the analysis is retrospective and largely descriptive.

Evidence basis: Standardized INSPPIRE data collection and site radiology review.

Limiting factor: Incomplete baseline imaging data, no thrombophilia screening, incomplete genetics, and no inferential comparison.

Would raise score: Prospective standardized imaging, complete covariates, and prespecified analysis.

4/10

Evidence strength

The 1.2% frequency estimate is useful, but treatment and safety conclusions rest on two anticoagulated patients.

Evidence basis: 410 imaged children and case-level outcome reporting.

Limiting factor: Very small event denominator and no randomized or adjusted treatment comparison.

Would raise score: Substantially larger prospective event series or comparative study.

4/10

External validity

INSPPIRE is multicenter, but it enrolls ARP/CP rather than all pediatric acute pancreatitis and excludes isolated AP without CP.

Evidence basis: Multiple pediatric centers and heterogeneous clinical imaging.

Limiting factor: Selected disease spectrum plus incomplete baseline ultrasound ascertainment.

Would raise score: Population-based or all-comer pediatric AP cohorts with standardized imaging.

4/10

Implementation value

The study supports awareness of rare vascular complications but cannot choose an imaging strategy or anticoagulation policy.

Evidence basis: Detailed lesion, treatment, and outcome descriptions.

Limiting factor: No dose/duration framework, no comparative effectiveness, and insufficient safety denominator.

Would raise score: Prospective management data with treatment criteria, dosing, duration, and clinically meaningful outcomes.

Validity

The multicenter registry and explicit case-level tables are strengths, but the analysis is descriptive, retrospective, and constrained by only five vascular events. Imaging was clinically obtained rather than protocolized, baseline ultrasound was incompletely captured, thrombophilia screening was absent, and genetic testing was incomplete. No adjusted analysis can meaningfully separate candidate risk factors from chance or disease severity.

Practice translation

The paper supports clinical awareness that vascular events can occur in pediatric ARP/CP and may cluster with more severe inflammatory episodes. It does not support routine screening of all patients with a specific modality, nor does it establish when to initiate anticoagulation, how long to treat, or whether anticoagulation improves recanalization or long-term outcomes. The anticoagulant safety statement is based on two treated children.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Keep splanchnic thrombosis and pseudoaneurysm on the complication differential in pediatric ARP/CP, particularly during more severe attacks.

Do not overclaim

Do not convert 0 adverse events in 2 enoxaparin-treated children into a reliable safety rate or infer anticoagulation efficacy from the recanalization counts.

Research need

Prospective standardized imaging and larger comparative cohorts are needed to quantify incidence, identify risk factors, and evaluate anticoagulation.
