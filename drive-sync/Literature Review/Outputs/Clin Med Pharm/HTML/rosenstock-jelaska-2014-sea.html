One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. High value for the specific question of adding empagliflozin to high-dose MDI insulin; secondary value for broader SGLT2 formulary reasoning. It should not carry the argument for current inpatient equivalence.

The trial randomized 563 obese adults with inadequately controlled T2DM despite MDI insulin to placebo, empagliflozin 10 mg, or empagliflozin 25 mg. Insulin was held approximately stable for the first 18 weeks, titrated to glucose targets during weeks 19–40, and then stabilized again. At week 18, HbA1c fell by 0.50 percentage points with placebo, 0.94 with 10 mg, and 1.02 with 25 mg; adjusted differences versus placebo were −0.44 and −0.52 percentage points, both P<0.001. By week 52, empagliflozin groups maintained greater HbA1c reductions while requiring substantially less insulin intensification and losing about 2 kg versus slight weight gain with placebo.

Confirmed hypoglycemic events over 52 weeks were similar (58.0%, 51.1%, 57.7%); UTI-category events were also similar (15.4%, 15.6%, 15.3%). Genital-infection-category events increased dose-dependently (1.6%, 4.3%, 9.5%). The main interpretive constraint is that insulin titration was investigator-directed rather than independently enforced, and week-52 efficacy analyses used a per-protocol completer set. The narrow obese/high-dose-insulin/eGFR≥60 population further limits generalization.

Section condensation

Background and objective

The authors framed intensive insulin therapy as effective but difficult to optimize because of hypoglycemia, weight gain, treatment complexity, high insulin requirements, and insulin resistance. Because empagliflozin lowers glucose through renal glucose excretion independently of β-cell function, the study tested whether it could improve glycemic control and weight when added to MDI insulin without exacerbating hypoglycemia.

Methods

Adults with BMI 30–45 kg/m², HbA1c 7.5–10.0%, and stable MDI insulin >60 IU/day were randomized 1:1:1 after a placebo run-in. eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m² was excluded. The primary endpoint was HbA1c change at week 18; key week-52 secondary endpoints were insulin dose, body weight, and HbA1c. ANCOVA and hierarchical testing were prespecified; rescue-era data were generally handled with LOCF. Week-52 key efficacy analyses used PPS-completers-52.

Results

475/563 (84%) completed 52 weeks. At week 18, adjusted HbA1c changes were −0.50%, −0.94%, and −1.02%; empagliflozin-placebo differences were −0.44 (95% CI −0.59 to −0.29) and −0.52 (−0.67 to −0.37), both P<0.001. FPG and body weight also improved. Rescue therapy by week 18 was used in 8.0%, 1.6%, and 2.6%.

At week 52, insulin-dose changes were +10.2, +1.3, and −1.1 IU/day; differences versus placebo were −8.8 IU/day (P=0.004) and −11.2 IU/day (P<0.001). HbA1c changes were −0.81%, −1.18%, and −1.27%, while weight changed +0.44, −1.95, and −2.04 kg. HbA1c <7% was reached by 21.0%, 31.4%, and 41.7% among those starting ≥7%.

Safety

Overall AE and serious-AE rates were similar. Confirmed hypoglycemia was 58.0%, 51.1%, and 57.7%; severe hypoglycemia was 1.6%, 1.6%, and 0.5%. UTI-category events were 15.4%, 15.6%, and 15.3%, while genital-infection-category events were 1.6%, 4.3%, and 9.5%. One death from metastatic lung cancer occurred in the 25-mg group.

Hematocrit increased by about 4 percentage points with empagliflozin and uric acid fell. Small eGFR declines during treatment returned toward baseline after stopping. Electrolytes were unchanged and no major lipid differences were reported.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpreted the combination as enabling better glycemic control and weight loss with lower insulin requirements without an overall increase in hypoglycemia. They acknowledged that investigator-discretion insulin titration without an independent monitoring committee prevented a full assessment of the insulin-dose effect, and that conclusions were restricted to the selected study population.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 7/10

Directly informs empagliflozin efficacy/safety when added to intensive insulin, but not dapagliflozin interchangeability, inpatient use, or cardiorenal outcomes.

Evidence basis: Randomized add-on trial in difficult-to-control insulin-treated T2DM.

Limiting factor: Narrow question fit.

What changes the score: A direct empagliflozin–dapagliflozin or inpatient comparative study would raise this.

Novelty 7/10

For 2014, a dedicated SGLT2-inhibitor trial in high-dose MDI insulin was a distinct clinical setting.

Evidence basis: Authors describe it as the first dedicated trial in this population.

Limiting factor: Novelty is historical and mechanism/class evidence is now mature.

What changes the score: Contemporary head-to-head or implementation evidence would raise current novelty.

Method strength 7/10

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicountry design with prespecified endpoints and multiplicity control.

Evidence basis: 104 centers, 14 countries, 1:1:1 randomization, ANCOVA/hierarchical testing.

Limiting factor: Investigator-directed insulin titration and week-52 PPS-completer analyses weaken robustness.

What changes the score: Independent insulin titration oversight and full ITT week-52 estimands would raise the score.

Evidence strength 7/10

HbA1c, weight, insulin-dose, FPG, and safety estimates are quantitatively reported with uncertainty.

Evidence basis: Primary HbA1c differences had 95% CIs and P<0.001; safety denominators are explicit.

Limiting factor: Outcomes are mainly surrogate/metabolic, and 84% completed 52 weeks.

What changes the score: Patient-centered outcomes and modern estimands for missing data would raise the score.

External validity 5/10

The multinational sample helps, but enrollment was restricted to obese, high-dose MDI-insulin users with eGFR ≥60.

Evidence basis: BMI 30–45 kg/m², insulin >60 IU/day, 104 centers/14 countries.

Limiting factor: Not generalizable to broad T2DM, lower insulin doses, CKD below the trial threshold, or inpatient populations.

What changes the score: Broader renal function, BMI, insulin regimens, and pragmatic settings would raise the score.

Implementation value 5/10

Provides concrete doses, insulin-titration context, and adverse-event rates, but does not establish present-day formulary equivalence.

Evidence basis: 10/25 mg dosing, 52-week outcomes, hypoglycemia/UTI/genital-infection data.

Limiting factor: 2014 evidence and no direct dapagliflozin comparison or inpatient workflow outcomes.

What changes the score: Current head-to-head, cardiorenal, and inpatient comparative evidence would raise the score.

Main strengths

  • Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with multicountry enrollment.
  • Insulin-stable first 18 weeks isolates the glycemic effect of empagliflozin more cleanly than continuous free titration.
  • Effect estimates, confidence intervals, denominators, and major safety categories are explicit.
  • 52-week follow-up provides longer metabolic and safety context than short glycemic trials.

Main limitations

  • Insulin titration was investigator-directed and not independently monitored.
  • Week-52 key efficacy analyses used PPS-completers-52 rather than a purely intention-to-treat estimand.
  • 84% completed 52 weeks; LOCF was used in important efficacy analyses.
  • Population was selected: obese, high-dose-MDI users with eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m².
  • Industry funding and multiple author sponsor/employment relationships require attention to interpretive framing.
  • No direct comparison with dapagliflozin and no cardiovascular/renal outcome endpoint.
