One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In 187 patients with previously treated advanced IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma, ivosidenib 500 mg once daily produced a numerically longer final overall survival than placebo despite 70% placebo-to-ivosidenib crossover. The prespecified crossover-adjusted analysis showed a substantially larger survival effect. Safety was generally manageable and selected quality-of-life domains favored ivosidenib, but crossover and QOL attrition materially constrain interpretation.

Patients were randomized 2:1 to ivosidenib (n=126) or placebo (n=61) at 49 hospitals in six countries. The trial's primary endpoint, progression-free survival by blinded independent radiology review, had already been met at an earlier cutoff (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25-0.54; 1-sided P<.001). In the final OS analysis based on 150 deaths, median OS was 10.3 months with ivosidenib versus 7.5 months with placebo (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.56-1.12; 1-sided P=.09). Because 43 of 61 placebo-assigned patients crossed over, the prespecified rank-preserving structural failure time model estimated placebo median OS at 5.1 months and an adjusted HR of 0.49 (95% CI 0.34-0.70; 1-sided P<.001). These analyses answer related but different questions: the ITT estimate preserves randomization but is diluted by crossover, while the RPSFT estimate depends on modeling assumptions.

Safety did not show a large discontinuation penalty: study-drug discontinuation due to treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 7% with ivosidenib and 8% with placebo. Nausea was common (42% vs 29%); grade ≥3 ascites occurred in 9% vs 7%; QT prolongation occurred in 10% vs 3%. Six ivosidenib-treated patients had TEAEs leading to death, but none was judged treatment-related. Physical functioning was better preserved with ivosidenib at cycle 2 day 1 (difference 11.0 points, 95% CI 4.2-17.7; P=.002) and cycle 3 day 1 (12.3 points, 95% CI 3.9-20.8; P=.004), although later QOL comparisons involved small samples and exploratory P values were not multiplicity-adjusted.

Best use: High-value evidence for the efficacy/safety profile of targeted IDH1 inhibition in a biomarker-selected, previously treated cholangiocarcinoma population, especially when interpreting the relationship between PFS benefit, crossover, and final OS.
Do not overclaim: The unadjusted final OS comparison was not statistically significant; the stronger crossover-adjusted estimate relies on RPSFT assumptions. Subgroup estimates are small and imprecise in several strata, and QOL results are vulnerable to attrition and multiplicity.

Section condensation

Background and rationale

Advanced cholangiocarcinoma has poor survival and limited later-line options. IDH1 mutations define a molecular subset, especially in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. Ivosidenib is an oral mutant-IDH1 inhibitor with prior phase 1 activity and favorable tolerability. ClarIDHy was designed to test whether targeted IDH1 inhibition could improve outcomes in chemotherapy-refractory disease.

The earlier ClarIDHy analysis met its primary PFS endpoint (independent review HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25-0.54) with low objective response but substantial stable disease. The 2021 article reports mature final OS, updated safety, QOL, and baseline comutation analyses.

Methods

The trial enrolled 187 patients across 49 hospitals in France, Italy, South Korea, Spain, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Randomization was 2:1 with block size 6 and stratification by 1 versus 2 prior systemic regimens. Radiographic response was assessed every 6 weeks through week 48 and every 8 weeks thereafter. The independent monitoring board reviewed safety.

OS was the first key secondary endpoint and was analyzed after 150 deaths. The ITT analysis retained original assignment despite crossover; RPSFT estimated the counterfactual placebo survival that might have occurred without switching under a common-treatment-effect assumption. QOL focused on physical functioning, pain, and appetite loss, with supplementary mixed-effects analyses at cycle 2 and cycle 3.

Results: efficacy

Of 187 randomized patients, 93% had metastatic disease and 47% had received two prior lines of therapy. Forty-three placebo patients (70%) crossed over. Final median OS was 10.3 months with ivosidenib and 7.5 months with placebo (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.56-1.12; 1-sided P=.09). RPSFT-adjusted placebo median OS was 5.1 months with an adjusted HR of 0.49 (95% CI 0.34-0.70; 1-sided P<.001). Twelve-month survival was 43% versus 36%.

Median treatment duration was 2.8 months for ivosidenib and 1.6 months for placebo before crossover; 25 of 166 total patients exposed to ivosidenib remained on treatment at least one year. Subgroup HRs were directionally similar overall but several strata were very small, including extrahepatic disease, locally advanced disease, and Asia.

Results: safety and quality of life

Nausea was the most common all-grade TEAE (42% vs 29%). Grade ≥3 ascites was 9% vs 7%; anemia 7% vs 0%; increased bilirubin 6% vs 2%; hyponatremia 6% vs 10%. Serious TEAEs occurred in 34% of ivosidenib-treated patients and 24% of placebo-treated patients; only 3 ivosidenib patients (2%) had serious TEAEs considered treatment-related. QT prolongation was 10% vs 3%. No treatment-related deaths were reported.

At cycle 2 day 1, physical functioning favored ivosidenib by 11.0 points and pain by -10.4 points; physical-function benefit persisted at cycle 3 day 1. Exploratory cycle 2 findings also favored ivosidenib on emotional and cognitive functioning, dyspnea, anxiety, and tiredness. Interpretation is limited by rapid attrition, missing assessments, and no multiplicity adjustment for exploratory QOL comparisons.

Biomarkers and discussion

The most common baseline oncogenic comutations were PIK3CA, KRAS, BRAF, and FGFR2. No single-gene baseline comutation was significantly associated with OS, PFS, or treatment duration. Post-treatment 2-HG suppression provided pharmacodynamic support for target inhibition, and treatment duration appeared longer among patients with post-cycle-1 2-HG below 100 ng/mL.

The authors interpret the combined PFS, crossover-adjusted OS, tolerability, and QOL results as demonstrating clinical benefit. They explicitly acknowledge that crossover confounded the primary OS treatment-effect estimate, QOL sample sizes became small, and lack of on-study biopsies limited resistance analyses.

Supplement 1: protocol and statistical plan

The 137-page supplement documents the original protocol and amendments, eligibility framework, treatment schedule, crossover rules, assessment schedule, sample-size assumptions, analysis populations, and survival/QOL analysis plans. It confirms that approximately 186 participants were planned in a 2:1 allocation and that mature OS was planned after about 150 events.

The protocol/SAP strengthens reproducibility of the reported methods, particularly crossover handling and analysis timing. It also shows that crossover was an intentional design feature rather than a post hoc response to trial results.

Supplement 2: eMethods, eResults, and supporting visuals

The 20-page supplement expands RPSFT methodology, crossover eligibility, pharmacodynamic sampling, QOL modeling, missing-QOL reasons, subgroup OS analyses, safety tables, and comutation data. The subgroup forest plot does not establish heterogeneity because no interaction tests are shown; several subgroup confidence intervals are wide.

Supplementary QOL figures show the broadest nominal differences at cycle 2, with far fewer evaluable patients by cycle 3. The eTables quantify assessment completion and reinforce that QOL findings should be interpreted as supportive rather than definitive.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a pivotal randomized biomarker-selected trial with a clearly positive primary PFS endpoint and clinically relevant final OS/safety/QOL follow-up. The main interpretive burden is the high crossover rate, which weakens the unadjusted OS comparison and makes the adjusted estimate model-dependent.

Relevance

Directly addresses targeted therapy in advanced IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma.

Evidence: randomized phase 3 efficacy, survival, safety, QOL. Limit: source is 2021 and not a current-practice verification. Would rise: current guideline/regulatory integration.

Novelty

Establishes randomized phase 3 evidence for mutant-IDH1 inhibition in this disease.

Evidence: biomarker-selected placebo-controlled design. Limit: primary PFS had been published previously. Would rise: novel head-to-head or resistance-guided strategy.

Method strength

Strong randomization, blinding, central molecular confirmation, blinded PFS review, prespecified OS hierarchy, and protocol/SAP access.

Evidence strength

PFS benefit is robust; final ITT OS is directionally favorable but nonsignificant, while RPSFT-adjusted OS is favorable but assumption-dependent.

Limit: 70% crossover and QOL attrition. Would rise: convergent survival evidence from independent trials/real-world causal analyses.

7/10
External validity

Multinational, 49-site enrollment and inclusion of second-/third-line disease support applicability to fit advanced-disease patients.

Limit: molecularly selected rare population, mostly intrahepatic disease, ECOG largely 0-1. Would rise: broader performance-status and geographic representation.

8/10
Implementation value

Provides a clear dose, molecular eligibility concept, toxicity profile, and outcome expectations.

Limit: no current 2026 sequencing comparison in this packet. Would rise: contemporary comparative and sequencing data.

Validity and bias

  • Randomization/blinding: appropriate and well described; baseline arms were broadly balanced.
  • Endpoint hierarchy: protects type I error for OS testing after positive PFS.
  • Crossover: ethically and operationally useful but severely dilutes ITT OS separation. RPSFT is prespecified and informative, not assumption-free.
  • QOL: supportive but vulnerable to informative attrition; exploratory comparisons were not multiplicity-adjusted.
  • Subgroups: descriptive only; no interaction tests and several tiny strata.
  • Sponsor influence: sponsor participated in design, data analysis, manuscript development, and medical writing; several authors were sponsor employees. This does not invalidate results but increases the importance of protocol/SAP consistency and independent replication.

Clinical interpretation

The strongest randomized claim is delayed progression with ivosidenib. Final OS is more nuanced: observed ITT survival is numerically better but not statistically significant, whereas the adjusted estimate suggests a larger benefit if crossover had not occurred. The safety profile is compatible with sustained oral therapy for many patients, but QT prolongation and hepatobiliary laboratory abnormalities remain clinically relevant monitoring issues.

Practice and implementation takeaways

Use the biomarkerIDH1 mutation status is the treatment-defining eligibility feature in this trial; central molecular confirmation was required.
Keep OS estimands separateReport both ITT HR 0.79 and RPSFT-adjusted HR 0.49; do not present the adjusted estimate as if it were the randomized ITT result.
Monitor tolerabilityRelevant signals include nausea, ascites, bilirubin elevation, hyponatremia, anemia, and QT prolongation.
Use QOL as supportive evidencePhysical-function preservation is consistent with clinical benefit, but later-cycle QOL estimates come from small, selected samples.
Avoid subgroup overinterpretationSeveral subgroup CIs are very wide; nominal differences do not establish treatment-effect heterogeneity.
