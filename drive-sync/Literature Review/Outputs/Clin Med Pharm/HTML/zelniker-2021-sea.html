One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. Best used to understand how baseline CKD risk changes absolute cardiovascular benefit even when relative dapagliflozin effects are broadly similar.

What the source reports

Kidney risk was strongly prognostic. In placebo-treated participants, 4-year CV death/HHF incidence rose from 3.9% with 0 CKD markers to 8.3% with 1 and 17.4% with 2; MACE rose from 7.5% to 11.6% to 18.9%. After multivariable adjustment, the 2-marker group had an adjusted HR of 2.97 for CV death/HHF and 2.00 for MACE versus the 0-marker group.

Relative dapagliflozin effects on CV death/HHF were similar across marker groups (HR 0.87, 0.87, and 0.58; P for interaction=.24), but absolute risk differences widened from −0.5% to −1.0% to −8.3% (P for interaction=.02). The authors estimated an NNT of 13 over 4 years in patients with both eGFR <60 and UACR ≥30 mg/g.

Independent appraisal

The strongest inference is not that kidney dysfunction causes a larger relative dapagliflozin effect; the interaction test for CV death/HHF did not support that. Rather, higher baseline event risk generates greater absolute benefit for a treatment whose relative effect is generally preserved. The dramatic 2-marker absolute difference is clinically notable but rests on only 548 participants and a combined-marker analysis that the authors state was not prespecified.

Safety nuance: The paper describes safety as broadly similar across subgroups, but Table 2 contains a DKA imbalance in the 0-marker group (21 vs 6; P=.009). There was no significant interaction by CKD-marker burden (P=.36), so this does not establish kidney-status effect modification; it should still remain visible rather than be erased by the broad safety summary.

Methods condensation

Study population and treatment

DECLARE‑TIMI 58 randomized 17,160 patients with type 2 diabetes after a run-in phase to dapagliflozin or placebo in addition to standard care. Established-ASCVD participants were at least 40 years old with ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, or peripheral arterial disease; the multiple-risk-factor cohort used sex-specific age thresholds plus dyslipidemia, hypertension, or current smoking. Screening creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min was excluded.

The protocol describes a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3b study with 1:1 allocation to dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily or matching placebo. The final SAP defined the full analysis set by randomization and randomized treatment assignment, supporting the article's intention-to-treat treatment-effect analysis.

Kidney strata, outcomes, and statistics

The analysis classified baseline eGFR as <60 vs ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m² and UACR as <30 vs ≥30 mg/g, then counted 0, 1, or 2 CKD markers. The eGFR and UACR subgroup definitions were prespecified; the combined marker-count analysis was not. Sensitivity analyses used KDIGO risk categories and finer eGFR/UACR strata.

Primary efficacy outcomes were CV death/HHF and MACE; additional outcomes included components and all-cause death, with major hypoglycemia, amputation, DKA, and fracture as safety outcomes. Cox models tested treatment interaction across subgroups; treatment models were stratified by ASCVD and hematuria. Reported P values were two-sided with nominal α=.05 and no multiplicity adjustment for these analyses.

Results condensation

Baseline kidney-risk distribution

At baseline, 1,265 participants (7.4%) had eGFR <60 and 5,199 (30.9%) had albuminuria. Among participants with both measures, 10,958 (65.1%) had 0 markers, 5,336 (31.7%) had 1, and 548 (3.3%) had 2. Greater CKD burden coincided with older age, longer diabetes duration, more ASCVD/HF, more insulin use, and less metformin use, reinforcing that marker count was also a strong baseline-risk classifier.

Efficacy

CV death/HHF relative effects were HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.72–1.06), 0.87 (0.72–1.05), and 0.58 (0.36–0.94) across 0, 1, and 2 markers; P for interaction=.24. Corresponding absolute risk differences were −0.5%, −1.0%, and −8.3%; P for interaction=.02. MACE showed no relative-effect interaction (P=.65 in Figure 2) and a numerically larger absolute reduction in the 2-marker subgroup.

All-cause mortality showed an interaction by marker count (P=.04) and by UACR (P=.007): HR 1.11 for UACR <30, 0.77 for UACR 30–300, and 0.73 for UACR >300 mg/g. In the exploratory 718-person CREDENCE-like subgroup (eGFR 30–<90 with UACR >300), HRs were 0.68 for CV death/HHF and 0.83 for MACE, both with wide confidence intervals.

Risk-factor and safety results

At 6 months, dapagliflozin's placebo-adjusted HbA1c reduction was attenuated at lower eGFR (−0.39 percentage points for eGFR <60 vs −0.70 for eGFR ≥90; P<.001 interaction), while systolic BP, diastolic BP, and BMI changes were more similar across eGFR groups. This supports the authors' argument that cardiorenal benefit is not simply proportional to glucose lowering.

Safety interaction tests did not indicate systematic modification by CKD-marker count for amputation, DKA, fracture, or major hypoglycemia. However, individual cells included DKA 21 vs 6 in the 0-marker group (P=.009) and major hypoglycemia 21 vs 35 in the 1-marker group (P=.04); these nominal subgroup comparisons were not multiplicity-adjusted.

Supplement 1 — protocol and SAP

The protocol confirms the randomized double-blind 1:1 design, dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily vs matching placebo, planned scale, event-adjudication framework, and prespecified subgroup-analysis architecture. The SAP confirms FAS/ITT principles and subgroup Cox interaction testing. Its change log documents addition of eGFR slope/renal composite analyses and moving amputation-related events into the safety section. These materials increase confidence in parent-trial methods but do not make the later combined 0/1/2 CKD-marker analysis prespecified; the article explicitly states that it was not.

Source limitations

  • Subgroup analyses are exploratory and have limited statistical power.
  • No multiplicity adjustment was applied to the numerous reported subgroup comparisons.
  • The combined CKD-marker count analysis was not prespecified.
  • Screening creatinine clearance <60 mL/min was excluded, leaving relatively few participants with low eGFR and only 548 with both reduced eGFR and albuminuria.
  • Several nominal subgroup interactions, especially mortality by UACR, should be treated as hypothesis-generating unless externally replicated.
  • Industry funding and extensive author-industry relationships do not invalidate results but are relevant provenance when appraising interpretation and reporting.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Preserve absolute-risk framing.
Higher-risk kidney phenotypes may derive larger absolute benefit even when relative treatment effects are stable.
Do not convert nonsignificant relative interactions into a stronger subgroup-efficacy claim.
The CV death/HHF HR interaction was P=.24.
Keep severe-CKD generalizability bounded.
The parent trial's screening renal criterion and small 2-marker subgroup matter.
Use the protocol/SAP for provenance.
