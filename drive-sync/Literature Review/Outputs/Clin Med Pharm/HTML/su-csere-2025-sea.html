One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. Strongly relevant for SGLT2 class-effect/formulary reasoning, but the active-agent ranking should be treated as hypothesis-strength comparative evidence rather than a basis for preferring one agent.

The review asks a clinically useful but difficult question: among patients who have both heart failure and type 2 diabetes, what class-level effects do SGLT2 inhibitors have, and do individual agents appear meaningfully different? The authors combined 17 randomized trials containing 17,809 dual-diagnosis participants. Pairwise meta-analysis found lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.78–0.98), all-cause hospitalization (HR 0.74, 0.62–0.88), and hospitalization for heart failure (HR 0.70, 0.63–0.77); cardiovascular death did not reach statistical significance (HR 0.87, 0.76–1.00). Safety/secondary analyses found fewer serious adverse events and hyperkalemia but more hypotension and genital infection; eGFR improved in the supplementary continuous-outcome analysis.

The network analysis is more fragile. Dapagliflozin was the only drug with a statistically significant all-cause mortality reduction versus placebo, while canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, and sotagliflozin all reduced HHF versus placebo and standard of care. Canagliflozin nevertheless ranked first by p-score for ACM, CVD, and HHF. That rank is not equivalent to a significant head-to-head advantage: the authors explicitly note that canagliflozin ranking rests on only two RCTs with 1,543 dual-diagnosis patients, and most network information came from dapagliflozin/empagliflozin/placebo comparisons.

Key interpretation: this source supports a clinically meaningful SGLT2 class effect in HF + T2DM. It does not provide strong direct evidence that canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or empagliflozin should be preferred over one another.

Methods

Design and eligibility

The authors followed PRISMA-NMA, registered the protocol in PROSPERO, searched five bibliographic databases plus preprint/registry sources through April 15, 2025, and used duplicate screening/extraction. Eligible RCTs had HF + T2DM as the main population or a reportable dual-diagnosis subgroup, compared an SGLT2 inhibitor with placebo/standard of care, reported at least one outcome, and provided at least six months of intervention. Conference abstracts, crossover trials, non-human work, and trials against other active diabetes/HF drugs were excluded.

Analysis

Pairwise random-effects models used inverse variance, Paule-Mandel tau², Hartung-Knapp adjustment when at least five RCTs were available, HR as the primary dichotomous measure, RR as secondary, and MD for continuous outcomes. The authors also used prediction intervals and subgroup analyses. Frequentist NMA assessed transitivity from clinical/study characteristics, evaluated direct–indirect consistency, and ranked treatments using p-scores. RoB 2 and GRADE were used for study bias and outcome certainty.

Results

Evidence base

The flow went from 3,201 records to 1,537 after deduplication, 120 full texts assessed, and 75 articles representing 17 unique RCTs. The target evidence base contained 17,809 patients with HF + T2DM. Maximum follow-up ranged 6–78 months. The intervention network included canagliflozin (2 trials), dapagliflozin (6), empagliflozin (6), ertugliflozin (1), ipragliflozin (1), and sotagliflozin (1); 13 trials used placebo and four used standard care.

Canagliflozin had the highest p-scores for ACM (0.86), CVD (0.82), and HHF (0.88); dapagliflozin ranked second for ACM/CVD, and sotagliflozin ranked second for HHF. ACH could not be network-analyzed because of sparse data. Network consistency was reported as good.

Subgroups

Signals varied by EF category and selected study characteristics. ACM benefit was stronger in mixed-EF and HFrEF strata (both HR 0.76) than HFpEF (HR 0.98; interaction p=0.03). Renal dysfunction showed a similar mixed/HFrEF versus HFpEF pattern. The striking HHF estimate in studies including prediabetes (HR 0.15) came from one RCT, limiting inference. Amputation signals in selected strata were also driven by sparse data, including a T2DM-primary-population signal based on CANVAS Program alone.

Source-integrity findings

  • HHF certainty: abstract/Table 2 = low; Results prose = moderate.
  • Hypotension: supplementary Results S1/Figure S5 support HR 1.17 (1.01–1.34), coherent with the stated 17% increase; parsed main-text wording contains an inconsistent 1.71.
  • eGFR: supplement reports +3.00 mL/min/1.73m² (1.71–4.30), while Table 2 shows a conflicting lower-direction value.
  • RR vs HR: authors explicitly attribute several SoF/forest discrepancies to missing event counts in CANVAS Program.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance, 9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses class effect and individual SGLT2 inhibitor comparisons in HF + T2DM, closely matching formulary rationale questions.

Evidence basis: 17-RCT dual-diagnosis evidence base; primary and safety outcomes; individual-drug NMA.

Limiting factor: The decision question is drug selection, while most active-agent comparisons are indirect.

Would raise/lower: Head-to-head pragmatic trials in the same dual-diagnosis population.

Novelty, 7/10

Rationale: Adds a 2025 search, broad outcome set, GRADE, and individual-drug ranking to an already studied SGLT2 evidence base.

Evidence basis: Search through April 15, 2025; 17 RCTs; PMA + NMA + p-score ranking.

Limiting factor: Builds on several prior SGLT2 meta-analyses and networks.

Would raise/lower: New direct active-comparator RCT evidence or validated comparative effectiveness.

Method strength, 7/10

Rationale: Registered protocol, broad search, duplicate screening/extraction, RoB 2, GRADE, random-effects PMA, and formal NMA consistency checks are substantial strengths.

Evidence basis: PROSPERO CRD42022366372; PRISMA-NMA; Paule-Mandel/Hartung-Knapp; prediction intervals; netmeta.

Limiting factor: Subjective transitivity assessment, sparse subnetworks, mixed main-population/subgroup evidence, and outcome modifications.

Would raise/lower: Prospective IPD meta-analysis or direct head-to-head randomized comparisons.

Evidence strength, 6/10

Rationale: Class-level HHF and SAE signals are reasonably consistent, but several outcomes have low/very-low GRADE certainty and comparative drug rankings are indirect and sparse.

Evidence basis: HHF HR 0.70 with I² 2%; SAE HR 0.86 with I² 0%; multiple GRADE downgrades.

Limiting factor: Canagliflozin rank driven by only two RCTs/1,543 target patients; internal source discrepancies for HHF certainty and eGFR.

Would raise/lower: More precise, direct comparative trials with harmonized endpoints.

External validity, 6/10

Rationale: The evidence spans diverse RCT settings and EF categories, but much of the target population comes from subgroup extraction rather than trials designed for HF + T2DM.

Evidence basis: 17 RCTs, 17,809 dual-diagnosis participants, follow-up 6–78 months.

Limiting factor: Target subgroup is much smaller than the total 65,380 trial population; heterogeneity in HF severity, definitions, and concomitant therapy.

Would raise/lower: Dedicated multicenter HF + T2DM trials with prespecified active-drug comparisons.

Implementation value, 5/10

Rationale: Useful for supporting a class-effect argument and identifying safety signals, but insufficient alone to justify choosing one SGLT2 inhibitor over another on rank order.

Evidence basis: Class benefit across ACM/HHF/SAE; NMA supplies comparative estimates and ranks.

Limiting factor: No direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin trial within this review; p-score ranking can overstate sparse indirect evidence.

Would raise/lower: Direct comparative effectiveness plus current guideline/label/formulary constraints.

Strengths

  • Registered protocol, broad search, duplicate screening/extraction, RoB 2, and GRADE.
  • Uses HRs for time-to-event outcomes, prediction intervals, and multiple heterogeneity/consistency checks.
  • Targets the clinically relevant overlap population of HF + T2DM rather than extrapolating from only one disease.
  • Separates class-effect pairwise analysis from individual-drug NMA.

Main limitations

  • Individual-drug comparisons are indirect; p-score rank does not establish a head-to-head superiority claim.
  • Canagliflozin's first-place rank is based on only two trials and 1,543 target patients.
  • Much of the 17,809-person target evidence is subgroup data from much larger parent trials, not dedicated HF + T2DM trials.
  • Several important outcomes are sparse; outcome definitions, HF severity, doses, and concomitant treatment vary.
  • Three trials were high RoB and five had some concerns.
  • Several internal reporting discrepancies reduce confidence in exact secondary/certainty interpretation.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this for

Supporting the proposition that SGLT2 inhibition has a clinically important class benefit in HF + T2DM, especially for HHF, and for cataloging safety/renal signals.

Do not use this for

Claiming canagliflozin is clinically superior to dapagliflozin or empagliflozin, or making a formulary choice solely from p-score ranks.

Next evidence to read

Prioritize the 17 included RCTs, especially CANONICAL/CANVAS, DAPA-HF/DELIVER/DECLARE, EMPA-REG/EMPEROR, VERTIS-CV, and SOLOIST-WHF, and direct comparative effectiveness literature.

Practice boundary: this artifact appraises the supplied 2025 review. It does not independently verify current guideline recommendations, FDA labeling, inpatient holding rules, or formulary constraints; those require separate current-source verification.
