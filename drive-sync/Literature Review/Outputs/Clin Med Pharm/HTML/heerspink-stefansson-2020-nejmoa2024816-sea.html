One-page synthesis

Core finding. Among 4304 adults with albuminuric CKD (eGFR 25–75 mL/min/1.73 m²; UACR 200–5000 mg/g), dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily reduced the primary composite of sustained ≥50% eGFR decline, ESKD, or renal/CV death versus placebo: 9.2% vs 14.5%, HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.51–0.72; P<0.001), with NNT 19 (95% CI 15–27) over median 2.4 years.

The hierarchical secondary renal composite (HR 0.56), CV death/HF hospitalization (HR 0.71), and all-cause death (HR 0.69) also favored dapagliflozin. The primary effect was similar in participants with type 2 diabetes (HR 0.64) and without diabetes (HR 0.50). eGFR showed an expected acute dip followed by substantially slower chronic decline. The main safety counter-signal was more symptoms of volume depletion (5.9% vs 4.2%); serious adverse events overall were less frequent (29.5% vs 33.9%).

Interpretive constraints. The trial stopped early for efficacy, subgroup analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted, direct transport is bounded by albuminuric CKD entry criteria and exclusions, and post-trial eGFR was not collected to assess reversibility of the acute dip.

Methods and population

Design and oversight

DAPA-CKD was randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, and multicenter. Allocation was approximately 1:1 with stratification by type 2 diabetes status and UACR ≤1000 vs >1000 mg/g. Sponsor analyses were replicated by an independent academic group, and an independent data monitoring committee oversaw safety.

Eligibility

Adults ≥18 years required eGFR 25–75 mL/min/1.73 m², UACR 200–5000 mg/g, and stable maximally tolerated ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy for ≥4 weeks unless contraindicated. Important exclusions included type 1 diabetes, polycystic kidney disease, lupus nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, recent renal immunotherapy, organ transplantation, recent SGLT2 use/intolerance, NYHA class IV HF, recent major CV events/procedures, active malignancy, and protocol hepatic/pregnancy restrictions.

Outcomes and statistics

The primary time-to-event outcome combined sustained ≥50% eGFR decline, ESKD, and renal/CV death. A closed hierarchical sequence tested the renal composite, CV death/HF hospitalization, then all-cause death. The primary efficacy analysis was intention-to-treat using stratified Cox models adjusted for baseline eGFR. Prespecified subgroup analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted. eGFR was modeled with an acute/chronic two-slope mixed model with a day-14 knot.

Results

Participants and follow-up

4304 participants were randomized (2152 per group). Mean age was 61.8 years; 33.1% were women; mean eGFR was 43.1 mL/min/1.73 m²; median UACR was 949 mg/g; 67.5% had type 2 diabetes. Approximately 14.5% had eGFR below 30. Median follow-up was 2.4 years; 99.7% completed trial follow-up or died during follow-up.

Kidney function trajectory

Total eGFR slope to month 30 was −2.86 vs −3.79 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (difference 0.93, 95% CI 0.61–1.25). An acute 2-week dip was larger with dapagliflozin (−3.97 vs −0.82), followed by slower chronic decline (−1.67 vs −3.59 per year; difference 1.92, 95% CI 1.61–2.24).

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a pivotal, methodologically strong randomized CKD trial with direct cardiorenal outcomes and a large nondiabetic subgroup.
Relevance
10/10
Direct kidney and cardiovascular outcomes in CKD with and without type 2 diabetes.
Limit: Entry criteria restrict direct transport to albuminuric CKD.
Would change: Broader randomized CKD populations would extend applicability.
Novelty
10/10
Established kidney benefit of dapagliflozin in CKD including a large nondiabetic subgroup.
Limit: The source does not resolve all mechanisms.
Would change: Mechanistic endpoint studies could add explanatory novelty.
Method strength
9/10
Randomized, double-blind, multicenter, adjudicated outcomes, independent analysis replication.
Limit: Sponsor involvement and efficacy-driven early stopping remain relevant.
Would change: Longer prespecified follow-up without early termination would strengthen precision.
Evidence strength
9/10
Large effects with tight confidence intervals and a controlled hierarchical testing strategy.
Limit: Some component outcomes and subgroups were not confirmatory.
Would change: Replication in similarly rigorous CKD trials and longer follow-up would strengthen certainty.
External validity
8/10
386 sites in 21 countries and inclusion of participants without diabetes and eGFR below 30.
Limit: UACR ≥200, eGFR ≥25, and multiple disease exclusions narrow transport.
Would change: Randomized evidence in nonalbuminuric CKD and excluded etiologies would raise this score.
Implementation value
9/10
Dose, eligibility, outcomes, safety, and subgroup boundaries are clear enough for evidence synthesis.
Limit: The trial is not itself a current guideline or formulary policy.
Would change: Contemporary guideline/label alignment would be needed for direct practice implementation.

Principal strengths

  • Randomized double-blind design with adjudicated clinical endpoints.
  • Large, multinational sample and near-complete follow-up.
  • Prespecified hierarchical testing and precise effect estimates.
  • Independent replication of sponsor analyses.
  • Meaningful extension beyond diabetes-only CKD populations.

Principal limitations

  • Efficacy-driven early stopping may reduce precision for some secondary outcomes.
  • Subgroup estimates are exploratory with no multiplicity adjustment.
  • Entry required albuminuria and excluded several CKD etiologies and clinical states.
  • No post-treatment eGFR measurement to assess reversibility of the acute dip.
  • Sponsor involvement is substantial, though independent analytic replication is a mitigating feature.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this source for
Randomized evidence that dapagliflozin reduces major kidney/cardiorenal events in albuminuric CKD, including patients without diabetes.
Do not use it alone for
Nonalbuminuric CKD, eGFR below the enrollment boundary, excluded CKD etiologies, or current formulary/label conclusions.
Safety signal to preserve
Volume-depletion symptoms were more frequent, despite fewer serious adverse events overall.
Evidence nuance
CV death alone had HR 0.81 with CI crossing 1 and was not a confirmatory hierarchical test; the composite CV endpoint was positive.
