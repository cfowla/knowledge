One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. This is a large randomized trial that directly tests whether adjuvant chemotherapy adds benefit to endocrine therapy in women with HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer, one to three positive nodes, and a 21-gene recurrence score ≤25. The study did not find that increasing recurrence score within 0–25 predicted increasing relative chemotherapy benefit. Instead, menopausal status materially modified the treatment effect.

At median 5.3 years, 481 IDFS events had accrued (58% of expected). The treatment-by-recurrence-score interaction was not significant (P=0.35). Overall 5-year IDFS was 92.2% with chemoendocrine therapy and 91.0% with endocrine therapy only; adjusted HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.72–1.03; P=0.10). Recurrence score remained prognostic for IDFS (HR 1.05 per unit, 95% CI 1.04–1.07), but it was not predictive of relative chemotherapy benefit in the randomized score range.

The prespecified menopausal-status interaction was significant (P=0.008). Postmenopausal participants had essentially overlapping 5-year IDFS (91.3% chemoendocrine vs 91.9% endocrine only; HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.82–1.26) and no DRFS advantage (HR 1.05). Premenopausal participants had higher 5-year IDFS with chemoendocrine therapy (93.9% vs 89.0%; absolute difference 4.9 percentage points; HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.43–0.83) and higher DRFS (HR 0.58; 5-year absolute difference 3.3 points). The study cannot determine how much of the premenopausal effect is direct cytotoxic benefit versus treatment-induced ovarian suppression.

Safety signal from the supplement: using the reported maximum-grade counts, grade 3–5 adverse events were calculated at 27.6% vs 5.6% in postmenopausal chemoendocrine vs endocrine-only participants and 19.9% vs 3.7% in premenopausal participants. These are calculations from source counts, not percentages directly reported by the authors.

Section-by-section condensation

Background and rationale

The 21-gene recurrence score was already established as prognostic and treatment-informative in node-negative HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer, but its predictive role in women with one to three positive nodes remained uncertain. Prior evidence suggested chemotherapy benefit might depend on recurrence-score range, particularly in postmenopausal disease, motivating a prospective randomized test.

RxPONDER tested two linked hypotheses: whether the recurrence score was prognostic within 0–25 and whether higher score predicted greater relative chemotherapy benefit. The key clinical problem was whether node-positive patients with genomically lower-risk disease could safely avoid chemotherapy.

Methods — oversight, participants, and design

The NCI-sponsored trial enrolled adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative, noninflammatory N1 breast cancer without distant metastasis after surgery. Women with recurrence score >25 were ineligible; those with 0–25 were randomized 1:1 to chemoendocrine therapy or endocrine therapy alone. Randomization was stratified by recurrence-score category, menopausal status, and axillary surgery type. Planned follow-up was 15 years.

IDFS included invasive recurrence, new invasive primary cancer, or death; DRFS and overall survival were secondary. Analyses used the eligible ITT population with per-protocol sensitivity analyses. The primary Cox model tested treatment×continuous-recurrence-score interaction. A sample of 5,000 was designed for 86% power under prespecified assumptions, with interim monitoring based on expected IDFS events.

Results — participants and treatment

Of 9,383 screened women, 5,083 were randomized. After 65 post-randomization ineligibility determinations, 5,018 remained in baseline analyses; 34 later withdrew consent after assignment and were excluded from survival analyses, leaving 4,984. Baseline distribution was 33.2% premenopausal and 66.8% postmenopausal; 65.3% had one positive node. Treatment refusal was differential: 16.2% after assignment to chemoendocrine therapy versus 5.8% after assignment to endocrine-only therapy.

Among treated participants, anthracycline plus taxane was most common in premenopausal women, whereas taxane plus cyclophosphamide was most common in postmenopausal women. Ovarian suppression within 12 months occurred in 12.7% of premenopausal women overall, with greater use in the endocrine-only arm than the chemoendocrine arm.

Results — IDFS, recurrence score, and menopausal status

The primary recurrence-score predictive interaction was negative: interaction HR 1.02 (95% CI 0.98–1.05; P=0.35). The recurrence score nevertheless remained prognostic (HR 1.05 per unit). A prespecified menopausal-status interaction was significant (P=0.008), becoming the dominant treatment-effect modifier in the report.

Postmenopausal women had no IDFS benefit from chemotherapy (5-year IDFS 91.3% vs 91.9%; HR 1.02), including in the per-protocol sensitivity analysis. Premenopausal women had improved 5-year IDFS (93.9% vs 89.0%; HR 0.60), with benefit directionally consistent across major clinicopathologic subgroups. Post hoc recurrence-score categories did not show an increasing relative benefit pattern; absolute differences varied across categories.

Results — distant relapse and safety

DRFS mirrored IDFS by menopausal status. Postmenopausal participants had no significant DRFS difference (HR 1.05; 5-year absolute difference 0.1 percentage point). Premenopausal participants had improved DRFS with chemoendocrine therapy (HR 0.58; 5-year absolute difference 3.3 percentage points).

The supplement contains treatment-attributed adverse-event tables. Calculating grade 3–5 proportions from the reported maximum-grade counts shows substantially more severe toxicity with chemoendocrine therapy in both menopausal strata. Because these percentages are derived calculations, they should be distinguished from directly reported treatment-effect estimates.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the data as supporting chemotherapy omission in postmenopausal women with one to three positive nodes and recurrence score 0–25, while supporting chemotherapy benefit in premenopausal women even at low scores. They emphasize that recurrence score remained prognostic but did not predict increasing relative chemotherapy benefit within the randomized range.

Important limits are differential nonadherence, only 58% of expected IDFS events at reporting, immature overall survival, and inability to separate cytotoxic chemotherapy effects from ovarian-function suppression in premenopausal women. The trial was not designed as a noninferiority trial. Supplementary post-randomization treatment analyses are exploratory, preliminary, not multiplicity-adjusted, and potentially confounded.

Validity and interpretation

Internal validity
Randomized allocation, prespecified interaction testing, ITT analysis, interim monitoring, and sensitivity analyses are major strengths. Differential nonadherence is the principal threat to power and treatment-contrast purity.
Endpoint maturity
IDFS and DRFS are sufficiently mature to show the reported menopausal divergence, but the analysis occurred at 58% of expected IDFS events and overall survival was immature.
Subgroup credibility
Menopausal status was a prespecified stratification factor and interaction test. Some recurrence-score category analyses were post hoc and should not be treated as independent definitive thresholds.
Mechanistic uncertainty
The trial cannot distinguish direct cytotoxic chemotherapy benefit from ovarian suppression/menopause effects in premenopausal women.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Source-supported postmenopausal conclusion
Within the studied eligibility boundary, chemotherapy did not improve IDFS or DRFS; the authors conclude it can be omitted without compromising these outcomes.
Source-supported premenopausal conclusion
Chemoendocrine therapy improved IDFS and DRFS across recurrence scores 0–25, but the mechanism may include ovarian suppression.
Do not extrapolate
Do not directly apply these results to recurrence score >25, more than three positive nodes, HER2-positive or HR-negative tumors, inflammatory/metastatic disease, or populations not represented by the trial.
Do not overclaim
The source does not establish mature overall-survival benefit, a recurrence-score cutoff within 0–25 for chemotherapy benefit, or equivalence of chemotherapy and ovarian suppression.
