KDIGO 2025 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Immunoglobulin A Nephropathy (IgAN) and Immunoglobulin A Vasculitis (IgAV)

Kidney Int. 2025;108(4S):S1-S71. DOI 10.1016/j.kint.2025.04.004. Appraisal generated 2026-08-21.

Source classification. Focused KDIGO clinical practice guideline update with a 71-page main guideline and 151-page data supplement. The evidence search was updated on August 22, 2024. The guideline uses GRADE for formal recommendations and separate ungraded Practice Points.

Summary

The 2025 update shifts IgAN management toward earlier treatment at proteinuria of at least 0.5 g/d, lifetime reduction of kidney-function loss, and simultaneous treatment of disease-specific injury and generic CKD consequences. The formal treatment recommendations support Nefecon, reduced-dose systemic glucocorticoids when Nefecon is unavailable, maximally tolerated ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy, sparsentan, and SGLT2 inhibition. The IgAV section recommends against systemic glucocorticoids solely to prevent nephritis in isolated extrarenal disease.

The framework does not treat biopsy morphology as a validated therapy-selection tool. MEST-C and crescent findings remain important for prognosis and clinical interpretation, but the guideline states that no prospective evidence supports choosing a specific new treatment from histology alone. The same caution applies to the International IgAN Prediction Tools, which estimate progression risk but are not validated for selecting a treatment regimen.

The six recommendations comprise four Level 2B suggestions and two Level 1B recommendations. The guideline also contains 27 ungraded Practice Points covering diagnosis, prognosis, treatment selection, dosing, adverse-effect risk, pediatric care, pregnancy, IgAVN, and rapidly progressive disease.

Clinical framework and implementation

  • Diagnosis. IgAN and IgAVN require kidney biopsy for diagnosis. In adults with possible IgAN, biopsy should be considered at proteinuria of at least 0.5 g/d. Adult IgAV biopsy thresholds include persistent proteinuria of at least 0.5 g/d for more than 4 weeks, kidney impairment, or rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis.
  • Risk and goals. Proteinuria of at least 0.5 g/d identifies progressive-risk IgAN. The long-term goal is kidney-function loss below 1 ml/min/year for most adults, with proteinuria below 0.5 g/d and ideally below 0.3 g/d when feasible.
  • Supportive treatment. The guideline recommends maximally tolerated ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy for all IgAN patients unless contraindicated. It also suggests SGLT2 inhibition and, for progressive-risk IgAN, sparsentan as an alternative endothelin and angiotensin pathway strategy.
  • Disease-targeted treatment. A 9-month Nefecon course is suggested for progressive-risk IgAN. Where Nefecon is unavailable, reduced-dose systemic glucocorticoids with antimicrobial prophylaxis are suggested.
  • Special populations. The guideline gives separate pathways for nephrotic syndrome, AKI with visible hematuria, rapidly progressive IgAN, pregnancy planning, children, IgAVN, and pediatric IgAVN.

Evidence base

The update restricted intervention effectiveness and safety questions to randomized trials. Searches in MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL were run April 19, 2023 and updated August 22, 2024. Across the concurrent IgAN/IgAV, nephrotic syndrome, and minimal change disease updates, 4,621 citations were screened. The IgAN update identified 27 new eligible articles representing 21 new randomized trials. The Evidence Review Team used duplicate risk-of-bias appraisal, random-effects meta-analysis, GRADE certainty assessment, and Summary of Findings tables.

Evaluation

Methods and evidence. Strong. The guideline uses explicit search methods, independent evidence review, risk-of-bias assessment, GRADE, public review, and linked Summary of Findings tables. The evidence base remains uneven because some treatment recommendations depend on one pivotal trial or subgroup analyses from broader CKD trials.
Clinical clarity. Strong. Thresholds, treatment goals, dosing, prophylaxis, contraindications, and special situations are usually explicit. The separation of graded recommendations from Practice Points is clear.
Applicability. Moderate to strong. The guideline discusses trial-population differences, country-specific evidence, access, affordability, regulatory variation, and limited generalizability of SGLT2 trial subgroups. Several new therapies may remain unavailable or unaffordable in resource-limited settings.
Patient involvement. Limited. No patients or caregivers participated in the Work Group, and the update did not conduct a patient scoping exercise or new formal qualitative synthesis of patient experiences.
Currency. Good at publication but vulnerable to rapid obsolescence. The search closed in August 2024 while the IgAN therapeutic field was changing quickly. A 2026 KDIGO commentary in the same source packet documents additional therapies that appeared after this guideline went to press.

Source limitations and integrity findings

  • The evidence review was not exhaustive outside the RCT-focused update. Specialty or regional databases and manual journal searching were not used.
  • No new formal qualitative patient-experience synthesis or comprehensive economic evaluation was performed for this update.
  • Main guideline reference 86 prints the DAPA-CKD IgAN analysis as Kidney International volume 388, pages 117-127. The data supplement prints the same article as 2021;100:215-224. The conflict is preserved rather than silently corrected.
  • Biopsy findings and prediction tools support prognosis and risk discussion, but the source does not validate them for choosing among the new treatments.
