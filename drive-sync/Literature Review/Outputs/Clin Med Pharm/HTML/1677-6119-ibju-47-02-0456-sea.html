One-page synthesis

The editorial describes a 40-patient prospective trial in refractory IC/BPS. The reported cohort included 37 women and 3 men with a mean age of 55.5 ± 11.1 years. Patients received four monthly intravesical injections of 10 mL autologous PRP. The comment says Global Response Assessment, the primary endpoint, was 70% after the fourth injection and 67.5% three months later. It also reports lower O'Leary-Sant symptom and visual analog scale scores, plus reductions in urinary frequency and nocturia.

The editorial's main contribution is interpretation rather than new evidence. It links PRP to wound repair and urothelial regeneration, then argues that PRP may be useful in IC/BPS if other researchers reproduce the findings. The source does not report a comparator, confidence intervals, responder counts, attrition, PRP preparation details, or adverse-event results. That last omission matters because the title describes the underlying trial as safe.

Source content

Title and cited trial

The source is an editorial comment on the 2019 Neurourology and Urodynamics prospective clinical trial by Jhang, Lin, and Kuo. The title characterizes intravesical PRP as effective and safe in refractory interstitial cystitis.

Comment, trial summary

The editorial reports four monthly 10 mL intravesical autologous PRP injections in 40 refractory IC/BPS patients. Prior treatments listed include lifestyle changes, hydrodistention, NSAIDs, pentosan polysulfate, tricyclic antidepressants, intravesical hyaluronic acid, and botulinum toxin A. Diagnosis relied on symptoms and cystoscopic findings. Patients with Hunner lesions were excluded.

The primary endpoint was Global Response Assessment. The comment reports 70% after the fourth injection and 67.5% three months later. It says O'Leary-Sant symptom scores and visual analog scale scores decreased, and that urinary frequency and nocturia also decreased. It does not provide the statistical estimates needed to judge those changes.

Comment, mechanism and interpretation

The editorial describes PRP as relatively inexpensive and easy to prepare. It discusses platelet roles in tissue regeneration and lists growth factors and signaling proteins contained in platelet alpha granules. It also cites PRP use in several regenerative fields and prior consideration as a polypropylene mesh coating in female urology.

The author proposes a pathophysiologic rationale based on increased urothelial permeability related to proteoglycan deficiency. The closing claim is conditional. PRP may be clinically useful if independent researchers reproduce the results.

References and disclosure

The source lists four numbered references, mainly on PRP biology and regenerative applications. It declares no conflict of interest.
