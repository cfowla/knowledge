One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In this natural experiment created by departmental switching between FCM and ISM, FCM was associated with substantially more hypophosphataemia, while ISM was associated with more mild hypersensitivity reactions. A 32-patient within-person subgroup showed the phosphate difference in the same direction.

The study used routine clinical data from November 2011 through April 2014. Hypophosphataemia was assessed infusion-wise at prespecified windows; HSRs were abstracted from records and assessed patient-wise. At week 2, HP <2 mg/dL occurred in 64/84 FCM series versus 9/33 ISM series, with a reported prevalence odds ratio of 6.7 (95% CI 3.1-14.6; P<0.01). Severe HP (<1 mg/dL) occurred only after FCM in Table 2. Figure 2 also showed an inverse correlation between ferritin rise and phosphate change (Spearman rho -0.36; P<0.01).

HSRs were recorded in 14/231 patients (6.1%). Drug-specific frequencies were 4/162 (2.5%; 95% CI 0.7-6.2%) for FCM and 11/103 (10.7%; 95% CI 5.5-18.3%) for ISM, with P<0.01. All main-text HSR events were grade I or II and affected patients recovered within a few days. However, the paper contains a material internal inconsistency: the main text reports 16 HSR events, while Supplementary Table S1 is explicitly labeled as 17 HSRs and its listed series sum to 17.

Internal consistency issue: “16 HSR events” in the main results conflicts with “17 HSRs” in Supplementary Table S1. The article also says there was no potential HSR crossover even though one patient is described as reacting to both FCM and ISM; Table S1 shows that patient reacting to FCM, ISM, and then FCM again. These points lower confidence in the precision of the HSR narrative.

Section condensation

Introduction

The authors frame IV iron choice as a trade-off between hypophosphataemia and hypersensitivity. Their department had sequentially changed first-choice therapy from iron sucrose to FCM, then to ISM for cost reasons, and back to FCM after observing HSRs. This practice sequence supplied the clinical setting for the comparison.

Materials and methods

All patients receiving IV iron in the department during the study window were identified through an electronic reimbursement-linked registry. Treatments spanning several infusions over 2-3 weeks were treated as one infusion-series. Phosphate was grouped into baseline, week-2, week-5, and week-10 windows, with the lowest value in each window used. HP was <2.0 mg/dL and severe HP <1.0 mg/dL. HSRs were graded by the Ring and Messmer scale.

Analyses included t tests, chi-square/Fisher tests, Spearman correlations, and crude logistic-regression prevalence odds ratios. The design is observational and retrospective; treatment choice changed primarily with departmental policy and pricing, which reduces but does not eliminate confounding. Phosphate follow-up was incomplete in 28%.

Results

Population and exposure

There were 231 patients and 308 infusion series: 192 FCM and 116 ISM. Patient-level exposure groups were 125 FCM only, 67 ISM only, and 39 both. Median dose was 1000 mg (range 500-2500 mg). The cohort was young-to-middle-aged (median 41 years) and predominantly female; 144 patients (62%) had IBD.

The paired subgroup reinforced the signal: among 32 patients receiving both drugs with follow-up, phosphate was lower after FCM at weeks 2 and 5 (both plotted P<0.01). The week-2 ferritin rise was also larger after FCM but did not reach significance in the paired analysis (P=0.09).

Hypersensitivity reactions

Fourteen patients had documented HSRs. The main text reports 9 grade-I and 7 grade-II events, no grade-III/IV reactions, and recovery within days. By drug exposure, HSR was reported in 4/162 FCM-exposed patients and 11/103 ISM-exposed patients. Nine HSR patients had received both drugs, allowing a limited sequence/rechallenge description.

HSR count discrepancy: main text = 16 events; Supplementary Table S1 title/rows = 17 events. The study’s reported 6.4% event frequency also lacks an explicit nearby denominator.

Table 2 - hypophosphataemia

This table contains the study’s strongest quantitative comparative signal. The FCM-associated PORs are large at weeks 2 and 5 for HP and large phosphate drops, but confidence intervals are wide, especially for the >50% decrease endpoint.

Table 3 - HSR-associated characteristics

HSR patients were not clearly different by age, sex, diagnosis, or known non-iron hypersensitivity. A greater fraction of HSR patients had received both iron treatments (9/14 vs 30/217; P<0.01), which also reflects more exposure opportunities and should not be read causally.

Supplementary Table S1 - individual HSR sequences

The table is clinically informative for rechallenge patterns, but it creates the key count discrepancy. It lists 14 patients and 17 reactions. Patient 11 reacted twice to FCM; patient 13 reacted to FCM, then ISM, then FCM.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. The paper is useful historical comparative safety evidence and contains a strong FCM-hypophosphataemia signal plus a notable ISM-HSR signal, but retrospective design, missing phosphate data, single-centre scope, mixed analytic units, and internal HSR inconsistencies limit how much weight it can carry alone.
7.5/10
Directly addresses comparative IV-iron safety trade-offs.
Basis: Head-to-head real-world exposure to FCM and ISM, including a within-person subgroup.
Limit: Narrow setting and 2017 evidence.
Would change: Broader prospective comparative data would raise relevance to practice decisions.
7.0/10
The same-population drug-switch setting and within-person subgroup were distinctive for the time.
Basis: Authors state no prior head-to-head comparison in the same population.
Limit: Retrospective natural experiment rather than planned comparative study.
Would change: Prospective protocolized comparison would increase novelty value.
4.5/10
Useful natural experiment but methodologically limited.
Basis: Complete registry ascertainment, defined time windows, explicit thresholds, paired subgroup.
Limit: Retrospective single centre, 28% incomplete phosphate follow-up, crude PORs, potential time-period confounding.
Would change: Prospective randomization or robust adjusted quasi-experimental analysis would materially raise the score.
5.0/10
The HP signal is quantitatively strong and internally consistent; HSR evidence is less secure.
Basis: Large early PORs for HP, severe HP only after FCM, paired subgroup in same direction.
Limit: Wide CIs, HSR count discrepancy, unclear 6.4% denominator, no severe HSRs.
Would change: Consistent event accounting and independent prospective replication would raise confidence.
4.0/10
Generalizability is limited.
Basis: Unselected routine-care cohort and broad age range.
Limit: Single centre, 62% IBD, local drug-switch/pricing context.
Would change: Multicentre data across varied diagnoses/settings would raise external validity.
5.5/10
Provides practical safety-monitoring hypotheses but not a definitive treatment-selection rule.
Basis: Defined phosphate time windows and clear drug-specific adverse-event patterns.
Limit: Retrospective evidence and author-proposed preventive strategies were not tested.
Would change: Prospective trials of monitoring/mitigation strategies would increase implementation value.

Strengths

  • Registry-based capture of all infusions linked to reimbursement.
  • Same department used both products as first choice at different periods.
  • Within-person subgroup of 32 patients reduces between-person biological variation for phosphate/ferritin comparisons.
  • Clinically interpretable phosphate thresholds and time windows.

Limitations

  • Retrospective single-centre design with no randomization.
  • 28% incomplete prespecified phosphate follow-up, with potential symptom-driven measurement bias.
  • High IBD prevalence may introduce competing causes of low phosphate.
  • HP analyzed per infusion-series while HSR analyzed per patient.
  • HSR event count conflict (16 vs 17) and unclear denominator for the reported 6.4% event frequency.
  • The “no crossover” claim is not transparently reconciled with a patient reacting to both drugs.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this source forHistorical comparative safety signal detection, especially the magnitude and timing of FCM-associated phosphate decline.
Do not use it alone forA current product-selection policy, prophylactic phosphate strategy, or infusion-rate protocol.
Monitoring lessonThe study’s time-window approach highlights that phosphate effects were most prominent early and could persist to week 5.
Evidence-handling lessonKeep HP and HSR denominators/analytic units separate and preserve the 16-vs-17 HSR discrepancy in downstream evidence models.
