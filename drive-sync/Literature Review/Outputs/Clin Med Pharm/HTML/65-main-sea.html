One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The paper is compact and clinically memorable because the index patient was HLA B*5701-negative, developed marked transaminase elevations after an antiretroviral switch to abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine, and improved after abacavir withdrawal. Its evidentiary role is signal detection, not risk quantification.

The index case was a 33-year-old woman with HIV who had tolerated tenofovir/emtricitabine/nevirapine for seven years. She was switched to abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine because of concern about long-term tenofovir toxicity. She was HLA B*5701-negative, had negative viral hepatitis testing and previously normal transaminases, and was reported not to be taking concomitant medications or acetaminophen. Eight weeks after the switch, AST and ALT rose to 222 and 518 UI/mL, respectively. Abacavir was discontinued, tenofovir was resumed, and AST/ALT normalized within six weeks.

The authors also retrospectively searched a clinical database of 1004 abacavir recipients from 2000-2013. Eighty-three had been on a stable nevirapine-containing regimen before starting abacavir. They report 26 discontinuations, including 11 for virologic failure, 5 for rash, 2 for gastrointestinal effects, and 2 for hepatotoxicity; both hepatotoxicity cases were HLA B*5701-negative. A major quantitative problem is present: the paper states "26 out of 83 (24%)", but 26/83 recalculates to 31.3%. That internal inconsistency and the uncontrolled retrospective design materially limit confidence in the cohort estimates.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames abacavir-induced liver toxicity as rare and usually associated with HLA B*5701 positivity, then presents an HLA B*5701-negative case after switching from tenofovir to abacavir while nevirapine remained stable. It also states that the authors investigated reasons for abacavir discontinuation in patients treated with abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine.

Introduction

The introduction situates hepatotoxicity among important adverse effects of antiretroviral therapy and lists several possible mechanisms, including direct toxicity, immune reconstitution in viral hepatitis, hypersensitivity, and mitochondrial toxicity. It notes that HLA B*5701-negative abacavir hepatotoxicity had only rarely been reported and that prior reported cases occurred on stable nevirapine regimens after switching the nucleoside analogue.

The authors' additional aim was to estimate how often abacavir-related liver toxicity appeared in their own population receiving abacavir, lamivudine, and nevirapine and to characterize reasons for abacavir discontinuation in that regimen context.

Case report

The index patient was 33 years old and switched after seven years of tenofovir/emtricitabine/nevirapine to abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine. She was HLA B*5701-negative and hepatitis-virus negative, with previously normal transaminases. Eight weeks later AST and ALT rose to 222 and 518 UI/mL. She remained asymptomatic. Hepatitis serologies, HCV RNA, and autoantibody screening were negative; the authors report no concomitant medications and no acetaminophen. Abacavir was stopped, tenofovir restarted, and AST/ALT normalized within six weeks.

For the descriptive cohort, the authors searched 1004 abacavir recipients and identified 83 who had been on stable nevirapine before abacavir. The cohort included 51 men and 32 women, median age 46 years (range 30-72). The article lists 26 discontinuations for multiple reasons. Two discontinuations were attributed to hepatotoxicity and both patients were HLA B*5701-negative. Nine of 11 virologic failures (82%) occurred in patients with long prior treatment histories that included regimens the article describes as no longer recommended.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors argue that HLA B*5701-negative abacavir liver injury is uncommon but clinically relevant. They consider a pharmacokinetic interaction between abacavir and nevirapine unlikely and discuss possible pharmacodynamic bioactivation mechanisms, while acknowledging that safety and efficacy data for the abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine combination were sparse. They also caution that prior suboptimal antiretroviral regimens could have influenced observed virologic failures.

The conclusion recommends vigilance for hepatitis signs during abacavir treatment and suggests considering abacavir/lamivudine/nevirapine as a simplification strategy only in patients without prior suboptimal antiretroviral regimens. These are author recommendations from a limited 2016 evidence base and should not be treated as current practice guidance without external verification.

Clinical validity lens

Population: one HLA B*5701-negative index case plus an 83-patient subset on stable nevirapine before abacavir. Exposure: abacavir added/substituted within an antiretroviral regimen. Comparator: no formal comparator group. Outcomes: transaminase elevation, dechallenge response, discontinuation reasons, hepatotoxicity, and virologic failure.

  • Internal validity: The index-case temporal sequence and negative alternative-cause testing are supportive, but causality remains observational and unblinded; the cohort relies on retrospective attribution of discontinuation reasons.
  • Bias/confounding: Selection of the 83-patient NVP subset, historical treatment exposure, HCV coinfection in one hepatotoxicity case, and treatment-history confounding all matter.
  • Precision/statistics: No confidence intervals or comparative effect estimates are reported. The 26/83 arithmetic inconsistency raises a data-quality concern.
  • Generalisability: Single-center, older treatment era, and a specific regimen combination sharply limit translation.
  • Practice translation: Use as a signal-recognition/historical source, not as standalone current guidance or a denominator for counseling risk.

Confidence in appraisal: high for judging this supplied article's internal design and reporting limits, because the full three-page source was inspected. Confidence is deliberately limited for any statement about current practice because no external 2026 verification was requested or performed.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
Keep the paper as a compact historical signal that clinically relevant liver injury can be reported despite a negative HLA B*5701 test.
Do not overclaim
Do not treat 2/83 as a reliable incidence estimate or infer that nevirapine-abacavir interaction is established.
Data-quality flag
Any secondary use of the discontinuation rate should correct or explicitly flag the 26/83 versus 24% inconsistency.
Read next
The reference queue should prioritize the prior HLA-negative hepatotoxicity reports (refs 3-4), HLA-B*5701 testing papers (refs 7-8), mechanistic bioactivation papers (refs 10-12), and regimen-efficacy reports (refs 13-14).
