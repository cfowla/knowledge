One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Modern myeloma management is increasingly risk-adapted and immunotherapy-intensive: anti-CD38/VRd quadruplets dominate newly diagnosed therapy; ASCT remains important but may be delayed in selected standard-risk patients; maintenance is intensified with anti-CD38 plus lenalidomide; CAR-T and bispecific antibodies move into earlier relapse; and daratumumab is proposed for high-risk smoldering myeloma.
Diagnosis
IMWG-style clonal plasma-cell/plasmacytoma requirement plus CRAB or biomarker MDEs; use modern cross-sectional imaging and complete monoclonal-protein testing.
Risk
Use IMS/IMWG cytogenetic high-risk combinations and mSMART context rather than treating isolated abnormalities as equivalent.
New diagnosis
Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd is the author’s default for fit patients; frailty determines intensity; early versus delayed ASCT remains preference-sensitive in standard risk.
Maintenance
Lenalidomide + daratumumab/isatuximab is favored, with risk- and MRD-informed duration; bortezomib + lenalidomide is an alternative in high risk.
Relapse
Sequence by refractoriness and prior BCMA/anti-CD38 exposure; cilta-cel, bispecifics, Tec-Dara, belantamab combinations, and standard triplets are central options.
SMM
AQUILA drives a shift toward daratumumab for high-risk SMM while continuing observation for lower-risk SMM.
Most important source-level cautions: (1) FLC-MDE urine-protein criterion differs between narrative and Table 1; (2) late SMM progression is 1.5%/year in Section 1 and 1%/year in Section 9; (3) Figure 1 lists DRd/IRd for frail patients whereas prose recommends DRd/Isa-Rd; (4) Table 9 contains an unusual DREAMM-8 CR+VGPR value of 99% that should be verified in the primary trial.

Section-by-section condensation

1. Disease Overview

MM is framed as a plasma-cell malignancy preceded in nearly all cases by MGUS, with SMM as an intermediate clinical state in some patients. The review emphasizes bone disease, anemia, hypercalcemia, renal failure, infection risk, and extramedullary disease as major manifestations.

The epidemiologic and progression figures are useful orientation but are secondary summaries. Notably, the late SMM progression rate differs between Section 1 (1.5%/year after 10 years) and Section 9 (1%/year).

2. Diagnosis

The review uses the revised IMWG construct: clonal marrow plasma cells >=10% or a biopsy-proven plasmacytoma plus one or more MDEs. It recommends SPEP, serum immunofixation, and FLC testing, FISH/genomic assessment at diagnosis, and low-dose WB-CT or PET/CT for bone disease, with MRI for selected indications.

A source-level inconsistency matters: the abstract and diagnosis narrative add urine M protein >=200 mg/24h to the FLC-ratio biomarker MDE, while Table 1 does not. This should be verified against the governing criteria before operationalizing a CDS rule.

3. Molecular Classification

The article presents myeloma as a collection of cytogenetically distinct diseases, led by hyperdiploid and IgH-translocated categories, with secondary abnormalities such as gain(1q), del(1p), del(17p), and MYC events influencing course and prognosis.

The practical point is not merely taxonomy: the author argues that disease phase and combinations of abnormalities change interpretation, limiting one-size-fits-all risk categories.

4. Prognosis and Risk Stratification

Modern survival is described as long in many patients, especially transplant-eligible populations, but shaped by host factors, tumor burden, cytogenetics, and response. The review highlights the 2025 IMS/IMWG high-risk definition and uses mSMART for added treatment-oriented detail.

The author explicitly notes that current risk models have limitations under modern therapy and favors more granular genomic stratification. That is an important caveat when turning categorical risk labels into treatment rules.

5. Treatment Options

The treatment landscape includes IMiDs, proteasome inhibitors, anti-CD38 antibodies, BCMA- and GPRC5D-directed bispecifics, CAR-T products, and belantamab mafodotin. Table 6 functions as a practical regimen/dosing reference.

Because the article is a rapidly updated clinical review, regulatory status, labeling, and local protocol should be checked before direct implementation, especially for newer combinations and dose-optimization strategies.

6. Newly Diagnosed Myeloma

For transplant-eligible disease, the author favors Dara-VRd or Isa-VRd, generally followed by ASCT, while permitting delayed ASCT in selected standard-risk responders. For transplant-ineligible patients, frailty rather than age alone drives intensity; nonfrail patients receive quadruplets and frail patients receive reduced-intensity triplets.

The phase III table strongly supports PFS gains from anti-CD38 quadruplets and from early ASCT, but survival differences are smaller or absent in several comparisons. This distinction supports shared decision-making rather than treating PFS improvement as synonymous with survival benefit.

6.3-6.4 Transplant, Consolidation, Maintenance

ASCT remains a core option, but tandem ASCT and routine post-ASCT consolidation are not recommended outside trials. Maintenance is recommended for all patients, with lenalidomide plus daratumumab or isatuximab as the author’s default, duration modified by risk and MRD context.

The maintenance section is highly actionable but is expert synthesis rather than a graded guideline. The source also flags second-cancer risk with lenalidomide and proposes anti-CD38 stopping rules that should be checked against trial protocol, label, and current consensus before policy use.

7. Relapsed Multiple Myeloma

Relapse treatment is organized by prior exposure/refractoriness, access to cell therapy/bispecifics, and ASCT history. The review moves CAR-T and bispecifics into first/second relapse, while retaining standard triplets, belantamab combinations, talquetamab, venetoclax for t(11;14), selinexor, and alkylator-based strategies for later or refractory disease.

Tables 8-9 show major PFS advantages for several immunotherapy strategies. Operational constraints are substantial: CRS/neurotoxicity, infection risk, cytopenias, manufacturing delay for CAR-T, unique GPRC5D toxicity, ocular toxicity with belantamab, and the need to individualize sequencing.

8. Supportive Care

Bone-modifying therapy, infection prevention, antiviral/PJP prophylaxis, and IVIG in selected immunotherapy contexts are treated as integral components of myeloma care. The article favors lower-intensity bisphosphonate scheduling in some patients and denosumab when renal dysfunction is significant.

These recommendations are practical but span areas where evidence strength and institutional practice vary. They should be mapped to local antimicrobial, vaccination, IVIG, renal, and bone-health policies rather than copied wholesale.

9. Smoldering Multiple Myeloma

The author divides current SMM into low- and high-risk groups and uses Mayo 2018 features plus other imaging/cytogenetic/evolving markers. The AQUILA summary is practice-shaping: daratumumab for 3 years improved time to active myeloma and reported 5-year survival compared with monitoring.

The author recommends daratumumab for newly diagnosed high-risk SMM and observation for others. Because this is a major shift from historical observation, implementation should be anchored to the primary AQUILA publication, current approval language, and a clearly defined high-risk population.

Strengths

  • Exceptionally current 2026 clinical synthesis with explicit treatment algorithms and dosing tables.
  • Preserves major randomized-trial magnitudes rather than relying only on directional statements.
  • Separates standard-risk/high-risk, transplant eligibility, frailty, refractoriness, and treatment-access constraints.
  • Includes maintenance duration, supportive care, and SMM rather than focusing only on induction/relapse.

Limitations

  • No reproducible literature-search method, evidence-grading system, or formal risk-of-bias assessment.
  • Single-author recommendations mix trial evidence, expert preference, access considerations, and extrapolation.
  • Several internal inconsistencies require resolution before machine implementation.
  • Trial results are secondary reports; eligibility, endpoint definitions, subgroup caveats, and full safety data require primary-source review.
