Synthesis

The correspondence tests two interpretive questions rather than the randomized treatment effect itself: whether dapagliflozin’s benefit could be explained by natriuresis/volume effects and how a kidney-protective effect might operate without diabetes. In reply, the trial authors reported that 1882 participants (43.7%) used diuretics at baseline and that the primary composite treatment effect was consistent with versus without baseline diuretic use (P for interaction=0.96).

The authors also made an important measurement-boundary statement: DAPA-CKD did not obtain 24-hour urine samples or measure urine volume to assess dietary sodium intake. Mechanistic statements about glomerular hypertension and albuminuria were tied to prior studies and should not be reclassified as mechanisms directly proven by DAPA-CKD.

Letter-by-letter condensation

Vogt

Vogt proposed that natriuresis and correction of volume expansion might explain some of the observed cardiorenal benefit and requested sodium-intake and diuretic-use data.

Yasuda and Isobe

They emphasized tubuloglomerular feedback as a possible mechanism in nondiabetic CKD, asked about albuminuria among participants without diabetes, and suggested renal functional reserve testing as a way to identify earlier CKD populations that were not enrolled.

Authors' reply

The reply reported 43.7% baseline diuretic use and no evidence of effect modification by baseline diuretics (interaction P=0.96). It explicitly states that 24-hour urine and urine volume were not collected for sodium assessment. The reply also cites prior mechanistic and secondary-analysis literature when discussing albuminuria, glomerular hypertension, diabetes status, and CKD cause.

Evaluation and appraisal
