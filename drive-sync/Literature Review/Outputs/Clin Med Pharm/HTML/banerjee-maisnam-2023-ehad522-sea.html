Scope warning. This is a meta-analysis of randomized SGLT2 inhibitor versus placebo trials stratified by baseline MRA use. MRA use was not randomized; causal claims about MRA–SGLT2i synergy are therefore not established.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. SGLT2 inhibitor benefit on HF hospitalization and the HHF/CVD composite was similar with and without baseline MRA use. A single chronic-HF cardiovascular-death interaction suggested a potentially larger relative effect with MRA use, while pooled safety analyses suggested SGLT2 inhibitors may blunt MRA-associated hyperkalaemia at the cost of a possible increase in volume depletion.

The review pooled five trial datasets and 21,947 participants across HFrEF and HFpEF. HHF/CVD estimates were 0.75 (95% CI 0.68–0.81) with baseline MRA and 0.79 (0.72–0.86) without MRA (interaction P=0.43); HHF estimates were 0.74 (0.67–0.83) and 0.71 (0.63–0.80), respectively (P=0.53). For cardiovascular death in chronic HF, estimates were 0.81 (0.72–0.91) and 0.98 (0.86–1.13), with interaction P=0.034. All-cause mortality and kidney effects did not show significant MRA interaction.

Safety is clinically useful but should be interpreted cautiously. Volume depletion was higher in baseline MRA users randomized to SGLT2i (RR 1.21, 1.02–1.42), whereas renal adverse events did not increase. Mild and severe hyperkalaemia were reduced by SGLT2i overall, and exploratory nonrandomized comparisons suggested the MRA-associated hyperkalaemia excess was smaller in SGLT2i recipients than placebo recipients. This supports monitoring-aware coadministration, not a claim that the drug classes are prospectively proven synergistic.

Section condensation

Introduction

The article frames SGLT2 inhibitors and MRAs as mechanistically distinct disease-modifying therapies in HF, with SGLT2i foundational across EF phenotypes and steroidal MRAs established particularly in HFrEF. The clinical question is whether concurrent MRA therapy alters SGLT2i cardiovascular benefit or safety.

The rationale emphasizes complementary cardiorenal mechanisms and the practical tension that MRAs can cause hyperkalaemia and volume-related adverse effects. These mechanistic arguments are background, not proof of additive clinical efficacy.

Methods

The authors registered the review (PROSPERO CRD42023397129), searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and trial registries through 4 February 2023, and included adult HF randomized trials or post-hoc RCT analyses with at least 6 months follow-up that compared SGLT2i with placebo and reported outcomes by baseline MRA use. Screening and extraction were duplicated; RoB was assessed with the revised Cochrane tool.

Time-to-event estimates were pooled with fixed-effect inverse-variance methods; exploratory adverse-event comparisons used Mantel–Haenszel risk ratios. Subgroups included MRA status and HF phenotype, and a sensitivity analysis excluded SOLOIST-WHF. The main inferential limitation is structural: randomization applied to SGLT2i, not MRA status.

Results

Five studies (n=21,947; T2DM n=10,805) were included. SGLT2i reduced HHF/CVD and HHF similarly in MRA users and non-users. The chronic-HF cardiovascular-death interaction was P=0.034, but HFrEF- and HFpEF-specific interactions were not significant. All-cause mortality and kidney outcomes showed no significant interaction by MRA status.

Safety analyses found a higher volume-depletion RR in MRA users, no clear increase in renal adverse events, and lower mild/severe hyperkalaemia with SGLT2i. Exploratory MRA-versus-no-MRA comparisons suggested the hyperkalaemia excess associated with MRA use was attenuated in SGLT2i recipients. These exploratory comparisons remain confounded by nonrandomized MRA exposure.

Discussion and limitations

The authors propose complementary anti-inflammatory, cardiorenal, natriuretic and antifibrotic mechanisms as possible explanations for additive benefit. They also argue that SGLT2i may protect against MRA-associated hyperkalaemia and that volume-depletion risk warrants early vigilance, particularly in older patients and those with renal impairment.

Key limitations are nonrandomized MRA use, baseline imbalance, lack of time-varying MRA exposure adjustment, incomplete historical MRA exposure, low power among HFrEF non-users, outcome-definition differences, and limited evidence in eGFR 30–45. The article appropriately labels synergy as hypothesis-generating.

Conclusion

Baseline MRA use did not materially alter SGLT2i effects on HHF/CVD, HHF, or all-cause mortality. The authors highlight a possible greater relative CVD reduction in chronic HF with concomitant MRA and a potential mitigation of MRA-associated hyperkalaemia.

Prospective adequately powered randomized trials are required before the combination can be considered proven synergistic.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as a focused synthesis supporting compatibility of SGLT2i with background MRA therapy and informing potassium/volume monitoring; not as causal proof of MRA–SGLT2i synergy.

Relevance — 9/10

Rationale: Directly relevant to combined SGLT2i/MRA heart-failure pharmacotherapy across EF phenotypes.

Evidence basis: Large HF pooled dataset with efficacy, renal and electrolyte outcomes.

Principal limit: Does not directly compare dapagliflozin with empagliflozin or test inpatient formulary interchange.

Would raise/lower: A direct head-to-head or implementation study would increase formulary-specific relevance.

Novelty — 7/10

Rationale: Adds a focused synthesis of baseline MRA effect modification and MRA-associated safety.

Evidence basis: Five major HF trial datasets spanning HFrEF/HFpEF and sotagliflozin.

Principal limit: Most component trials and subgroup analyses were already published; the synthesis is incremental.

Would raise/lower: Prospectively randomized combination evidence would substantially raise novelty.

Method strength — 6/10

Rationale: Systematic search, prespecified registration, duplicate review, and low trial-level RoB are strengths.

Evidence basis: PROSPERO registration; four databases/registries; five studies; fixed-effect meta-analysis and sensitivity/subgroup analyses.

Principal limit: Baseline MRA use was not randomized, interaction analyses were vulnerable to confounding/multiplicity, and only five datasets contributed.

Would raise/lower: Randomized factorial or prespecified treatment-strategy trials with harmonized endpoints would raise this score.

Evidence strength — 5/10

Rationale: The randomized SGLT2i-versus-placebo effects are credible, but causal claims about additive MRA benefit are weak.

Evidence basis: Consistent HHF/renal effects across MRA strata; hyperkalaemia signal is biologically plausible and quantitatively supported.

Principal limit: MRA subgroup comparisons are observational/post-hoc; CVD interaction P=0.034 is a single hypothesis-generating signal.

Would raise/lower: Prospective randomized combination trials with corrected interaction testing would raise certainty.

External validity — 7/10

Rationale: The pooled population spans HFrEF and HFpEF and includes 21,947 participants.

Evidence basis: Five international landmark HF trial datasets; diabetes and non-diabetes represented.

Principal limit: RCT populations and MRA users differ from non-users; sparse evidence in eGFR 30–45 and limited finerenone data.

Would raise/lower: Broader pragmatic cohorts/trials and dedicated CKD strata would improve generalizability.

Implementation value — 6/10

Rationale: Supports co-administration with attention to volume status and potassium but should not be used to claim synergy.

Evidence basis: No material loss of SGLT2i HHF benefit with MRA use; lower hyperkalaemia risk signal; volume-depletion signal.

Principal limit: No randomized MRA assignment, dose strategy, initiation sequence, or inpatient protocol comparison.

Would raise/lower: A pragmatic combination-treatment protocol with safety monitoring outcomes would increase actionability.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now
SGLT2i HF benefits appear preserved with background MRA therapy. Co-administration is supported by the pooled trial context.
Monitor
Watch volume status, renal function, blood pressure, and potassium, particularly early after combination therapy and in older or renally impaired patients.
Do not overclaim
Do not describe the CVD interaction or hyperkalaemia subgroup findings as randomized proof of synergy; MRA status was not randomized.
Formulary use
Useful for class-level SGLT2i/MRA compatibility, but it does not directly establish dapagliflozin–empagliflozin interchangeability.
