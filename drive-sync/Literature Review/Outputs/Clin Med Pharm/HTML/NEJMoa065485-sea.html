One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In adults with anemia and non-dialysis chronic kidney disease, an epoetin alfa strategy targeting hemoglobin 13.5 g/dL produced more major cardiovascular/death composite events than a strategy targeting 11.3 g/dL, while providing no incremental overall quality-of-life benefit and requiring substantially more epoetin alfa.

CHOIR randomized 1432 adults with hemoglobin <11.0 g/dL and CKD (MDRD eGFR 15-50 mL/min/1.73 m²) to high- or low-hemoglobin targets. Median follow-up was 16 months. The primary composite of death, myocardial infarction, CHF hospitalization without renal replacement therapy, or stroke occurred in 125/715 (17.5%) versus 97/717 (13.5%): HR 1.34 (95% CI 1.03-1.74), P=0.03. Sensitivity analyses remained directionally consistent. At 15 months, Figure 3 showed a 4.7-percentage-point Kaplan-Meier separation (15.8% vs 11.1%).

The high-target arm did not fully reach its assigned goal: mean achieved hemoglobin was about 12.6 g/dL, and mean weekly epoetin dose was 11,215 units versus 6,276 units. Quality-of-life improvement was generally similar across LASA, KDQ, and SF-36 measures. Serious adverse events were more common with the high target (54.8% vs 48.5%, P=0.02), driven in part by more serious CHF events (11.2% vs 7.4%, P=0.02). The trial was stopped early at the second interim review because conditional power for showing benefit of the high-target strategy was <5%, not because a prespecified efficacy or futility boundary had been crossed.

Critical interpretation: CHOIR demonstrates harm from the assigned high-target strategy, not a clean causal separation between hemoglobin concentration and ESA dose. The high-target group received nearly twice the weekly epoetin dose and still averaged below the 13.5 g/dL target; the authors explicitly stated that the mechanism of poorer outcomes was unresolved.

Section condensation

Abstract

The article frames the uncertainty around how completely anemia should be corrected in CKD. In the open-label randomized trial, 715 patients were assigned to a 13.5 g/dL target and 717 to an 11.3 g/dL target; the primary composite combined death, MI, CHF hospitalization without renal replacement therapy, and stroke.

The abstract reports 222 primary events, with more events in the high-target group (125 vs 97; HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.03-1.74; P=0.03), similar quality-of-life improvement, and more serious adverse events. The authors conclude that the higher target increased risk without incremental quality-of-life benefit.

Introduction / background

The rationale arose from observational and smaller interventional data linking higher hemoglobin with better cardiovascular outcomes, left-ventricular structure, and quality of life, against a prior randomized dialysis trial that had suggested possible harm at higher hematocrit. At the time, KDOQI recommendations had expanded the upper hemoglobin target to 13.0 g/dL partly on quality-of-life grounds.

CHOIR explicitly tested the hypothesis that raising hemoglobin to 13.5 g/dL with epoetin alfa would reduce cardiovascular complications and death compared with a target of 11.3 g/dL in non-dialysis CKD.

Methods

CHOIR enrolled 1432 adults at 130 US sites. Eligibility required Hb <11.0 g/dL and MDRD eGFR 15-50 mL/min/1.73 m² without dialysis. Patients were randomized by computer-generated permuted blocks. The target amendment occurred after 347 patients had enrolled, when only 132 of 1939 eventual patient-years had accrued. Events were independently adjudicated by a DCRI committee blinded to group assignment, despite the open-label treatment design.

The primary end point was time to first death, MI, CHF hospitalization without RRT, or stroke, with explicit component definitions. The study planned 80% power for a 25% relative reduction with at least 295 events and four interim analyses using O'Brien-Fleming boundaries and conditional-power/futility guidance. Kaplan-Meier/log-rank methods were used for time-to-event analyses. The supplement specifies a 20,000-unit/week maximum, a universal initial dose of 10,000 units SC weekly for three weeks, subsequent algorithmic titration, and permitted every-other-week dosing after one month on a stable dose.

Results

The DSMB recommended termination in May 2005 at the second interim analysis because conditional power for proving high-target benefit was <5% across plausible remaining effects; no formal efficacy or futility boundary had been crossed. Median follow-up was 16 months. Primary events occurred in 17.5% versus 13.5% (HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.03-1.74; P=0.03), and results remained similar in per-protocol and extended follow-up sensitivity analyses. Adding RRT to the composite also favored the low target (HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.07-1.54; P=0.007).

The high-target strategy generated a larger Hb rise (+2.5 vs +1.2 g/dL) and required roughly 11,215 versus 6,276 units/week. Overall quality-of-life gains were similar. Cardiovascular hospitalization (HR 1.23, 95% CI 1.01-1.48) and all-cause hospitalization (HR 1.18, 95% CI 1.02-1.37) were higher with the high target. Serious adverse events occurred in 54.8% versus 48.5% (P=0.02), with serious CHF events in 11.2% versus 7.4% (P=0.02).

Discussion

The authors interpret the primary result as evidence against routine near-normalization of hemoglobin in this population. They emphasize that death and CHF hospitalization made up most composite events, while individual components did not independently reach statistical significance. They also argue that the higher target had no quality-of-life advantage and would increase epoetin cost because of higher dose requirements.

The discussion places CHOIR against the then-existing tension between observational evidence and randomized trials. It also notes uncertainty about renal progression and acknowledges several limitations: censoring at RRT, substantial withdrawal, death as a competing risk for component analyses, and the open-label design for endpoints with subjective elements. The authors could not explain whether worse outcomes were driven by hemoglobin level, epoetin dose, or another mechanism.

Conclusion

The authors conclude that targeting 13.5 rather than 11.3 g/dL increased risk without incremental quality-of-life benefit and recommend, in their 2006 context, an 11-12 g/dL target rather than an 11-13 g/dL range. That recommendation is part of the historical source and should not be read as a current guideline statement.

Supplementary Appendix - Epoetin Dosing

Both groups: epoetin alfa subcutaneous.
Maximum total dose: 20,000 units/week.
Initial regimen: 10,000 units SC once weekly for 3 consecutive weeks, regardless of weight or baseline Hb.
Subsequent regimen: weekly dosing by a prespecified Hb-based algorithm beginning with the Hb assessment 2 weeks after the first dose.

Interpretation: the supplement materially clarifies treatment intensity but does not provide a separate efficacy dataset.

Evaluation & appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a landmark randomized trial for understanding why aggressive ESA hemoglobin normalization in non-dialysis CKD can be harmful despite plausible observational and quality-of-life arguments.
9/10

Relevance

Rationale: Directly addresses a clinically important ESA hemoglobin-target question using hard cardiovascular outcomes in non-dialysis CKD.

Evidence basis: Randomized comparison of 13.5 vs 11.3 g/dL targets in 1432 patients.

Principal limiting factor: Population and practice context are specific to 2006 ESA management.

What would change the score: Would rise with direct fit to a current decision; fall if the question is dialysis, oncology, or a different ESA context.

9/10

Novelty

Rationale: Historically, the trial directly challenged observational and surrogate-based expectations that more complete anemia correction would improve outcomes.

Evidence basis: Large event-driven randomized trial designed around cardiovascular events and death.

Principal limiting factor: Novelty is historical; the result is now part of established landmark evidence.

What would change the score: Would rise only in a source-era assessment; current novelty should not be inferred from this score.

8/10

Method strength

Rationale: Large multicenter randomization, intention-to-treat analysis, blinded endpoint adjudication, prespecified monitoring, and multiple sensitivity analyses support internal validity.

Evidence basis: 1432 patients at 130 US sites; adjudication committee blinded to assignment; Kaplan-Meier/log-rank framework; consistent sensitivity analyses.

Principal limiting factor: Open-label treatment, early stopping, substantial censoring/withdrawal, and a target amendment after enrollment began.

What would change the score: Would rise with double-blinding, complete follow-up, and completion to the planned event count; fall if attrition were demonstrably differential or informative.

8/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: The primary composite showed statistically significant harm with consistent direction across sensitivity analyses and more serious adverse events at the high target.

Evidence basis: 125/715 vs 97/717; HR 1.34 (95% CI 1.03-1.74), P=0.03; 30- and 90-day sensitivity HR 1.30; SAE 54.8% vs 48.5%, P=0.02.

Principal limiting factor: Individual composite components were not significant, the trial stopped early, and the high target was not fully achieved.

What would change the score: Would rise with more events, narrower confidence intervals, and replication designed to separate target from dose effects; fall if stopping/attrition materially biased the effect.

7/10

External validity

Rationale: The 130-site US sample was broad for CKD, but eligibility and treatment conditions limit generalization.

Evidence basis: Adults with Hb <11 g/dL and MDRD eGFR 15-50 mL/min/1.73 m² across 130 sites.

Principal limiting factor: Excluded dialysis, active cancer, prior epoetin use, unstable angina, uncontrolled hypertension, and several anemia/bleeding states; achieved high-group mean Hb was ~12.6 g/dL.

What would change the score: Would rise with diverse contemporary cohorts and replication across ESA agents/settings; fall for populations outside the trial eligibility envelope.

8/10

Implementation value

Rationale: The trial is highly useful as a warning against aggressively targeting near-normal hemoglobin with higher ESA exposure, but it is not a current dosing protocol.

Evidence basis: Higher target increased the primary composite, required ~11,215 vs 6,276 units/week, and did not improve overall quality of life.

Principal limiting factor: The paper is from 2006 and does not incorporate current labels, guidelines, or contemporary CKD/ESA practice.

What would change the score: Would rise for historical evidence synthesis; for current bedside implementation, it requires contemporary guideline and labeling verification.

Validity

Strengths: randomized multicenter design; clinically meaningful composite; explicit component definitions; blinded central adjudication; intention-to-treat analysis; prespecified interim monitoring; consistent per-protocol and extended-window sensitivity analyses; complete reporting of important adverse events and quality-of-life measures.

Threats: open-label assignment; trial termination before planned events; 38.3% withdrew before termination without a primary event, including 16.9% because of RRT; censoring at RRT; death as a competing risk for component outcomes; protocol target amendment after enrollment began; and incomplete target attainment, especially in the high-target arm. The target strategy also entailed materially greater epoetin exposure, so the experiment does not isolate hemoglobin concentration from drug dose.

Evidence interpretation

The primary endpoint is statistically positive for harm, but the confidence interval is relatively close to 1.0 and no individual component reached P<0.05. That is not a contradiction: the prespecified composite had greater event count and power, while death and CHF hospitalization both trended in the same harmful direction. Serious adverse-event and hospitalization findings add coherence.

The study does not establish that a specific achieved hemoglobin threshold itself causes harm. The high-target group averaged about 12.6 g/dL and required nearly twice the weekly epoetin dose. The authors explicitly call for further work on the roles of hemoglobin level and dose. A mechanistic causal claim beyond the randomized target strategy would therefore be overreach.

Sponsor / conflict considerations

Ortho Biotech Clinical Affairs and Johnson & Johnson Pharmaceutical Research and Development supported the study. The principal investigators developed the protocol and amendments with DCRI and the sponsor; DCRI acquired and queried data, developed and locked the database, and performed primary analyses, while sponsor secondary analyses were verified by DCRI. The article also reports multiple relevant financial relationships, including employment of two authors by Ortho Biotech. These relationships warrant attention but do not by themselves invalidate the randomized result; the disclosed DCRI data/analysis role is an important countervailing safeguard.
