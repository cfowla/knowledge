One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In 4076 high-risk adults with type 2 diabetes, once-weekly efpeglenatide reduced first MACE versus placebo (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58–0.92) and reduced the composite renal outcome (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.57–0.79) over a median 1.81 years, with more gastrointestinal adverse effects.

The trial extends GLP-1 receptor agonist cardiovascular-outcome evidence to efpeglenatide, an exendin-based agent. The primary MACE result met noninferiority and superiority criteria; the renal composite also favored efpeglenatide. The effect pattern was generally consistent across prespecified subgroups, including baseline SGLT2-inhibitor use, although some subgroup confidence intervals were wide.

Confidence is moderated by the short follow-up, early final visits before the planned 330 primary events (314 accrued), and enrollment concentrated in patients with established cardiovascular or kidney disease. Safety was dominated by increased gastrointestinal intolerance and treatment discontinuation rather than a clear signal for pancreatitis or acute kidney failure.

Clinical PICO

PopulationAdults with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c >7%, and established cardiovascular disease or qualifying kidney disease plus cardiovascular risk; 344 sites in 28 countries.
InterventionEfpeglenatide 4 mg or 6 mg subcutaneously once weekly after titration.
ComparatorMatching placebo once weekly.
Primary outcomeFirst MACE: nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or cardiovascular/undetermined-cause death.
Key contextRandomization stratified by current/potential SGLT2-inhibitor use; pooled efpeglenatide dose groups for primary comparison.

Methods and design

Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to efpeglenatide 4 mg, efpeglenatide 6 mg, or placebo. Matching syringes maintained masking. Participants were followed regardless of adherence; an independent committee blinded to treatment adjudicated deaths, myocardial infarctions, strokes, unstable-angina and heart-failure hospitalizations, and pancreatic events. The primary comparison pooled the two efpeglenatide groups.

The event-driven design anticipated about 4000 participants and 330 primary MACE events, with noninferiority margins of 1.8 and 1.3. The protocol/SAP documents the prespecified analysis framework. Final visits began before 330 events because of a sponsor funding decision reported as unrelated to trial data; 314 primary events ultimately occurred.

At final on-treatment assessment, efpeglenatide was associated with lower HbA1c (−1.24 percentage points), lower body weight (−2.6 kg), lower UACR (−21%), and slightly higher eGFR (+0.9 ml/min/1.73 m²) than placebo. These intermediate changes are supportive but should not substitute for the adjudicated clinical endpoints.

Protocol and Statistical Analysis Plan

Purpose: Preserve prespecified design and analysis boundaries

The 335-page protocol/SAP documents the event-driven target of approximately 330 primary MACE events, the 1.8 and 1.3 noninferiority margins, intention-to-treat follow-up, randomization stratification by SGLT2-inhibitor use, and the prespecified analysis framework.

Limits: Final visits began before 330 events because of a sponsor funding decision reported as unrelated to trial data; the article ultimately reports 314 primary events.

Limitations and uncertainty

  • Median follow-up was 1.81 years, shorter than the originally modeled maximum follow-up.
  • Only 314 primary events occurred versus 330 planned, reducing power relative to the design target.
  • The cohort was selected for established cardiovascular or kidney disease, limiting extrapolation to lower-risk type 2 diabetes.
  • Only 15.2% were using an SGLT2 inhibitor at baseline; the SGLT2 subgroup point estimates are compatible with consistency but the baseline-user interval is wide (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.37–1.30).
  • Sanofi funded the trial; the article reports sponsor participation in design/operations and states that the funding-driven early final-visit decision was unrelated to trial data. The steering committee and PHRI analysis/oversight provide important counterweights but do not erase sponsorship as an appraisal consideration.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use: high-quality landmark evidence on GLP-1 receptor agonist cardiovascular/renal outcomes and source material for class-evidence synthesis. Do not use alone as a 2026 prescribing or formulary authority.

Relevance 8/10

Landmark randomized cardiovascular/renal outcomes evidence for a GLP-1 receptor agonist in high-risk type 2 diabetes.

Evidence basis
Patient-centered cardiovascular and renal endpoints in 4076 randomized participants.
Principal limiting factor
Efpeglenatide itself is not sufficient to define current class-wide practice.
Would raise
Direct current guideline/label integration and head-to-head implementation data.
Would lower
Evidence that the source is no longer applicable to contemporary therapy.

Novelty 8/10

At publication, it extended cardiovascular-outcome evidence to an exendin-based once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist.

Evidence basis
Demonstrated MACE and renal benefit with efpeglenatide, including participants using SGLT2 inhibitors.
Principal limiting factor
Novelty is historical rather than current in 2026.
Would raise
Independent replication or mechanistic clarification unique to efpeglenatide.
Would lower
If results were fully redundant with earlier GLP-1 RA trials.

Method strength 8/10

Randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled, multicountry design with adjudicated clinical outcomes and high retention.

Evidence basis
344 sites/28 countries; 1:1:1 randomization; endpoint adjudication; 96.7% primary-outcome ascertainment.
Principal limiting factor
Final visits began before the planned 330 primary events; 314 occurred.
Would raise
Full event target, longer follow-up, and independent replication.
Would lower
Material differential attrition, unblinding, or adjudication concerns.

Evidence strength 8/10

Primary MACE and renal composite effects are quantitatively clear and supported by confidence intervals.

Evidence basis
MACE HR 0.73 (0.58–0.92); renal composite HR 0.68 (0.57–0.79).
Principal limiting factor
Short median follow-up and some secondary/component estimates are imprecise.
Would raise

Would lower

External validity 6/10

Findings apply best to high-risk type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular or kidney disease.

Evidence basis

Principal limiting factor
Selection for high-risk disease limits lower-risk generalizability; only 15.2% used SGLT2 inhibitors at baseline.
Would raise

Would lower
Evidence of strong effect modification in contemporary populations.

Confidence in appraisal: high for source-faithful trial appraisal because the primary article, protocol, and appendix were reconciled; moderate for current-practice translation because current external guidance/regulatory positioning was intentionally not substituted for the packet source.

Implementation / practice translation

Use now
Use the trial to support the proposition that efpeglenatide reduced MACE and a broad renal composite in high-risk type 2 diabetes.
Do not overclaim
Do not convert subgroup point estimates into proof of differential benefit, and do not equate the macroalbuminuria-heavy renal composite with definitive kidney-failure prevention.
Safety
Expect increased gastrointestinal intolerance and modestly more treatment discontinuation; no clear pancreatitis signal emerged in this trial.
Current-practice boundary
Pair this historical efficacy evidence with current guidelines, labels, availability, and formulary constraints before making 2026 treatment decisions.
