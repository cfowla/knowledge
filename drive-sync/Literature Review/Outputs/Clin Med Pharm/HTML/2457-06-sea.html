One-page synthesis

Core thesis. A clinical MRSA isolate can be catalase-negative while still being S. aureus subsp. aureus. In this strain, multiple phenotypic and molecular identification methods supported the species assignment, while catalase-gene sequencing identified a five-base deletion associated with a frameshift, premature termination, and loss of catalase activity.

The isolate came from routine tracheal secretion sampling in a 65-year-old surgical ICU patient without apparent infection at the time. The organism repeatedly remained catalase-negative through five subcultures but grew aerobically and anaerobically, was strongly coagulase- and DNase-positive, was identified as S. aureus by two commercial systems, and was confirmed by 16S rRNA sequencing. It showed an endemic-MRSA-like resistance phenotype and carried mecA.

Sequencing showed 99.60% identity to katA from Mu50/N315 and a five-base AAACG deletion at positions 1388–1392, producing a reading-frame shift and premature translation termination at bp 1418. The authors argue that catalase is not absolutely required for pathogenicity, but this case does not demonstrate infection or transmission: the strain was repeatedly found only in the same patient and not in other patients.

Section condensation

Background and rationale

The authors frame catalase as an oxidative-stress defense and virulence-associated enzyme in S. aureus. Catalase-negative staphylococci were known, including S. aureus subsp. anaerobius, but prior facultatively anaerobic catalase-negative S. aureus reports had not been molecularly characterized. The paper therefore focuses on defining an MRSA isolate that is catalase-negative yet otherwise consistent with S. aureus subsp. aureus.

Patient and isolate context

The strain was recovered from tracheal secretion obtained during routine microbiological investigation of a 65-year-old multimorbid man in the surgical ICU. The source explicitly states that no signs of infection were apparent at the time of sampling. Creamy beta-hemolytic colonies typical of S. aureus were recovered.

Phenotypic and species identification

Catalase testing remained negative after both aerobic and anaerobic incubation through the fifth subculture, while the isolate grew well in both conditions. Slide coagulase and DNase tests were strongly positive. The BBL CRYSTAL and API Staph systems identified S. aureus with probabilities of 98.6% and 97.8%, respectively; 16S rRNA sequencing confirmed S. aureus subsp. aureus. The strain was deposited as DSM 18827.

Antimicrobial susceptibility and MRSA confirmation

Disk diffusion following CLSI guidance found resistance to penicillin, oxacillin, cefaclor, cefuroxime, erythromycin, clindamycin, and ciprofloxacin, with susceptibility to gentamicin, rifampin, sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim, vancomycin, teicoplanin, and linezolid. The authors considered this consistent with local endemic MRSA. PCR detection of mecA and S. aureus-specific genes further supported MRSA identification.

Catalase-gene sequencing and molecular result

The catalase gene was PCR-amplified and both strands were sequenced by dye-terminator chemistry. The sequence was 99.60% identical to katA from Mu50/N315, with six nucleotide differences: a silent T substitution at bp 1152 and a five-base AAACG deletion at bp 1388–1392. The deletion shifted the reading frame, replaced downstream amino acids, and introduced premature termination at bp 1418; the paper links this change to loss of enzymatic activity.

Compared with katA from MSSA476, COL, NCTC 8325, USA300, and MW2, identity was 99.54% and the same five-base deletion was again confirmed. The authors distinguish this mutation from the previously described katB lesion in S. aureus subsp. anaerobius, while noting a similar functional consequence: frameshift, early stop, and catalase loss.

Interpretation, pathogenicity, and transmission

The discussion situates catalase in hydrogen-peroxide detoxification, oxidative-stress survival, long-term starvation, and ecological competition. The authors infer from prior catalase-negative isolates that catalase is not an absolute requirement for pathogenicity, but they explicitly leave open the possibility of reduced pathogenicity or transmission efficiency.

For this isolate, clinical inference is limited: it was repeatedly recovered from the same patient, was not found in other ICU or hospital patients, and was not linked to apparent infection. The report therefore supports an organism-identification and molecular-mechanism observation more strongly than a virulence or transmission conclusion.

Data availability

The catalase-gene sequence was submitted to GenBank as accession EF140590.

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best use: a compact diagnostic-microbiology edge case demonstrating that catalase negativity does not by itself exclude S. aureus, plus a molecular example of a katA loss-of-function lesion. Implementation / practice takeaways

Diagnostic interpretation

A catalase-negative result can occur in an isolate otherwise supported as S. aureus. When phenotype and organism identity conflict, this report supports confirmation with additional independent methods rather than exclusion based on catalase alone.

Molecular mechanism

A small deletion in the catalase structural gene can produce a frameshift and premature termination consistent with loss of catalase activity.

Clinical restraint

The isolate was not associated with apparent infection in this patient, so the paper should not be used to estimate the pathogenicity, transmissibility, or treatment implications of catalase-negative MRSA.

Read next

The most relevant follow-up literature is the prior catalase-negative S. aureus case literature and Sanz et al. on loss-of-function catalase mutations in subsp. anaerobius.
