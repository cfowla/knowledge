Scope warning: This appraisal describes and critiques the supplied 2022 review. It is not a current guideline or a complete basis for present-day SGLT2 formulary interchangeability.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used as a compact map of landmark SGLT2 cardiorenal trials and a hypothesis-generating comparison of agent rankings. Do not use the P-score hierarchy as direct evidence that one agent is clinically superior to another.

The review synthesized 10 randomized outcome trials of canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, and sotagliflozin. Relative to placebo, the reported point estimates generally favored SGLT2 inhibition for all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, heart-failure hospitalization, and renal composite outcomes. The most favorable P-score rankings were empagliflozin for all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, canagliflozin for HF hospitalization, and dapagliflozin for the renal composite.

The strongest inference is class-level benefit versus placebo, not agent-to-agent superiority. The network is placebo-centered, active-agent comparisons are mostly indirect and often imprecise, trial populations and endpoint definitions are heterogeneous, and no clear CINeMA/GRADE certainty framework or formal transitivity/inconsistency analysis is reported. Internal source conflicts further weaken confidence in exact rankings: total sample size differs between abstract and Results; renal-composite definitions differ; two renal HRs conflict between Results and the abstract/supplement; and DAPA-HF diabetes status is described inconsistently.

Methods and coverage

Search and eligibility

PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library were searched from inception through 11 November 2021, English only. Eligible RCTs enrolled adults with T2DM, age ≥18 years, HbA1c 6.5–10.5%, treatment duration ≥13 weeks, and at least one cardiovascular or renal outcome. Duplicate independent selection/extraction was reported.

The review used PRISMA-NMA and a registered PROSPERO protocol. Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment and Review Manager were used for study quality. The synthesis used frequentist netmeta in R 4.1.2, hazard ratios with 95% intervals, indirect comparisons, and P-score rankings.

Clinical question

Population: patients represented in the included SGLT2 outcome trials, described by the review primarily as T2DM, with CKD/HF-enriched trials included. Intervention: individual SGLT2 inhibitors. Comparator: placebo and indirect active-agent comparisons. Outcomes: all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, HF hospitalization, renal composite.

Coverage: all five main-text figures, the PRISMA workflow, and both supplementary tables were reconciled as structured blocks. Pure bibliography pages were not atomized; their 51 references were extracted into a separate task queue.

Results

Study set

The search yielded 4,170 records, 2,097 after duplicate removal, 89 full texts, and 10 included trials: CANVAS, CANVAS-R, CREDENCE, DECLARE-TIMI 58, DAPA-CKD, DAPA-HF, EMPA-REG OUTCOME, VERTIS-CV, SCORED, and SOLOIST-WHF. The abstract reports 68,723 participants; the Results section reports 67,823.

All trials were placebo comparisons. The review reports 4,824 deaths among 67,823 participants (7.11%). Nine trials with 63,079 participants contributed to the renal composite.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 9/10

Directly addresses comparative cardiovascular and renal effects across SGLT2 inhibitors.

Evidence basis: 10 RCT network; outcomes align with formulary questions.

Principal limit: The review is from 2022 and predates later evidence.

Would raise/lower: Current high-quality agent-comparison evidence consistent with the same question.

Novelty: 5/10

Agent-level ranking across major cardiorenal outcomes was useful at publication but is not methodologically novel now.

Evidence basis: Frequentist network ranking across five agents.

Principal limit: Primarily reuses landmark trials already widely synthesized.

Would raise/lower: A newer network with contemporary trials, safety, and robust certainty analysis.

Method strength: 5/10

Systematic RCT synthesis is a strength, but the placebo-centered network and incomplete NMA diagnostics materially limit comparative inference.

Evidence basis: Registered review, duplicate extraction, Cochrane RoB, netmeta.

Principal limit: No clear transitivity/inconsistency assessment; heterogeneous populations and endpoint definitions.

Would raise/lower: Prespecified NMA diagnostics, certainty framework, harmonized endpoints, and stronger direct evidence.

Evidence strength: 5/10

Class-versus-placebo effects are supported better than active-agent rankings.

Evidence basis: Large randomized trial base with HRs and CIs.

Principal limit: Internal numerical/definition conflicts and indirect rankings; several CIs cross 1.

Would raise/lower: Error-free reconciled data plus CINeMA/GRADE and direct head-to-head evidence.

External validity: 5/10

The trial mix spans T2DM, CKD, and HF populations, but that heterogeneity complicates application to a single formulary population.

Evidence basis: Multiple large international outcomes trials.

Principal limit: Population eligibility and diabetes-status descriptions are inconsistent; baseline renal/CV risk varies.

Would raise/lower: Population-stratified analyses with consistent eligibility and endpoint definitions.

Implementation value: 4/10

Useful as a map of landmark evidence, but insufficient for choosing an interchangeable preferred SGLT2 inhibitor.

Evidence basis: Provides comparative point estimates and rankings.

Principal limit: Safety, dosing, contraindications, cost, contemporary indications, and certainty are not integrated.

Would raise/lower: Current comparative effectiveness/safety data and implementation constraints.

Main strengths

  • Registered systematic-review workflow with broad database search and duplicate extraction.
  • Large randomized outcomes-trial evidence base and clinically important mortality/HF/renal endpoints.
  • Agent-specific effect estimates, indirect comparisons, and explicit ranking plots.

Main limitations

  • Placebo-centered network makes comparative agent rankings mainly indirect.
  • Clinical populations, baseline kidney function, and CV-risk definitions are heterogeneous.
  • Renal endpoint definitions vary within the article and across trials.
  • No clear formal transitivity/inconsistency analysis or CINeMA/GRADE certainty assessment is reported.
  • Internal numeric conflicts undermine confidence in exact data transcription and rankings.
  • Safety outcomes and implementation constraints are not developed enough for formulary selection.
  • Conclusion language about benefits regardless of sex/diabetes status is not supported by a presented subgroup analysis in this review.
Interpretation boundary: Point estimates below 1 were repeatedly described by the authors as “reductions,” including estimates whose 95% CIs crossed 1. Directional point estimates should not be treated as demonstrated superiority.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Use as a map of which landmark trials underpin class-level cardiovascular and renal benefits and as a source of hypotheses about possible agent differences.

Do not use for

Do not use P-score rank alone to choose a preferred formulary agent, assert interchangeability, or infer head-to-head superiority.

What to verify next

Current guideline/label indications, newer comparative evidence, CKD/HF population-specific data, safety, dosing/renal thresholds, and direct/credible active-agent comparisons.
