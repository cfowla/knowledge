One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. ACCOMPLISH is a large outcome-driven head-to-head trial showing fewer composite cardiovascular events with benazepril plus amlodipine than with benazepril plus hydrochlorothiazide in older, high-risk hypertensive patients, despite only a small difference in achieved blood pressure.

The trial randomized 11,506 participants to fixed-combination benazepril-amlodipine or benazepril-hydrochlorothiazide. The primary composite occurred in 9.6% versus 11.8% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72-0.90; P<0.001), an absolute difference of 2.2 percentage points. A harder secondary composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke also favored the amlodipine combination (5.0% vs 6.3%; HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.67-0.92; P=0.002). The trial stopped early after crossing its prespecified efficacy boundary.

Interpretation is strengthened by randomization, double blinding, large sample size, central blinded endpoint adjudication, and intention-to-treat analysis. It is constrained by early termination, a broad primary composite that included revascularization and unstable-angina hospitalization, sponsor funding and operational/statistical involvement, and a population enriched for older patients with diabetes and prior cardiovascular disease. The comparison is specifically against hydrochlorothiazide, so the article does not establish equivalence of its result to every thiazide or thiazide-like strategy.

Section-by-section condensation

Abstract

The authors framed uncertainty about the optimal initial combination regimen for hypertension and hypothesized that an ACE inhibitor plus a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker would reduce cardiovascular events more than an ACE inhibitor plus a thiazide. The randomized double-blind trial assigned 11,506 high-risk hypertensive patients to benazepril-amlodipine or benazepril-hydrochlorothiazide.

The trial was stopped early after mean follow-up of about 36 months. The primary event rate was 9.6% versus 11.8% (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72-0.90; P<0.001), and the secondary composite of CV death, nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke favored benazepril-amlodipine (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.67-0.92; P=0.002). The abstract concludes superiority of the amlodipine combination in this high-risk population.

Introduction / rationale

The article notes that most high-risk hypertensive patients require multiple agents and that prior single-agent trials often required add-on therapy, complicating attribution of outcome differences. Contemporary guidance cited by the authors favored thiazide-containing combinations, while mechanistic and intermediate-outcome literature suggested possible vascular benefits from ACE inhibitor + amlodipine therapy.

ACCOMPLISH therefore tested two initial combination strategies directly, with the clinical hypothesis that benazepril plus amlodipine would improve cardiovascular outcomes compared with benazepril plus hydrochlorothiazide.

Methods

The multicenter double-blind trial recruited from 548 centers in the United States, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, and Finland. Patients had hypertension plus high cardiovascular risk. Entry occurred without a washout; randomization was centralized 1:1. Both groups used single-capsule regimens and could receive selected additional antihypertensives. Prespecified endpoints were independently adjudicated by committees unaware of treatment allocation.

The primary analysis was intention-to-treat. Kaplan-Meier curves and the log-rank test assessed time to first primary event; univariate Cox regression estimated hazard ratios. An O'Brien-Fleming spending approach governed interim analyses. The original target of 1,642 primary events for 90% power was amended in October 2007 to 1,199 events for 80% power.

Results

Of 13,782 screened patients, 11,506 were randomized. Baseline characteristics were similar. Mean follow-up was 35.7 versus 35.6 months, and only 117 participants (1.0%) were ultimately lost to follow-up. Achieved blood pressure was 131.6/73.3 versus 132.5/74.4 mm Hg; average control below 140/90 mm Hg was 75.4% versus 72.4%.

The primary endpoint favored benazepril-amlodipine (552 events, 9.6%) over benazepril-hydrochlorothiazide (679, 11.8%): HR 0.80 (0.72-0.90), P<0.001. The secondary CV death/nonfatal MI/nonfatal stroke composite was 5.0% versus 6.3%, HR 0.79 (0.67-0.92), P=0.002. Fatal/nonfatal MI and coronary revascularization were individually lower; CV death, stroke, unstable-angina hospitalization, sudden-arrest resuscitation, all-cause death, and HF hospitalization did not show statistically significant between-group differences at conventional thresholds.

Discussion and stated limitations

The authors interpret the small achieved blood-pressure difference together with the outcome difference as evidence that the regimen comparison had clinical relevance beyond merely obtaining high rates of BP control. They also caution that the result should not be read as evidence that diuretics are ineffective, and discuss possible differences between hydrochlorothiazide and chlorthalidone and possible combination-specific vascular effects.

The authors identify limitations: the primary composite was intentionally broad and included revascularization and unstable-angina hospitalization, which can involve clinician judgment; many participants had prior coronary disease and diabetes and were not fully representative of the general hypertensive population; and the diuretic-based combination may not have been optimal for some patients. These constraints temper the generalization of the trial's superiority finding.

Evaluation and appraisal

9/10

Relevance

Rationale: Directly addresses antihypertensive combination choice using cardiovascular outcomes in a high-risk population.

Evidence basis: Large comparative clinical-outcomes trial with explicit dosing and clinically meaningful endpoints.

Principal limiting factor: Applies to a high-risk, older population rather than uncomplicated hypertension.

What would change the score: Higher if the review question specifically concerns high-risk combination therapy; lower for low-risk or first-line monotherapy questions.

9/10

Novelty

Rationale: The study compared two initial fixed-combination strategies using morbidity and mortality outcomes rather than only blood-pressure surrogates.

Evidence basis: Randomized head-to-head design between ACE inhibitor + CCB and ACE inhibitor + thiazide strategies.

Principal limiting factor: Novelty is historical to the 2008 evidence context.

What would change the score: Would be lower if judged only against later evidence rather than its publication-era contribution.

8/10

Method strength

Rationale: Randomization, double blinding, centralized adjudication, intention-to-treat analysis, and prespecified interim boundaries support internal validity.

Evidence basis: 11,506 randomized participants; blinded endpoint adjudication; Kaplan-Meier/log-rank/Cox analysis; DSMC.

Principal limiting factor: Early termination and substantial sponsor operational/statistical involvement constrain confidence in exact effect magnitude and independence.

What would change the score: Higher with full independent replication and complete protocol/supplement review; lower if concealed allocation/blinding details proved inadequate.

8/10

Evidence strength

Rationale: The primary endpoint showed a clear effect with narrow uncertainty and key secondary composites were directionally consistent.

Evidence basis: Primary HR 0.80 (0.72-0.90), P<0.001; secondary CV death/MI/stroke HR 0.79 (0.67-0.92), P=0.002.

Principal limiting factor: Several individual components were nonsignificant, and the broad composite included procedure/admission outcomes partly susceptible to clinician judgment.

What would change the score: Higher with concordant replication on hard endpoints alone; lower if benefit were driven primarily by subjective components.

6/10

External validity

Rationale: The multinational sample was large but selected for high cardiovascular risk and had heavy diabetes/coronary-disease burden.

Evidence basis: Mean age 68.4 years; ~60% diabetes; 548 centers in U.S. and Nordic countries.

Principal limiting factor: Not fully representative of the broad hypertension population; comparator was specifically hydrochlorothiazide.

What would change the score: Higher with diverse lower-risk populations and alternative thiazide/thiazide-like comparators.

7/10

Implementation value

Rationale: The trial provides directly usable regimen doses, titration, BP targets, outcome estimates, and safety frequencies.

Evidence basis: Initial 20/5 mg vs 20/12.5 mg combinations, benazepril 40 mg, amlodipine 10 mg or HCTZ 25 mg maxima.

Principal limiting factor: The artifact does not verify current guidelines or whether present-day practice should prefer these exact regimens.

What would change the score: Higher when paired with current guideline and comparative-effectiveness evidence.

Validity

Strengths: randomized double-blind design; large sample; explicit dosing and titration; central blinded adjudication; intention-to-treat analysis; time-to-event methods; prespecified interim monitoring; high follow-up completeness. Constraints: early stopping reduced planned observation; sponsor involvement extended through funding, data infrastructure, operations, and statistical analysis; the primary composite contained outcomes more sensitive to clinical decision-making than death/MI/stroke.

Sponsor and disclosure context

The trial was supported by Novartis. The Methods state that sponsor personnel participated in operations/data gathering and that analyses were performed by the sponsor in coordination with the executive committee, with statistical analysis conducted by a Novartis group under a plan devised by the executive committee. Several authors reported financial relationships with Novartis and other companies; four authors were Novartis employees, and three of those employees were reported to own Novartis stock. Independent DSMC and endpoint-adjudication structures partially mitigate but do not erase this conflict context.

Practice translation

The source supports a conclusion that, in the studied high-risk population and using the tested doses, benazepril plus amlodipine reduced the primary composite relative to benazepril plus hydrochlorothiazide. It does not establish that all ACE inhibitor + CCB combinations are superior to all thiazide/thiazide-like combinations, nor does it determine present-day guideline preference.

Do not overclaim: individual component results were mixed; overall mortality and HF hospitalization were not significantly different; the comparator was hydrochlorothiazide rather than every possible diuretic strategy; and current clinical recommendations were not verified for this source-only appraisal.
