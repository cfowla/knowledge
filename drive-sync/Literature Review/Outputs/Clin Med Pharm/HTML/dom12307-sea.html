One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. Best used as a historical quantitative synthesis of early empagliflozin trials, not as a stand-alone current-practice source.

The review asked whether empagliflozin improves glycaemic control and safety outcomes in adults with type 2 diabetes compared with placebo or other antidiabetic drugs. It included 10 randomized studies (11 records; 6,203 participants) lasting 12–90 weeks. Against placebo, empagliflozin lowered HbA1c by about 0.6 percentage points at both 10 mg and 25 mg daily, reduced body weight by about 1.8 kg, and lowered blood pressure. Limited active-comparator data suggested HbA1c effects similar to metformin or sitagliptin while retaining weight and blood-pressure advantages.

Safety findings were mixed but internally coherent: hypoglycaemia and urinary tract infection were not increased in the pooled analyses, whereas genital tract infection was increased roughly three- to four-fold. The evidence base was materially weakened by short duration, surrogate outcomes, only three active-controlled trials, no cardiovascular-outcome results, and high risk of bias in 8/10 trials because LOCF was used for missing HbA1c data. All included trials were industry funded. These constraints are central to interpreting the otherwise precise pooled metabolic estimates.

Methods and evidence base

The authors searched Medline, Embase, and Cochrane through 19 December 2013, supplemented by diabetes-meeting abstracts/e-posters (2009–2013), forward citation searching, the manufacturer website, and ClinicalTrials.gov through 17 March 2014. Eligibility required randomized trials in adults with type 2 diabetes, empagliflozin versus placebo or another antidiabetic drug, and at least 12 weeks of treatment. Screening, extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment were conducted by independent reviewers with adjudication.

Continuous outcomes were pooled as inverse-variance random-effects weighted mean differences; dichotomous outcomes as Mantel-Haenszel random-effects odds ratios. Heterogeneity used Cochran Q and I². The review analyzed 10 mg and 25 mg doses separately and separated placebo from active comparators. Eight of ten included studies were rated high risk of bias because LOCF was used for missing HbA1c data; only two were rated low risk. Funnel-plot inspection and Egger testing (p=0.56) did not indicate small-study effects.

Study flow: 172 database records + 5 additional records → 35 duplicates removed → 142 screened → 43 full texts assessed → 32 excluded → 11 records / 10 randomized studies included.

Major hypoglycaemia was uncommon (8/3,165 participants in empagliflozin arms vs 6/1,695 in placebo arms) and occurred in participants with renal impairment and/or basal insulin background therapy. Seven trials reported all-cause mortality: 5 deaths among 2,874 empagliflozin participants and 5 among 1,704 controls. None of the included trials reported cardiovascular outcome results.

CKD evidence reported in Supplementary Table S2

The review reproduced trial-level placebo-corrected HbA1c changes for empagliflozin 25 mg after 52 weeks: stage 2 CKD -0.66% (95% CI -0.88 to -0.44), stage 3A -0.54% (-0.73 to -0.35), stage 3B -0.37% (-0.59 to -0.15), and stage 4 +0.48% (-0.06 to +1.02). These are secondary reports of underlying trial results, not pooled estimates generated by this review.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 7/10

Rationale: Directly addresses empagliflozin efficacy and safety and is pertinent to SGLT2 formulary evidence, but it is an early 2014 evidence snapshot.

Evidence basis: 10 RCTs / 6,203 participants with drug-specific pooled efficacy and safety estimates.

Principal limit: No contemporary cardiovascular/renal outcome evidence and no direct dapagliflozin comparison.

What changes it: Higher if used for historical drug-efficacy context; lower if used alone for current formulary equivalence.

Novelty 8/10

Rationale: At publication, the review provided an empagliflozin-specific synthesis when individual-trial evidence was still emerging.

Evidence basis: Broad systematic search and pooled drug-specific results across doses and comparators.

Principal limit: The review itself notes prior SGLT2-class meta-analyses and sparse empagliflozin data.

What changes it: Higher with unique long-term outcomes; lower when judged only from a modern perspective.

Method strength 7/10

Rationale: The review used PRISMA reporting, duplicate screening/extraction, Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, random-effects synthesis, heterogeneity checks, and publication-bias assessment.

Evidence basis: Methods are explicit and reproducible; grey literature was searched.

Principal limit: Four records were abstract/e-poster only, and meta-analytic estimates inherit weaknesses of the trials.

What changes it: Higher with protocol registration and stronger primary-trial missing-data methods.

Evidence strength 5/10

Rationale: Metabolic efficacy signals are consistent, but the evidence base was short, surrogate-driven, and frequently high risk of bias from LOCF.

Evidence basis: HbA1c, weight, blood pressure, and several safety outcomes were quantitatively pooled.

Principal limit: Eight of ten trials were high risk of bias; all were industry funded; hard cardiovascular outcomes were absent.

What changes it: Higher with long-duration hard-outcome trials and lower risk of bias.

External validity 6/10

Rationale: The trials included varied background therapies and some renal impairment/hypertension populations, but subgroup inference was limited.

Evidence basis: Ten studies spanning monotherapy and several add-on regimens.

Principal limit: No participant-level data for effect modification; early trial populations and short durations limit broad practice generalization.

What changes it: Higher with diverse real-world populations and prespecified subgroup evidence.

Implementation value 5/10

Rationale: Useful for historical estimates of glycaemic, weight, blood-pressure, and genital-infection effects, but insufficient as a stand-alone current practice basis.

Evidence basis: Clinically interpretable pooled effect sizes and safety signals.

Principal limit: No cardiovascular outcomes, few active-comparator trials, no direct SGLT2 head-to-head evidence.

What changes it: Higher when combined with later outcome and comparative-effectiveness evidence.

Main limitations: short trials and surrogate endpoints; 8/10 high risk of bias due LOCF; all included trials industry funded; only three active-comparator trials; no participant-level subgroup analysis; no direct SGLT2 head-to-head trials; no cardiovascular outcome results.
Main strengths: broad database and grey-literature search, dual review/extraction, explicit Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, random-effects pooling, heterogeneity reporting, dose-specific analysis, active-comparator separation, and publication-bias assessment.

Implementation / practice takeaways

  • Use the review for historical magnitude estimates: HbA1c approximately -0.6% versus placebo, weight approximately -1.8 kg, systolic BP about -3.5 to -4.2 mm Hg.
  • Preserve the genital-infection signal as a consistent safety finding; pooled UTI and hypoglycaemia estimates did not show an increase.
  • Do not infer contemporary empagliflozin–dapagliflozin interchangeability from this review. It contains no direct SGLT2 head-to-head trial and only indirect discussion of other class members.
  • Do not use this source alone to make claims about cardiovascular or renal hard outcomes; the included trials did not report cardiovascular outcomes and were short.
  • When reusing pooled estimates, retain the risk-of-bias and heterogeneity context, especially LOCF and I²=66–85% in some 25-mg analyses.
