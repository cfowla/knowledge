One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In patients with type 2 diabetes and nephropathy, losartan added to conventional antihypertensive therapy reduced progression to major renal endpoints compared with placebo, with no significant reduction in all-cause mortality or the overall cardiovascular composite.

The trial randomized 1513 patients to losartan or placebo and used a composite primary endpoint of confirmed doubling of serum creatinine, end-stage renal disease (ESRD), or death. The primary endpoint occurred in 43.5% versus 47.1% of patients, corresponding to a 16% risk reduction (95% CI 2%-28%; P=0.02). The renal components drove the benefit: doubling of serum creatinine was reduced by 25% and ESRD by 28%, whereas mortality was unchanged. Blood-pressure-adjusted analyses retained similar renal effects.

Cardiovascular results were mixed. The prespecified cardiovascular morbidity/mortality composite was not significantly different (32.9% vs 35.2%; risk reduction 10%; P=0.26), but first hospitalization for heart failure was lower with losartan (11.9% vs 16.7%; risk reduction 32%; P=0.005). Proteinuria fell by an average of 35%, and decline in renal function was slower. The article reports generally similar tolerability to placebo, while study-drug discontinuation was common in both arms.

Best-supported conclusion: The source provides strong randomized evidence for a renal-protective effect of losartan in the studied population, above the small observed between-group blood-pressure difference.
Do not overclaim: The trial did not show an all-cause mortality benefit or a significant benefit for the overall cardiovascular composite, and it did not directly compare losartan with an ACE inhibitor.

Methods and validity-relevant design

Design and setting

Investigator-initiated, multinational, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial at 250 centers in 28 countries. Endpoint adjudication was performed by an independent committee blinded to treatment assignment.

Eligibility

Participants were 31-70 years old with type 2 diabetes and nephropathy. Nephropathy required substantial albuminuria/proteinuria plus serum creatinine 1.3-3.0 mg/dL, with a higher lower-bound creatinine threshold for men over 60 kg. Exclusions included type 1 diabetes, nondiabetic renal disease, recent major cardiovascular events/procedures, and any history of heart failure.

Treatment

Losartan began at 50 mg once daily and could be increased to 100 mg once daily if seated trough BP remained above 140/90 mm Hg; 71% received 100 mg. Other antihypertensives were permitted except ACE inhibitors and other ARBs. Placebo was matched to the same background-therapy framework.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary endpoint was time to first confirmed doubling of serum creatinine, ESRD, or death. ESRD was long-term dialysis or renal transplantation. The cardiovascular secondary composite included MI, stroke, first hospitalization for heart failure or unstable angina, revascularization, or cardiovascular death. Primary analyses were intention-to-treat using Cox regression, with proteinuria stratification and geographic-region adjustment; the critical P value for the primary hypothesis was 0.048 because of interim monitoring.

Early termination

The treatment-blinded steering committee stopped the study early because new external evidence suggested ACE inhibitors might reduce cardiovascular events in patients with renal impairment. The resulting mean follow-up was 3.4 years rather than the originally planned longer follow-up.

Safety and treatment persistence

Study treatment was discontinued by 46.5% in the losartan group and 53.5% in placebo. Adverse clinical events led to discontinuation in 17.2% versus 21.7%. Creatinine- or potassium-related discontinuation was uncommon in both groups (creatinine 1.5% vs 1.2%; potassium 1.1% vs 0.5%). Outcome status for dialysis, transplantation, and death was obtained for all but three losartan patients.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a landmark randomized trial with clinically meaningful renal endpoints, clear methods, and a result pattern that remains internally coherent across hard renal outcomes and renal surrogates.

Rationale: Directly addresses diabetic nephropathy, renal progression, antihypertensive pharmacotherapy, and hard kidney outcomes.

Evidence basis: Large clinical RCT with clinically important renal endpoints.

Limiting factor: Current-practice placement is outside this historical source.

Evidence basis: Authors position the trial against prior type 1 diabetes and smaller type 2 studies.

Limiting factor: Historical novelty is judged from the source framing rather than a full contemporaneous literature reconstruction.

Method strength
9/10

Rationale: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multinational design with blinded endpoint adjudication and prespecified analyses.

Evidence basis: 1513 participants; 250 centers; 28 countries; ITT Cox analysis; sensitivity analyses.

Limiting factor: Early stopping for external evidence and high study-drug discontinuation complicate exposure duration.

Would raise/lower: Longer planned follow-up and lower discontinuation would strengthen the design further.

Evidence strength
9/10

Rationale: Hard renal endpoints were statistically significant and internally consistent with proteinuria and renal-function slopes.

Evidence basis: Primary composite and renal components had significant effect estimates with 95% CIs; mortality was neutral.

Limiting factor: The primary composite mixes renal outcomes with death, but benefit is demonstrably driven by renal components.

Would raise/lower: Direct active-comparator evidence and longer follow-up would increase confidence in comparative effectiveness.

External validity
7/10

Rationale: Multinational enrollment improves geographic breadth, but eligibility was selective.

Evidence basis: 28-country enrollment with overt nephropathy and impaired renal function.

Limiting factor: Age capped at 70; recent major cardiovascular disease and heart failure were excluded; trial excluded ACEi/ARB background therapy.

Would raise/lower: Evidence in older, higher-CV-risk, and broader contemporary treatment populations would improve generalizability.

Implementation value
7/10

Rationale: The dosing, BP target, monitoring schedule, endpoint definitions, and background-therapy constraints are clearly described.

Evidence basis: Losartan titration and allowed antihypertensive classes are explicit.

Limiting factor: The article predates contemporary treatment combinations and does not compare current alternatives.

Would raise/lower: Current guideline/label verification and modern combination-therapy evidence are needed before direct practice implementation.

Key strengths

  • Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multinational design.
  • Blinded endpoint adjudication and prespecified intention-to-treat time-to-event analysis.
  • Hard kidney outcomes (ESRD and confirmed doubling of serum creatinine), not only proteinuria.
  • Consistent direction across primary renal outcomes, proteinuria, and renal-function decline.
  • Blood-pressure-adjusted analyses preserved the renal effect.

Key limitations / bias considerations

  • Study stopped earlier than originally planned because of external ACE-inhibitor evidence.
  • Large proportions discontinued randomized study medication, although outcome follow-up was maintained for most participants.
  • No direct ACE-inhibitor comparison; the article explicitly cautions against extrapolating between classes.
  • Heart failure and recent high-risk cardiovascular conditions were excluded, constraining cardiovascular generalizability.
  • Merck funded the study; sponsor employees participated in governance/authorship and three authors were Merck employees. Independent blinded endpoint adjudication mitigates but does not eliminate sponsorship concerns.
