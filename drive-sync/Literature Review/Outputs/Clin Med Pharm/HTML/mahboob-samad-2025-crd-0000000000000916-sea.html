One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. Highly relevant to an empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin formulary evidence review, but the observational design, substantial heterogeneity, and sensitivity instability make it supportive rather than definitive comparative evidence.

The review pooled eight observational real-world comparative studies involving 428,940 participants. Across six cardiovascular outcome families, the random-effects point estimates did not show statistically significant differences between empagliflozin and dapagliflozin: all-cause death RR 0.91, cardiovascular death RR 1.12, myocardial infarction RR 1.01, stroke RR 0.90, MACE RR 1.03, and heart failure-related events RR 1.07. Confidence intervals crossed 1 for every primary pooled estimate.

The apparent similarity is less stable than the abstract alone suggests. Heterogeneity was high for all-cause death (I² 94%), cardiovascular death (84%), MI (77%), MACE (94%), and HF-related events (86%). Leave-one-out analysis changed all-cause mortality to RR 0.76 (0.72–0.80) after excluding Bonnesen 2024 and cardiovascular death to RR 1.19 (1.05–1.34) after excluding Engström 2024. MACE definitions also varied materially across studies. These features argue against treating the pooled null estimates as proof of equivalence.

Key interpretation boundary: absence of a detected difference is not an equivalence or noninferiority demonstration. No direct head-to-head randomized trial was included.

Methods

Search and selection

The authors report PRISMA and Cochrane guidance. Two reviewers independently searched PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from inception to November 7, 2024 without language or publication-date restrictions, and bibliographies of previous meta-analyses were reviewed manually. Supplementary Table 1 reports 287 PubMed, 343 Embase, and 47 Cochrane records.

Eligible studies involved adults with type 2 diabetes and compared cardiovascular outcomes involving dapagliflozin and empagliflozin. Reviews, editorials, commentaries, and reports without relevant cardiovascular events were excluded. The eligibility wording also says studies comparing either agent with placebo or other SGLT2 inhibitors were included because no direct head-to-head RCT was available; however, the final included set is described as eight direct comparative observational studies.

Outcomes and analysis

Primary outcomes were all-cause death and cardiovascular death. Secondary outcomes were stroke, myocardial infarction, MACE, and HF-related events, with HF-related events defined as HF hospitalization or HF-related death. ROBINS-I was used for observational-study risk of bias. RevMan 5.4.1, a random-effects model, risk ratios with 95% CIs, I², and leave-one-out sensitivity analyses were used.

Evaluation and appraisal

9/10

Relevance

Directly addresses empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin cardiovascular effectiveness, making it highly relevant to SGLT2 formulary comparison.

Evidence basis: Eight comparative real-world cohorts and six cardiovascular outcome families.

Limiting factor: Does not evaluate inpatient substitution, safety, indication parity, or operational policy.

Would raise/lower: Direct head-to-head randomized evidence or a broader formulary decision analysis would raise this score.

7/10

Novelty

Synthesizes several newer direct real-world comparisons into one cardiovascular comparison.

Evidence basis: Includes eight observational comparative studies through November 2024.

Limiting factor: The question had already been addressed by earlier comparative meta-analyses and cohorts.

Would raise/lower: A prespecified individual-patient-data analysis or randomized head-to-head trial would increase novelty.

5/10

Method strength

Search, ROBINS-I assessment, random-effects pooling, and sensitivity analyses are useful, but all included evidence is observational and reporting inconsistencies weaken confidence.

Evidence basis: Three databases, duplicate review, ROBINS-I, random effects, leave-one-out analyses.

Limiting factor: Very high heterogeneity for several endpoints; no registration, certainty framework, or publication-bias assessment reported; PRISMA exclusion wording conflicts with included designs.

Would raise/lower: Prospective registration, formal certainty assessment, bias diagnostics, and randomized comparative evidence would raise the score.

5/10

Evidence strength

The pooled estimates are mostly null, but several are highly heterogeneous and two sensitivity analyses become statistically significant after removal of one study.

Evidence basis: Large aggregate N and outcome-specific RRs with CIs across comparative cohorts.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding, variable outcome definitions, high I², and sensitivity instability limit causal interpretation.

Would raise/lower: Consistent head-to-head randomized results or low-heterogeneity, well-controlled target-trial emulations across diverse settings would raise the score.

7/10

External validity

Large real-world cohorts improve practice relevance, but the evidence is concentrated in Scandinavian and East Asian health systems.

Evidence basis: 428,940 participants across Denmark, Sweden, Norway, South Korea, Japan, and Taiwan.

Limiting factor: Limited direct representation of other health systems and clinically important subgroups; observational treatment selection may vary by country.

Would raise/lower: Replication in broader geographic and demographic populations with harmonized endpoints would raise the score.

6/10

Implementation value

Useful for judging absence of obvious cardiovascular superiority, but insufficient alone to justify therapeutic interchangeability.

Evidence basis: Direct comparative CV outcomes are relevant to agent selection.

Limiting factor: Comparative safety, current indications, inpatient use, renal outcomes, cost, adherence, and formulary operations were not analyzed.

Would raise/lower: A decision-focused synthesis integrating those domains would raise the score.

Independent appraisal

What the source supports: Across the available comparative observational cohorts, there is no robust pooled signal that either empagliflozin or dapagliflozin consistently dominates the other across the six analyzed cardiovascular outcome families.

What it does not support: It does not demonstrate cardiovascular equivalence or noninferiority, does not establish causal interchangeability, and does not directly support claims of similar safety or superior cost-effectiveness. The high heterogeneity and two sensitivity analyses that become significant after removal of one study are material.

Confidence in appraisal: high. The full supplied main article and supplement were available and all figures/tables were reconciled; the main uncertainty is in the underlying observational evidence, not source access.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Useful now
Use as supportive comparative evidence that obvious cardiovascular superiority of one agent over the other has not been demonstrated in current real-world cohorts.
Do not overclaim
Do not translate nonsignificant pooled differences into therapeutic equivalence or automatic inpatient interchangeability.
Formulary decision gap
Integrate direct comparative renal outcomes, HF evidence, safety, labeling/indications, inpatient guidance, operational constraints, acquisition cost, and patient-specific access before a formulary interchange conclusion.
