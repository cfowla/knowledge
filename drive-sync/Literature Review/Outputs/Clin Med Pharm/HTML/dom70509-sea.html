One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Among adults with type 2 diabetes undergoing surgery, documented preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor use was associated with lower odds and lower standardized risk of postoperative AKI after inverse-probability weighting, with directionally consistent sensitivity, subgroup, and negative-control analyses.

The primary cohort contained 2,499 eligible operations after exclusions and overlap trimming. AKI within 7 days occurred in 137/738 SGLT2i users (18.6%) and 454/1,761 non-users (25.8%). The adjusted odds ratio was 0.71 (95% CI 0.56–0.91; p=0.007), with a standardized risk difference of −0.049 (95% CI −0.082 to −0.014; p=0.005). Estimates remained protective when chronic kidney disease replaced baseline creatinine in the adjustment model and when patients with eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m² were excluded. Emergency and non-emergency subgroups also showed similar point estimates, with no evidence of a risk-difference interaction.

The analysis is stronger than a routine retrospective association because the authors specified a target-trial framework, used a directed acyclic graph to select adjustment variables, weighted toward the average treatment effect among the treated, trimmed non-overlap, checked post-weighting balance, ran prespecified sensitivity/subgroup analyses, and used both an exposure and outcome negative control. The central residual problem is still non-random treatment assignment. Exact SGLT2i initiation and preoperative withholding durations were unavailable, postoperative creatinine measurement was not standardized, and the study came from a single tertiary centre. These constraints limit causal certainty and direct protocol translation.

Section condensation

Abstract

The study asked whether preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor use was associated with postoperative AKI in adults with type 2 diabetes undergoing surgery. The authors emulated a target trial with routinely collected clinical data and used inverse-probability weighting to compare preoperative SGLT2i users with non-users.

The weighted primary analysis favored SGLT2i exposure for AKI within 7 postoperative days (OR 0.71; 95% CI 0.56–0.91). The authors interpret the findings as evidence of potential perioperative renal protection and call for larger randomized studies.

Introduction

Postoperative AKI is common, clinically important, and particularly relevant in diabetes. SGLT2 inhibitors reduce kidney events in chronic cardiorenal settings, but perioperative evidence has been limited and concentrated in cardiac or elective surgery, while perioperative practice is also shaped by ketoacidosis concerns.

The study was designed to broaden that evidence base across surgical specialties and emergency status, using causal-inference methods rather than a conventional unadjusted retrospective comparison.

Materials and Methods

Eligible admissions were adults ≥18 years with type 2 diabetes receiving glucose-lowering medication, admitted for at least 24 hours, undergoing surgery under general anaesthesia, with a baseline creatinine in the prior 365 days and at least one creatinine within 7 postoperative days. Type 1 diabetes, diet-controlled type 2 diabetes, non-surgical procedures, preoperative AKI, missing required covariates, and non-overlap in propensity support were excluded. Exposure was documented pre-admission/preoperative use of empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or ertugliflozin; exact treatment duration and withholding duration were unavailable.

The primary endpoint was KDIGO creatinine-defined AKI within 7 days: serum creatinine rise ≥26.5 μmol/L within 48 hours or >1.5× baseline within 7 days. DAG-selected covariates included age, sex, Charlson comorbidity burden, systolic blood pressure, baseline creatinine, HbA1c, RAAS inhibitor use, insulin use, surgical specialty, surgical duration, and emergency status. Propensity-score IPTW targeted the average treatment effect among treated; standardized differences <0.10 indicated balance. Sensitivity analyses substituted CKD for baseline creatinine and excluded eGFR <15; subgroup analyses separated emergency and non-emergency surgery; negative controls used DPP4 inhibitor exposure and thrombocytosis outcome.

Results

From 4,842 procedures, 4,147 met the surgery definition; 2,842 remained after missing-data exclusions, 2,499 entered the primary weighted analysis, and 230 were removed for lack of propensity overlap. The final primary cohort included 738 SGLT2i users and 1,761 non-users. Post-weighting standardized differences were 0.00–0.02 across supplemental balance checks.

Primary AKI was 18.6% versus 25.8%, corresponding to OR 0.71 (0.56–0.91) and standardized risk difference −0.049 (−0.082 to −0.014). Alternative renal adjustment, severe-eGFR exclusion, and emergency/non-emergency subgroups all retained protective estimates. Neither the DPP4-inhibitor exposure negative control nor the thrombocytosis outcome negative control showed a statistically supported association.

Discussion

The authors place the results alongside smaller perioperative studies, including cardiac-surgery empagliflozin trials, and argue that this study extends the signal to a broader surgical population with substantial emergency surgery. Proposed renal mechanisms are biologically plausible but were not directly tested in this study.

The authors emphasize causal-inference design features and concordant sensitivity/negative-control results, but acknowledge residual confounding and exposure-timing uncertainty. Their discussion suggests the findings are compatible with current elective preoperative withholding while hypothesizing retained renal benefit; because actual withholding duration was unavailable, that specific practice interpretation is less directly supported than the observed association itself.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths include a large mixed-specialty surgical cohort, creatinine-defined rather than code-defined AKI, inclusion of emergency and non-emergency surgery, explicit target-trial emulation, DAG-guided confounding control, weighting with overlap trimming, sensitivity analyses, and two negative controls.

Limitations include potential unmeasured confounding, nonstandard timing/frequency of postoperative creatinine testing, selection induced by requiring postoperative creatinine data and complete covariates, single-centre design, unknown SGLT2i start and withholding durations, and restriction to patients with type 2 diabetes receiving glucose-lowering medication.

Conclusion

The source concludes that preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor use was associated with reduced odds of postoperative AKI in patients with type 2 diabetes undergoing surgery. It explicitly calls for larger randomized studies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. High clinical relevance and unusually careful observational causal-inference work make it worth preserving, but the result should remain hypothesis-strengthening rather than practice-changing.
Relevance · 9/10
Rationale: Directly addresses perioperative SGLT2i exposure and AKI in a broad treated-T2DM surgical population.
Limit: does not resolve ketoacidosis risk or the optimal withholding interval.
Would rise: simultaneous clinically useful safety and management endpoints.
Novelty · 8/10
Rationale: Extends prior cardiac/elective evidence to a large mixed-specialty cohort with substantial emergency surgery.
Limit: related perioperative renal-protection signals already existed.
Would rise: prospective randomization or mechanistic perioperative phenotyping.
Method strength · 7/10
Rationale: Target-trial specification, DAG adjustment, IPTW, overlap trimming, balance checks, sensitivities, subgroups, and negative controls are strong observational methods.
Limit: treatment remains non-random and exposure timing is incompletely measured.
Would rise: randomized assignment or robust prospective treatment-timing capture.
Evidence strength · 6/10
Rationale: The primary effect is quantitatively coherent across sensitivity analyses and negative controls do not show obvious systemic bias.
Limit: residual confounding and selection/measurement issues can still explain part of the association.
Would rise: multicentre RCT confirmation with prespecified renal and ketoacidosis outcomes.
External validity · 6/10
Rationale: Broad surgical specialties and both emergency/non-emergency procedures support wider relevance than cardiac-only cohorts.
Limit: single Australian tertiary centre and treated-T2DM-only cohort.
Would rise: replication across health systems, surgical settings, and SGLT2i indications.
Implementation value · 6/10
Rationale: Useful for evidence synthesis, perioperative risk discussion, and trial design.
Limit: unknown withholding duration prevents a defensible protocol threshold from being inferred.
Would rise: randomized evidence linked to a specified perioperative SGLT2i management strategy.

Bias and interpretation

Principal limiting factor: measured-covariate balance is excellent, but unmeasured confounding remains possible. Patients prescribed SGLT2 inhibitors may differ systematically in cardiorenal management, access, prescribing era, comorbidity phenotype, or perioperative care in ways not fully captured by the modeled variables.
Selection/measurement issue: postoperative creatinine testing was not standardized, and inclusion required postoperative creatinine plus complete baseline covariates. This can influence who enters the analysis and who has AKI detected.
What strengthens confidence: directionally consistent primary and sensitivity estimates, broadly similar emergency/non-emergency effects, excellent measured balance after weighting, and null negative-control analyses.

Practice translation

Use now
Use the study as supportive evidence that preoperative SGLT2i exposure is not obviously associated with excess postoperative AKI and may be associated with lower AKI risk in treated type 2 diabetes.
Do not use for
Do not use this observational association to override perioperative SGLT2i safety/withholding protocols, define a new withholding interval, or claim proven renal protection.
Research value
Provides a strong target-trial-emulation template and useful effect estimates for multicentre randomized or prospective perioperative studies.

Clinical question / PICO

Population: Adults with treated type 2 diabetes undergoing surgery under general anaesthesia.
Exposure: Documented preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor use.
Comparator: No preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor use.
Outcome: Creatinine-defined postoperative AKI within 7 days.
Design: Retrospective target-trial emulation using IPTW; not randomized.

Bottom line: Clinically important and methodologically serious observational evidence supports a renal-protective association, but it is insufficient by itself to change perioperative SGLT2i management. The most defensible next step is randomized confirmation with explicit drug timing, AKI ascertainment, ketoacidosis surveillance, and prespecified emergency/elective strata.
