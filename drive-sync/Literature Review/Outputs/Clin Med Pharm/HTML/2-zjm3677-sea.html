One-page synthesis

Core thesis: An 18-year-old military trainee had two USA300 MRSA abscesses nine weeks apart while intermittently using 4% chlorhexidine. The second isolate (C02) was qacA-positive and had a chlorhexidine MIC of 0.8 micrograms/mL versus 0.3 micrograms/mL for the first isolate (C01), an approximately 2.7-fold increase. C02 carried qacA on a large, apparently novel plasmid and differed from C01 by more than 140 chromosomal SNPs.

The case is unusually well characterized for a single-patient report: antimicrobial susceptibility, PFGE/MLST/SCCmec/PVL testing, qacA/B and norA analyses, qacA sequencing/expression, a pSK1-spanning PCR panel, PacBio whole-genome sequencing, and plasmid annotation all contribute to the mechanistic description. The laboratory result is internally coherent: C02 is qacA-positive and expresses qacA, while C01 does not, and C02 shows the higher chlorhexidine MIC.

The central causal claim must remain constrained. The patient self-reported chlorhexidine use every other week rather than the assigned weekly schedule; there is no untreated comparator; chlorhexidine breakpoints are not established; and the two MRSA isolates are genetically distinct rather than the same clone observed before and after exposure. The source itself states that the clinical impact cannot be determined and that chlorhexidine may have contributed. The strongest use is therefore surveillance rationale and hypothesis generation, not direct practice change.

Section condensation

Abstract

The authors report two community-associated USA300 MRSA cutaneous abscesses in one patient during chlorhexidine use. The second clinical isolate had reduced chlorhexidine susceptibility and a novel qacA-encoding plasmid, prompting a surveillance-oriented conclusion.

Case report: clinical course and exposure

The patient was an 18-year-old infantry trainee without a prior abscess history. The first left-knee abscess was incised and drained; MRSA was cultured; and a 10-day twice-daily double-strength TMP-SMX course plus wound care was followed by resolution within 14 days. Nine weeks later a second MRSA abscess occurred on the left foot and was treated similarly.

The patient belonged to a prevention-trial arm assigned weekly 4% chlorhexidine showering but reported use every other week: approximately one or two uses before the first episode and four or five before the second. This is exposure history, not a controlled within-case intervention comparison.

Case report: microbiology and chlorhexidine susceptibility

Both isolates were ST8/USA300, SCCmec IV, PVL-positive, mecA-positive, and mupA-negative. The second isolate became levofloxacin-resistant and was qacA-positive, whereas C01 was levofloxacin-susceptible and qacA-negative. A separate ST30 MSSA strain was identified in the nares at the second episode.

Broth testing across 0-1 microgram/mL chlorhexidine yielded MICs of 0.3 for C01 and 0.8 for C02 (about 2.7-fold higher). norA promoter sequence and expression were not meaningfully different, whereas qacA was detected and expressed only in C02.

Case report: plasmid and genomic characterization

qacA sequencing supported the qacA rather than qacB identity (six of seven distinguishing residues), with an alternative lysine start codon. Sequence immediately upstream of qacA was more than 99% similar to pSK1, but only primer pair 9 of a 10-pair pSK1 panel amplified correctly, supporting a pSK1-like rather than canonical pSK1 context.

PacBio sequencing produced closed chromosomes and plasmids. C01 and C02 differed by more than 140 chromosomal SNPs and therefore were considered genetically distinct. C02 also carried gyrA S84L and grlA F80Y substitutions associated with quinolone resistance. pC02 was 61,537 bp versus 27,044 bp for pC01; less than 40% of pC02 matched regions in other NCBI sequences, leading the authors to describe it as apparently novel.

Interpretation and limitations

The authors contextualize qacA as an efflux determinant associated with reduced susceptibility to cationic biocides. They explicitly note that chlorhexidine-resistance breakpoints are not established and that qacA/B presence and elevated MIC can correlate poorly. They also note that the C02 MIC of 0.8 micrograms/mL is below the at-least-4 micrograms/mL values described in some prior reports.

The report cannot determine the overall clinical impact of reduced susceptibility; it establishes only that a second abscess occurred. The authors state that intermittent chlorhexidine use may have contributed and recommend surveillance/study as use expands.

Accession numbers and funding

The C01/C02 genomes and plasmids were deposited as CP012118, CP012119, CP012120, and CP012121. Funding included the Infectious Disease Clinical Research Program and multiple U.S. federal sources; the authors state that their views do not necessarily represent those agencies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. High-value as a molecularly rich sentinel case and surveillance signal; insufficient for causal or practice-changing conclusions about chlorhexidine.
Relevance7/10

Directly relevant to MRSA resistance, antiseptic stewardship, and infection prevention. Evidence basis: qacA/MIC/genomic characterization. Limiter: single case. Would rise: if paired with surveillance or cohort data.

Novelty7/10

The authors report a first community-associated case of increased MIC during chlorhexidine use and an apparently novel qacA plasmid. Limiter: first-report novelty does not establish frequency. Would fall: if prior comparable cases were demonstrated.

Method strength5/10

Extensive phenotyping, PCR, expression analysis, and whole-genome/plasmid sequencing strengthen characterization. Limiter: no controlled design, replication, or prospective causal test. Would rise: serial isolate sampling with controls and replicated MIC testing.

Evidence strength4/10

Strong for the descriptive fact that C02 was qacA-positive with higher MIC; weak for the claim that chlorhexidine selected it. Limiter: C01 and C02 were genetically distinct. Would rise: demonstration of within-lineage acquisition under measured exposure or epidemiologic association.

External validity2/10

One young military trainee and two isolates cannot estimate prevalence or risk across populations. Would rise: multicenter surveillance with defined susceptibility methods and denominators.

Implementation value5/10

Useful for awareness, laboratory surveillance design, and stewardship questions. Limiter: no validated breakpoint or management threshold. Would rise: clinical-outcome-linked susceptibility standards and prospective resistance monitoring.

Critical validity points

  • Causality: Temporal association is insufficient; exposure was self-reported and the isolates were genetically distinct.
  • Phenotype interpretation: There is no established chlorhexidine resistance breakpoint in the source, and genotype/MIC correlation can be imperfect.
  • Clinical endpoint: The second abscess is clinically important, but the report cannot attribute recurrence to the elevated MIC or qacA.
  • Mechanistic value: qacA expression and plasmid sequencing make the resistance mechanism biologically plausible for reduced susceptibility in C02.

Practice / implementation takeaways

Use nowRecognize reduced chlorhexidine susceptibility and qacA carriage as surveillance targets when investigating recurrent MRSA or decolonization failures.
Do not overclaimDo not use this case alone to stop routine chlorhexidine prevention/decolonization strategies or to define a clinical resistance cutoff.
Laboratory implicationIf a program investigates antiseptic susceptibility, preserve isolate identity, measured MIC method, qacA/B genotype/expression, and genomic context so changes can be separated from strain replacement.
Research implicationPrioritize longitudinal surveillance linking chlorhexidine exposure intensity, strain relatedness, qacA acquisition, MIC change, and clinical outcomes.
