One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. High-quality short-term mechanistic glycemic evidence with a strong primary overall signal; read the primary result and time-in-range/variability figures, but treat stratum and secondary P values as descriptive/nominal and do not use the trial as long-term clinical-outcome evidence.

The trial randomized 100 adults with inadequately controlled T2D to dapagliflozin 10 mg/day or placebo for four weeks on stable metformin or insulin-based background therapy. Continuous glucose monitoring captured a 7-day pretreatment period and week 4. The primary endpoint improved clearly: 24-hour mean glucose fell from 177.9 to 160.7 mg/dL with dapagliflozin and rose from 182.6 to 187.5 mg/dL with placebo, for an adjusted treatment difference of -24.0 mg/dL (P<0.001). Time in 70–180 mg/dL increased by 12.2 percentage points in the dapagliflozin arm while decreasing 2.8 points with placebo; hyperglycemic time fell 12.6 points with dapagliflozin and rose 3.5 points with placebo. Time below 70 mg/dL changed little, and time below 54 mg/dL remained 0% in both arms.

Secondary measures generally moved in the expected glycemic direction: adjusted differences favored dapagliflozin for 2-h PPG (-36.3 mg/dL), FPG (-29.7 mg/dL), HbA1c (-0.23 percentage points), fructosamine (-10.8 mg/dL), MAGE (-15.3 mg/dL), 24-h glucose SD (-4.7 mg/dL), HBGI (-3.4), and time 70–140 mg/dL (+14.4 points). The study was powered only for the overall primary analysis; secondary/exploratory and stratum P values were nominal. The metformin stratum showed a larger 24-h mean-glucose treatment difference (-36.2 mg/dL) than the insulin stratum (-11.9 mg/dL), but the strata differed substantially in disease severity and insulin dosing was not captured in detail, so the apparent interaction should not be treated as established.

Safety signal within this short exposure: TEAEs occurred in 36% vs 20%; UTI was 6% in each arm. Six insulin-stratum participants (three per arm) had nonsevere symptomatic/probable symptomatic hypoglycemia. No deaths occurred. The small sample and four-week exposure cannot characterize uncommon or longer-term harms.

Section condensation

Introduction

The paper motivates CGM as a way to capture daily glucose dynamics not represented by HbA1c or single-time-point glucose measures. Its study question is pharmacodynamic: whether adding dapagliflozin improves the 24-hour glucose profile in adults with T2D on metformin or insulin-based background therapy.

Materials and Methods

At 34 US centers, 100 adults were randomized 1:1 to dapagliflozin 10 mg daily or placebo for four weeks, stratified by metformin versus insulin background therapy. Dexcom G4 PLATINUM CGM recorded every five minutes during a 7-day lead-in assessment and week 4; participants and investigators were blinded to CGM readings. Eligibility required age 18–75, HbA1c 7.5–10.5%, BMI ≤45 kg/m², fasting C-peptide ≥1.5 ng/mL, stable background treatment for ≥8 weeks, and ≥70% lead-in CGM compliance.

The primary endpoint was change in 24-hour mean glucose calculated from CGM AUC. Secondary endpoints included time below 70, 70–180, and above 180 mg/dL; 2-h PPG, FPG, HbA1c, fructosamine; and variability measures including MAGE, distance traveled, and 24-h SD. ANCOVA adjusted for baseline and included treatment and stratum; linear mixed-effects models addressed missing data. The trial was powered only for a 14.5 mg/dL overall primary difference; secondary/exploratory and stratum P values were nominal.

Results — population and primary CGM endpoint

All 100 randomized participants were treated and entered ITT/safety analyses; 97 completed with adequate paired CGM data. The treatment groups were broadly balanced, but insulin-stratum patients tended to be older, heavier, more hyperglycemic, and to have longer diabetes duration than the metformin stratum.

Dapagliflozin produced the clearest finding in the primary endpoint: adjusted difference -24.0 mg/dL in change in 24-hour mean glucose (P<0.001). The shift was accompanied by increased target-range time and reduced hyperglycemic time without meaningful time below 54 mg/dL.

Results — strata and secondary endpoints

Metformin-stratum and insulin-stratum 24-hour mean-glucose treatment differences were -36.2 and -11.9 mg/dL, respectively, but these were nonconfirmatory subgroup findings. The same caution applies to stratum time-in-range and secondary endpoint P values. The overall direction across PPG, FPG, HbA1c, fructosamine, MAGE, SD, HBGI, and narrower time-in-range generally favored dapagliflozin, while distance traveled did not show a clear treatment difference.

Safety

TEAEs occurred in 18/50 dapagliflozin versus 10/50 placebo participants. UTI occurred in 3/50 in each arm. Two dapagliflozin participants discontinued because of AEs, and one dapagliflozin participant had a severe elevated-creatinine/decreased-GFR TEAE without discontinuation. Six symptomatic/probable symptomatic hypoglycemia cases occurred in the insulin stratum, three in each arm; all were nonsevere.

Discussion and interpretation

The authors interpret the CGM data as evidence that dapagliflozin lowers mean glucose across the 24-hour day and improves several variability measures. Their suggestion of a stronger effect on metformin than insulin is appropriately hedged: the study was not powered for strata, insulin participants had more advanced disease, mealtime insulin was allowed, and insulin-dose changes were not captured in detail.

Independent appraisal should also emphasize the four-week duration, N=100, surrogate endpoints, and sponsor/author relationships. These do not negate the randomized primary glycemic result, but they sharply limit safety, subgroup, and practice-changing claims.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance8/10

Direct randomized CGM evidence for dapagliflozin glycemic effects in common metformin/insulin background regimens.

Evidence: Primary and secondary glycemic endpoints were directly measured with dense CGM.

Limit: The study is older and does not address cardiorenal outcomes.

Would raise/lower: A longer, contemporary trial tied to patient-centered outcomes would raise utility.

Novelty7/10

At publication, detailed 24-hour dapagliflozin CGM profiling and variability analyses added mechanistic resolution beyond HbA1c/FPG.

Evidence: 24-hour CGM, MADz profiles, MAGE, time-in-range analyses.

Limit: SGLT2 glycemic efficacy was already established, and novelty is lower now.

Would raise/lower: Novelty would be higher with validated clinical-outcome links or head-to-head treatment comparisons.

Method strength7/10

Randomization, double blinding, placebo control, multicenter enrollment, prespecified primary endpoint, and blinded CGM support internal validity.

Evidence: 1:1 randomized treatment, ANCOVA with baseline adjustment, 34 centers, objective CGM.

Limit: N=100, four-week duration, nonconfirmatory secondary/subgroup analyses, and uncontrolled insulin-dose changes.

Would raise/lower: Larger sample, longer follow-up, multiplicity control, and captured insulin-dose changes would raise the score.

Evidence strength7/10

The primary overall CGM effect is clear and statistically strong, but most additional endpoints are nominal and the evidence remains surrogate and short-term.

Evidence: Primary treatment difference -24.0 mg/dL, P<0.001; consistent TIR/hyperglycemia direction.

Limit: Secondary and stratum P values were not intended for confirmatory inference.

Would raise/lower: Confirmatory multiplicity-controlled secondary endpoints and longer-term outcomes would raise certainty.

External validity5/10

The 34-center US design helps, but eligibility constraints, short treatment, and heterogeneous insulin therapy limit generalization.

Evidence: Adults with T2D on metformin or insulin-based regimens were represented.

Limit: Insulin stratum differed in disease severity and detailed insulin dosing was not captured.

Would raise/lower: Broader populations, longer follow-up, and standardized/captured background therapy would improve transportability.

Implementation value6/10

Useful for understanding dapagliflozin's 24-hour glucose profile and hypoglycemia pattern, but insufficient alone to change formulary or long-term clinical practice.

Evidence: Dense CGM and meal-test results provide practical pharmacodynamic context.

Limit: No cardiovascular/renal outcomes and limited safety exposure.

Would raise/lower: Integration with contemporary comparative effectiveness and outcome trials would raise implementation value.

Main threats to overinterpretation: subgroup/secondary multiplicity; short exposure; insulin-dose confounding and missing dose detail; baseline severity differences between strata; surrogate outcomes; AstraZeneca sponsorship and multiple author relationships.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use this source for

Short-term pharmacodynamic evidence that dapagliflozin lowers 24-hour mean glucose, reduces hyperglycemic exposure, and can increase CGM target-range time in T2D.

Do not use it for

Long-term safety, cardiorenal outcome benefit, formulary superiority over another SGLT2 inhibitor, or confirmation that metformin-background patients benefit more than insulin-background patients.

Read next

Outcome trials and direct comparative evidence should carry more weight for current formulary decisions; this paper functions best as mechanistic glycemic context.
