One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. This is a methodologically serious real-world complement to SGLT2 trials, especially for kidney outcomes and CKD stratification. Its value for a dapagliflozin-only formulary decision is indirect because dapagliflozin was not studied.

The study used EHR data from 20 US PCORnet health systems to compare 20,279 empagliflozin initiators with 41,918 DPP-4 inhibitor initiators. Post-LASSO overlap weighting balanced a large prespecified covariate set. Over median follow-up of 1.08 years, the detailed Table 2 estimate for the primary composite of sustained 40% eGFR decline, incident ESKD, or all-cause death was HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.65–0.86), with weighted rates 26.65 versus 36.65 per 1,000 person-years. The association persisted in propensity-matched and COVID-era sensitivity analyses and was directionally consistent in CKD and non-CKD strata.

Cardiovascular findings were mixed rather than uniformly positive. MI/stroke/death was lower (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.70–0.94), while heart-failure hospitalization alone was not (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.84–1.11). Genital mycotic infection was higher with empagliflozin (HR 1.72, 95% CI 1.58–1.88); other prespecified safety estimates were imprecise and not statistically clear. The strongest interpretation is comparative effectiveness of empagliflozin versus DPP-4 inhibitors in routine care, not proof of a class-wide or within-SGLT2 interchangeability claim.

Source-consistency note. The abstract and detailed tables differ slightly for the primary composite confidence interval, mortality HR, CKD-subgroup estimate, and MI/stroke/death confidence interval. Methods

Design and population

Adults aged ≥18 years with T2D newly prescribed empagliflozin or a DPP-4 inhibitor during 2016–2020 were identified in PCORnet EHR data, with follow-up data through 2021. The design required 12 months of prior data and health-system engagement. It excluded type 1 diabetes, polycystic kidney disease, prior transplant/dialysis, baseline eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m², and missing baseline eGFR.

The active-comparator new-user design is stronger than a treated-versus-untreated comparison but still observational. Prescriptions were treated as exposure; fills and adherence were not confirmed. CKD was defined by eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² without albuminuria.

Outcomes and analysis

The primary composite was sustained 40% eGFR decline, incident ESKD, or all-cause death. Cardiovascular outcomes included HF hospitalization, mortality composites, and MACE definitions; safety outcomes included DKA, severe hypoglycemia, genital mycotic infection, dialysis-requiring AKI, severe UTI, and urinary tract cancer. Follow-up was capped at 2 years and censored for treatment crossover in the main analysis.

Confounding control used post-LASSO overlap weighting with prespecified covariates and subgroup interactions, modeled separately by CKD status; all post-weighting absolute standardized mean differences were <0.1. Weighted Cox models estimated HRs; GLP-1RA initiation was time-varying. Propensity matching, intention-to-treat, 6-month discontinuation, and COVID-era exclusion analyses tested robustness.

CKD stratification

For the primary composite, Table 3 reports HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.52–0.86) in baseline CKD and 0.79 (0.67–0.94) without baseline CKD. CKD subgroup reductions were also reported for sustained 40% eGFR decline (0.61, 0.41–0.91), mortality (0.69, 0.49–0.96), and MI/stroke/death (0.74, 0.57–0.97). Figure 3 did not show material effect modification across age, sex, prior cardiovascular event, recent HF hospitalization, or metformin use; the continuous-eGFR interaction within CKD was p=0.80.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10

Rationale: Directly relevant active-comparator cardiorenal evidence for empagliflozin in T2D; indirect for a dapagliflozin-only formulary decision.

Evidence basis: Large T2D cohort, kidney/CV/safety endpoints, CKD stratification.

Limiting factor: No direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin comparison.

Would raise/lower: A direct within-class comparative-effectiveness study with similar outcome rigor would raise this score.

Novelty 7/10

Rationale: Adds lab-rich, multisystem EHR evidence with a DPP-4 active comparator and CKD/non-CKD analyses.

Evidence basis: 20 PCORnet health systems and laboratory-defined kidney endpoints.

Limiting factor: Similar SGLT2-versus-DPP-4 observational studies already existed.

Would raise/lower: Newer data with direct SGLT2 comparisons or stronger causal designs would raise this score.

Method strength 7/10

Rationale: Strong observational design features reduce but cannot eliminate confounding.

Evidence basis: New-user active comparator, post-LASSO overlap weighting, balance checks, time-dependent GLP-1RA covariate, multiple sensitivities.

Limiting factor: Nonrandomized exposure; adherence and some outcomes are imperfectly measured.

Would raise/lower: Prospective randomization or a validated target-trial emulation with richer adherence data would raise this score.

Evidence strength 6/10

Rationale: Primary kidney findings are consistent across weighting/matching and key sensitivities, but observational limitations and source-level numeric inconsistencies remain.

Evidence basis: Primary HR ~0.75; matched HR 0.75; COVID sensitivity HR 0.70; CKD and non-CKD associations in same direction.

Limiting factor: Residual confounding, code-based outcomes, nonadjudicated HF outcome, prescription-not-fill exposure, minor internal discrepancies.

Would raise/lower: Replication with adjudicated outcomes and direct drug comparison would raise this score.

External validity 8/10

Rationale: The cohort is large, geographically distributed, and more diverse than many trials.

Evidence basis: 62,197 adults across 20 US health systems; 22.2% Black and 50% women.

Limiting factor: Requires health-system engagement and excludes baseline eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m².

Would raise/lower: Including advanced CKD and additional non-US or safety-net populations would raise this score.

Implementation value 6/10

Rationale: Useful for contextualizing empagliflozin effectiveness and safety, but not sufficient to justify within-class substitution.

Evidence basis: Provides absolute incidence rates, CKD strata, active-comparator estimates, and genital-mycotic-infection signal.

Limiting factor: Does not test dapagliflozin or inpatient formulary interchangeability.

Would raise/lower: A direct empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin study or formulary-relevant comparative synthesis would raise this score.

Main strengths

Large multisystem cohort; active-comparator new-user design; laboratory-derived kidney outcomes; extensive covariate adjustment; explicit balance checks; CKD-stratified modeling; multiple sensitivity analyses; absolute incidence rates alongside relative estimates; supplement with operational definitions.

Main limitations

Residual confounding cannot be excluded. Prescriptions do not prove exposure. Some outcomes are code-based and nonadjudicated, eGFR measurement timing is not protocolized, CKD lacks albuminuria, and advanced CKD was excluded. The minor abstract/table discrepancies reduce reporting precision. Sponsor involvement and author relationships warrant awareness but do not by themselves invalidate the results.

Do not use for: claiming dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are clinically interchangeable, proving an inpatient formulary class effect, or substituting observational HRs for randomized causal estimates.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use

Use as supportive real-world evidence that empagliflozin’s kidney benefit seen in trials appears in routine T2D care against an active glucose-lowering comparator.

Monitor

Keep the genital mycotic infection signal visible when translating benefits to practice; other safety estimates are less precise.

Do not overclaim

The article does not directly compare SGLT2 inhibitors and cannot settle a dapagliflozin-only formulary conversion question by itself.
