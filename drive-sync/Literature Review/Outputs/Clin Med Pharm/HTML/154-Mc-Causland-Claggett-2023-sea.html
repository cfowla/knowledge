One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In DELIVER participants with HF and LVEF >40%, baseline eGFR did not significantly modify dapagliflozin's benefit on cardiovascular death/worsening HF. Dapagliflozin caused an early eGFR dip, then slowed chronic and total eGFR decline. Hard kidney composite events were uncommon and not significantly reduced.

The analysis included 6262 participants with baseline eGFR, mean age 72 years and mean eGFR 61 mL/min/1.73 m²; 49% had eGFR below 60. Primary cardiovascular HRs were 0.84 (95% CI 0.70-1.00), 0.68 (0.54-0.87), and 0.93 (0.76-1.14) across eGFR ≥60, 45-<60, and <45 strata, with P for interaction .16. Continuous eGFR interaction was also non-significant (P=.45).

After the initial month, chronic eGFR slope was 0.0 with dapagliflozin vs -1.4 mL/min/1.73 m²/year with placebo, difference +1.4 (95% CI 1.0-1.8; P<.001). Total slope difference was +0.5 (95% CI 0.1-0.9; P=.01). The post hoc kidney composite was 79/3131 vs 73/3131, HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.79-1.49). These kidney-composite data are weak for efficacy inference because event counts were low and alternative definitions were partly post hoc.

Interpretive boundary. The cardiovascular effect-modification analysis is prespecified; the eGFR slope is exploratory; the nonsustained kidney composite is post hoc. Multiple analyses were performed without multiplicity correction, so nominal subgroup interactions should not be elevated to confirmatory findings.

Methods and clinical question

PICO: Adults ≥40 years with symptomatic HF, LVEF >40%, elevated NT-proBNP, and structural heart disease; dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily versus matching placebo; cardiovascular death/worsening HF, eGFR trajectories, kidney composites, and protocol-specified safety events. Key exclusions included eGFR <25 mL/min/1.73 m², persistent SBP <95 mm Hg, and type 1 diabetes.

Cox models tested treatment interactions with categorical and continuous baseline eGFR, stratified by baseline diabetes. Repeated-measures mixed-effects models estimated acute (baseline-month 1), chronic (month 1-36), and total eGFR slopes. Analyses used two-sided alpha .05 without multiplicity correction. Supplement 1 confirms the protocol/SAP lineage: final protocol v4.0 (2020-11-12), sponsor SAP v5.0 (2021-12-08), and academic SAP v1.3 (2022-04-15).

Results and interpretation

Cardiovascular efficacy across kidney function

The primary cardiovascular outcome was higher in lower-eGFR patients, but relative dapagliflozin benefit did not significantly differ by eGFR category (interaction P=.16) or continuous eGFR (P=.45). A categorical interaction for HF events alone was nominally P=.04, while the continuous interaction was P=.30; because the analysis family was not multiplicity-corrected, this isolated signal is hypothesis-generating rather than a robust contradiction to the primary effect-modification result.

Kidney function

Dapagliflozin produced an expected acute decline through month 1 (difference -3.3 mL/min/1.73 m²) followed by materially slower chronic decline (difference +1.4 mL/min/1.73 m²/year) and a smaller but significant improvement in total slope (+0.5 mL/min/1.73 m²/year). Exploratory subgroup work suggested a larger chronic-slope effect in participants with diabetes and at higher LVEF, but these analyses do not establish differential clinical kidney benefit.

Hard kidney outcomes

The post hoc nonsustained ≥50% composite was uncommon and neutral (HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.79-1.49). Supplementary definitions were unstable: the nonsustained ≥40% threshold favored placebo (HR 1.33, 95% CI 1.08-1.64), while sustained definitions had very few events and wide uncertainty. The pattern supports caution against treating eGFR slope improvement as proof of fewer hard kidney events in this population and follow-up window.

Safety

Safety event frequency increased as baseline eGFR fell, but most comparisons were broadly balanced between randomized groups. Selected nominal differences favored dapagliflozin for serious AEs or treatment interruption in middle/higher eGFR strata. Kidney AEs and AKI were similar in the lowest eGFR stratum. Two definite/probable DKA events occurred in dapagliflozin recipients with eGFR <45; the absolute event count was very small.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. High clinical relevance for HF/CKD prescribing and interpretation of SGLT2-related eGFR changes; strong randomized parent trial, but kidney endpoints in this analysis are exploratory and underpowered.
Relevance: 9/10

Directly informs dapagliflozin use across common CKD strata in HFmrEF/HFpEF.

Basis: Large contemporary HF trial and clinically familiar renal thresholds.

Limit: Does not include eGFR <25 or albuminuria stratification.

Would change: Dedicated evidence in advanced CKD/HFpEF would raise applicability.

Novelty: 7/10

Adds kidney-function effect-modification and slope analyses to DELIVER.

Basis: Prespecified kidney subgroup and longitudinal eGFR work.

Limit: SGLT2 acute-dip/chronic-slope pattern was already established in related settings.

Would change: Hard kidney outcome evidence would add novelty.

Method strength: 8/10

Randomized blinded parent trial with prespecified analyses and detailed protocol/SAP.

Basis: Randomization, placebo control, repeated eGFR, interaction modeling.

Limit: Some kidney composites are post hoc and multiplicity was not corrected.

Would change: Predefined multiplicity strategy and kidney-event powering would raise strength.

Evidence strength: 7/10

Strong for lack of major CV effect modification and eGFR trajectory; weaker for hard kidney outcomes.

Basis: Consistent HRs, interaction tests, slope estimates with CIs.

Limit: Very low kidney-event counts and wide intervals.

Would change: Longer follow-up with more adjudicated kidney events would raise confidence.

External validity: 7/10

Broad international HFmrEF/HFpEF population with substantial CKD representation.

Basis: Nearly half had eGFR <60 and multiple geographic regions were enrolled.

Limit: eGFR <25 excluded; albuminuria unavailable.

Would change: Inclusion of advanced CKD and UACR strata would improve transport.

Implementation value: 8/10

Useful for counseling clinicians about early eGFR dip and continued HF benefit across kidney function.

Basis: Dose and kidney strata are clinically actionable.

Limit: Does not establish hard renal-event prevention in this population.

Would change: Practice algorithms integrating eGFR/UACR and long-term kidney outcomes would increase utility.

Main strengths

Randomized blinded parent trial; large sample; repeated kidney function over 36 months; prespecified CV effect-modification question; transparent protocol/SAP bundle; detailed supplemental sensitivity and safety tables.

Main limitations

Kidney events were sparse; nonsustained kidney composite was post hoc; no albuminuria; eGFR below 25 excluded; median follow-up 2.3 years; multiple testing without correction; sponsor involvement in trial operations and primary analysis creates a governance consideration despite independent academic analysis of this paper.

Practice translation: The source supports maintaining confidence in dapagliflozin's HF benefit across the studied eGFR range and recognizing the expected early eGFR dip followed by slower chronic decline. It does not by itself prove prevention of hard kidney outcomes in HFmrEF/HFpEF.
