One-page synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The review is useful for class-level metabolic context and BMI-stratified hypotheses, but its reporting inconsistencies and lack of direct hard cardiorenal outcomes limit practice-changing value.

The review synthesized 168 RCTs involving 72,195 adults with T2DM. GLP-1 receptor agonists produced greater average body-weight reduction than SGLT-2 inhibitors (MD −1.08 kg, 95% CrI −1.78 to −0.36) and greater HbA1c lowering (direct class contrast −0.38 percentage points, −0.52 to −0.25). The weight subgroup analysis suggested SGLT-2 inhibitors were more effective at BMI 25–<30 kg/m², while GLP-1 receptor agonists were more effective above BMI 30 kg/m²; however, the BMI≥35 estimate has an impossible printed credible interval and should not be treated as numerically reliable without source correction.

SGLT-2 inhibitors showed stronger blood-pressure lowering, especially DBP, while GLP-1 receptor agonists showed more favorable LDL-c and total-cholesterol changes. The Bayesian network was accompanied by DIC model selection, PSRF/trace diagnostics, node-splitting, low reported heterogeneity, prediction intervals, CINeMA, small-trial sensitivity analyses, and a frequentist NMA. Those strengths are offset by contradictory drug counts, a discussion paragraph that misattributes key estimates, direction-of-effect ambiguity in Table 1, and malformed supplemental sensitivity/frequentist cells.

Section condensation — Methods

The review was prospectively registered in PROSPERO and reported under PRISMA 2020/PRISMA-NMA. CENTRAL, Web of Science, PubMed, and Embase were searched from inception through March 30, 2025, with additional EASD/ADA proceedings, ClinicalTrials.gov, and PROSPERO searching. Eligible studies were peer-reviewed full-text RCTs in adults with clinically diagnosed T2DM, at least 12 weeks of follow-up, monotherapy with an SGLT-2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist, and relevant body-weight/HbA1c/BP/lipid outcomes. Non-English publications, crossover trials, conference abstracts, and non-peer-reviewed studies were excluded.

Two reviewers extracted data with third-reviewer cross-checking; RoB 2, funnel plots/Egger testing, and CINeMA were used for quality assessment. Bayesian NMA used gemtc, vague priors, DIC-based fixed/random model selection, four MCMC chains, 20,000 burn-in and 60,000 sampling iterations, PSRF/trace diagnostics, node-splitting, I², and SUCRA. Sensitivity analyses included frequentist random-effects NMA, BMI stratification, and exclusion of trials with n<50.

Section condensation — Results, Discussion, Limitations

Body weight: 152 RCTs/54,413 participants contributed. GLP-1RA vs placebo MD was −3.03 kg (95% CrI −3.64 to −2.43); SGLT-2i vs placebo −1.96 kg (−2.42 to −1.50); and GLP-1RA vs SGLT-2i −1.08 kg (−1.78 to −0.36). Semaglutide ranked best among named agents (−4.34 kg, −4.81 to −3.87). BMI subgrouping suggested SGLT-2i superiority at BMI 25–<30 and GLP-1RA superiority above BMI 30, but the BMI≥35 printed interval is internally impossible.

Other metabolic outcomes: 156 RCTs/65,643 patients contributed to HbA1c; GLP-1RA had greater lowering than SGLT-2i. SBP fell with both classes, while only SGLT-2i showed a significant DBP reduction versus placebo. Both classes lowered triglycerides; GLP-1RA lowered LDL-c and total cholesterol, whereas SGLT-2i did not. The authors extrapolated these metabolic differences toward phenotype-specific cardiovascular positioning, but the review itself did not analyze direct cardiovascular endpoints. Reported limitations include short trial duration, inability to quantify sarcopenia, exclusion of non-diabetic and normal-weight populations, and small lower-BMI trials.

Source-integrity findings
  • Abstract / Table 1, BMI ≥35 kg/m²: The reported GLP-1RA vs SGLT-2i point estimate (−1.11 kg) lies outside the stated 95% CrI (−6.73 to −4.60), so the interval cannot be mathematically valid as printed. Preserved exactly as reported; not corrected.
  • Results p3 vs Discussion p8: Results reports 8 GLP-1 receptor agonists and 9 SGLT-2 inhibitors, while the strengths paragraph reports 7 GLP-1 receptor agonists and 8 SGLT-2 inhibitors. Results count retained as the main study-characteristics statement; discrepancy flagged.
  • Discussion p8 vs Results/Figure 4: Discussion attributes weight MD −1.96 kg and LDL-c −0.24 mmol/L to GLP-1 receptor agonists; the main results report GLP-1RA weight MD −3.03 kg and LDL-c −0.14 mmol/L versus placebo, while −1.96 kg is the SGLT-2i weight estimate. No silent correction; appraisal flags the contradiction.
  • Table 1 note: The printed direction-of-effect note (MD<0 favors column; MD>0 favors row) appears inconsistent with the narrative interpretation of the GLP-1RA vs SGLT-2i BMI subgroup estimates. Table values preserved; directional interpretation treated cautiously.
  • Abstract vs Figure 4: GLP-1RA triglyceride 95% CrI lower bound is −0.22 mmol/L in the abstract but approximately −0.21 mmol/L in Figure 4. Figure 4 value used for the figure-derived atom; abstract discrepancy flagged.
  • Supplement Appendices 11–12: Appendix/table numbering is internally inconsistent (e.g., Appendix 11 labeled “Appendix 1 1,” sensitivity table labeled S9.1, frequentist table labeled S10.1 despite another S10.1 in Appendix 10). Coverage uses semantic appendix labels rather than trusting duplicate table numbers.
  • Supplement sensitivity table: HbA1c sensitivity values are on a body-weight-like scale and do not match the primary HbA1c estimates, consistent with a likely transcription/copy-paste error. These malformed values were not normalized into atoms.
  • Supplement frequentist NMA table: Several entries contain malformed or internally impossible estimate/interval formatting. Frequentist validation is retained only as the source’s qualitative sensitivity claim; malformed cells are not converted into quantitative atoms.

Evaluation and Appraisal

8/10

Relevance

Directly compares GLP-1RA and SGLT-2i metabolic effects in T2DM and is useful class-level context.

Evidence basis: 168 RCTs; class and drug networks; BMI subgrouping.

Limiting factor: It does not answer dapagliflozin-versus-empagliflozin interchangeability or hard cardiorenal outcomes.

Would raise/lower: A direct within-SGLT2 comparative effectiveness/safety analysis tied to inpatient formulary decisions would raise this score.

7/10

Novelty

The large network and BMI-stratified weight analysis add clinically interesting differentiation.

Evidence basis: 72,195 participants, 168 RCTs, individual-drug ranking and BMI subgroups.

Limiting factor: Several themes overlap prior GLP-1RA/SGLT2 comparative reviews.

Would raise/lower: Replication of the BMI interaction with individual-patient data would raise novelty and confidence.

6/10

Method strength

Prospective registration, broad search, RoB 2/CINeMA, Bayesian NMA, prediction intervals, and sensitivity analyses are strengths.

Evidence basis: PROSPERO; four databases plus grey literature; MCMC diagnostics; node-splitting; frequentist replication.

Limiting factor: Multiple internal numeric and table-format inconsistencies weaken confidence in reporting fidelity.

Would raise/lower: Clean reconciled tables/data and independent reproducibility would raise the score.

6/10

Evidence strength

Most key estimates include uncertainty and model diagnostics, but the outcomes are metabolic surrogates and several source values conflict.

Evidence basis: CrIs, prediction intervals, low reported heterogeneity, no reported network inconsistency, moderate-to-high CINeMA for most comparisons.

Limiting factor: Short follow-up, indirect comparisons, malformed subgroup/sensitivity cells, and no hard cardiovascular endpoints.

Would raise/lower: Consistent source data plus direct long-term outcome evidence would raise the score.

5/10

External validity

The evidence base is large but limited to adults with T2DM and 12–78 week trials.

Evidence basis: 168 RCTs across many agents.

Limiting factor: Non-English studies, non-diabetic populations, normal-weight T2DM, and long-term follow-up are absent or limited.

Would raise/lower: Broader populations and longer pragmatic follow-up would raise the score.

5/10

Implementation value

Useful for phenotype-oriented class context, especially obesity versus hypertension emphasis.

Evidence basis: BMI subgroup, HbA1c, BP, and lipid estimates.

Limiting factor: No direct evidence that supports switching between individual SGLT2 inhibitors or inpatient interchangeability.

Would raise/lower: Direct head-to-head SGLT2 comparative outcomes, safety, and formulary implementation evidence would raise the score.

Best use: class-level metabolic comparison and hypothesis generation about BMI- or phenotype-oriented selection.

Do not use for: concluding dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are interchangeable; hard cardiovascular/renal equivalence; long-term safety; or correcting malformed source values without checking another authoritative version.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate. Full main text and supplement were available, but internal reporting defects materially constrain confidence.

Implementation / Practice Takeaways

Use
Weight, HbA1c, BP, and lipid class-level estimates as context when framing distinct metabolic profiles.
Qualify
BMI subgroup findings—especially BMI≥35—because source reporting is internally inconsistent and lower-BMI trials were smaller.
Do not infer
No direct support for SGLT-2 formulary interchangeability or individual-agent cardiorenal equivalence.
