One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Living kidney donor evaluation should move away from isolated yes or no thresholds for one risk factor at a time. KDIGO instead asks programs to assess the candidate's combined demographic and health profile, estimate absolute postdonation risk when possible, compare it with a program-defined acceptance threshold, and preserve donor autonomy once the medical risk is acceptable.

The guideline covers the full donor pathway. It starts with voluntariness, consent, compatibility, perioperative assessment, kidney function, albuminuria, hematuria, stones, blood pressure, metabolic risk, infection, cancer, genetic disease, pregnancy, and psychosocial assessment. It then addresses nephrectomy, ethics and policy, and lifelong follow-up. The framework is intentionally broader than the evidence base. Of 146 formal guidance statements, 140 are ungraded. Six are Level 2 suggestions supported by low or very low certainty evidence.

The most durable contribution is the decision architecture. Programs should define defensible risk thresholds, apply the same thresholds to comparable candidates, use absolute rather than relative risks, communicate uncertainty, and give accepted candidates the final decision about donation. The CKD Prognosis Consortium model was used to show how multi-factor kidney-failure risk might be estimated. KDIGO repeatedly calls that model a proof of concept rather than a final individual-risk calculator.

The main limitation is evidence age and certainty. The formal systematic search ended in 2014, the Work Group added evidence through January 2017, and many donor questions cannot be answered with randomized trials. The companion 2020 CJASN article improves usability through a checklist and five scenarios, but it is an application review rather than a new systematic guideline update.

Methods and evidence framework

The Evidence Review Team searched Ovid MEDLINE, Ovid EMBASE, and the Cochrane Library for systematic reviews, randomized trials, and observational studies. The search identified 4,530 citations. After title and abstract screening, 414 reports underwent full-text review. Supplemental citation searching added 70 reports, for 484 full-text reports in total.

For long-term donor outcomes, eligible studies needed a healthy nondonor comparison group with similar baseline health characteristics. Comparisons with the general population were excluded because donors are healthier at baseline. Long-term outcome extraction generally required more than 100 participants and a mean follow-up of at least five years. The guideline used GRADE for questions covered by the formal systematic review. Other necessary statements were issued as ungraded guidance based on other literature, prior KDIGO guidance, ethics, common practice, and Work Group consensus.

The guideline also generated evidence de novo. KDIGO collaborated with the CKD Prognosis Consortium to model kidney-failure risk in low-risk people using multiple demographic and health characteristics. The model was intended to demonstrate an integrated risk framework. It was not presented as a validated final answer for individual donor selection.

Method boundary. The review was comprehensive but not exhaustive. KDIGO states that hand searches were not performed and that review articles and textbook chapters were not systematically searched. The draft guideline underwent public review in November and December 2015.

Chapter condensation

Chapter 1. Goals of evaluation, framework for decision-making, and roles and responsibilities

KDIGO replaces isolated single-factor eligibility rules with an integrated risk assessment. Each program should define defensible thresholds for serious postdonation outcomes, apply them consistently, and communicate absolute risks and uncertainty. A candidate below the program threshold may choose whether to proceed. A program may decline a candidate whose projected risk is above its threshold.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 18.

Chapter 2. Informed consent

Consent requires capacity, privacy, voluntariness, time to decide, and an opportunity to withdraw without pressure. Disclosure should cover the evaluation process, findings that may emerge, individualized risks and benefits, treatment alternatives for the intended recipient, information handling, and available support.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 8.

Chapter 3. Compatibility testing, incompatible transplantation, and paired donation

Donor and recipient compatibility assessment includes repeat ABO typing, blood group A subtype testing when relevant, HLA typing, and assessment of donor-specific HLA antibodies. Incompatible pairs should receive counseling about paired donation and incompatibility management strategies.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 6.

Chapter 4. Preoperative evaluation and management

Donor candidates should receive the same perioperative assessment used for other noncardiac surgery. Smokers should be advised to stop at least four weeks before donation and to maintain lifelong abstinence.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 2.

Chapter 5. Predonation kidney function

Kidney function should be expressed as GFR. Creatinine-based eGFR is the initial test and should be confirmed. GFR at least 90 mL/min per 1.73 m2 is acceptable, 60 to 89 requires individualized risk assessment, and below 60 should exclude donation. The more affected kidney should be donated when asymmetry or a localized abnormality does not preclude donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 10.

Chapter 6. Predonation albuminuria

Albuminuria, not total protein, is the preferred kidney-damage measure. AER below 30 mg/day is acceptable, 30 to 100 requires individualized assessment, and above 100 should exclude donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 6.

Chapter 7. Predonation hematuria

Persistent microscopic hematuria requires a cause-directed evaluation for infection, malignancy, stones, or glomerular disease. Reversible hematuria that resolves may be acceptable. IgA nephropathy should exclude donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 4.

Chapter 8. Kidney stones

Candidates should be asked about prior stones and have predonation imaging reviewed for stones. Acceptance depends on recurrence risk and the consequences of stones in a solitary kidney. General evidence-based stone-prevention measures remain appropriate after donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 5.

Chapter 9. Hyperuricemia, gout, and mineral and bone disease

Candidates should be asked about gout. Counseling may include the expected rise in uric acid after donation and methods to reduce future gout risk.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 3.

Chapter 10. Predonation blood pressure

Blood pressure should be measured accurately on at least two occasions, with ambulatory or standardized repeat measurement when status is uncertain. Normal BP is acceptable. Selected candidates controlled below 140/90 mm Hg with one or two drugs and no target-organ damage may be accepted after individualized risk assessment.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 6.

Chapter 11. Predonation metabolic and lifestyle risk factors

The guideline addresses obesity, glucose intolerance, dyslipidemia, and tobacco use as contributors to long-term kidney and cardiovascular risk. BMI above 30 kg/m2, prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, and active tobacco use are not handled by a single universal cutoff. Programs should evaluate the full demographic and health profile. Type 1 diabetes should exclude donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 15.

Chapter 12. Preventing infection transmission

Programs should assess HIV, HBV, HCV, CMV, EBV, syphilis, urinary infection, and exposure-specific infections. HIV, HBV, and HCV screening should be current within 28 days of donation. A potentially transmissible infection requires explicit donor, recipient, and program risk-benefit assessment.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 7.

Chapter 13. Cancer screening

Cancer screening should be current and consistent with regional practice. Active malignancy generally excludes donation, with narrow case-by-case exceptions when transmission risk and donor risk are low. The chapter also addresses renal cysts and small renal cell carcinoma.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 6.

Chapter 14. Evaluation of genetic kidney disease

Family history and the cause of kidney failure in a genetically related recipient should be clarified whenever possible. A donor candidate with a genetic kidney disease capable of causing kidney failure should not donate. ADPKD should exclude donation. APOL1 genotyping may be offered to candidates with sub-Saharan African ancestors, with explicit counseling about uncertainty in individual postdonation risk.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 8.

Chapter 15. Pregnancy

Pregnancy itself precludes donation, but future childbearing does not. Women should be asked about prior hypertensive pregnancy disorders and future plans. The guideline advises counseling that postdonation pregnancy may carry a greater likelihood of gestational hypertension or preeclampsia.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 11.

Chapter 16. Psychosocial evaluation

A trained professional should perform an in-person psychosocial evaluation, with part of the assessment conducted without people who could influence the candidate. Programs should define psychosocial exclusion criteria and provide support when needed. Donor quality of life is usually good, but adverse psychosocial outcomes occur in a minority.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 7.

Chapter 17. Acceptable surgical approaches for donor nephrectomy

All donor candidates need anatomic imaging and surgery by an experienced surgeon. Laparoscopic approaches are generally preferred. The chapter addresses right versus left nephrectomy, multiple renal arteries, and vessel ligation. Bilateral renal artery disease involving both ostia should exclude donation.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 9.

Chapter 18. Ethical, legal and policy considerations

The ethical and policy section centers autonomy, compliance with local law, transparent program policy, financial neutrality, public solicitation, paired and nondirected donation communication, and access to kidney replacement therapy for donors who later develop kidney failure.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 11.

Chapter 19. Postdonation follow-up care

Every donor should receive a personalized follow-up plan before donation. At least annually, follow-up should include blood pressure, BMI, serum creatinine with GFR estimation, albuminuria, healthy-lifestyle review, and psychosocial review. Donors who meet CKD criteria should be managed according to the applicable CKD guideline.

Formal guidance statements in this chapter: 4.

Appraisal

Verdict: Read first for the donor-evaluation architecture. Verify current practice before using it as a 2026 policy source. The guideline is unusually strong as an operational framework for risk, consent, selection, and follow-up. Its evidence base is much less uniform than its breadth. Most guidance is ungraded, and the systematic review is now old.
10/10
Relevance

Directly addresses medical, surgical, psychosocial, ethical, and follow-up decisions across the living donor pathway.

9/10
Novelty

The integrated absolute-risk framework was a major departure from isolated single-factor thresholds and remains the source's clearest conceptual contribution.

8/10
Method strength

Independent evidence review, explicit eligibility rules, GRADE, public review, and transparent separation of graded from ungraded guidance. The search was not exhaustive and ended in 2014.

5/10
Evidence strength

Only six guidance statements are graded. Many donor questions rely on observational data, indirect evidence, or consensus because randomized designs are impractical or unethical.

7/10
External validity

The overall framework is broadly portable, but the quantitative kidney-failure model was US-calibrated and has weaker precision for several populations.

10/10
Implementation value

The document provides concrete thresholds, test sequences, consent content, infection screening, surgical rules, psychosocial criteria, and annual follow-up elements.

Main strengths

  • The guideline separates donor autonomy from the transplant program's duty to set a medical risk ceiling.
  • Absolute risk and uncertainty are central to counseling rather than afterthoughts.
  • Kidney function, albuminuria, blood pressure, metabolic risk, family history, and other factors are considered together rather than in isolation.
  • Consent, psychosocial care, financial effects, and long-term follow-up are treated as core donor-safety issues.
  • The companion article translates the framework into usable clinical scenarios without pretending to be a new evidence update.

Main weaknesses

  • 140 of 146 formal statements are ungraded.
  • The formal search stopped in September 2014. Later evidence was added selectively through January 2017 rather than through a new full systematic search.
  • The risk-prediction model is a proof of concept with calibration and generalizability limitations.
  • Several long-term outcomes are based on observational cohorts with limited lifetime follow-up.
  • Access to measured GFR, genetic testing, specialized imaging, paired donation, psychosocial services, and long-term donor surveillance varies by health system.

Implementation

For a transplant program, the source supports a clear workflow. Define the outcomes that matter, set an acceptable absolute-risk threshold, use validated measurements where available, evaluate risk factors together, document uncertainty, and apply the same program rules consistently. Preserve a private route for the candidate to withdraw. Give accepted donors a written long-term care plan before nephrectomy.

Three clinical thresholds are especially operational. GFR at least 90 mL/min per 1.73 m2 is acceptable, 60 to 89 requires individualized assessment, and below 60 should exclude donation. Urine AER below 30 mg/day is acceptable, 30 to 100 requires individualized assessment, and above 100 should exclude donation. Controlled hypertension below 140/90 mm Hg on one or two agents without target-organ damage may be acceptable after individualized assessment.

Annual postdonation follow-up should include blood pressure, BMI, serum creatinine with GFR estimation, albuminuria, lifestyle review, and psychosocial review. This artifact does not establish whether every 2017 threshold or policy remains current in 2026 because external verification was not requested.
