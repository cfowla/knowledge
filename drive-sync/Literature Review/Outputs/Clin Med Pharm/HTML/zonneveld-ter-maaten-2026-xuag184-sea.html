One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first for acute-HF SGLT2i initiation evidence. The strongest finding is a consistent increase in cumulative diuresis through Day 5. The paper pools individual participant data from small acute-HF trials in which empagliflozin or dapagliflozin was started early during admission. Treatment increased urine output without a parallel increase in measured natriuresis. Separate mortality and HF-rehospitalization estimates trended in favor of SGLT2i but did not reach statistical significance. Hypokalaemia occurred less often with SGLT2i, while the prespecified creatinine-worsening measures did not differ.

The authors go further than the pooled evidence justifies when they call SGLT2 inhibitors a cornerstone of acute-HF management and advocate immediate initiation. The data support early initiation as plausible and likely useful. They do not establish a class-member interchange, do not provide a comprehensive safety estimate for ketoacidosis or genital infection, and do not independently prove a mortality benefit.

Methods

Study identification and harmonization

MEDLINE/PubMed was searched from inception through 1 July 2024, with additional review of investigators' files, eligible-study references, and Cochrane citations. English-language RCTs were eligible when they enrolled acute or decompensated HF, compared an SGLT2 inhibitor with placebo or another control, and supplied diuretic-response data. Investigators provided IPD against a predefined variable list. The team harmonized variables into one database and resolved cross-study discrepancies by consensus.

The search found 52 records, assessed 16 full texts, identified 8 unique trials, and obtained usable IPD from 6. One study could not be used because of EU restrictions on collaboration with Russia. Another study's consent did not allow IPD sharing. Five included trials had low RoB2 assessments. One open-label study had some concern for outcome measurement.

Timing, endpoints, and analysis

Five trials enrolled within 24 hours; one allowed enrollment within 96 hours. The analysis standardized Day 1 to the first study-drug day. Study drug began on the admission day in 64% and within 48 hours in 93%. The primary outcome was 24-hour cumulative diuresis. Secondary outcomes covered diuresis through Day 5, natriuresis, weight, renal and electrolyte measures, mortality, HF rehospitalization, and a hierarchy of 30-day death, 30-day HF rehospitalization, then 24-hour diuresis.

Linear, logistic, and Cox models adjusted for source study as a fixed effect. Sensitivity analyses restricted the set to placebo-controlled trials, modeled source study as a random effect, and added aggregate data from an excluded trial where possible. Follow-up was uneven. DAPA-RESPONSE-AHF only provided categorical event windows, so the analysis assigned Days 0 to 30 events to Day 30 and Days 31 to 60 events to Day 45 for time-to-event models.

Subgroups and sensitivity analyses

Most treatment-by-subgroup interactions were nonsignificant. One adjusted interaction deserves attention: after including study as a fixed effect, the 24-hour diuresis effect was 664 mL with prior HF and 171 mL without prior HF, corrected P-interaction=.045. Supplementary Table S5 also contains an internally impossible NT-proBNP subgroup interval, HR 0.32 with 95% CI 0.38 to 1.35. That row was not used as a normalized quantitative result.

Placebo-only sensitivity analyses produced stronger short-term clinical estimates, including HR 0.34 for the 30-day composite and HR 0.42 for the 60-day composite. Supplementary Table S6 is harder to interpret for 24-hour diuresis because its raw medians are 2250 mL with SGLT2i and 3050 mL with placebo while the row reports P=.005 without an effect estimate. No direction or adjusted effect was inferred from that row.

Safety

New hypokalaemia was less frequent with SGLT2i at Day 2, 6% versus 16%, P=.001, and Day 3, 6% versus 14%, P=.007. Hyperkalaemia was uncommon. Prespecified serum-creatinine worsening measures did not differ significantly. These findings support short-term cardiorenal tolerability in the measured domains.

The safety evidence is incomplete. The pooled datasets did not contain enough ketoacidosis or genital-infection data for meaningful analysis. The paper therefore cannot support a comprehensive adverse-event comparison for early inpatient use.

Limitations and source-integrity review

  • Only six RCTs and 623 participants contributed IPD.
  • Two identified trials lacked usable IPD.
  • Comparator arms differed across trials, and several source trials were open label.
  • Follow-up duration and outcome availability differed. Only four trials contributed the composite clinical endpoint.
  • One trial required assigned event times for categorical follow-up windows.
  • Loop-diuretic dose during treatment could not be adjusted for.
  • Natriuresis and SGLT2i-specific safety endpoints were incomplete.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance9/10

Directly addresses early inpatient SGLT2i use in acute HF with decongestion and short-term clinical outcomes.

Evidence: Six acute-HF RCTs with IPD and clinically useful timing information.

Limit: The evidence is about class-level acute-HF initiation, not empagliflozin-to-dapagliflozin interchangeability.

Would raise/lower: A larger pragmatic head-to-head inpatient trial would raise this score.

Novelty8/10

Adds IPD harmonization, early decongestion endpoints, subgroup models, and pooled short-term outcomes beyond prior aggregate meta-analyses.

Evidence: 623 individual participants from six RCTs with harmonized outcome definitions.

Limit: Some component trials and aggregate meta-analyses were already published.

Would raise/lower: Independent IPD replication with more trials and longer follow-up would raise this score.

Method strength7/10

The IPD design and prespecified harmonization are strong, but the evidence base is small and heterogeneous.

Evidence: PRISMA-IPD reporting, PROSPERO registration, fixed-study models, sensitivity analyses, and RoB2 review.

Limit: Mixed control arms, open-label component trials, incomplete outcome availability, and assigned event times in one study.

Would raise/lower: Uniform placebo control, prospective shared endpoints, and exact event times would raise this score.

Evidence strength6/10

The diuresis signal is consistent, but the clinical-outcome claims rest on secondary endpoints with fewer contributing trials and short follow-up.

Evidence: 24-hour adjusted difference 604 mL; 60-day composite HR 0.62; win ratio 1.45.

Limit: Separate mortality and HF rehospitalization were nonsignificant, only four trials contributed the composite, and safety data were incomplete.

Would raise/lower: A dedicated adequately powered acute-HF outcomes trial would raise this score.

External validity6/10

The pooled population spans several acute-HF trials but has substantial selection and data-availability constraints.

Evidence: Six trials included both empagliflozin and dapagliflozin and a range of LVEF and kidney function.

Limit: 66% had diabetes, some trial populations were narrow, two eligible studies lacked IPD, and follow-up was short.

Would raise/lower: Broader multinational pragmatic enrollment with uniform endpoints would raise this score.

Implementation value7/10

The paper supports early inpatient initiation as a reasonable strategy but cannot by itself define formulary interchangeability or a complete safety protocol.

Evidence: Treatment usually began within 24 hours and decongestion results were available through Day 5.

Limit: The source does not compare dapagliflozin directly with empagliflozin and does not adequately assess ketoacidosis or genital infection.

Would raise/lower: Direct class-member comparisons and implementation-focused safety data would raise this score.

Bottom line

The article is useful evidence for starting an SGLT2 inhibitor early during an acute-HF admission. The diuresis result is the most convincing and reproducible part of the packet. The clinical-outcome signal is promising but less secure because it depends on secondary endpoints, fewer trials, short follow-up, uneven event capture, and one study's assigned event times. The paper does not answer whether dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are clinically interchangeable in the hospital.

Practice and formulary takeaways

  • Use this source to support the feasibility and decongestive effect of early inpatient SGLT2i initiation in acute HF.
  • Do not cite it as proof that one SGLT2 inhibitor can replace another. Both empagliflozin and dapagliflozin contributed, but the meta-analysis is not a direct head-to-head comparison.
  • Do not convert the 60-day composite into a stand-alone mortality claim. Separate mortality estimates were nonsignificant.
  • Pair any inpatient implementation policy with separate guideline, labeling, renal-function, peri-procedural, ketoacidosis-risk, and patient-selection review.
