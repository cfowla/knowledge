One-page synthesis

Core thesis. In adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related complication and without diabetes, once-weekly tirzepatide for 72 weeks produced large, dose-related reductions in body weight compared with placebo when both groups received lifestyle intervention. Common adverse events were predominantly gastrointestinal, especially during dose escalation.

Why the study matters

SURMOUNT-1 established a large randomized efficacy signal for a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist in obesity. Under the treatment-regimen estimand, mean week-72 weight change was −15.0%, −19.5%, and −20.9% for 5, 10, and 15 mg, respectively, versus −3.1% with placebo.

What is most convincing

The signal is large relative to placebo, has narrow confidence intervals, is dose-responsive, appears across continuous and categorical endpoints, and remains strong under a second efficacy estimand. Participant-level distribution plots support a broad shift rather than a few extreme responders.

What should not be overclaimed

The trial does not establish indefinite durability, post-discontinuation weight maintenance, rare long-term harms, hard cardiovascular benefit, or superiority to another obesity medication. Cardiometabolic improvements are largely surrogate outcomes.

Most important appraisal issue

The package is unusually transparent about estimands, missing-data handling, protocol revisions, and the SAP. Selected key-secondary endpoints and analysis details were revised during the trial; the primary objectives remained stable. The documents do not support an inference that revisions were data-driven.

Methods and analytic framework

PICO

Population: adults ≥18 years; BMI ≥30 kg/m² or ≥27 kg/m² plus at least one weight-related complication; ≥1 unsuccessful dietary effort; no diabetes.

Intervention: tirzepatide 5, 10, or 15 mg subcutaneously once weekly for 72 weeks. All participants began at 2.5 mg and escalated by 2.5 mg every 4 weeks to assigned maintenance dose.

Comparator: matching placebo.

Co-intervention: lifestyle counseling for an ~500-kcal/day deficit and ≥150 min/week physical activity.

Coprimary outcomes: percent body-weight change and proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss at week 72.

Randomization and masking

Randomization was 1:1:1:1 and stratified by country, sex, and prediabetes. The main study was double-blind. Protocol materials specify masking of participants, investigators, site personnel, and monitors, while a limited unblinded sponsor function supported interim/locked analyses and an independent DMC reviewed unblinded safety results.

This is a conventional and strong causal design for efficacy. The sponsor role remains relevant to independent appraisal.

Treatment-regimen estimand

Designed to estimate the effect at week 72 regardless of treatment adherence or discontinuation. Continuous outcomes used multiple imputation plus ANCOVA; binary outcomes used logistic regression. The missing-data strategy distinguished COVID-only missingness from other intercurrent events, including retrieved-dropout information.

Efficacy estimand

Targets a hypothetical effect had participants remained on randomized treatment. Repeated continuous outcomes used MMRM under a missing-at-random framework; binary outcomes were derived through imputation/prediction procedures tied to the continuous model.

Results — section condensation

Participants and retention

From December 2019 through April 2022, 2539 participants were randomized: 630 to 5 mg, 636 to 10 mg, 630 to 15 mg, and 643 to placebo. Mean age was 44.9 years; 67.5% were women, 70.6% were White, mean weight was 104.8 kg, mean BMI was 38.0 kg/m², and 40.6% had prediabetes. Only 5.5% were in the BMI 27–<30 kg/m² category.

Overall, 86.0% completed the primary trial period. Completion was 88.4–89.8% across tirzepatide groups versus 77.0% with placebo; on-treatment completion was 83.6–85.7% versus 73.6%. The differential placebo attrition is visible in Figure S2 and increases reliance on the prespecified estimand and missing-data assumptions.

Primary efficacy

Under the treatment-regimen estimand, mean weight change at week 72 was −15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg), and −20.9% (15 mg), versus −3.1% with placebo. Corresponding treatment differences were −11.9 percentage points (95% CI −13.4 to −10.4), −16.4 (−17.9 to −14.8), and −17.8 (−19.3 to −16.3); all P<0.001. At least 5% weight loss occurred in 85.1%, 88.9%, and 90.9% versus 34.5% with placebo.

Higher categorical thresholds remained strongly separated: ≥20% loss occurred in 30.0%, 50.1%, and 56.7% of the 5-, 10-, and 15-mg groups versus 3.1% with placebo. The ≥25% threshold was exploratory (15.3%, 32.3%, 36.2% vs 1.5%). The efficacy estimand showed even larger mean losses (−16.0%, −21.4%, −22.5% vs −2.4%), making the central result robust to the two prespecified estimand perspectives.

Secondary and metabolic outcomes

Prespecified pooled analyses favored tirzepatide for week-20 weight, SF-36 physical-function score, systolic blood pressure, triglycerides, non-HDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and fasting insulin. Among those with baseline prediabetes, 95.3% of tirzepatide-treated participants were normoglycemic at week 72 versus 61.9% with placebo. These findings support broad metabolic improvement but are largely intermediate or surrogate outcomes.

In the DXA substudy with 160 evaluable participants, total fat mass fell 33.9% with pooled tirzepatide versus 8.2% with placebo (estimated treatment difference −25.7 percentage points; 95% CI −31.4 to −20.0), and the fat-to-lean mass ratio improved. Substudy selection and smaller sample size limit precision and generalizability relative to the full trial.

Safety

Any adverse event occurred in roughly 79–82% of tirzepatide-treated participants versus 72.0% with placebo. Nausea (24.6–33.3%), diarrhea (18.7–23.0%), and vomiting (8.3–12.2%) were more frequent with tirzepatide and were predominantly mild or moderate, clustering during dose escalation. Adverse events led to treatment discontinuation in 4.3%, 7.1%, and 6.2% of tirzepatide groups versus 2.6% with placebo.

Serious adverse-event rates were similar (5.1–6.9% across tirzepatide vs 6.8% placebo). Confirmed pancreatitis occurred in one participant in each tirzepatide group and none in placebo; deaths were 4, 2, 1, and 4 across the four groups. Rare-event counts are too small for definitive comparative-harm inference.

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret the degree of weight loss as substantial and sustained through 72 weeks, with improvement across several cardiometabolic measures. They caution against cross-trial comparisons with other obesity interventions and state that outcome trials are needed to determine whether risk-factor improvements translate into fewer clinical events.

Source-stated limitations include potential selection of particularly motivated volunteers, relatively normal baseline cardiometabolic risk values, the small overweight-with-comorbidity subgroup, and the need for longer follow-up. Independent appraisal adds sponsor involvement, placebo attrition imbalance, and late revision of selected secondary endpoints as issues to keep visible without converting them into evidence of bias.

Main strengths

  • Large, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial.
  • Very large effect sizes with narrow confidence intervals and dose response.
  • Two prespecified estimands address different intercurrent-event questions.
  • Detailed supplement, participant flow, safety tables, and protocol/SAP materials enable independent review.
  • Multiplicity framework and FDA-driven statistical revisions are documented.

Main limitations

  • 72-week primary horizon does not resolve multiyear durability or post-discontinuation regain.
  • Placebo retention was materially lower than tirzepatide retention.
  • Trial excludes diabetes and multiple higher-risk conditions; only 5.5% were BMI 27–<30.
  • Female and White participants predominate globally.
  • Sponsor designed/oversaw the trial and analyzed data.
  • Selected secondary endpoints/analysis details were revised during the trial.
  • Rare harms and hard clinical outcomes remain underpowered/unanswered here.
Confidence in appraisal: high. The main article, full supplementary appendix, and final protocol/SAP materials were available, allowing the central efficacy, safety, attrition, and analysis claims to be cross-checked across the source package.

Implementation and practice takeaways

What to retain

  • Expected trial-level mean weight loss is dose-responsive and large by 72 weeks.
  • A substantial fraction achieved ≥15% and ≥20% loss at 10–15 mg.
  • GI adverse events are common and concentrate during escalation.
  • The 20-week titration structure is central to interpreting early tolerability and efficacy.
  • Attrition and estimand choice matter when communicating expected real-world effectiveness.

What not to infer

  • Do not infer current indication, contraindications, dosing instructions, or warnings from this paper alone.
  • Do not claim cardiovascular event reduction from BP/lipid/insulin improvements.
  • Do not treat the exploratory ≥25% threshold as equivalent to multiplicity-controlled primary/key-secondary evidence.
  • Do not assume rare adverse-event rates are precisely estimated.
  • Do not attribute protocol amendments to outcome knowledge without evidence.
For clinical use: pair this evidence with the current product label, contemporary obesity guidelines, current cardiovascular-outcomes evidence, patient-specific contraindications/monitoring, and local access/formulary information.
