One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The ACC summary frames HFpEF as a multisystem syndrome and presents the 2026 pathway as a practical update built around accurate diagnosis, phenotype-aware care, newer pharmacologic options, nonpharmacologic treatment, and aggressive comorbidity management.

The page highlights newer trial support for SGLT2 inhibitors, nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and incretin-based therapies. It also lists angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, diuretics, and beta-blockers among therapies addressed by the pathway. The summary says treatment should extend beyond heart failure symptoms to cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic health.

The diagnostic message is just as prominent. The workflow starts with dyspnea or edema, then calls for HFpEF scoring systems, echocardiography, natriuretic peptides, and differential diagnosis for mimics before treatment. The page adds sex-specific considerations and a multidisciplinary care model. It gives no trial-level estimates, evidence grades, dosing instructions, monitoring parameters, diagnostic cutoffs, or contraindications. Those omissions prevent this summary from standing alone as a practice protocol.

Section condensation

Lead summary

The ACC page describes the 2026 ECDP as an update to the 2023 pathway. It shifts the conceptual frame away from a single cardiac disorder toward a multisystem syndrome associated with visceral adiposity, inflammation, metabolic dysfunction, and clinically distinct phenotypes.

The page presents individualized care as a consequence of that phenotype diversity. It does not define phenotypes or reproduce the evidence used to establish them.

New Evidence

The summary says new positive clinical trial data support SGLT2 inhibitors, nonsteroidal MRAs, and incretin-based therapies. It argues that these treatments may benefit cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic health as well as heart failure outcomes.

No trial names, sample sizes, effect estimates, confidence intervals, safety results, or evidence grades appear in the supplied PDF. The statement is a secondary summary of the evidence behind the ECDP.

Accurate Diagnosis

The page describes diagnosis as a central part of the ECDP. It lists differential diagnosis, HFpEF scoring systems, assessment for mimics, and sex-specific considerations. The visual workflow also names echocardiography and natriuretic peptides.

The stated aim is correct identification followed by early evidence- and guideline-based therapy. The summary does not provide score cutoffs, natriuretic peptide thresholds, echocardiographic criteria, or a differential diagnosis list.

Comprehensive Management

The summary names SGLT2 inhibitors, MRAs, incretin-based therapies, angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, diuretics, and beta-blockers. It also names exercise and caloric restriction as nonpharmacologic interventions.

Comorbidity management includes coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, and obesity. The page does not provide drug sequencing, doses, titration endpoints, renal limits, safety monitoring, or phenotype-specific treatment rules.

Author caution and citation

The ECDP authors acknowledge that recommendations may need revision as science changes. They describe the pathway as practical guidance for contemporary HFpEF decision-making, treatment implementation, and comorbidity management.

The page cites the full JACC ECDP by Kittleson, Panjrath, Bates, and colleagues, DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.06.018. That citation is the only formal reference listed in the supplied summary.

Workflow reconciliation

The PDF contains one substantive management visual. It was reconstructed as structured text because its logic is more important than its graphic styling.

Patient presents with dyspnea, edema, or both
  -> primary care clinician and cardiologist

ASSESS
Use HFpEF scoring systems
Check echocardiogram and natriuretic peptides
  -> differential diagnosis for HFpEF mimics
  -> multidisciplinary team

TREAT
Initiate optimal medical therapy
Optimize management of comorbidities
