One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Long-term IBD care requires systematic preventive and maintenance work beyond control of intestinal inflammation, with shared responsibility between gastroenterology and primary care for cancer surveillance, infection screening, vaccination, bone and nutritional health, mental health, reproductive counseling, cardiovascular risk, medication monitoring, and smoking cessation.

The review is highly operational. It consolidates screening in Table 1 and prevention in Table 2, then adds narrative detail on colorectal-cancer risk stratification, immunosuppression-related infection risk, pregnancy medication safety, vaccine timing, nutrient replacement, and laboratory monitoring. Quantitative anchors include increased CRC incidence/mortality in large Scandinavian cohorts, an approximately doubled tuberculosis risk with TNF antagonists in a 29-trial meta-analysis, large relative reductions in HBV reactivation with antiviral prophylaxis, and pooled depression/anxiety prevalence estimates.

The principal limitation is methodological: the article is a narrative educational review and does not report a systematic evidence-search process, formal risk-of-bias assessment, or a unified grading framework for recommendations. The recommendations also span multiple guideline bodies and several time-sensitive vaccine policies. The supplied PDF references an online Supplement Figure 1 and Supplement Table 1 but those were not supplied as companion materials.

Section condensation

Introduction

The article frames IBD as a lifelong relapsing disease whose burden extends beyond intestinal symptoms to structural bowel damage, cancer, thromboembolic risk, extraintestinal manifestations, fatigue, psychiatric comorbidity, financial toxicity, and adverse effects of chronic immunosuppression. Modern treatment is described as treat-to-target: clinical symptoms are insufficient by themselves, and fecal calprotectin, C-reactive protein, and periodic endoscopy are used to confirm inflammatory control.

The authors emphasize early effective therapy, avoidance of long-term systemic corticosteroids, and a shared-care model. Gastroenterology usually leads disease-specific endoscopic surveillance and infection screening before advanced therapy, while primary care and specialty teams share vaccination, routine cancer screening, bone health, nutrition, mental health, cardiovascular prevention, and adherence to monitoring.

Screening

For colonic IBD, CRC surveillance is central: initial colonoscopy is generally recommended 8-10 years after symptom onset, while PSC is an exception with surveillance at IBD diagnosis and annually thereafter. Higher-risk disease can justify annual surveillance and lower-risk patients may extend to every 2-3 years after consecutive negative examinations. The review also addresses annual skin examination during immunosuppressive therapy, age-appropriate cervical screening with possible shorter intervals during immunosuppression, individualized anal-cancer surveillance in high-risk perianal Crohn disease, and DEXA for patients with corticosteroid exposure or other osteoporosis risks.

Infection screening is organized around immunosuppression: LTBI and HBV are key pre-treatment targets, with HBV prophylaxis emphasized for high-risk serologic profiles. HCV and HIV screening generally follows population guidance, with some societies supporting pre-immunosuppression HIV testing. The section also recommends nutritional surveillance, annual depression/anxiety screening, and preconception counseling with remission before pregnancy and avoidance of methotrexate, JAK inhibitors, and sphingosine-1-phosphate modulators in pregnancy.

Prevention

The prevention section is vaccine-heavy and distinguishes live from nonlive vaccines around immunosuppression. It covers recombinant zoster, pneumococcal, influenza, HPV, COVID-19, hepatitis A/B, RSV, and MMR vaccination, including age, immune-status, and timing criteria. A serology-guided hepatitis B revaccination pathway is described for patients whose anti-HBs remains below 10 mIU/mL after a prior series.

Nonvaccine prevention includes sun protection and annual dermatologic surveillance, calcium/vitamin D and resistance/weight-bearing exercise for bone health, IV iron when hemoglobin is below 10 g/dL, parenteral B12 for documented deficiency, aggressive traditional cardiovascular risk-factor control, CBC/liver/creatinine monitoring every 3-6 months during long-term immunosuppressive therapy, renal surveillance with 5-aminosalicylates, lipid monitoring with JAK inhibitors, and structured smoking-cessation support.

Practice Improvement

The authors translate the review into measurable care processes. Proposed health-maintenance indicators include smoking cessation counseling, immunization documentation, adherence to CRC surveillance, LTBI and HBV screening before advanced therapy, TPMT testing before thiopurines, timely transition away from corticosteroids, and C. difficile testing during acute IBD flares regardless of recent antibiotic exposure.

The section points readers to AGA, ACG, and ECCO guidelines and best-practice updates. The Tool Kit then links to patient and professional resources, and the final patient-information page converts the review into plain-language prompts about colonoscopy, bone density, vaccines, infection screening, skin protection, mental health, exercise, diet, and smoking.

Do not use for: acute IBD treatment decisions, comparative efficacy of advanced therapies, or as a substitute for current CDC/ACIP/AGA/ACG/ECCO guidance.
Confidence in appraisal: high for source content and structure because the full 16-page supplied PDF was inspected; moderate for practice durability because several recommendations are time-sensitive and the review is not itself a guideline.

Main strengths

  • Broad coverage of practical health-maintenance domains with explicit thresholds and intervals.
  • Strong integration of primary care and gastroenterology responsibilities.
  • Quantitative anchors are preserved where clinically meaningful.
  • Two compact tables make the review implementation-friendly.
  • Recent citations include 2025-2026 guideline and observational evidence.

Main limitations

  • No reported systematic search strategy, study-selection method, risk-of-bias assessment, or formal evidence-certainty framework.
  • Recommendations are synthesized from multiple organizations and source types; not all are clearly labeled with recommendation strength.
  • Vaccination guidance can change rapidly and should be version-checked before operational implementation.
  • Supplement Figure 1 and Supplement Table 1 were referenced but unavailable in the supplied materials.
  • As a secondary review, its quantitative findings should not be mistaken for results generated by the article itself.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Build a maintenance checklist

Map CRC surveillance, skin/cervical screening, DEXA, nutrition, mental health, LTBI/HBV/HIV/HCV, vaccination, pregnancy counseling, cardiovascular risk, and smoking into a recurring health-maintenance panel.

Make immunosuppression a trigger

Starting or escalating immunosuppression should trigger infection screening, vaccine review, pregnancy counseling when relevant, and therapy-specific laboratory monitoring.

Assign ownership

Use explicit PCP/GI responsibility fields so preventive tasks are not assumed to belong to the other service.

Version time-sensitive rules

Vaccination schedules and age cutoffs should be stored with guideline source/date rather than hard-coded indefinitely.

Practice bottom line: This review is unusually useful as a checklist seed. Its strongest value is not as a final evidence authority but as a structured inventory of the preventive, screening, monitoring, and shared-care tasks that should be deliberately owned in adult IBD care.
