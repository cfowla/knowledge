Scope warning. This trial addresses temporary interruption of warfarin for elective procedures in selected patients with atrial fibrillation/flutter. It excluded mechanical prosthetic valves, recent thromboembolism, severe renal insufficiency, thrombocytopenia, and specified high-risk surgeries; it should not be generalized beyond those boundaries without additional evidence.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. BRIDGE is the pivotal randomized trial establishing that routine therapeutic-dose heparin bridging is generally unnecessary for the studied warfarin-treated AF population and increases bleeding.

Clinical question. In warfarin-treated adults with atrial fibrillation/flutter undergoing elective procedures that require warfarin interruption, does perioperative therapeutic-dose dalteparin bridging reduce arterial thromboembolism enough to justify its bleeding risk?

Answer. Among 1,813 participants with outcome data, arterial thromboembolism occurred in 0.4% without bridging and 0.3% with bridging (risk difference 0.1 percentage points; 95% CI -0.6 to 0.8; P=0.01 for noninferiority). Major bleeding was lower without bridging: 1.3% versus 3.2% (RR 0.41; 95% CI 0.20-0.78; P=0.005). Minor bleeding was also lower (12.0% vs 20.9%).

Why it matters. The trial directly overturned a common prophylactic practice by showing no detectable thromboembolic benefit within its prespecified noninferiority framework while demonstrating a clinically important bleeding penalty. The key caution is that very-high-stroke-risk patients and high-risk surgeries were sparse or excluded.

Methods and population

Design and enrollment

BRIDGE randomized 1,884 patients at 108 U.S. and Canadian sites from July 2009 through December 2014. Participants were adults with chronic atrial fibrillation/flutter treated with warfarin for at least 3 months, target INR 2.0-3.0, requiring elective interruption, and with at least one CHADS2 risk factor. Mean age was 71.7 years; 73.4% were men; mean CHADS2 score was 2.3 and 38.3% had a score ≥3.

Major exclusions included mechanical valves, recent arterial thromboembolism/VTE, recent major bleeding, CrCl <30 mL/min, platelets <100×10^9/L, pregnancy, HIT/heparin allergy, and selected cardiac/intracranial/intraspinal procedures.

Intervention and comparator

Warfarin was stopped 5 days before the procedure and resumed on the evening of or day after the procedure. Bridging used dalteparin 100 IU/kg SC twice daily beginning 3 days before the procedure, with the last dose about 24 hours preprocedure. Matching placebo represented no bridging.

Postprocedure dalteparin/placebo restarted 12-24 hours after low-bleeding-risk procedures or 48-72 hours after high-bleeding-risk procedures and continued until INR ≥2.0 once. The mean administered dalteparin dose was 9,093±2,240 IU twice daily.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary efficacy endpoint was 30-day arterial thromboembolism (stroke, TIA, systemic embolism); the primary safety endpoint was major bleeding. Outcomes were independently and blindly adjudicated. The efficacy analysis used a 1.0-percentage-point noninferiority margin, one-sided alpha 0.025; major bleeding was tested for superiority.

The Supplementary Appendix provides detailed event definitions. The protocol documents the prespecified randomized double-blind design, therapeutic-dose dalteparin regimen, primary endpoints, and original sample-size assumptions. The published article reports later sample-size revisions in response to lower-than-expected blinded event rates.

Principal limitations

Very-high-risk AF patients (CHADS2 5-6) were uncommon; mechanical valves were excluded; several high-risk procedures were not represented; and the observed arterial-thromboembolism rate (~0.4%) was lower than anticipated. The authors specifically noted that the 1.0-percentage-point noninferiority margin became large relative to the observed event rate.

Bottom line

Within the studied population, routine therapeutic-dose LMWH bridging during temporary warfarin interruption offers no demonstrated thromboembolic advantage and increases major and minor bleeding. The result is strong for typical elective-procedure AF patients represented in BRIDGE, but should not be extrapolated to mechanical-valve patients or poorly represented very-high-risk scenarios.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Use BRIDGE as direct randomized evidence against routine therapeutic-dose LMWH bridging for eligible warfarin-treated AF patients undergoing elective interruption.

Do not overgeneralize

Do not use BRIDGE alone to decide management for mechanical valves, recent thromboembolism, severe renal impairment, thrombocytopenia, or major high-risk surgeries excluded/underrepresented.

Operational detail

The trial protocol provides a concrete warfarin stop/restart and LMWH timing framework, but local perioperative guidance and patient-specific bleeding/thromboembolic risk remain necessary.
