Synthesis

Verdict: Skim deeply. The main value is clarification of high-LVEF, mortality, generalizability, and kidney-function questions rather than new confirmatory evidence.

The letters challenge whether DELIVER’s LVEF ≥60% finding could be explained by improved-EF patients, whether real-world cost and external validity justify broad implementation, whether kidney disease influenced the high-EF signal, and whether all-cause hospitalization or background MRA therapy modified benefit.

The authors’ reply adds one useful unpublished/secondary analysis: after excluding improved-EF participants, the baseline-LVEF ≥60% primary HR was 0.81 (95% CI 0.64–1.04). They reiterate that DELIVER lacked power for CV death, cite pooled/meta-analytic mortality results, and argue for generalizability across age/frailty and care settings. The reply also reports mean eGFR 59±19 in LVEF ≥60% participants and states benefit was seen irrespective of eGFR.

What the letters add

  • Zweck & Westenfeld: question high-LVEF consistency and the role of improved EF.
  • Redfors & Omerovic: emphasize real-world effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, and pragmatic implementation.
  • Weir: asks whether CKD phenotype explains benefit at LVEF ≥60%.
  • Gabrielli, Oliva & Zuccalà: ask about all-cause hospitalization and possible interaction with MRA background therapy.

Useful for clarification but not standalone evidence.

Limit: Does not resolve all practical questions or replace guidelines.
