One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. High-value 2026 hepatology roadmap and workflow reference; use it to identify what deserves primary-source follow-up, not as the sole evidentiary basis for practice change.

The review organizes rapid hepatology change around a common theme: earlier detection, non-invasive risk stratification, and mechanism-based disease modification. MASLD/MASH is the clearest example, with approved resmetirom and semaglutide, a large antifibrotic pipeline, and NITs increasingly used for prognosis and response monitoring. HBV care is simplified around fibrosis/viral burden and emerging biomarkers; HDV has moved from near-therapeutic nihilism toward bulevirtide and phase 2 HBsAg/RNA-targeting combinations with possible functional-cure trajectories. PBC now has multiple second-line agents and stronger risk stratification, whereas PSC remains without licensed disease-modifying therapy.

For cirrhosis complications, the review highlights broader non-invasive identification of CSPH, expanding TIPS indications, revised HRS-AKI fluid/albumin thinking, and clinically centered hepatic-encephalopathy management. HCC is framed as an immunotherapy era with meaningful long-term survivors. The article is strongest as a current synthesis and implementation map. Its principal weakness is methodological: it is not a systematic review, provides no reproducible evidence-selection/risk-of-bias process, and sometimes compresses trial/guideline details enough that primary sources remain necessary.

Key source-level cautions: narrative text and Figure 1 differ on MASLD prevalence in T2D (nearly 70% vs 65%); Figure 9 calls seladelpar/elafibranor “FXR agonists” despite section 4.1.2 identifying them as PPAR agonists; many treatment claims are secondary reports of primary trials.

Section condensation

Introduction and scope

This expert-meeting review presents the 2026 Paris International Liver Meeting as an inflection point from complication management toward earlier detection, prognostic stratification, and disease modification. It is organized by clinical domain rather than by a formal systematic-review framework, and repeatedly uses meeting presentations plus cited literature to construct a forward-looking synthesis.

The source reports participation by 700+ specialists from 67 countries, but it does not describe a systematic search strategy, study-selection process, duplicate extraction, formal risk-of-bias assessment, or evidence-certainty grading. That limits reproducibility and means apparent consensus should not be equated with guideline-level evidence.

MASLD/MASH: systemic disease, NITs, lifestyle, and therapeutics

The MASLD section reframes MASH as a systemic CKLM disorder. It reports ~38% global MASLD prevalence, 5%-7% MASH prevalence, heavy T2D enrichment, and emphasizes fibrosis as the dominant prognostic marker. Non-invasive tests are presented as moving from staging toward prognostication, patient selection, and response monitoring. Lifestyle remains foundational: the figure gives >=5% weight loss for liver fat, 7%-10% for inflammation, and >=10% for fibrosis; >=150 min/week moderate-to-vigorous activity is linked to >=30% imaging liver-fat reduction.

The most practice-relevant trial summaries are resmetirom and semaglutide. For resmetirom 80 mg, the review reports MASH resolution 25.9% vs 9.7% placebo and >=1-stage fibrosis improvement 24.2% vs 14.2%. For semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly, it reports MASH resolution 62.9% vs 34.3% and fibrosis improvement 36.8% vs 22.4%, plus -10.5% vs -2.0% body-weight change. The review also surveys a large pipeline, but many claims are cross-trial or phase 2 summaries; these should be used to prioritize primary papers, not as comparative-effectiveness evidence.

HBV and HDV

HBV management is portrayed as simplifying around fibrosis and viral burden rather than HBeAg/age categories. The source summarizes treatment thresholds using HBsAg positivity, detectable HBV DNA, LSM, ALT, HCC risk and transmission risk. Quantitative HBsAg, HBcrAg and HBV RNA are discussed as tools for residual viral activity and treatment-discontinuation decisions.

For HDV, the review moves from chronic suppression toward potential functional cure. Bulevirtide is described as the current anchor therapy, with week-96 data supporting persistence and similar responses at 2 mg and 10 mg. The phase 2 SOLSTICE combination of tobevibart plus elebsiran is highlighted: at week 48, 91% reached HBsAg <10 IU/mL and 66% had undetectable HDV RNA, versus 21% and 48% with tobevibart alone. These are secondary-reported phase 2 results and need primary-trial confirmation for exact interpretation.

PBC and PSC

PBC is presented as a precision-care model integrating age, UDCA biochemical response, and LSM. The source uses LSM >10 kPa as an important prognostic marker and summarizes phase 3 elafibranor/seladelpar data, with seladelpar showing pruritus benefit. Contemporary transplant-free survival is reported >85%-90% at 10 years, while fatigue remains common (50%-80%) and under-treated. A major implementation failure is that fewer than half of eligible high-risk patients receive second-line therapy.

PSC remains the counterexample: the review calls it a therapeutic desert without licensed disease-modifying therapy, with 70%-80% IBD association and severe long-term outcomes. Ursodeoxycholic acid is described as biochemically active without survival benefit, and high doses as harmful. Norucholic acid and elafibranor are framed as pipeline signals rather than established treatment.

Hepatocellular carcinoma

The source summarizes standard surveillance as ultrasound plus AFP every 6 months, with cross-sectional imaging for nodules >1 cm, and reviews emerging biomarker/liquid-biopsy/AI approaches. It emphasizes the transition in advanced HCC to immunotherapy-based combinations and reports atezolizumab-bevacizumab median OS 19.2 vs 13.2 months with sorafenib, exploratory STRIDE 5-year OS of 20%, and a 36% response rate for nivolumab-ipilimumab.

For intermediate-stage disease, TARE and immunotherapy/anti-angiogenic combinations are discussed as challengers to TACE. The source appropriately notes that 58% vs 31% TARE-versus-TACE downstaging evidence is based on phase 2/retrospective data rather than phase 3 head-to-head trials. It also notes failure of some triple-agent phase 3 programs to exceed doublet efficacy, supporting a shift toward biomarkers and novel targets.

Portal hypertension, TIPS, HRS-AKI, and hepatic encephalopathy

The cirrhosis section highlights non-invasive CSPH classification: Baveno VII LSM >25 kPa rule-in and LSM <15 kPa plus platelets >=150 G/L rule-out, reported NPV 99%. Adding spleen stiffness can reduce the indeterminate zone from 48% to 9%. Salvage TIPS prognosis is framed with MELD/lactate, and preemptive TIPS is expanding into gastric-variceal bleeding.

HRS-AKI guidance is summarized as moving away from obligatory 48-hour albumin challenge, but the review flags that 34% of patients in one study responded between 24 and 48 hours. For hepatic encephalopathy, the source reports 1-year mortality 64% after HE onset vs 17% without decompensation, reinforces clinical diagnosis over ammonia alone, and describes lactulose first-line with rifaximin for recurrence prevention. The section is clinically useful but mostly secondary synthesis of guidelines and cited studies.

Clinical translation and research priorities

The closing section argues that trial success will not translate automatically into population benefit. It identifies adherence, gastrointestinal tolerability, heterogeneous response, provider/payer barriers, and insufficient uptake of second-line PBC therapy as implementation bottlenecks. It also calls for long-term MASH safety/durability data and evidence in pregnant, pediatric, and non-obese populations.

This is one of the review's strongest contributions: it separates drug-development momentum from implementation science and patient-centered outcomes. The limitation is that proposed priorities are expert synthesis rather than a formally ranked research agenda.

Evaluation & appraisal

9/10
Relevance

Broadly covers high-impact 2026 hepatology changes across MASLD, viral hepatitis, PBC/PSC, HCC and cirrhosis.

Evidence basis: Dense clinical synthesis with numerous actionable thresholds and trial signals.

Principal limit: Breadth reduces depth within each topic.

Would raise score: Primary-source retrieval for the highest-impact claims would raise utility further.

8/10
Novelty

Captures a very current cross-domain snapshot and links disparate advances under precision/disease-modification themes.

Evidence basis: Includes 2025-2026 trial/guideline developments and meeting synthesis.

Principal limit: Many individual findings were already available in primary trials/guidelines.

Would raise score: Novelty would be higher if the review contributed a formal new evidence synthesis method or original meta-analysis.

4/10
Method strength

The article is a narrative expert-meeting review without a reported systematic search, selection, risk-of-bias, or certainty-grading method.

Evidence basis: Source structure and citations are extensive, but methods for evidence inclusion are not reproducible.

Principal limit: Selection and emphasis may reflect expert/meeting availability and narrative framing.

Would raise score: A prespecified systematic search, dual review, risk-of-bias assessment and evidence grading would materially raise the score.

6/10
Evidence strength

Many claims rest on strong underlying RCTs/guidelines, but the article itself reports them secondarily and often without full uncertainty/denominator detail.

Evidence basis: Key phase 3 estimates are preserved for MASH and HCC; several sections explicitly acknowledge limitations.

Principal limit: Secondary reporting, cross-trial comparisons and phase 2 signals constrain direct evidentiary weight.

Would raise score: Direct appraisal of the cited primary studies and full confidence intervals/adverse-event data would raise confidence.

6/10
External validity

The source intentionally spans international hepatology and multiple etiologies, but not all populations or health systems are equally represented.

Evidence basis: International expert participation and broad disease coverage.

Principal limit: Underrepresented groups are explicitly acknowledged; practice access and regional guideline differences remain.

Would raise score: Real-world comparative implementation data across diverse populations would raise the score.

8/10
Implementation value

Provides clear algorithms, thresholds, treatment classes and care gaps that can guide reading, pathway review and CDS content planning.

Evidence basis: Figures 2, 5, 6 and 10 are directly workflow-oriented; closing section highlights implementation barriers.

Principal limit: It should not be used as the sole practice authority for drug selection or guideline thresholds.

Would raise score: Linking each recommendation to current primary guideline text and local operational constraints would raise the score.

Methodological limitation: The source gives no explicit systematic literature search, study-selection criteria, formal risk-of-bias assessment, certainty grading, or meta-analytic method. Scores therefore reflect the review as a synthesis artifact, not the quality of every underlying cited study.

Practice translation

Best use

Roadmap for 2026 hepatology priorities; source discovery; identifying thresholds/algorithms to verify; CDS/content planning; cross-domain teaching.

Do not use for

Sole-source prescribing, guideline adjudication, comparative-effectiveness conclusions across different trials, or unverified adoption of emerging phase 2 therapies.

High-value follow-up

Primary resmetirom/semaglutide MASH trials; EASL HBV guidance; MYR301/SOLSTICE HDV data; PBC RESPONSE/ELATIVE trials; HCC phase 3 regimens; Baveno VII/VIII and GAVAPROSEC.

Implementation focus

NIT workflow design, multidisciplinary CKLM care, PBC second-line therapy uptake, standardized HDV testing, real-world persistence/tolerability, and patient-reported symptom burden.

External currency check

A selective authoritative check was performed because the source makes current clinical-practice claims. FDA labeling current in July 2026 confirms resmetirom (Rezdiffra) for adults with noncirrhotic MASH/NASH with F2-F3 fibrosis, and FDA's accelerated-approval list records Wegovy (semaglutide) for noncirrhotic MASH with F2-F3 fibrosis approved August 15, 2025. These checks support the review's statement that both agents are approved in the United States; they do not independently verify every treatment or guideline statement in the review.
