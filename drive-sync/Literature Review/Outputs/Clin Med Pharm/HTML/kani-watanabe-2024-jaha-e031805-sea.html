Scope warning. This source compares active agents through indirect placebo-connected networks. It does not provide randomized head-to-head evidence and does not establish therapeutic equivalence or a definitive agent hierarchy.

One-page synthesis

The review pooled 21 placebo-controlled RCTs enrolling 96,196 participants. It compared empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, and sotagliflozin for cardiovascular, heart-failure, kidney, and safety outcomes. Across the overall network, no consistent statistically significant difference appeared among agents for the primary composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization, all-cause death, cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, or kidney disease progression. Acute kidney injury was lower with empagliflozin versus sotagliflozin, HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.92, and with dapagliflozin versus sotagliflozin, HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.56 to 0.96.

Subgroup results generated the main comparative signals. In participants without CKD, empagliflozin was favored over ertugliflozin for the primary composite, HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.98, and over dapagliflozin for all-cause death and cardiovascular death. It was also favored over canagliflozin for cardiovascular death. Sensitivity analyses produced several related signals, but they were not uniform across outcomes and analyses. These findings remain indirect, derive from a network with placebo as the only common comparator, and arise amid many subgroup contrasts.

Verdict. Read soon for comparative class and formulary context. Do not use it as a stand-alone agent-selection hierarchy.

Methods and source structure

The investigators searched MEDLINE, EMBASE, and CENTRAL from inception through August 31, 2023. Two reviewers independently selected studies, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias with RoB2. The analysis used random-effects frequentist network meta-analysis in netmeta, reported heterogeneity with Q, tau, and I-squared, and assessed publication bias with funnel plots and Egger tests. Prespecified subgroups were diabetes, CKD, and heart-failure phenotype. Sensitivity analyses excluded EMPA-REG OUTCOME and VERTIS-CV.

Eligibility required adult randomized placebo-controlled evidence, at least 3 months of treatment, at least 500 participants, and a relevant outcome. The supplement's PRISMA flow reports 4,518 records identified, 2,124 screened after duplicate removal, 86 full-text reports assessed, 34 full-text reports excluded, 21 RCTs included, and 52 reports included.

Results

Overall network

No statistically significant active-agent differences were detected for the primary composite, all-cause death, cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, or kidney disease progression. The primary-composite network showed low heterogeneity, Q=8.12, p=0.52, tau=0, I-squared=0%.

Diabetes and no diabetes

Among participants with diabetes, overall efficacy contrasts remained non-significant. Acute kidney injury favored empagliflozin versus sotagliflozin, HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.00, and dapagliflozin versus sotagliflozin, HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57 to 1.00. Among participants without diabetes, only empagliflozin and dapagliflozin were represented and no statistically significant between-agent difference was detected.

CKD and no CKD

The CKD subgroup showed no statistically significant active-agent efficacy differences. In participants without CKD, empagliflozin was favored over ertugliflozin for the primary composite, HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.98; over dapagliflozin for all-cause death, HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.52 to 0.99, and cardiovascular death, HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.89; and over canagliflozin for cardiovascular death, HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.96. Primary-outcome heterogeneity was I-squared 0% in CKD and 3.4% without CKD.

Heart-failure phenotypes

No statistically significant active-agent efficacy differences were detected in the HFrEF or HFpEF subgroup networks. The discussion notes lower dapagliflozin point estimates for several HFrEF outcomes, but the contrasts were not statistically significant and phenotype coverage was incomplete.

Safety and sensitivity

No statistically significant active-agent differences were detected for limb amputation, fracture, hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, urinary tract infection, or genital infection. The source reports higher orthostatic-hypotension risk for ertugliflozin and empagliflozin versus canagliflozin, and for empagliflozin versus dapagliflozin. Rare-event confidence intervals were wide, and the article itself identifies safety precision as a limitation.

Excluding EMPA-REG OUTCOME produced three notable subgroup contrasts: canagliflozin versus empagliflozin for heart-failure hospitalization in CKD, HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.54 to 0.95; ertugliflozin versus empagliflozin for the primary composite without CKD, HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.66; and dapagliflozin versus empagliflozin for heart-failure hospitalization without CKD, HR 1.31, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.70. Excluding VERTIS-CV retained several no-CKD contrasts favoring empagliflozin, including cardiovascular death and all-cause death.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 8/10

Directly addresses comparative SGLT2 inhibitor effectiveness across diabetes, CKD, and HF phenotypes.

Evidence basis. 96,196 participants across 21 randomized trials and clinically relevant cardiorenal outcomes.

Principal limit. The question is agent selection, but the evidence is indirect.

Would raise score. Head-to-head comparative trials in the target subgroups would raise the score.

Novelty: 7/10

The subgroup-focused five-agent network adds comparative context beyond class-versus-placebo evidence.

Evidence basis. The network integrates five SGLT2 inhibitors and multiple underlying-condition strata.

Principal limit. Most source trials were designed for placebo comparisons, not comparative agent selection.

Would raise score. Patient-level comparative analyses or direct randomized comparisons would raise the score.

Method strength: 6/10

Search, duplicate review, RoB2, registered protocol, and random-effects NMA are strengths.

Evidence basis. Three databases, PROSPERO registration, duplicate extraction, RoB2, prespecified subgroups and sensitivity analyses.

Principal limit. The placebo-only star network has no closed loops, so direct-versus-indirect consistency cannot be tested.

Would raise score. A connected network with direct active-agent evidence and individual-participant data would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 5/10

The underlying trials are strong, but agent-to-agent claims rely on indirect contrasts and multiple subgroup comparisons.

Evidence basis. Large randomized evidence base with generally low risk of bias and low heterogeneity for major efficacy networks.

Principal limit. Sparse subgroups, multiplicity, rare safety outcomes, and wide intervals weaken superiority claims.

Would raise score. Replicated direct comparative effects with multiplicity control would raise the score.

External validity: 6/10

The evidence spans diabetes, CKD, and HF trials but subgroup availability differs sharply by agent and indication.

Evidence basis. Broad cardiorenal trial portfolio and follow-up from 3 months to 4.2 years.

Principal limit. No-diabetes and HF phenotype networks do not contain all agents; trial-level aggregation limits patient-level transportability.

Would raise score. More complete agent coverage and patient-level effect-modification analysis would raise the score.

Implementation value: 5/10

Useful for formulary context and for identifying where comparative evidence is weak, but insufficient for a strict agent hierarchy.

Evidence basis. The source highlights overall null contrasts and specific no-CKD signals with sensitivity checks.

Principal limit. Indirect estimates cannot establish therapeutic equivalence or definitive superiority.

Would raise score. Head-to-head effectiveness, safety, dosing, cost, formulary, and current-guideline integration would raise the score.

Main strengths

Large randomized evidence base, registered protocol, broad database search, duplicate review, RoB2 assessment, explicit subgroup definitions, sensitivity analyses, and generally low heterogeneity support a careful class-level comparison.

Main limitations

The network has no randomized active-agent head-to-head edges and no closed loops. Trial-level aggregation limits patient-level effect modification. Doses of the same agent were pooled. Several subgroup networks are sparse or omit agents. Rare safety events have imprecise estimates. Multiple subgroup and network comparisons increase false-positive risk.

Practice translation boundary. The paper can support the statement that major efficacy differences were not consistently detected across this placebo-connected network. It cannot prove interchangeability, therapeutic equivalence, or definitive superiority. Current treatment selection would also require guideline, label, patient-specific, and formulary review, which was outside this source-only run.
