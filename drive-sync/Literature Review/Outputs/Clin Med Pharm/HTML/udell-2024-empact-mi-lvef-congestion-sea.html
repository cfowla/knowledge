Summary

Bottom line. Within the high-risk post-MI population enrolled in EMPACT-MI, lower LVEF and clinical congestion identified progressively higher heart failure risk. Empagliflozin reduced first and recurrent heart failure hospitalizations, and the estimated treatment effect did not materially vary across baseline LVEF or congestion status. The trial did not show an all-cause mortality benefit.

The paper answers two different questions. The placebo-arm analyses ask whether LVEF and congestion predict risk. Those are prognostic associations, not randomized comparisons of LVEF or congestion. The empagliflozin analyses ask whether randomized treatment benefit varies by those baseline factors. Keeping those questions separate is essential to interpreting the paper.

The parent EMPACT-MI primary composite of first heart failure hospitalization or all-cause death was neutral. This secondary analysis does not change that result. Its most important treatment finding is the reduction in the heart failure hospitalization component, including recurrent events, with no evidence that baseline LVEF or congestion modified that benefit.

Clinical question and methods

PICO

  • Population: clinically stable patients randomized within 14 days after acute MI, with newly reduced LVEF below 45%, congestion requiring treatment, or both, plus at least one enrichment factor for heart failure risk.
  • Intervention: empagliflozin 10 mg orally once daily on top of standard care.
  • Comparator: matching placebo on top of standard care.
  • Outcomes: first HF hospitalization or all-cause death, first HF hospitalization, total HF hospitalizations, exploratory investigator-reported HF adverse events, and selected safety outcomes.

Patients with preexisting heart failure, type 1 diabetes, or planned SGLT2 or SGLT1/2 inhibitor therapy were excluded. LVEF was measured locally before randomization. When a range rather than a number was reported, the midpoint was used for analysis. Congestion required symptoms or signs plus treatment during the index hospitalization.

Time-to-first outcomes used Cox models. Total HF hospitalization analyses used negative binomial regression. Models adjusted for major baseline risk factors and tested treatment-by-subgroup interactions. Continuous LVEF analyses used cubic splines. Heart failure hospitalization endpoints were assessed by blinded site investigators using prespecified definitions, not by a central endpoint committee.

Risk associated with LVEF and congestion

Among placebo-assigned participants, each 10-point reduction in baseline LVEF was associated with higher adjusted risk of the composite endpoint, first HF hospitalization, and total HF hospitalizations. The corresponding effect estimates were HR 1.49, HR 1.64, and RR 1.89. Congestion also independently identified higher risk, with HR 1.52 for the composite endpoint, HR 1.94 for first HF hospitalization, and RR 2.03 for total HF hospitalizations.

Risk gradient. LVEF below 35% plus congestion was the highest-risk phenotype. The article used LVEF 35% to below 45% without congestion as the lowest-risk referent. For total HF hospitalizations, the highest-risk group had RR 7.46, 95% CI 3.99-13.97, versus that referent.

Other combined subgroups had wider intervals and several crossed 1. The paper's defensible conclusion is consistency of effect across subgroups, not superiority in one small phenotype.

What this should change in interpretation

  • Use LVEF and clinical congestion together for post-MI risk framing. Congestion adds prognostic information beyond moderate LV dysfunction.
  • Within the EMPACT-MI eligibility range, the HF hospitalization benefit of empagliflozin was not confined to a low-LVEF subgroup or to patients with congestion.
  • Keep the outcome hierarchy intact. The evidence supports fewer HF hospitalizations, not lower all-cause mortality.
  • Do not extrapolate this analysis to otherwise low-risk post-MI patients with preserved LVEF and no congestion. Only 158 participants had LVEF at least 45% without congestion, and that group was a protocol deviation.
  • Do not use an isolated subgroup confidence interval as proof of differential treatment response. The interaction tests were the appropriate tests of heterogeneity and were negative.
Practice boundary. This is a trial secondary analysis, not a guideline or drug label. It is useful for understanding who was at risk and whether the randomized HF hospitalization effect was consistent across the enrolled phenotypes. Current guideline, regulatory, formulary, and patient-specific decisions require separate verification.

Limitations and source-integrity findings

  • LVEF was measured locally without a core echocardiography laboratory.
  • The exact timing of baseline LVEF measurement was not recorded.
  • HF endpoints were assessed by blinded site investigators rather than centrally adjudicated by an expert committee.
  • The subgroup analysis did not adjust statistical inference for multiple comparisons.
  • The high-risk enrichment criteria and exclusion of preexisting HF limit generalization beyond the trial population.
  • Several treatment subgroup cells had few events and wide confidence intervals.
Supplemental Table 3 source issue. The congestion column prints diastolic blood pressure as 19.8 ± 14.6 mmHg, compared with 73.5 ± 9.9 mmHg without congestion, while P=0.5082. That value is internally implausible and inconsistent with the surrounding blood-pressure data. It was preserved as a source-integrity flag and not silently corrected or used as an atomized result.

Appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. Read the parent EMPACT-MI primary publication first for the trial's primary outcome. Use this paper next for risk stratification by LVEF and congestion, recurrent HF hospitalization effects, and treatment-effect consistency across the enrolled phenotypes.
