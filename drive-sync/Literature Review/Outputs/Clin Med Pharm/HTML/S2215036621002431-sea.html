One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. Strong comparative framework and useful relapse-prevention signal, but certainty is constrained by risk of bias, thin networks, inconsistency, and sparse reporting of background pharmacotherapy.

The review compared 20 psychosocial and psychological intervention categories across 72 randomized trials with 10,364 participants. The primary 12-month relapse network included 63 trials, 19 intervention nodes, and 9,010 participants. Six interventions had 95% CIs excluding no difference versus treatment as usual: family intervention (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.24–0.52), relapse prevention programme (0.33, 0.14–0.79), cognitive behavioural therapy (0.45, 0.27–0.75), family psychoeducation (0.56, 0.39–0.82), integrated intervention (0.62, 0.44–0.87), and patient psychoeducation (0.63, 0.42–0.94). Author-calculated relapse rates were 15–25% for these nodes versus 35% for treatment as usual.

The authors placed greatest weight on family interventions, family psychoeducation, and CBT because their findings were more robust across additional analyses and their network nodes were better connected. Relapse prevention programmes were promising but supported by only two studies with 92 participants. Patient psychoeducation and integrated interventions were favorable in the main analysis but less stable across sensitivity/subgroup analyses. CINeMA confidence ranged from moderate to very low. One included study was rated low overall risk of bias, 51 moderate/some concerns, and 33 high risk.

Methods and clinical question

PICO

Population: adults with schizophrenia or related disorders in a stable state; trials in which all participants were acutely ill, had specified concomitant disorders, or were prodromal/at-risk were excluded. Interventions: psychosocial or psychological approaches intended to prevent relapse, generally added to pharmacotherapy. Comparators: treatment as usual or another psychosocial intervention. Outcome: relapse, prioritizing operationalized criteria, then psychiatric hospitalization, then clinical judgment; primary time point up to 12 months, with up-to-6-month and beyond-12-month analyses.

Searches covered EMBASE, MEDLINE, PsycINFO, BIOSIS, Cochrane CENTRAL, WHO ICTRP, ClinicalTrials.gov through January 20, 2020, and PubMed through April 14, 2020. Two reviewers independently performed selection and extraction, with dedicated handling of Chinese-language studies. Authors were contacted for missing/additional data; unpublished data were obtained for nine studies. Pairwise and network meta-analyses used random-effects models; ORs were used for binary outcomes and SMDs for continuous outcomes. The review examined transitivity, heterogeneity, inconsistency, small-study effects, risk of bias, sensitivity analyses, subgroup analyses, and CINeMA confidence.

Supplement-specific methods that affect interpretation

The appendix documents a post-hoc use of the updated RoB 2.0 tool. One cluster-randomized trial was corrected using reported ICCs, with ICC 0.1 substituted where outcome-specific ICCs were unavailable. Absolute event rates were calculated from network ORs using the meta-analytic treatment-as-usual relapse rate as the assumed control risk.

Time-course signal

Family intervention and family psychoeducation were favorable at 12 months and beyond 12 months but not at 6 months. Assertive community treatment was favorable only at 6 months and had a broad CI. CBT was favorable at 12 months but showed essentially no difference from treatment as usual at 6 months or beyond 12 months. The authors caution that different time points were often informed by different studies.

Robustness

Most sensitivity and subgroup analyses aligned with the primary analysis. The important exceptions were assertive community treatment, integrated intervention, patient psychoeducation, and rehabilitation. For integrated intervention, patient psychoeducation, and rehabilitation, at least six additional analyses had CIs that included no difference despite favorable main-analysis estimates.

Secondary outcomes, acceptability, and tolerability

Secondary efficacy outcomes broadly tracked the relapse pattern. Most interventions improved overall symptoms versus controls, with exceptions that included motivational interviewing, telemedicine, cognitive training, and case management. Family interventions were more effective than most comparators for functioning; mindfulness and CBT outperformed treatment as usual for functioning. CBT had lower dropout due to inefficacy than treatment as usual.

Overall dropout rates were mostly similar across interventions, except integrated interventions had better acceptability than treatment as usual. Integrated interventions also had less non-compliance, while relapse prevention programmes had more non-compliance than most other nodes. Other intervention-related adverse events were too sparse for analysis. Death was rare and did not differ among treatments; data were insufficient for several outcomes including depressive symptoms, adherence, quality of life, and suicide.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance: 8/10

Rationale: Directly addresses comparative relapse prevention in schizophrenia with clinically interpretable interventions.

Evidence basis: Broad RCT network and relapse-centered eligibility.

Limiting factor: Psychosocial intervention availability and fit vary by setting.

Would raise/lower: Current guideline concordance and updated post-2020 evidence would increase practice relevance.

Novelty: 8/10

Rationale: The authors present this as the first network meta-analysis comparing this range of psychosocial relapse-prevention strategies.

Evidence basis: Twenty intervention nodes and multiple time points were synthesized.

Limiting factor: The source is from 2021 and many component therapies were already established.

Would raise/lower: A current update showing whether rankings and certainty persist would raise novelty.

Method strength: 8/10

Rationale: Registered, dual-review systematic methods, random-effects NMA, RoB 2, transitivity checks, sensitivity analyses, and CINeMA are substantial strengths.

Evidence basis: 72 RCTs/10,364 participants overall; 63 RCTs/9,010 at 12 months.

Limiting factor: Thin networks, unavoidable masking limitations, and some inconsistency remain.

Would raise/lower: More well-connected head-to-head trials with standardized relapse definitions would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 7/10

Rationale: Six interventions had 12-month CIs excluding no effect versus TAU, but CINeMA confidence ranged from moderate to very low.

Evidence basis: Primary ORs, sensitivity analyses, heterogeneity/inconsistency checks, and risk-of-bias assessment were reported.

Limiting factor: Only one study had low overall risk of bias; 33 were high risk, and several favorable nodes were unstable in sensitivity analyses.

Would raise/lower: More low-risk trials and higher-confidence network estimates would raise the score.

External validity: 6/10

Rationale: The evidence spans many trials and settings but applies to stable adults receiving background pharmacotherapy.

Evidence basis: Trials dated 1971–2019; mean age 32.3 years; inpatient and outpatient settings were included.

Limiting factor: Ethnicity was rarely reported, baseline symptoms were generally mild, and medication details were sparse.

Would raise/lower: Better demographic reporting, broader severity representation, and explicit pharmacotherapy characterization would raise the score.

Implementation value: 7/10

Rationale: The synthesis distinguishes more robust family/CBT strategies from less stable or weakly supported nodes.

Evidence basis: Family interventions, family psychoeducation, and CBT were robust across the authors’ additional analyses.

Limiting factor: Intervention content, intensity, staffing, and resource requirements vary; current guideline currency was not verified in this source-only production pass.

Would raise/lower: Contemporary implementation studies and current guideline alignment would raise the score.

Main strengths

Registered protocol; broad published/unpublished search; duplicate study selection and extraction; author contact for missing data; explicit relapse hierarchy; random-effects NMA; transitivity checks; RoB 2; sensitivity and subgroup analyses; small-study-effect assessment; CINeMA certainty appraisal; supplement gives enough detail to inspect the analytic decisions.

Main limitations

Many comparisons were based on one or two trials, producing thin networks. Risk of bias was usually some concerns or high, and participant/therapist masking is structurally difficult. Some outcomes showed heterogeneity/inconsistency. Relapse was often poorly defined. All psychosocial interventions were added to pharmacotherapy, yet medication regimens and doses were rarely reported. Ethnicity was rarely available. The favorable findings for patient psychoeducation, integrated intervention, and relapse prevention programmes were less robust or less well supported than the authors’ top three priorities.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Best-supported source-level priorities
Family intervention, family psychoeducation, and CBT had the strongest combination of favorable relapse estimates and robustness in this review.
Promising but less certain
Relapse prevention programmes had a strong point estimate but only two studies/92 participants; integrated intervention and patient psychoeducation were less stable in additional analyses.
Interpret as add-on care
The source evaluates psychosocial/psychological interventions on top of pharmacotherapy, not as replacements for antipsychotic maintenance treatment.
Do not over-rank
The authors explicitly avoided interpreting ranking hierarchies as definitive because of thin networks and uncertainty.

Bottom line: This review supports a durable signal for family-based interventions and a 12-month signal for CBT, but its own certainty assessment and sensitivity analyses argue against treating the network ranking as a precise treatment hierarchy. Current practice alignment requires contemporary guideline/evidence verification outside this source-only production task.
