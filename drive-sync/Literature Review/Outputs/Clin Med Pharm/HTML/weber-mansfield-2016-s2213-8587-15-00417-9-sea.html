Metadata and scope

Design
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial
Population
449 patients in the 10 mg/placebo comparison; T2D + inadequately controlled hypertension
Intervention
Dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily for 12 weeks vs placebo, on stable background therapy
Co-primary endpoints
Change in seated SBP and HbA1c at week 12

Scope warning. This trial estimates short-term glycaemic and blood-pressure effects in a selected population. It does not establish cardiovascular-outcome benefit, interchangeability with another SGLT2 inhibitor, or effectiveness in patients with advanced kidney dysfunction.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read soon. Best used as a high-quality historical efficacy study quantifying the incremental BP and HbA1c effect of dapagliflozin when added to established antihypertensive therapy. Do not use the post-hoc antihypertensive-class differences as definitive treatment-selection evidence.

The randomized comparison showed a modest but clear additional blood-pressure effect: seated SBP fell 11.90 mm Hg with dapagliflozin and 7.62 mm Hg with placebo, producing a placebo-corrected difference of −4.28 mm Hg (95% CI −6.54 to −2.02; p=0.0002). HbA1c fell 0.63 percentage points with dapagliflozin versus 0.02 with placebo, for a placebo-corrected difference of −0.61 percentage points (95% CI −0.76 to −0.46; p<0.0001). Twenty-four-hour ambulatory SBP also favoured dapagliflozin by −4.45 mm Hg (95% CI −7.14 to −1.76).

The supplement reinforces that BP lowering was present across daytime, night-time, and trough ambulatory measures. Safety over 12 weeks was broadly similar overall (44% vs 42% with ≥1 adverse event), although hypoglycaemia, genital/urinary infections, and several renal/volume-depletion event categories were numerically more frequent with dapagliflozin; absolute event counts were small. The strongest interpretive constraint is that antihypertensive-class differences were post hoc: office-SBP estimates were −5.76 mm Hg with background β-blocker, −5.13 mm Hg with calcium-channel blocker, and −2.38 mm Hg with a thiazide/diuretic, with the diuretic subgroup CI crossing zero.

Section condensation

Background and rationale

Hypertension is common in type 2 diabetes and often requires multidrug therapy. The investigators positioned SGLT2 inhibition as a glucose-lowering mechanism with an established modest BP effect, but with limited evidence at the time about how that effect behaved when layered onto specific antihypertensive combinations.

Methods

The trial enrolled adults at 311 centres in 16 countries. Participants had HbA1c 7.0–10.5%, seated BP 140–165/85–105 mm Hg at enrolment and randomization, and mean 24-hour ambulatory BP ≥130/80 mm Hg. Background therapy had to include stable glucose-lowering treatment, a stable ACE inhibitor or ARB, and one additional antihypertensive agent. Patients were randomized 1:1 to dapagliflozin 10 mg or placebo, stratified by additional antihypertensive class and insulin use.

Co-primary outcomes were week-12 changes in seated SBP and HbA1c. Secondary outcomes included 24-hour ambulatory SBP, seated DBP, and uric acid; exploratory outcomes included BP-control thresholds, fasting glucose, bodyweight, and additional ambulatory time windows. A closed hierarchical procedure controlled multiplicity. The full analysis set required treatment plus baseline and post-baseline efficacy measurements.

Results

The co-primary outcomes both favoured dapagliflozin: SBP difference −4.28 mm Hg and HbA1c difference −0.61 percentage points. Ambulatory SBP difference was −4.45 mm Hg. Seated DBP did not show a statistically clear between-group difference (−0.97 mm Hg; 95% CI −2.32 to 0.39; p=0.16). Dapagliflozin also lowered fasting glucose, uric acid, and bodyweight relative to placebo.

At week 12, 38% vs 33% achieved BP <140/90 and 8% vs 5% achieved <130/80. One week after discontinuation, the seated-SBP difference had attenuated to −1.20 mm Hg (95% CI −3.56 to 1.15), consistent with a treatment-dependent effect rather than persistent post-treatment separation.

Discussion and limitations

The authors emphasized the combined glycaemic and BP effect and proposed a diuretic-like contribution to multidrug BP control. They highlighted apparently larger SBP reductions when the additional antihypertensive was a β-blocker or calcium-channel blocker than a thiazide/diuretic.

That heterogeneity claim is much weaker than the main randomized comparison because it arose from post-hoc, relatively small subgroups. The trial also excluded CrCl <60 mL/min, included few patients with moderate kidney impairment, ran for only 12 weeks, and was not designed for major cardiovascular outcomes. Sponsor participation extended through study design, data handling/analysis/interpretation, and the submission decision.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance7/10

Directly quantifies adjunct blood-pressure and glycaemic effects of dapagliflozin in treated hypertension.

Evidence: Randomized co-primary BP/HbA1c evidence.
Limiting factor: Narrow 12-week efficacy frame; not a hard-outcome trial.
Would change: Would rise with direct relevance to a BP-specific formulary question.
Novelty7/10

The dedicated BP co-primary endpoint and antihypertensive-class analyses were distinctive for early SGLT2 evidence.

Evidence: Office and ambulatory BP endpoints with class-stratified analyses.
Limiting factor: Later SGLT2 outcome trials substantially supersede the clinical importance of this early efficacy question.
Would change: Would rise if the subgroup interaction were prespecified and replicated.
Method strength8/10

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre design with multiplicity control and ambulatory BP measurement.

Evidence: 311 centres; stratified randomization; closed testing; repeated-measures/ANCOVA analyses.
Limiting factor: Protocol amendment removed lower-dose arms; key class subgroup analyses were post hoc.
Would change: Would rise with prespecified powered subgroup hypotheses and independent sponsorship.
Evidence strength7/10

The co-primary 12-week effects are directly supported with confidence intervals and appropriate randomized comparisons.

Evidence: SBP difference −4.28 mm Hg; HbA1c difference −0.61 percentage points; both statistically robust.
Limiting factor: Short duration, sponsor involvement, and exploratory/post-hoc secondary claims constrain inference.
Would change: Would rise with longer follow-up and independent replication.
External validity5/10

The international multicentre sample is broad geographically but clinically selected.

Evidence: 16 countries and five continents.
Limiting factor: Strict BP/glycaemic criteria, CrCl ≥60 mL/min, few with moderate renal impairment, and exclusion of unstable cardiovascular disease.
Would change: Would rise with broader kidney function and cardiovascular-risk representation.
Implementation value6/10

Useful for estimating the incremental magnitude of BP and HbA1c lowering when dapagliflozin is layered onto antihypertensive therapy.

Evidence: Quantifies office, ambulatory, daytime/night-time/trough BP and common safety outcomes.
Limiting factor: It does not test clinical cardiovascular outcomes or establish therapeutic interchangeability with another SGLT2 inhibitor.
Would change: Would rise with head-to-head class-comparison or outcomes data.
Main appraisal boundary: the randomized overall treatment effect is considerably stronger evidence than the post-hoc antihypertensive-class pattern. The latter should not be promoted into a reported interaction or clinical recommendation beyond what the source directly supports.

Clinical-trial lens

PICO: Adults with T2D and inadequately controlled hypertension on stable glucose-lowering treatment + ACEi/ARB + one additional antihypertensive; dapagliflozin 10 mg daily vs placebo for 12 weeks; co-primary outcomes seated SBP and HbA1c.

Validity: randomization, masking, standardized BP measurement, ambulatory BP corroboration, multiplicity control, and confidence intervals support the primary inference. External validity is reduced by strict BP/glycaemic and renal thresholds, exclusion of unstable cardiovascular disease, and short follow-up. Sponsor involvement is a relevant risk-to-independence consideration but does not erase the randomized result.

Practice translation: the trial supports expecting a roughly 4 mm Hg incremental systolic-BP effect and ~0.6 percentage-point HbA1c effect in a similar short-term population. It should not independently drive current cardiorenal-outcomes decisions or cross-agent formulary interchange because those questions were not tested.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use
Magnitude estimate for adjunct SBP/HbA1c lowering with dapagliflozin in treated hypertension.
Do not overclaim
Do not interpret the post-hoc class subgroups as a proven interaction or preferred antihypertensive pairing.
Safety context
Short-term overall AE burden was similar, with small numeric imbalances in predictable SGLT2-related event categories.
Generalizability
CrCl <60 mL/min and unstable cardiovascular disease were excluded, limiting direct extension to higher-risk populations.
