One-page synthesis

The study asks whether antiseizure medication choice changes thromboembolic, bleeding, or mortality risk among adults with epilepsy treated with DOACs. The abstract describes a retrospective cohort analysis designed as four target-trial emulations with active comparators and 1:1 propensity score matching. Lamotrigine or lacosamide served as the pooled reference. The analytic cohort included 9,529 initiators drawn from an international federated EHR network.

The largest reported signals were higher thromboembolic risk with levetiracetam and strong enzyme-inducing ASMs, higher mortality with levetiracetam and valproate, lower major bleeding with strong enzyme-inducing ASMs, and markedly higher intracranial major bleeding with valproate. These findings are clinically important enough to justify reading the full article. They are not sufficient on their own to change an individual patient's ASM because the accessible abstract does not expose absolute event rates, detailed covariate balance, outcome coding, adherence rules, full sensitivity analyses, or residual-confounding assessment.

Accessible source condensation

Design and population

The abstract reports a retrospective cohort study that emulated a target trial for each ASM group. Four 1:1 propensity-matched cohorts were used to approximate randomization and estimate outcomes under sustained ASM monotherapy. Adults aged 18 years or older with epilepsy were drawn from TriNetX. Reported exclusions included recent VKA use, strong non-ASM CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein modulators, and major vascular events in the prior year. Follow-up started at a 90-day landmark.

Exposures and outcomes

Levetiracetam, valproate, moderate enzyme-inducing ASMs, and strong enzyme-inducing ASMs were each compared with a pooled lamotrigine/lacosamide reference. The primary outcome set included a thromboembolic composite, major bleeding, and all-cause mortality. The thromboembolic composite included ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, and systemic or peripheral embolism. A VKA-treated analysis was reported as a sensitivity analysis.

Participant flow

The abstract reports 2.29 million adults with epilepsy in the source network, 40,932 who were DOAC eligible, 26,962 with DOAC-ASM overlap within 6 months, 10,209 eligible for monotherapy, and 9,529 initiators in the analytic cohort. Mean age was 63 years with an SD of 18, and 51% were female. Levetiracetam accounted for 57% of initiators, strong enzyme-inducing ASMs 15%, valproate 11%, and moderate enzyme-inducing ASMs 4%.

Practice takeaways

What this can support

Prioritize the full article for review when choosing an ASM for a patient who also requires a DOAC. Treat levetiracetam, strong enzyme-inducing ASMs, and valproate as exposure groups that deserve closer interaction and outcome review.

What this cannot support

Do not switch a stable ASM or declare lamotrigine/lacosamide superior solely from these abstract-level observational associations. Seizure control, indication, dosing, DOAC choice, comorbidity, confounding, and absolute risk remain unresolved here.
