One-page synthesis

The 2017 update changed a focused set of CKD-MBD recommendations rather than rewriting the full 2009 guideline. The most consequential shifts were practical. DXA BMD testing became reasonable when it could change treatment, mandatory pre-antiresorptive bone biopsy was removed, phosphate-lowering therapy moved away from preventive treatment of normal phosphate toward persistent or progressive hyperphosphatemia, calcium loading was treated more cautiously, and routine calcitriol or vitamin D analog use in nondialysis CKD was discouraged.

The evidence base is methodologically organized but often clinically thin. KDIGO used systematic searches, dual risk-of-bias review, evidence profiles, and GRADE. Yet many patient-centered outcomes were supported by low or very-low certainty evidence, short trials, post hoc subgroup analyses, or observational associations. The guideline often gives sound decision structure while acknowledging that the evidence cannot prove that changing a surrogate marker improves survival, fracture risk, or cardiovascular outcomes.

The update is especially useful for understanding why CKD-MBD treatment should be individualized around serial calcium, phosphate, and PTH values instead of single targets. It is also useful for the 2017 evidence lineage behind BMD use, calcium-binder restriction, and PTH therapy. The packet itself does not establish whether this 2017 document remains the most current KDIGO CKD-MBD guidance in 2026, so current implementation requires separate currency verification.

What changed in 2017

Bone diagnosis and osteoporosis treatment

Four prospective adult cohorts supported DXA BMD as a fracture-risk tool across CKD G3a-G5D. KDIGO therefore reversed the 2009 position against routine BMD use and now suggests testing when the result will affect treatment. The guideline still treats bone biopsy as the diagnostic reference for renal osteodystrophy, but it no longer makes biopsy a prerequisite before antiresorptive therapy.

That change matters because the earlier concern that bisphosphonates cause adynamic bone disease was not definitively demonstrated in CKD. KDIGO still advises caution, treatment of underlying renal osteodystrophy, and individualized risk-benefit judgment.

Phosphate, calcium, and PTH

KDIGO narrowed phosphate treatment toward progressive or persistent hyperphosphatemia. It found no trial proof that treating normal phosphate improves patient-centered outcomes and flagged possible calcium loading and calcification harm. Calcium-based binders were therefore restricted more generally.

For nondialysis CKD, modest PTH elevation may be adaptive. The update moved from reacting to any value above the assay limit toward evaluating persistent or progressive elevation and correcting modifiable factors first. Routine active vitamin D use was discouraged because newer trials did not improve clinically relevant outcomes and increased hypercalcemia.

Methods and evidence development

The Evidence Review Team searched MEDLINE and CENTRAL from December 2006 through September 2015 and accepted Work Group-supplied evidence through February 2017. The appendix reports 13,530 retrieved records, 1,844 duplicates, 11,686 abstract reviews, 1,519 full-text reviews, and 117 included studies published in 128 articles. Core eligibility required English-language original data, at least 10 participants with CKD, at least 6 months of follow-up, and relevance to a prespecified research question. Question-specific sample-size and design thresholds were then added.

One reviewer extracted data and a second confirmed it. Two reviewers independently assessed risk of bias. KDIGO used the Cochrane tool for randomized trials and the Quality in Prognosis Studies tool for observational work. Evidence profiles and matrices supported a GRADE-based process. Recommendation strength was Level 1, Level 2, or not graded, while overall evidence quality used A through D.

Main strengths

  • Explicit systematic-review methods and prespecified research questions.
  • Independent risk-of-bias assessment and GRADE-linked recommendation grading.
  • The evidence supplement exposes study-level and evidence-profile reasoning rather than hiding it behind recommendation prose.
  • The update is careful about surrogate markers and repeatedly acknowledges missing patient-centered outcome evidence.

Main limitations

  • This was a selective update, so unchanged 2009 recommendations were not all re-reviewed.
  • Many key recommendations rest on low or very-low certainty evidence.
  • Several osteoporosis conclusions come from post hoc analyses or populations that did not have typical biochemical CKD-MBD.
  • Advanced CKD fracture-prevention evidence and post-transplant treatment data beyond 12 months were sparse.
  • The formal evidence search is historical. The packet alone cannot establish 2026 currency.

Practice interpretation

Best use. Use this guideline to understand the 2017 KDIGO decision structure for CKD-MBD, especially serial interpretation of calcium, phosphate, and PTH; when BMD or bone biopsy can change management; phosphate-binder calcium exposure; and PTH-lowering choices.

Do not use for. Do not treat observational phosphate or calcium associations as proof that lowering either marker improves survival. Do not treat the guideline's summaries of cited trials as substitutes for primary-study extraction when exact efficacy or safety estimates are needed. Do not assume 2017 treatment details are current in 2026 without a separate currency check.

Implementation takeaways. Avoid single-value target chasing. Treat overt, persistent biochemical abnormalities in context. Use BMD when it will change management. Reserve bone biopsy for decisions where histology will alter therapy rather than as a universal gate to antiresorptive treatment. In nondialysis adults, correct modifiable PTH drivers before active vitamin D and do not use active vitamin D routinely.
