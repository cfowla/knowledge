Executive synthesis

Verdict. This is a focused, clinically usable update with a transparent RCT-centered evidence review. Its strongest changes concern glucocorticoid reduction, avacopan, plasma exchange selection, and rituximab-based maintenance. Much of the day-to-day detail still rests on ungraded Practice Points rather than high-certainty comparative evidence.

The guideline keeps glucocorticoids plus rituximab or cyclophosphamide as the graded standard for new-onset AAV. It then narrows how clinicians should use that backbone. Rituximab is favored for relapsing and PR3-ANCA disease and when fertility or glucocorticoid-sparing concerns matter. Cyclophosphamide remains important when kidney function is severely reduced, because prospective rituximab-only evidence is thin above a serum creatinine of 4 mg/dl. Avacopan is accepted as a glucocorticoid alternative in combination with rituximab or cyclophosphamide, with the most plausible clinical value in people at high risk of glucocorticoid toxicity.

The plasma-exchange section is deliberately selective. PEXIVAS did not show a long-term reduction in death or kidney failure overall, but the updated synthesis still supports consideration when serum creatinine exceeds 3.4 mg/dl, dialysis is required, serum creatinine is rising rapidly, or diffuse alveolar hemorrhage causes hypoxemia. The argument is a tradeoff between a short-term reduction in kidney failure and more serious infections. Anti-GBM overlap remains a separate indication to add plasma exchange.

Maintenance therapy is graded more weakly than induction. KDIGO recommends rituximab or azathioprine plus low-dose glucocorticoids after remission, but the text prefers rituximab in relapsing and PR3-ANCA disease. Duration is individualized between 18 months and 4 years. The source repeatedly separates relapse prevention from treatment toxicity, especially infection and secondary immunodeficiency.

Operational dosing in the guideline

  • Rituximab: 375 mg/m2 weekly for 4 weeks or 1 g at weeks 0 and 2.
  • Oral cyclophosphamide: 2 mg/kg/day for 3 months, with age and kidney-function reductions; treatment may continue for ongoing activity to a maximum of 6 months.
  • Intravenous cyclophosphamide: 15 mg/kg at weeks 0, 2, 4, 7, 10, and 13, with later pulses if required and dose reductions for age and GFR below 30 ml/min per 1.73 m2.
  • Avacopan: 30 mg twice daily as an alternative to glucocorticoids with rituximab- or cyclophosphamide-based induction.
  • Low-dose TMP-SMX or an alternative is advised for Pneumocystis prophylaxis through the cyclophosphamide course or for 6 months after rituximab induction. Longer prophylaxis can be considered with repeated rituximab or ongoing immunosuppression.
  • For rituximab, the text advises baseline IgG and repeat measurement every 6 months.

Plasma exchange

PEXIVAS did not reduce the composite of kidney failure or death over a median 2.9 years in the broad severe-AAV population. The guideline nevertheless retains a selective role based on an updated meta-analysis. It reports a 12-month absolute kidney-failure risk reduction of 4.6%, 95% CI 1.2%-6.8%, when serum creatinine was 3.4-5.7 mg/dl and about 6% when serum creatinine exceeded 5.7 mg/dl or dialysis was required. The tradeoff was more serious infection.

Maintenance, relapse, and transplantation

The graded maintenance recommendation is Level 1C, which is a useful signal in itself. The guideline is firm about the need for relapse prevention, but the comparative evidence is thinner than for induction. Table S19 pools two rituximab-versus-azathioprine studies and reports major relapse RR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45-0.77, with moderate certainty. Infection is much less certain, RR 0.98 with a wide 95% CI of 0.57-1.68. MAINRITSAN3, represented in Table S31, found fewer relapses with continued rituximab versus placebo, RR 0.16, 95% CI 0.04-0.66, in 97 patients.

Figure 14 gives two scheduled rituximab approaches: MAINRITSAN uses 500 mg twice at complete remission followed by 500 mg at months 6, 12, and 18. RITAZAREM uses 1000 mg after induction and at months 4, 8, 12, and 16. Azathioprine starts at 1.5-2 mg/kg/day, with schedules that taper after the first year or extend reduced-dose treatment to 4 years. MMF is listed at 2000 mg/day in divided doses for 2 years when used for maintenance.

Relapsing disease should be reinduced, preferably with rituximab. The guideline also warns about cumulative cyclophosphamide exposure. For transplantation, it advises at least 6 months of complete clinical remission and states that persistent ANCA alone should not delay transplant.

Methods and evidence framework

The Brown University Evidence Review Team updated searches in PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts, with third-reviewer resolution. One ERT member extracted data and two others confirmed it. Two reviewers independently performed Cochrane risk-of-bias assessments. Dichotomous effects were pooled as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals using random-effects methods. KDIGO used GRADE for evidence certainty and recommendation strength.

The update deliberately restricted intervention effectiveness and safety questions to RCTs. That choice improves internal validity for comparative treatment questions but narrows the evidence that can directly support special populations, rare harms, long-term toxicity, implementation questions, and severe presentations that trials excluded.

Patient involvement limitation. The Work Group did not include patients or caregivers. The methods section notes limited qualitative evidence on patient priorities and preferences. Values and preferences were therefore judged largely by the Work Group.

What the source supports well

  • A rituximab- or cyclophosphamide-based induction framework with reduced glucocorticoid exposure.
  • Rituximab preference in relapsing and PR3-ANCA phenotypes.
  • Avacopan as a glucocorticoid alternative for selected patients.
  • A selective, risk-based role for plasma exchange rather than routine use in all severe AAV.
  • Rituximab or azathioprine-based maintenance, with duration tailored to relapse risk and toxicity.

Where caution remains

  • Rituximab-only induction evidence is limited when serum creatinine is above 4 mg/dl.
  • ADVOCATE excluded eGFR below 15 ml/min per 1.73 m2 and mechanically ventilated alveolar hemorrhage.
  • Maintenance comparisons rely on fewer trials, smaller samples, and lower certainty than induction.
  • ANCA trends predict relapse only modestly and should not replace clinical assessment.
  • Long-term infection, hypogammaglobulinemia, malignancy, fertility, and cumulative immunosuppression risks need evidence beyond the RCT-only update frame.
