One-page synthesis

Core thesis: after prolonged interruption of every-8-week long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine injections, cabotegravir concentrations fell much more sharply than rilpivirine concentrations in four virologically suppressed patients, creating a potentially unbalanced pharmacokinetic tail even though all four remained virologically suppressed at re-engagement.

The series describes four male patients, median age 47 years (range 27–55), with long prior exposure to the long-acting regimen (median 203 weeks; reported range 61–393) and a median 17-week interval since the last injection (range 16–19). Reasons for delayed injections were incarceration, worsening pre-existing depression in two cases, and a prolonged work-related stay abroad. Paired trough concentrations were available from routine stable every-8-week dosing and from re-engagement in care.

Cabotegravir concentrations declined by a median 92% (range 82%–97%), compared with a 20% median decline for rilpivirine (range 14%–24%). At re-engagement, median cabotegravir was 92 ng/mL (65–257) and median rilpivirine was 194 ng/mL (67–365). The authors report cabotegravir below both literature-cited target levels in all four patients while rilpivirine remained above those levels. HIV-1 RNA remained below the limit of detection in all four; median CD4 count was approximately 1000 cells/mm³. One patient resumed long-acting therapy and three temporarily returned to oral antiretroviral therapy.

Most important limitation: n=4. The concentration imbalance is real within these cases, but the report cannot estimate how often this occurs, identify predictors, quantify resistance risk, or show that a specific management strategy improves outcomes.

Section condensation

Background and rationale

The article frames long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine as an every-2-month regimen with a usual ±7-day dosing window. Missed or delayed injections are clinically important because both drugs decline slowly after administration, creating a pharmacokinetic tail in which exposure can eventually become subtherapeutic. The authors cite two proposed cabotegravir/rilpivirine target pairs: 664/50 ng/mL and 1120/32 ng/mL.

The report focuses on an unusually prolonged missed-injection interval rather than ordinary small scheduling deviations. These cited target concentrations are external literature context used to interpret the cases; the four-patient series does not itself validate those thresholds.

Case-series population and sampling

Four virologically suppressed male patients had a median age of 47 years (27–55), no concomitant medications, and long-term LAI exposure reported as a median 203 weeks (61–393). Delays occurred for incarceration, worsening depression in two cases, and work-related travel. The median time since the last administration was 17 weeks (16–19).

For each participant, plasma concentrations were available at two points: a steady-state trough obtained during routine every-8-week dosing and a trough measured at re-engagement. This paired within-patient structure is the methodological strength of the report, although assay details and exact patient-level numerical values are not provided in the text.

Pharmacokinetic and clinical results

Cabotegravir declined much more than rilpivirine: median reductions were 92% (82%–97%) and 20% (14%–24%), respectively. At re-engagement, median cabotegravir was 92 ng/mL (65–257), below both literature-cited targets in all four patients, while median rilpivirine was 194 ng/mL (67–365) and remained above the cited target levels in all four.

Despite the pharmacokinetic imbalance, all four remained HIV-1 RNA below the assay limit of detection and had a median CD4 count of approximately 1000 cells/mm³. No major intercurrent clinical events were reported. One patient resumed LAI therapy; three temporarily returned to oral antiretroviral treatment.

Discussion and conclusion

The authors interpret the pattern as asynchronous elimination, consistent with literature-reported apparent elimination half-lives of 13–28 weeks for long-acting rilpivirine and 6–12 weeks for cabotegravir. They propose that disproportionate cabotegravir decline could create a temporary period of unbalanced exposure or “functional monotherapy,” raising a theoretical risk of resistance selection even though no virologic failure occurred in the series.

They caution that comparison with prior cabotegravir tail-phase data is difficult because populations, clinical contexts, and pharmacokinetic approaches differ, and interindividual cabotegravir variability may be important. Their final claim is appropriately framed as real-world evidence of differential decay that warrants further investigation rather than proof of a clinical outcome.

Funding, conflicts, and data availability

The authors state that the work was carried out as part of routine work. Several authors disclosed personal fees from ViiV Healthcare, Gilead Sciences, Janssen-Cilag, Pfizer, and/or MSD outside the submitted work; the remaining authors declared no conflicts.

Data are stated to be available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. The disclosure is transparent, but independent replication remains important given the tiny sample and commercial relevance of the drug products.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Skim deeply. This two-page report contains one clinically important pharmacokinetic signal and a useful figure, but its evidentiary ceiling is low because it is a four-case descriptive series.
Relevance8/10

Rationale: Directly relevant to long-acting antiretroviral pharmacokinetics, therapeutic drug monitoring, missed-dose risk, and clinical re-engagement.

Evidence basis: Paired cabotegravir/rilpivirine trough concentrations plus virologic status after prolonged injection delays.

Principal limiting factor: The series is narrow and does not evaluate a management intervention.

What would change the score: Prospective evaluation across larger and more diverse cohorts with clinically linked outcomes would raise the score.

Novelty7/10

Rationale: The report captures a clinically uncommon prolonged-delay scenario with paired within-patient concentrations for both components of the long-acting regimen.

Evidence basis: Four real-world cases with measurements during stable dosing and after re-engagement.

Principal limiting factor: The core mechanistic expectation of different pharmacokinetic tails is not itself new.

What would change the score: Systematic longitudinal sampling or modeling that predicts the imbalance across delay durations would increase novelty.

Method strength3/10

Rationale: The paired design is informative, but the evidence is a four-patient descriptive case series without a control group or inferential analysis.

Evidence basis: Within-patient trough comparisons at two clinically meaningful time points.

Principal limiting factor: Very small sample; case selection process, exact injection doses, assay methods, and detailed per-patient values are not reported in the letter.

What would change the score: A prespecified multicenter cohort with standardized sampling, assay methods, and complete patient-level data would materially improve method strength.

Evidence strength4/10

Rationale: The reported data directly support differential concentration decay in these four patients, but they do not establish frequency, causal risk, or resistance outcomes.

Evidence basis: Cabotegravir median decline 92% versus rilpivirine 20%, with all four still virologically suppressed at re-engagement.

Principal limiting factor: No virologic failures occurred; the functional-monotherapy/resistance concern is therefore theoretical in this series.

What would change the score: Observed resistance or failure outcomes, repeated longitudinal samples, and replication would strengthen the evidence.

External validity2/10

Rationale: Generalizability is highly limited by four male patients from a single clinical context and heterogeneous reasons for delay.

Evidence basis: All participants were male; median age 47 years; prolonged prior LAI exposure; delays 16–19 weeks.

Principal limiting factor: The sample is too small to characterize between-patient variability or broader populations.

What would change the score: Larger multicenter cohorts including women, broader ages, comorbidities, concomitant medications, and varied delay durations would raise external validity.

Implementation value5/10

Rationale: The report is useful as a warning signal for the pharmacokinetic tail but is insufficient to define a new missed-dose protocol.

Evidence basis: It identifies a potentially unbalanced tail in which cabotegravir can fall far more sharply than rilpivirine after prolonged delay.

Principal limiting factor: No intervention, therapeutic-drug-monitoring strategy, or alternative management pathway was tested.

What would change the score: Prospective management studies linking concentrations to re-loading, oral bridging, virologic outcomes, and resistance would increase implementation value.

Validity

Strengths

  • Within-patient paired concentrations reduce some between-person confounding.
  • Both components of the LAI regimen were measured at the same clinically relevant time points.
  • Magnitude and ranges of concentration decline are reported.
  • Virologic status and clinical disposition at re-engagement are provided.
  • Conflicts and data-availability statement are explicit.

Limitations

  • Four patients; all male; single clinical context.
  • No comparator cohort, inferential statistics, or prospective sampling plan.
  • Exact injection doses and assay methodology are not reported in the letter.
  • No patient-level numeric table, resistance-testing results, or longitudinal post-re-engagement pharmacokinetics.
  • Therapeutic targets and half-lives are imported from cited literature rather than validated here.
  • No virologic failure occurred, so resistance risk remains a mechanistic/theoretical inference.

Appraisal bottom line

Trust for the narrow claim: moderate — the data are adequate to say that cabotegravir decayed more than rilpivirine in these four cases. Trust for broader clinical consequences: low — the study cannot establish how often the imbalance occurs, whether it causes resistance, or which management strategy is best.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Useful now

Use the report as a pharmacokinetic warning signal when thinking about prolonged missed injections: the two components of the long-acting regimen may not decline in parallel, and cabotegravir exposure may become disproportionately low.

Do not overclaim

Do not use this four-case series alone to define a new dosing window, re-loading schedule, oral-bridging strategy, therapeutic concentration target, or resistance-risk threshold.

What to monitor in future evidence

Patient-level time since injection, serial cabotegravir and rilpivirine concentrations, virologic rebound, resistance emergence, restart/bridging strategy, adherence barriers, and outcomes after re-engagement.

Research priority

Prospective pharmacokinetic modeling and multicenter longitudinal cohorts that connect the changing cabotegravir:rilpivirine exposure balance to virologic and resistance outcomes.

Best use: concise mechanistic and clinical-context evidence for why prolonged missed LAI visits merit careful re-engagement assessment. Not sufficient for: practice-changing management recommendations.
