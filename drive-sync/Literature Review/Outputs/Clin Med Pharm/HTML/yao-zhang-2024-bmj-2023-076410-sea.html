Scope warning: This source compares GLP-1 receptor agonist strategies in adults with type 2 diabetes. It does not establish empagliflozin-dapagliflozin interchangeability and should not be used alone to justify an SGLT2 formulary decision.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis: Across 76 RCTs (39,246 participants), GLP-1RA therapies improved glycaemic outcomes versus placebo. Tirzepatide produced the largest HbA1c and fasting-glucose reductions in the main network, while CagriSema produced the largest body-weight reduction. Gastrointestinal adverse events and treatment discontinuation were increased for several agents and tended to worsen with higher doses.

The evidence base is methodologically substantial: four databases, prospective registration, duplicate extraction, RoB 2, frequentist random-effects NMA, SUCRA, CINeMA, local/global inconsistency testing, publication-bias checks, sensitivity analyses, meta-regression, and extensive dose/follow-up subgroup work. The 242-page supplement materially strengthens reproducibility and interpretability.

Interpretation is constrained by indirect comparisons, sparse evidence for newer agents, heterogeneous populations/follow-up, imputed SDs for some outcomes, open-label/industry-funded trials, and several source-internal reporting discrepancies. Most primary findings are directionally robust, but rankings should not be treated as equivalent to direct head-to-head superiority.

Section condensation

Introduction / objective

The article addresses a rapidly expanding GLP-1RA class that now includes single, dual, and triple receptor agonists. Its stated goal is comparative estimation of glycaemic efficacy, weight and lipid effects, and adverse-event profiles in adults with type 2 diabetes.

The clinical question is comparative rather than absolute: which available agents appear most effective, and what safety trade-offs accompany those effects.

Methods

Eligible RCTs enrolled adults with T2D, compared a GLP-1RA with placebo or another GLP-1RA, and followed participants for at least 12 weeks. Four databases were searched from inception with two updates through 19 August 2023. Two reviewers independently extracted data; a third adjudicated. Primary outcomes were HbA1c, fasting glucose, and body weight.

Random-effects frequentist NMA used restricted maximum likelihood. Mean differences summarized continuous outcomes and odds ratios binary adverse events. The authors assessed RoB 2, local/global inconsistency, heterogeneity, CINeMA confidence, publication bias, sensitivity analyses, meta-regression, dose effects, follow-up effects, and receptor-class subgroups. Missing SDs could be imputed/borrowed from similar trials, and figure-only data could be digitized.

Results

Study flow and evidence base

The search identified 16,316 records; after 2,673 duplicates, 13,643 records were screened and 483 full texts assessed. The final network included 76 RCTs, 39,246 participants, and 15 GLP-1RA strategies. Ten trials (13%) were rated high risk overall; the authors reported generally low-to-moderate heterogeneity and mostly moderate/high CINeMA confidence.

Some local inconsistency occurred, but global inconsistency was not prominent for most outcomes. Sensitivity analyses and meta-regression did not materially overturn the primary conclusions.

HbA1c

Fifty-six trials (26,343 participants) contributed. The largest placebo-referenced reductions were tirzepatide -2.10 percentage points (95% CI -2.47 to -1.74), mazdutide -2.09 (-3.10 to -1.09), CagriSema -1.80 (-2.87 to -0.73), orforglipron -1.49 (-2.12 to -0.85), and semaglutide -1.40 (-1.67 to -1.12). Tirzepatide ranked highest (SUCRA 94.2%).

All 15 displayed agents had confidence intervals below zero in Figure 3, but the magnitude and certainty varied by agent and node density.

Fasting blood glucose

Forty-seven RCTs (17,163 participants) contributed. Tirzepatide had the largest reported reduction (-3.12 mmol/L, 95% CI -3.59 to -2.66; SUCRA 97.2%), followed by CagriSema -2.79 (-4.30 to -1.28) and orforglipron -2.09 (-2.86 to -1.31).

Source-integrity issue: Results prose says all 15 GLP-1RAs significantly lowered fasting glucose, but Figure 5 displays 14 agents and does not include ITCA 650. The artifact preserves this mismatch rather than imputing a missing value.

Body weight

Fifty-three trials (21,349 participants) contributed. The largest reductions were CagriSema -14.03 kg (95% CI -17.05 to -11.00), tirzepatide -8.47 (-9.68 to -7.26), retatrutide -7.87 (-9.95 to -5.79), orforglipron -4.88 (-6.93 to -2.83), and semaglutide -3.13 (-3.95 to -2.31). Liraglutide also had a statistically clear reduction (-1.33 kg, -2.08 to -0.59).

Source-integrity issue: main Figure 7 reports lixisenatide -0.62 kg (95% CI -1.51 to 0.87), while Supplementary Table S8.3 reports -0.32 kg with the same confidence interval.

Secondary outcomes and safety

Tirzepatide, semaglutide, and liraglutide significantly reduced waist circumference; PEG-loxenatide increased HDL-C; semaglutide lowered LDL-C and total cholesterol; ITCA 650 and tirzepatide lowered triglycerides. Several agents increased discontinuation due to adverse events, nausea, vomiting, and/or diarrhoea relative to placebo.

High-dose analyses reinforced a dose-tolerability trade-off. Tirzepatide 15 mg weekly increased discontinuation due to adverse events (OR 2.26, 95% CI 1.35 to 3.77), while dose-dependent gastrointestinal effects were seen across several GLP-1RA strategies.

Discussion and conclusions

The authors interpret the network as evidence that all included GLP-1RA strategies improve glycaemic outcomes, with tirzepatide strongest for glycaemic control and CagriSema strongest for weight reduction. They emphasize gastrointestinal tolerability as a practical constraint, particularly at higher doses.

Stated limitations include population/follow-up heterogeneity, incomplete control of diet and exercise, incomplete reporting of randomization/blinding in some trials, sparse evidence for certain agents, and open-label/industry-funded studies.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. High-quality and unusually complete comparative incretin evidence, particularly useful for drug-class selection and dose/safety context. It is not a direct source for SGLT2 formulary interchangeability.

Relevance: 8.5/10

High relevance to comparative incretin pharmacotherapy and evidence synthesis; direct relevance to an SGLT2-only formulary decision is limited.

Evidence basis: 76-trial GLP-1RA NMA with detailed glycaemic, weight, lipid, and safety outcomes.

Principal limiting factor: It does not compare dapagliflozin with empagliflozin.

What would change the score: Direct comparative effectiveness against SGLT2 inhibitors or practice-setting implementation evidence would raise this score.

Novelty: 8.5/10

Strongly updated comparative evidence including newer dual/triple agonists and oral orforglipron.

Evidence basis: Network includes tirzepatide, retatrutide, mazdutide, CagriSema, orforglipron and established GLP-1RAs.

Principal limiting factor: Some newer agents are represented by few trials.

What would change the score: Additional mature phase 3 data and long-term outcomes would strengthen novelty and stability.

Evidence basis: PRISMA-NMA methods plus 76 randomized trials and extensive supplement.

Principal limiting factor: Sparse nodes, imputed SDs, open-label/industry trials, and some local inconsistency remain.

What would change the score: More direct head-to-head trials and fewer imputed data would raise the score.

Evidence strength: 7.5/10

Evidence basis: Large pooled sample with consistent primary/sensitivity findings and explicit certainty assessment.

Principal limiting factor: Several treatments have few studies and some network results are placebo-connected rather than head-to-head.

What would change the score: More direct comparisons and outcome-level certainty consistency would raise the score.

External validity: 7.0/10

Broad adult T2D trial evidence supports general glycaemic applicability, but trial populations, follow-up, cointerventions, and emerging-agent evidence vary.

Evidence basis: 39,246 participants across 76 RCTs.

Principal limiting factor: Diet/exercise and background therapy heterogeneity plus sparse newer-agent data.

What would change the score: More pragmatic, diverse, long-term comparative trials would raise the score.

Implementation value: 7.5/10

Useful for choosing among GLP-1RA options and anticipating GI tolerability; not a stand-alone formulary interchangeability source.

Evidence basis: Drug-specific effect sizes, dose/follow-up subgroups, and adverse-event networks.

What would change the score: Direct implementation, cost-effectiveness, and practice-setting evidence would raise the score.

Validity

Strengths: registered protocol; broad search; RCT-only eligibility; duplicate extraction; RoB2; random-effects NMA; explicit inconsistency, heterogeneity, confidence, publication-bias, sensitivity, and subgroup analyses; exceptionally detailed supplement.

Constraints: indirect comparisons dominate some nodes; newer agents have sparse evidence; missing SDs were sometimes imputed; clinical cointerventions and follow-up varied; some trials were open-label or industry-funded; source-internal numeric/label inconsistencies reduce mechanical trust in isolated cells.

Clinical translation

The source supports comparative expectations for HbA1c, fasting glucose, body weight, selected lipid measures, and GI tolerability across GLP-1RA strategies. It does not establish cardiovascular or renal outcome superiority between all agents, does not substitute for labels/guidelines, and should not be treated as direct evidence that one SGLT2 inhibitor can be interchanged for another.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Use now

Use as a comparative efficacy/safety reference when choosing among GLP-1RA and multi-agonist options in T2D, especially when HbA1c and body-weight goals are important.

Monitor

GI adverse effects and discontinuation risk, especially with higher doses and highly efficacious agents.

Do not overclaim

SUCRA rankings and placebo-referenced network estimates are not equivalent to direct head-to-head clinical superiority.

SGLT2 formulary context

Useful as GLP-1RA background/comparator evidence, but insufficient for empagliflozin-versus-dapagliflozin interchangeability or inpatient formulary policy.
