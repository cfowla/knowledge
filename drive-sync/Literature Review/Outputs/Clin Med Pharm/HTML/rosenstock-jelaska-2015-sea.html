One-page synthesis

The trial randomized 494 adults with type 2 diabetes and HbA1c above 7% and no more than 10% despite stable basal insulin to placebo, empagliflozin 10 mg, or empagliflozin 25 mg for 78 weeks. Basal insulin was fixed through week 18, which gave the primary HbA1c comparison a relatively clean treatment contrast. After week 18, investigators could adjust insulin. Mean baseline HbA1c was 8.2%, mean BMI 32.2 kg/m2, and mean eGFR 84 mL/min/1.73 m2.

At week 18, HbA1c fell 0.6 percentage points more with empagliflozin 10 mg and 0.7 points more with 25 mg than with placebo. Both confidence intervals excluded no difference and both p values were below 0.001. At week 78, HbA1c differences remained -0.5 and -0.6 points. Basal insulin dose rose by 5.5 IU in the placebo group but changed by -1.2 IU and -0.5 IU in the empagliflozin groups. Weight was 2.9 kg and 2.8 kg lower versus placebo at week 78. Confirmed hypoglycemia was similar across groups at 35% to 36%, while urinary tract and genital infection event categories were more frequent in the empagliflozin groups.

Verdict: Read soon. The paper is useful for direct empagliflozin add-on-to-basal-insulin efficacy and safety. It is not a head-to-head SGLT2 comparison and does not test cardiovascular or kidney outcome benefit.

Methods

Population and intervention

Adults had screening HbA1c above 7% and no more than 10%, BMI no more than 45 kg/m2, and stable basal glargine, detemir, or NPH insulin. Stable metformin and sulfonylurea were allowed. Major exclusions included eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, recent myocardial infarction, stroke, or transient ischemic attack, frequent hypoglycemia in the investigator's judgment, and recent use of chronic short-acting insulin or GLP-1 receptor agonists.

Randomization was 1:1:1 and stratified by screening HbA1c and center. The study ran at 97 centers in seven countries. Basal insulin stayed fixed for the first 18 weeks. From week 18 to 78, investigators could adjust it for confirmed fasting plasma glucose above 6.1 mmol/L. Metformin and sulfonylurea were to remain unchanged.

Endpoints and analysis

The primary endpoint was change in HbA1c at week 18. Key secondary endpoints were week-78 changes in basal insulin dose and HbA1c. Other endpoints included fasting plasma glucose, body weight, HbA1c target attainment, blood pressure, adverse events, hypoglycemia, and prospectively defined urinary tract and genital infection event categories.

The primary analysis used ANCOVA in week-18 completers with treatment and region as fixed effects and baseline HbA1c as a covariate. Values after rescue therapy were set to missing and imputed with last observation carried forward. The supplement describes a dose-specific hierarchical sequence at two-sided alpha 0.025 and an 88% power calculation for a 0.4-point HbA1c difference. MMRM and noncompleter-as-failure analyses supplied sensitivity checks.

Results

Disposition and baseline

Four hundred ninety-four patients were randomized, 170 to placebo, 169 to empagliflozin 10 mg, and 155 to empagliflozin 25 mg. Four hundred twenty-nine, 87%, completed 18 weeks and 360, 73%, completed 78 weeks. Mean age was 58.8 years. Women were 44% of the cohort. Eighty-nine percent had diabetes for more than 5 years.

HbA1c below 7% at week 18 occurred in 5% with placebo, 18% with empagliflozin 10 mg, and 19% with 25 mg. At week 78, the proportions were 7%, 12%, and 18%. The week-78 odds ratio versus placebo was 1.9, 95% CI 0.9 to 4.3, for 10 mg and 3.2, 95% CI 1.5 to 6.9, for 25 mg.

Blood pressure

At week 78, systolic blood pressure changed by 4.2 mmHg more in the downward direction with empagliflozin 10 mg than with placebo, 95% CI -7.0 to -1.3, p=0.004. The 25 mg difference was -2.4 mmHg, 95% CI -5.4 to 0.5, p=0.099. Diastolic blood pressure showed the same uneven dose pattern. The 10 mg arm differed from placebo at week 78, while the 25 mg confidence interval included no difference.

Two hypoglycemic events requiring assistance occurred in the 25 mg group and none in the placebo or 10 mg groups. One death occurred in the placebo arm and none in the empagliflozin arms. The paper reports no diabetic ketoacidosis or ketonuria.

Supplementary Table 4 shows small on-treatment eGFR declines in all groups. Mean changes at follow-up were -6.7 mL/min/1.73 m2 with placebo, -1.9 with empagliflozin 10 mg, and -0.8 with 25 mg. Hematocrit rose on treatment with both empagliflozin doses. These were laboratory observations, not prespecified clinical kidney outcome tests.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 8/10
Directly tests empagliflozin added to basal insulin over 78 weeks.
Evidence basis: randomized add-on trial with clinically familiar glycemic and safety outcomes. Limit: no dapagliflozin comparison or cardiorenal outcome endpoint. A direct formulary comparison would raise the score.
Novelty 7/10
The long add-on-to-insulin design addressed an active treatment question at publication.
Evidence basis: 78-week randomized exposure with insulin-dose and weight outcomes. Limit: the study extends an established SGLT2 pharmacologic concept rather than testing a new mechanism. Novel comparative evidence would raise the score.
Method strength 7/10
Randomization, double blinding, placebo control, predefined endpoints, and multiplicity control support internal validity.
Limit: completer analyses, LOCF after rescue censoring, and investigator-discretion insulin adjustment after week 18. A full intention-to-treat estimand with stronger missing-data handling and protocolized insulin titration would raise the score.
Evidence strength 7/10
The primary HbA1c effect is consistent across both doses with confidence intervals that exclude no difference.
Limit: 73% completed 78 weeks and longer-term secondary results depend more on postrandomization management and missing-data assumptions. Lower attrition and modern estimands would raise the score.
External validity 6/10
Ninety-seven centers across seven countries improve site diversity.
Limit: selected patients had HbA1c 7% to 10%, BMI no more than 45 kg/m2, eGFR at least 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, and stable basal insulin. Broader renal function and less selected insulin-treated populations would raise the score.
Implementation value 7/10
The doses, background-insulin rules, glycemic effects, weight effects, and infection signals are easy to translate into evidence review.
Limit: this 2015 trial does not establish current treatment policy or relative value versus another SGLT2 inhibitor. Current head-to-head, outcome, guideline, and formulary evidence would raise the score.

Bias and interpretation

The strongest causal inference is the week-18 HbA1c comparison because treatment was randomized and basal insulin was intended to remain fixed. The week-78 results remain randomized comparisons, but insulin could be adjusted and only 73% completed treatment. LOCF and completer-based analysis add assumptions that can matter when discontinuation or rescue therapy differs between groups.

Funding and employment ties are substantial. Boehringer Ingelheim and Eli Lilly funded the trial, writing support was funded by Boehringer Ingelheim, and five listed authors were Boehringer Ingelheim employees. Sponsorship does not invalidate the randomized results, but it is part of the risk-of-bias context and matters when interpreting selective emphasis.

Practice and implementation takeaways

Best use
Use the paper as direct evidence that empagliflozin added to basal insulin can lower HbA1c and weight without increasing the overall observed frequency of confirmed hypoglycemia in this selected trial population.
Safety signal
Keep the higher observed genital-infection and urinary-tract-infection categories visible. The trial was not large enough to settle uncommon safety outcomes.
Do not overuse
Do not treat the trial as evidence that empagliflozin is superior or equivalent to dapagliflozin, or as proof of cardiovascular or kidney outcome benefit.
Formulary relevance
For an SGLT2 formulary review, this paper is one efficacy and tolerability component. A current decision also needs comparative, cardiorenal, guideline, label, acquisition-cost, and operational evidence outside this source packet.
