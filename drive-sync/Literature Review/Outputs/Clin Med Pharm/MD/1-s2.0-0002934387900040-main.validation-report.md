## SEA appraisal summary

- **Verdict:** Read soon
- **Relevance:** 8/10
- **Novelty:** 7/10
- **Method strength:** 6/10
- **Evidence strength:** 6/10
- **External validity:** 3/10
- **Implementation value:** 5/10

The article is most useful as a historical foundation for bleeding-risk prediction during initiation of anticoagulant therapy. Strengths include explicit bleeding definitions, a derivation/testing split, quantitative presentation of candidate predictors, and a simple additive risk rule. Important limits include retrospective single-site chart review, only 28 major bleeding events, era-specific anticoagulation practice, stepwise model construction, internal rather than external validation, and absence of contemporary calibration or clinical-impact testing. Two final predictors occur during treatment, so the complete four-factor rule functions partly as a monitoring model rather than as a purely pretreatment risk model.
