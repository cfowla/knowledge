## Key appraisal finding requiring external verification

- The review states in its DOAC section that the 2021 ISTH SSC guidance supports standard-dose apixaban/rivaroxaban in severe obesity **without routine peak/trough drug-level monitoring**.
- The review’s Figure 5 and final Recommendations section then advise peak/trough anti-Xa monitoring for DOAC treatment, creating an internal inconsistency.
- External verification against the primary ISTH 2021 communication (Martin KA et al. J Thromb Haemost. 2021;19:1874–1882. DOI 10.1111/jth.15358) confirmed that ISTH suggests **not** regularly following peak or trough drug-specific DOAC levels in BMI >40 kg/m² or weight >120 kg, except for specific absorption assessment after bariatric surgery.
- Therefore, routine DOAC anti-Xa monitoring in this review should be treated as the review authors’ own conservative recommendation rather than ISTH-concordant guidance.

## Extraction / appraisal limitations

- The source is a narrative/comprehensive review rather than primary literature; underlying trial-level claims were not independently re-extracted from each cited primary publication.
- References (pages 18-22) were treated as provenance infrastructure and were not atomized.
- The review uses anti-Xa surrogate outcomes extensively for enoxaparin; these were preserved as reported rather than converted into patient-centered outcomes.
- The review does not report a formal systematic-review risk-of-bias assessment or evidence-grading framework for the complete article.
- External currency verification was targeted rather than exhaustive; it was used to verify the identified DOAC-monitoring conflict, not to replace the review with a new guideline review.
- The review itself does not provide a whole-article PRISMA flow, registered protocol, formal risk-of-bias assessment, or certainty-of-evidence grading.
- Many enoxaparin findings are based on anti-Xa surrogate outcomes with low clinical-event counts and heterogeneous target definitions.
