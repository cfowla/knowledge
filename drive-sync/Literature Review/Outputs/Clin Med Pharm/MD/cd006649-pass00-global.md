## 1. Review purpose and scope

### Clinical question

Among people with cancer and objectively confirmed acute venous thromboembolism (deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, or both), what are the comparative efficacy and safety of parenteral anticoagulants used for **initial treatment**, particularly:

1. low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) versus unfractionated heparin (UFH);
2. fondaparinux versus heparin (UFH and/or LMWH);
3. dalteparin versus tinzaparin?

The source defines initial parenteral anticoagulation as typically the first **5 to 10 days** of VTE treatment.

### Review objective

To compare the efficacy and safety of three parenteral anticoagulant strategies for the initial treatment of VTE in people with cancer:

- fixed-dose LMWH;
- adjusted-dose UFH;
- fondaparinux.

The included evidence also contains one head-to-head comparison of two LMWH products, dalteparin versus tinzaparin.

### Scope boundary

This review is about **initial parenteral anticoagulation**, not the full long-term treatment course for cancer-associated thrombosis. Most included trials transitioned participants to vitamin K antagonist therapy after the initial parenteral phase. The review therefore should not be read as a direct comparison of contemporary full-course anticoagulant strategies.

It is also a **secondary evidence synthesis**. The individual RCT results reproduced in the review remain primary-study evidence summarized by the Cochrane authors; they should not be represented as if the review itself enrolled participants.

## 2. PICO / eligibility scaffold

### Population

- People with cancer and objectively confirmed acute VTE.
- VTE could be acute DVT, PE, or both.
- Any age group was eligible, including children.
- Solid or hematologic malignancy was eligible.
- Any cancer stage was eligible.
- Eligibility did not depend on the type of cancer therapy.

### Intervention / comparator families

Eligible initial parenteral anticoagulants:

- **LMWH** — examples in the source include dalteparin, tinzaparin, nadroparin, enoxaparin, reviparin.
- **UFH** — generally adjusted-dose intravenous or subcutaneous heparin.
- **Fondaparinux**.

The review was interested in comparisons among these strategies. Studies in which thrombolytic therapy was part of the intervention were excluded.

### Primary outcome

- All-cause mortality.

### Secondary outcomes

- Symptomatic recurrent DVT.
- Symptomatic recurrent PE.
- Major bleeding.
- Minor bleeding.
- Postphlebitic syndrome.
- Quality of life.
- Thrombocytopenia.

Primary and secondary outcomes were assessed up to **3 months** after initiation of VTE treatment.

### Outcome-definition boundary

For recurrent DVT and PE, the review required objective diagnostic confirmation using prespecified modalities. For major and minor bleeding, the review accepted the included study authors' definitions rather than imposing one standardized bleeding definition.

## 3. Background concepts and terminology

The review frames cancer-associated thrombosis as a setting in which both recurrence and bleeding risks are elevated. The background describes cancer-related VTE risk as multifactorial and related to patient characteristics, tumor type/site, treatment, biomarkers, surgery, and therapies such as chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, central venous catheters, and immunotherapy.

The source directs readers to **Additional Table 1 / Glossary** for technical terminology. Relevant recurring terms for downstream passes include:

- VTE — venous thromboembolism
- DVT — deep venous thrombosis
- PE — pulmonary embolism
- LMWH — low molecular weight heparin
- UFH — unfractionated heparin
- VKA — vitamin K antagonist
- INR — international normalized ratio

- RR — risk ratio

- CI — confidence interval
- GRADE — certainty-of-evidence framework
- ITT — intention-to-treat

## 4. Search and study-selection architecture

### Search date and databases

The update search was conducted on **14 August 2021**.

Electronic databases:

- CENTRAL
- MEDLINE via Ovid, starting in 1946
- Embase via Ovid, starting in 1980

No language restrictions were applied.

### Additional search methods

The review additionally reports:

- handsearching ASCO conference proceedings from 1982 through February 2021;
- handsearching ASH proceedings from 2003 through 14 August 2021;
- searching ClinicalTrials.gov;
- searching the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform;
- reviewing reference lists of included papers and relevant systematic reviews;
- contacting experts regarding unpublished and ongoing trials.

The detailed electronic search strategies are reproduced in **Appendix 1**.

### Screening and eligibility

Four pairs of review authors independently screened titles/abstracts and full text. Potentially eligible reports were retrieved if at least one reviewer judged them potentially eligible. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer.

Reported agreement for study eligibility was **kappa = 0.94**.

### Search yield

- 11,484 records identified through database searching plus 1,136 additional records from other sources are shown in the study-flow figure.
- After duplicate removal, **3,073 unique citations** were screened.
- **147 full-text articles** were assessed for eligibility.
- **15 RCTs** were included in the qualitative synthesis.
- The 2021 search found **no new eligible studies**.

The source reports 13 included studies as full reports and two as abstracts.

## 5. Data extraction and analytic framework

### Data extraction

Review authors independently extracted study data using standardized forms, including:

- participant demographics and cancer characteristics;
- VTE presentation;
- treatment-arm numbers and follow-up;
- anticoagulant type, dose, schedule, duration, and co-interventions;
- mortality, recurrent VTE, bleeding, and other prespecified outcomes;
- funding, ethical approval, and conflicts of interest.

The reviewers attempted to contact study authors for incompletely reported data.

### Effect measures

- Dichotomous outcomes were analyzed with **risk ratios (RRs)**.
- The review reports absolute effects in the Summary of Findings tables as **risk differences per 1,000**.
- None of the relevant outcomes were meta-analyzed as continuous outcomes.
- The individual participant was the unit of analysis.

### Missing data

The review used explicit categories for definitely missing, definitely not missing, and potentially missing participant data.

The **primary meta-analysis used complete-case analysis**, excluding participants considered to have missing data.

Sensitivity analyses using increasingly stringent assumptions about missing participant outcomes were planned when a primary meta-analysis produced a statistically significant result. Because none of the analyzed outcomes had a statistically significant primary effect estimate, these missing-data sensitivity analyses were not performed.

### Non-compliance

The review attempted to preserve ITT principles for non-compliance while treating non-compliance as analytically distinct from missing data.

### Meta-analysis model

Study results were pooled using a **random-effects model**.

### Heterogeneity

Heterogeneity was assessed through:

- visual inspection of forest plots;
- the I² statistic;
- formal statistical testing.

Participant-characteristic subgroup analyses were planned but **not performed because data were unavailable**.

### Reporting bias

The review explored trial-registry/protocol availability and planned funnel plots for outcomes with at least 10 trials. No funnel plots were created because no outcome analysis contained 10 or more studies.

## 6. Risk-of-bias and certainty framework

### Study-level risk of bias

The review used the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool and assessed:

- random sequence generation;
- allocation concealment;
- blinding of participants/personnel;
- blinding of outcome assessment;
- incomplete outcome data;
- selective outcome reporting;
- other bias.

Global signals to carry into the evidence-base deep pass:

- Allocation concealment was judged adequate in 13 of 15 studies; two studies did not report it.
- Participants/personnel were blinded in 3 of 15 studies and not blinded in 12.
- Outcome assessors were judged blinded in 14 studies and probably not blinded in one.
- Most studies reported complete follow-up; three had incomplete or unclear cancer-subgroup follow-up.
- The review did not suspect selective outcome reporting for the prespecified outcomes, although cancer-subgroup data were missing for seven studies.
- No other systematic source of bias was suspected by the review authors.

### Certainty of evidence

Certainty was assessed at the **outcome level using GRADE**.

The Summary of Findings tables classify:

- LMWH versus UFH outcomes as **low certainty**;
- fondaparinux versus heparin outcomes as **low certainty**, except minor bleeding, which is **moderate certainty**;
- dalteparin versus tinzaparin outcomes as **low certainty**.

The dominant downgrading reason shown in the Summary of Findings tables is **serious or very serious imprecision**, with wide confidence intervals spanning potentially important benefit and harm.

### Internal wording issue requiring later reconciliation

The Discussion's "Quality of the evidence" paragraph states that the LMWH-versus-UFH and dalteparin-versus-tinzaparin comparisons were judged **moderate** despite also attributing the rating to "very serious imprecision." This conflicts with the Abstract, Plain Language Summary, and Summary of Findings tables, which label these outcomes **low certainty**.

**Do not silently repair this in Pass 0.** Treat it as a source-internal inconsistency to reconcile explicitly during final synthesis, with the Summary of Findings tables retained as the structured outcome-level evidence source.

## 7. Global evidence-base architecture

### Included evidence

The review includes **15 RCTs involving 1,615 participants with cancer and VTE**.

Comparison families:

1. **LMWH versus UFH:** 13 studies.
2. **Fondaparinux versus heparin:** 1 study.
3. **Dalteparin versus tinzaparin:** 1 study.

### Critical subgroup architecture

A central limitation is that participants with cancer were **subgroups within all 15 included trials**, rather than the trials being designed exclusively around cancer-associated VTE.

Cancer-subgroup data provenance varied:

- three studies directly reported cancer-subgroup data;
- three had follow-up publications reporting cancer-subgroup data;
- cancer-subgroup data for two studies were obtained from study authors;
- seven studies did not report cancer-subgroup data directly, and the review obtained those data from two previously published systematic reviews.

Those seven indirectly sourced studies were excluded from the main analysis and included in sensitivity analyses.

The review also notes that **none of the included studies specified the cancer types** of participants.

### Potentially eligible evidence not incorporated

The review reports that 11 full-text articles contained cancer subgroups but lacked usable cancer-subgroup outcome data. In the Discussion, the authors estimate that these studies could have contributed **340 additional participants** beyond the 1,615 included participants.

This missing subgroup evidence is a major source-wide applicability/completeness constraint.

## 8. High-level result map for navigation only

These values orient the deep passes; they are not the final SEA interpretation.

### Comparison 1 — LMWH versus UFH

**Mortality at 3 months**
- 418 participants, 5 RCTs
- RR 0.66
- 95% CI 0.40 to 1.10
- absolute effect: 57 fewer deaths per 1,000
- 95% CI: 101 fewer to 17 more
- GRADE: low certainty
- I² = 0%

**Recurrent VTE at 3 months**
- 422 participants, 3 RCTs
- RR 0.69
- 95% CI 0.27 to 1.76
- absolute effect: 30 fewer per 1,000
- 95% CI: 70 fewer to 73 more
- GRADE: low certainty
- I² = 46%

No cancer-subgroup data were available for bleeding, postphlebitic syndrome, quality of life, or thrombocytopenia for this comparison.

### Comparison 2 — Fondaparinux versus heparin

One RCT / cancer subgroup; 477 participants.

At 3 months:

- Mortality: RR 1.25 (95% CI 0.86 to 1.81); low certainty.
- Recurrent VTE: RR 0.93 (95% CI 0.56 to 1.54); low certainty.
- Major bleeding: RR 0.82 (95% CI 0.40 to 1.66); low certainty.
- Minor bleeding: RR 1.53 (95% CI 0.88 to 2.66); moderate certainty.

No data were available for postphlebitic syndrome, quality of life, or thrombocytopenia.

### Comparison 3 — Dalteparin versus tinzaparin

One RCT / cancer subgroup; 113 participants.

At 3 months:

- Mortality: RR 0.86 (95% CI 0.43 to 1.73); low certainty.
- Recurrent VTE: RR 0.44 (95% CI 0.09 to 2.16); low certainty.
- Major bleeding: RR 2.19 (95% CI 0.20 to 23.42); low certainty.
- Minor bleeding: RR 0.82 (95% CI 0.30 to 2.21); low certainty.

No data were available for postphlebitic syndrome, quality of life, or thrombocytopenia.

## 9. Authors' source-level interpretation

The authors conclude that LMWH is probably superior to UFH for initial VTE treatment in people with cancer and state that treatment choice should balance benefits and harms with patient values and preferences.

They call for trials restricted to people with cancer, availability of raw randomized-trial data for individual-participant-data meta-analysis, and more complete reporting of patient-important outcomes, including harms.

**Pass 0 does not endorse or reject this conclusion.** Later passes must test whether the wording is proportionate to the low-certainty, imprecise outcome estimates and the cancer-subgroup evidence architecture.

## 10. Source-wide limitations already identified by the authors

Carry these forward into later appraisal:

1. **Incomplete cancer-subgroup data**
   - 11 potentially eligible studies could not contribute usable cancer-subgroup data.
   - These studies could have added approximately 340 participants.

2. **Limited outcome completeness**
   - Only three included studies reported cancer-subgroup data for recurrent VTE.
   - No cancer-subgroup bleeding data were available for the LMWH-versus-UFH comparison.

3. **Cancer heterogeneity not characterizable**
   - Included trials did not specify cancer types.
   - Planned subgroup analyses by participant characteristics could not be performed.

4. **Subgroup evidence rather than cancer-specific trial designs**
   - All included evidence derived from cancer subgroups within broader VTE trials.

5. **Potential search limitation**
   - The authors note that an electronic search restricted to cancer terms could be problematic because relevant cancer-subgroup data may arise from trials not restricted to cancer populations.

6. **Imprecision**
   - Most outcome estimates have wide confidence intervals spanning possible benefit and harm.

7. **Small evidence sets per outcome**
   - No outcome contained enough studies for funnel-plot assessment.

8. **Incomplete harms and patient-centered outcomes**
   - Quality of life, postphlebitic syndrome, and thrombocytopenia were not reported in the principal comparisons.

## 16. Appraisal constraints to carry into later passes

Do not assign final scores until all deep passes are complete. Final appraisal must explicitly consider:

1. **Currency**
   - Source search ends 14 August 2021.
   - No external verification has been performed in this Pass 0.
   - Final practice translation must distinguish source accuracy from present-day currency.

2. **Secondary/subgroup evidence**
   - Cancer participants are subgroups of broader VTE trials.
   - Some cancer-subgroup data were obtained indirectly from prior systematic reviews.
   - Cancer type/stage information is limited or absent.

3. **Completeness**
   - Potentially eligible cancer-subgroup data were unavailable from 11 studies.
   - Harms and patient-centered outcomes are incompletely reported.

4. **Precision**
   - Most estimates have wide CIs spanning benefit and harm.
   - GRADE downgrading is dominated by imprecision.

5. **Small evidence sets**
   - Several comparison/outcome estimates come from one RCT.
   - Publication-bias assessment by funnel plot was not possible.

6. **Heterogeneity / effect modification**
   - Planned subgroup analyses were not feasible.
   - Cancer-specific effect modifiers cannot be meaningfully assessed.

7. **Risk of bias**
   - Participant/personnel blinding was absent in most studies.
   - Allocation concealment was unreported in two studies.
   - Some cancer-subgroup follow-up was incomplete or unclear.

8. **Review-process limitation**
   - Search terms focused on cancer even though relevant evidence can arise from broader VTE trials with cancer subgroups.

9. **Guideline interface**
   - ASH-supported work used somewhat different methods from the Cochrane review.
   - Do not conflate this review's results or GRADE ratings with the ASH guideline's.

10. **Internal source inconsistency**
    - GRADE certainty wording differs between the structured SOF material and one Discussion paragraph.

11. **Claim calibration**
    - The authors' "probably superior" language for LMWH versus UFH must be appraised against low-certainty evidence and confidence intervals crossing the null.
