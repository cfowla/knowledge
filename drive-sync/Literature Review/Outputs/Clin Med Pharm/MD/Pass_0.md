# 4. Global clinical assumptions

## 4.1 Organism identification and susceptibility

The treatment suggestions generally assume:

1. The causative pathogen has been identified.
2. In-vitro susceptibility to a proposed treatment has been demonstrated.
3. Clinically relevant beta-lactamase information should be incorporated when
   available.
4. Infection must be distinguished from colonization.

When multiple agents have comparable expected efficacy, selection should also
consider:

- safety
- tolerability
- cost
- third-party payer coverage
- ease of administration
- institutional formulary availability

## 4.2 Colonization versus infection

Distinguishing infection from colonization is treated as a foundational
antimicrobial-stewardship requirement. Unnecessary treatment can promote
resistance and expose patients to avoidable antimicrobial harm.

This distinction is especially relevant to organisms frequently recovered from
clinical specimens without necessarily representing invasive disease.

# 5. Empiric-therapy boundary

empiric_regimen_recommendations: generally outside scope

Nevertheless, empiric selection should consider:

1. Most likely pathogens.
2. Severity of illness.
3. Suspected source of infection.
4. Patient-specific factors, including:
   - severe beta-lactam allergy
   - profound immunosuppression
   - underlying renal disease
5. Prior microbiologic data and AST results from the preceding 12 months.
6. Antibiotic exposure during the preceding 3 months.
7. Local epidemiology and cumulative susceptibility data.

Once pathogen identification and AST data are available:

- reassess the empiric regimen;
- refine therapy according to susceptibility;
- incorporate clinically relevant beta-lactamases when available.

special_empiric_caution:
  - Common empiric regimens frequently lack activity against CRAB.
  - Common empiric regimens frequently lack activity against S. maltophilia.
  - Empiric inclusion of agents targeting these organisms should be individualized
    using prior cultures, host risk, clinical presentation, and toxicity risk.

# 6. Duration-of-therapy framework

specific_duration_regimens_provided: false

general_rule: >
  Infections caused by AMR pathogens generally do not require longer treatment
  solely because the organism is resistant.

duration_should_still_consider:
  - immune status
  - adequacy of source control
  - clinical response

## Inactive empiric therapy

### uUTI

For uncomplicated UTI confined to the bladder in an afebrile woman or man:

If the empiric agent later proves inactive in vitro but the patient has
nevertheless improved clinically, the guidance generally does NOT require:

- repeat urine cultures solely for this reason;
- changing therapy;
- extending the planned treatment course.

### Other infections, including cUTI

If the empiric agent proves inactive:

- switch to an agent with demonstrated susceptibility;
- generally count treatment duration from initiation of active therapy.

cUTI_definition_used_by_guidance: >
  UTI extending beyond the bladder, including pyelonephritis, febrile or
  bacteremic UTI, catheter-associated UTI, or prostatitis.

# 7. IV-to-oral transition framework

Oral transition should be pursued when feasible if ALL of the following are met:

1. The pathogen is susceptible to an appropriate oral agent.
2. The patient is hemodynamically stable.
3. The oral agent is expected to achieve adequate concentrations at the infection site.
4. Gastrointestinal absorption is expected to be sufficient.

This is a global framework; individual pathogen sections may impose additional
agent-specific restrictions.

# 13. Guideline disclaimer / validation boundary

The document explicitly states that:

- it is intended to support clinical decision-making;
- it cannot account for every patient circumstance;
- it is an educational synthesis of scientific and clinical evidence;
- it is not continuously updated;
- new evidence may emerge between development, publication, and use;
- it is not comprehensive;
- it does not represent the only appropriate treatment approach;
- clinical judgment remains paramount;
- application should be individualized to patient context.

SEA interpretation:
The final artifact should distinguish:

1. what the panel suggests;
2. the evidence summarized in support of that suggestion;
3. uncertainty acknowledged by the panel;
4. independent SEA appraisal of whether the evidence appears proportionate to
   the recommendation.

# 18. Appraisal flags to carry forward

These are appraisal targets, NOT final judgments:

1. No GRADE methodology.
2. Literature review is explicitly "comprehensive" but not necessarily systematic.
3. Clinical expertise and consensus contribute directly to recommendations.
4. Evidentiary maturity is expected to vary substantially by clinical question.
5. Some recommendations are likely supported predominantly by clinical trials;
   others may rely heavily on observational, PK/PD, animal, or in-vitro evidence.
6. Recommendation wording does not itself encode standardized evidence certainty.
7. United States specificity improves local applicability but limits international
   generalizability.
8. Breakpoints and therapeutic options are time-sensitive.
9. Several dosing recommendations differ from FDA labeling.
10. Supplemental dosing information is outside the currently evaluated PDF.
11. Colonization-versus-infection uncertainty is a central clinical validity issue
    for selected organisms.
12. Resistance emergence during therapy is an explicit implementation concern.
13. Final appraisal should evaluate whether the certainty and directness of the
    underlying evidence are proportionate to each Suggested approach.
