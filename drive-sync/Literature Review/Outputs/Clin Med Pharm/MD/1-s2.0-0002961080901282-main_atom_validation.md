## Extraction decisions and limitations

1. The source is a **retrospective, single-center chart review**, not a randomized comparison. ACT groups are observational exposure strata, so the atoms preserve association rather than causal effect.
2. Only **71% of cases had objective diagnostic support**; the source states that therapeutic-response analyses were restricted to objectively confirmed cases.
3. **Platelet counts were infrequently monitored**, limiting interpretation of thrombocytopenia frequency.
4. The study **did not specifically evaluate long-term outcomes**.
5. Historical dosing wording was preserved rather than silently corrected. In particular, the paper reports a loading dose as “50 units/kg/hour” and describes a future high-dose regimen as a “bolus of 300 units/kg/hour.” These phrases are source wording, not normalized contemporary dosing instructions.
6. Table I was checked against the rendered PDF because text extraction can lose inequality symbols. The table rows are **ACT ≥180 seconds**, **ACT <180 seconds**, **Age ≥70 years**, and **Age <70 years**.
7. No relative risks, odds ratios, NNT/NNH values, or other unreported comparative estimates were calculated. Quantitative atoms preserve reported counts, percentages, means, and significance statements.
8. No external current-practice verification was added to the atom dataset. The JSON is a source-faithful historical extraction, not a current anticoagulation recommendation.
