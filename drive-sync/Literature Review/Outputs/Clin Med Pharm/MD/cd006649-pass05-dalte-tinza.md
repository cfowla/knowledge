# 1. Comparison question

Within the cancer subgroup of Wells 2005, what are the comparative effects of **dalteparin versus tinzaparin** during initial treatment of venous thromboembolism on:

1. all-cause mortality at 3 months;
2. recurrent VTE at 3 months;
3. major bleeding at 3 months;
4. minor bleeding at 3 months?

The source additionally prespecified quality of life, postphlebitic syndrome, and thrombocytopenia, but these were not reported for this comparison.

---

# 2. Evidence architecture

## 2.1 Study and subgroup

The entire comparison comes from **one RCT, Wells 2005**.

The Cochrane review describes:

- **505 participants in the overall Wells trial**;
- **113 participants with cancer** contributing to this review;
- adults aged at least 18 years;
- upper- or lower-extremity DVT;
- outpatient treatment;
- **54 participants** in the dalteparin arm;
- **59 participants** in the tinzaparin arm;
- 3-month follow-up;
- complete follow-up in the cancer subgroup.

The cancer cohort is therefore a **subgroup of a broader VTE trial**, not a cancer-specific randomized trial.

## 2.2 Treatments

The study-characteristics table reports two once-daily subcutaneous LMWH regimens:

- **dalteparin 200 IU/kg SC once daily**;
- **tinzaparin 175 IU/kg SC once daily**.

The review's narrative description states that warfarin was begun within 24 hours of the first LMWH dose and continued for three months.

The Characteristics of Studies table labels tinzaparin as the "Intervention" and dalteparin as the "Control," whereas Summary of Findings 3 and Analyses 3.1–3.4 orient the comparison as **dalteparin versus tinzaparin**. This is a presentation/orientation difference, not a contradiction in the treatment arms. This pass uses the forest-plot/Summary-of-Findings orientation consistently: **dalteparin relative to tinzaparin**.

## 2.3 Outcomes reported

Wells 2005 contributes cancer-subgroup data for:

- mortality;
- symptomatic recurrent VTE;
- major bleeding;
- minor bleeding.

The review reports no usable data for:

- quality of life;
- postphlebitic syndrome;
- thrombocytopenia.

The study-characteristics table also states that screening and diagnostic testing for DVT/PE were **not reported** in that table.

# 3. Risk-of-bias context for Wells 2005

The Cochrane review judges Wells 2005 at **low risk of bias across all seven assessed domains**.

| Domain | Cochrane judgment | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Random sequence generation | Low risk | Computer-generated blocks; block size unknown to investigators |
| Allocation concealment | Low risk | Sequential opaque envelopes opened after consent |
| Blinding of participants/personnel | Low risk | Physicians and most nurses blinded; initial treatment nurse not involved in follow-up assessment |
| Blinding of outcome assessment | Low risk | Follow-up assessors described as blinded |
| Incomplete outcome data | Low risk | Complete follow-up |
| Selective reporting | Low risk | Relevant methods outcomes reported; study not registered and no published protocol identified |
| Other bias | Low risk | No early stopping for benefit; no other bias suspected |

The study is described as a **randomized controlled trial, single blind** in the characteristics table. The more detailed risk-of-bias support indicates that most care personnel and outcome assessors were blinded, although the nurse providing initial care knew the assigned treatment.

### Pass 5 appraisal

The review's study-level risk-of-bias assessment is relatively favorable. The dominant weakness of this comparison is therefore **not primarily the Cochrane risk-of-bias rating**; it is the small cancer subgroup, sparse event counts, wide confidence intervals, and limited cancer-specific characterization.

The absence of a registry or published protocol prevents an independent protocol-to-report comparison from the information preserved in this review, even though the review authors judged selective reporting to be low risk.

# 5. Mortality at 3 months

## 5.1 Source-reported effect

Summary of Findings 3 and Analysis 3.1 agree on:

- **113 participants**
- **1 RCT**
- dalteparin: **11/54 deaths**
- tinzaparin: **14/59 deaths**
- **RR 0.86**
- **95% CI 0.43 to 1.73**
- Z = **0.43**
- P = **0.67**
- heterogeneity: **not applicable**
- tinzaparin baseline risk: **237 deaths per 1,000**
- absolute effect with dalteparin: **33 fewer deaths per 1,000**
- **95% CI: 135 fewer to 173 more per 1,000**
- **25 total deaths**
- **GRADE: low certainty**
- downgrade: **two levels for very serious imprecision**

The review describes dalteparin as potentially reducing mortality "slightly."

## 5.2 Precision-focused interpretation

The point estimate is modestly below 1.0, but the interval is compatible with:

- a substantial mortality reduction;
- little or no difference;
- a substantial mortality increase.

The absolute interval spans from **135 fewer to 173 more deaths per 1,000**, which is broader than the point estimate is informative.

### Pass 5 appraisal

The mortality result does **not establish a mortality advantage** for dalteparin. The point estimate is directionally favorable, but 25 total deaths in a 113-person subgroup leave the estimate too imprecise to support a stable comparative conclusion.

The phrase "may reduce mortality slightly" accurately signals uncertainty better than a direction-only statement such as "dalteparin reduced mortality."

# 6. Recurrent VTE at 3 months

## 6.1 Source-reported effect

Analysis 3.2 and Summary of Findings 3 report:

- dalteparin: **2/54 recurrent VTE events**
- tinzaparin: **5/59 recurrent VTE events**
- **RR 0.44**
- **95% CI 0.09 to 2.16**
- Z = **1.02**
- P = **0.31**
- heterogeneity: **not applicable**
- tinzaparin baseline risk: **85 per 1,000**
- absolute effect with dalteparin: **47 fewer per 1,000**
- **95% CI: 77 fewer to 98 more per 1,000**
- **7 total recurrent-VTE events**
- **GRADE: low certainty**
- downgrade: **two levels for very serious imprecision**

The review says dalteparin "may reduce VTE recurrence."

## 6.2 Precision-focused interpretation

The RR point estimate of 0.44 could appear clinically important if read without its interval. The 95% CI of **0.09 to 2.16**, however, spans:

- very large potential benefit;
- no difference;
- more than a doubling of risk.

Only **seven total recurrent-VTE events** support the estimate.

### Pass 5 appraisal

This is **extremely sparse efficacy evidence**. The apparent relative reduction cannot be treated as a reliable demonstration of benefit. The event count and interval dominate interpretation.

The forest plot contains only one study, so there is no between-study heterogeneity estimate and no possibility of judging consistency across independent trials.

# 7. Major bleeding at 3 months

## 7.1 Source-reported effect

Analysis 3.3 and Summary of Findings 3 report:

- dalteparin: **2/54 major bleeding events**
- tinzaparin: **1/59 major bleeding event**
- **RR 2.19**
- **95% CI 0.20 to 23.42**
- Z = **0.65**
- P = **0.52**
- heterogeneity: **not applicable**
- tinzaparin baseline risk: **17 per 1,000**
- absolute effect with dalteparin: **20 more major bleeds per 1,000**
- **95% CI: 14 fewer to 380 more per 1,000**
- **3 total major bleeding events**
- **GRADE: low certainty**
- downgrade: **two levels for very serious imprecision**

The review describes dalteparin as potentially increasing major bleeding slightly.

## 7.2 Precision-focused interpretation

This is the most unstable numerical result in the comparison.

The relative estimate is **RR 2.19**, but the 95% CI stretches from **0.20 to 23.42**. The data are compatible with:

- a large reduction in major bleeding;
- no meaningful difference;
- an extremely large increase in major bleeding.

The entire estimate rests on **three major bleeding events**.

The absolute effect interval spans **14 fewer to 380 more major bleeds per 1,000**, illustrating the same instability in clinically interpretable units.

### Pass 5 appraisal

The direction of the point estimate should receive **very little interpretive weight**. Describing the result simply as an increase in major bleeding would be misleading without immediately presenting the event counts and uncertainty.

This comparison provides **insufficient evidence to determine whether major bleeding differs between dalteparin and tinzaparin**.

# 9. Four-outcome reconciliation

The central finding of this pass is not that the four point estimates point in different directions. It is that **none of the four outcomes is estimated with adequate precision to support a confident comparative claim**.

| Outcome | Direction of point estimate | What the interval allows | Interpretive stability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mortality | Favors dalteparin | Important benefit through important harm | Low |
| Recurrent VTE | Favors dalteparin | Very large benefit through >2× risk | Very low |
| Major bleeding | Favors tinzaparin | Large benefit for dalteparin through extreme harm | Extremely low |
| Minor bleeding | Favors dalteparin | Meaningful benefit through meaningful harm | Very low |

### Cross-outcome pattern

A superficial direction-only reading could suggest:

- fewer deaths with dalteparin;
- fewer recurrent VTE events with dalteparin;
- more major bleeding with dalteparin;
- fewer minor bleeds with dalteparin.

That framing is **not sufficiently faithful to the uncertainty**.

A more appropriate synthesis is:

> In the 113-person cancer subgroup of Wells 2005, all four 3-month comparative estimates were low-certainty and highly imprecise. The confidence intervals for mortality, recurrent VTE, major bleeding, and minor bleeding all crossed the null and permitted clinically important effects in opposing directions. The major-bleeding estimate was particularly unstable, being based on only three events.

# 12. Heterogeneity, publication bias, and sensitivity analysis

## Heterogeneity

For Analyses 3.1–3.4, heterogeneity is reported as **not applicable** because each analysis contains only one RCT.

This is not evidence of homogeneity. It means there are no independent studies across which statistical heterogeneity can be estimated.

## Publication bias

The review did not create funnel plots because none of its analyses included at least 10 studies. This comparison contains only one study, so publication-bias assessment by funnel plot is impossible.

## Missing-outcome sensitivity analysis

The review states that missing-data sensitivity analyses were planned when a primary outcome estimate was statistically significant. None of the comparison estimates was statistically significant, so those sensitivity analyses were not performed.

# 13. Applicability and indirectness

## 13.1 Cancer subgroup rather than cancer-specific RCT

The 113 participants with cancer represent a subgroup of the larger Wells trial.

The Cochrane review states globally that none of the 15 included studies specified the cancer types of participants. This prevents assessment of:

- tumor-specific treatment effect;
- cancer-stage-specific treatment effect;
- interaction with differing cancer therapies;
- whether the subgroup resembles a particular contemporary cancer-associated thrombosis population.

## 13.2 VTE phenotype

The characteristics table describes the cancer subgroup as having **upper- or lower-extremity DVT**. The source does not provide enough cancer-subgroup detail in this section to determine how findings differ across VTE phenotypes beyond that description.

## 13.3 Treatment context

Both LMWHs were administered once daily on an outpatient basis, and warfarin was begun within 24 hours and continued for three months.

Therefore, this comparison evaluates **initial LMWH selection within a treatment pathway that transitions to warfarin**, not a contemporary head-to-head comparison of prolonged LMWH monotherapy or modern full-course cancer-associated thrombosis strategies.

## 13.4 Safety definitions

The Cochrane review's general methods state that it accepted study-author definitions of major and minor bleeding. The inspected Wells 2005 characteristics material does not reproduce a detailed bleeding definition.

Accordingly, this pass can preserve the reported bleeding event counts but cannot independently appraise the exact clinical threshold used to distinguish major from minor bleeding from the review text alone.

## Pass 5 appraisal

The source's use of **"may"** appropriately signals uncertainty, but the directional phrases can still attract more attention than the intervals deserve.

The more important interpretation is:

- mortality: 25 events, CI permits benefit or harm;
- recurrent VTE: 7 events, CI permits very large benefit or >2-fold harm;
- major bleeding: 3 events, CI 0.20–23.42;
- minor bleeding: 14 events, CI permits meaningful benefit or harm.

The evidence therefore supports **uncertainty about comparative effects**, rather than a stable efficacy or safety profile favoring either LMWH.

# 16. What can and cannot be concluded

## Source-supported findings

This review supports that, in the Wells 2005 cancer subgroup:

- dalteparin and tinzaparin were compared in 113 participants;
- all four assessed outcomes were followed to 3 months;
- the forest-plot point estimates were 0.86 for mortality, 0.44 for recurrent VTE, 2.19 for major bleeding, and 0.82 for minor bleeding;
- every 95% CI crossed 1.0;
- all four outcomes were rated low certainty in Summary of Findings 3;
- very serious imprecision drove the two-level downgrade for each outcome;
- Wells 2005 was judged low risk of bias across the Cochrane domains;
- quality of life, postphlebitic syndrome, and thrombocytopenia were not reported.

## Reasonable Pass 5 appraisal

The comparison is **underpowered for reliable comparative efficacy or safety conclusions**.

The major-bleeding estimate is particularly non-informative because it is based on three events and has a 95% CI spanning more than two orders of magnitude on the RR scale.

The favorable study-level risk-of-bias assessment cannot compensate for sparse events and extreme statistical imprecision.

## Not supported by this source alone

This comparison does **not** establish:

- superiority of dalteparin over tinzaparin;
- superiority of tinzaparin over dalteparin;
- equivalence or non-inferiority;
- a reliable mortality benefit;
- a reliable recurrent-VTE benefit;
- a reliable major-bleeding difference;
- a reliable minor-bleeding difference;
- net clinical benefit favoring either LMWH;
- differential effects by cancer type or stage;
- generalization to a modern full-course anticoagulation strategy;
- current 2026 practice preference between dalteparin and tinzaparin.

# 19. Pass 5 synthesis for final SEA

The dalteparin-versus-tinzaparin comparison is based entirely on the **113-person cancer subgroup of Wells 2005**, with 54 participants receiving dalteparin and 59 receiving tinzaparin. The Cochrane review judged the study at low risk of bias across its assessed domains, but the outcome evidence is sparse: 25 deaths, 7 recurrent-VTE events, 3 major bleeds, and 14 minor bleeds. All four 95% confidence intervals cross the null and permit clinically important effects in opposing directions.

The numerical directions—RR 0.86 for mortality, 0.44 for recurrent VTE, 2.19 for major bleeding, and 0.82 for minor bleeding—should therefore not be interpreted as a coherent benefit/harm pattern. Summary of Findings 3 appropriately downgrades every outcome two levels for **very serious imprecision** and classifies each as **low certainty**. The comparison does not provide a reliable basis for preferring dalteparin or tinzaparin in people with cancer. That conclusion is a Pass 5 appraisal of the review evidence, not a current-practice recommendation.
