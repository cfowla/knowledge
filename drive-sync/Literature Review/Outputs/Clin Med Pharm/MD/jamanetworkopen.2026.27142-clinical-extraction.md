# Abandonment of Prescribed Buprenorphine for Opioid Use Disorder, 2020-2024 — Clinical Extraction

**Citation:** Jiang X, Zhang K, Chen Y, Shih Y, Nataraj N, Guy GP Jr. *Abandonment of Prescribed Buprenorphine for Opioid Use Disorder, 2020-2024.* JAMA Network Open. 2026;9(8):e2627142. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.27142. Published August 4, 2026.

## Key findings

- The retrospective linked EHR-claims cohort included 1,428 adults with a first buprenorphine prescription for OUD from January 1, 2020 through September 25, 2024. Prescription abandonment was defined as no dispensing/filling record within 30 days.
- Overall, 369 patients (25.8%) had an abandoned first prescription; 1,059 (74.2%) had it dispensed within 30 days.
- Abandonment increased from 19% in 2020 to 37% in 2024 (P for trend=.02). The 2024 estimate therefore represented more than 1 in 3 first prescriptions.
- In unadjusted comparisons, patients with abandoned prescriptions were younger (54.3 vs 57.4 years; P=.001), more often Hispanic (10.6% vs 4.8%; P=.003), more often commercially insured (51.8% vs 29.4%; P<.001), and had higher mean pharmacy deductibles ($483.80 vs $296.40; P=.006).
- Prior OUD/overdose diagnoses (40.1% vs 54.4%; P<.001), other substance use disorder diagnoses (15.5% vs 21.0%; P=.02), and mental health diagnoses (38.5% vs 52.5%; P<.001) were less frequent in the abandonment group. Sex, race, and tier-2 retail pharmacy copay did not differ significantly.
- The article does **not** report a multivariable model of independent predictors. These group differences should therefore be treated as bivariate associations, not adjusted causal or predictive effects.

## Implementation implications

The study identifies the prescription-to-dispensing transition as a substantial and worsening access failure point. For health systems, useful operational targets include measuring first-prescription fill completion, distinguishing patient non-fill from payer/pharmacy rejection when possible, and reducing the handoff burden through direct dispensing, stronger linkage to care, and reliable pharmacy availability. Commercial insurance status and pharmacy deductible levels are signals worth examining locally, but the study cannot establish that changing either feature would by itself reduce abandonment.

The authors hypothesize that language and pharmacy-access barriers may contribute to the higher abandonment proportion among Hispanic patients. That mechanism was not directly measured in this cohort and should not be represented as a demonstrated causal explanation.

## Limitations

Generalizability is limited by the commercial and Medicare Advantage population represented in the linked data. The outcome combines several different failure mechanisms because the data cannot distinguish patient non-fill from insurer or pharmacy rejection. Cash purchases, assistance programs, or other insurance not captured in the linked claims could also cause outcome misclassification. In addition, the characteristic analysis is descriptive/bivariate rather than adjusted, so residual confounding is substantial and the study does not provide an individual-level prediction model. The 2024 observation period ends September 25, and supplemental methods/data-sharing materials referenced by the article were not available through the retrieved route.
