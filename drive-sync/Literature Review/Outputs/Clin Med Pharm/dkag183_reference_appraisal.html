One-page synthesis

Core thesis: In high-risk onco-haematological febrile neutropenia, standard continuous-infusion beta-lactam regimens frequently achieved an aggressive PK/PD target during targeted therapy, but not reliably during empirical therapy. Augmented renal clearance was the dominant non-attainment signal, and piperacillin/tazobactam and ceftazidime/avibactam raised concern for beta-lactamase inhibitor underexposure.

Most reusable result

Aggressive PK/PD target attainment was 85.4% in targeted treatment versus 57.5% in empirical treatment. Overall target attainment was 169/256 patients (66.0%).

Main clinical signal

ARC strongly predicted target non-attainment (OR 12.288, 95% CI 3.314-45.569, P < 0.001); male sex and age <65 years also independently predicted non-attainment.

Practice implication

Do not assume continuous infusion alone solves early beta-lactam exposure in febrile neutropenia, especially for empiric breakpoint-based therapy, ARC, and BL/BLI combinations where inhibitor exposure may be the weak link.

Major interpretive boundary: Clinical cure was common (71.1%) but target attainment was not associated with improved clinical outcome in univariate analyses. The study is strongest as exposure/target-attainment evidence, not as proof that aggressive targets improve patient-centered outcomes in this population.

Clinical question / PICO

PICO

Population: adult onco-haematological patients with febrile neutropenia treated with continuous-infusion beta-lactams.

Intervention/exposure: standard continuous-infusion beta-lactam regimens with loading doses, maintenance dosing adjusted by eGFR, and one TDM sample between days 3 and 7.

Comparator: target attainment versus non-attainment; targeted versus empirical treatment; drug-specific exposure strata.

Outcomes: aggressive PK/PD target attainment, predictors of non-attainment, and clinical cure at end of treatment.

Target definition

The aggressive target was free steady-state concentration divided by MIC or EUCAST clinical breakpoint at least 4. For BL/BLI combinations, joint target attainment also required adequate beta-lactamase inhibitor exposure: tazobactam or avibactam free concentration above the fixed target concentration, or vaborbactam fAUC/MIC >24.

The study measured total plasma concentrations and estimated free concentrations using literature protein-binding assumptions, which is operationally practical but adds uncertainty in unstable, hypoalbuminemic, or highly variable patients.

Section condensation

Abstract

The authors frame febrile neutropenia in onco-haematological patients as a high-risk infection state where MDR Gram-negative pathogens and altered pharmacokinetics make beta-lactam exposure important. They test whether continuous infusion achieves an aggressive PK/PD target during the first week of empirical or targeted therapy.

Among 256 included patients, target attainment was much higher for targeted treatment than empirical treatment (85.4% versus 57.5%). ARC, male sex, and age <65 years predicted non-attainment; targeted therapy and meropenem use were associated with higher attainment.

Introduction

The introduction links febrile neutropenia management to early empirical antipseudomonal beta-lactams and increasing MDR/CR Gram-negative risk in onco-haematology. It positions newer BL/BLI combinations as important for resistant Enterobacterales and MDR Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

The rationale for aggressive targets comes largely from critically ill Gram-negative infection literature suggesting higher beta-lactam exposure may improve cure and reduce resistance emergence compared with conventional targets. The paper asks whether continuous infusion standard dosing can achieve these targets in febrile neutropenia, where direct evidence is limited.

Methods - study design and dosing

This was a prospective, single-center observational study from January to December 2024. Patients received empirical or targeted beta-lactam therapy by continuous infusion according to clinical need, with loading doses followed by eGFR-adjusted maintenance dosing. Agents included piperacillin/tazobactam, ceftazidime, meropenem, ceftazidime/avibactam, ceftolozane/tazobactam, meropenem/vaborbactam, and cefiderocol.

TDM was performed once between days 3 and 7, depending on feasibility. Concentrations were measured with validated LC-MS/HPLC methods; total concentrations were measured and free fractions estimated from assumed protein binding. ARC was defined by eGFR >120 mL/min/1.73 m2 in females or >130 mL/min/1.73 m2 in males.

Methods - outcomes and statistics

The primary outcome was aggressive PK/PD target attainment by drug and treatment context. The secondary outcome was clinical response at end of treatment, defined as defervescence for at least 24 hours, microbiological eradication when applicable, resolution of infection signs/symptoms, and no need for escalation.

The authors used descriptive statistics, Fisher exact or chi-square tests for categorical comparisons, and multivariate binary logistic regression after selecting covariates with univariate P ≤0.20. This supports predictor discovery, but not causal inference or dosing-policy proof.

Results - cohort

Of 357 prospectively enrolled patients, 101 were excluded because TDM was missing or outside the pre-defined window, leaving 256 patients. Median age was 58 years; 63.3% were male; 46.9% underwent HSCT; 76.9% had severe neutropenia at baseline; 37.1% had MDR rectal colonization at admission.

Most patients received piperacillin/tazobactam or meropenem (87.1%). Empirical therapy accounted for 174 patients (67.9%), targeted therapy for 82 (32.1%), and clinical cure occurred in 182/256 (71.1%).

Results - PK/PD attainment

Targeted therapy had high overall attainment: 70/82 (85.4%). Non-attainment in targeted therapy was concentrated in piperacillin/tazobactam (17/27 attained, 62.9%) and ceftazidime/avibactam (8/10 attained, 80.0%), with non-attainment attributed to insufficient BLI concentrations.

Empirical therapy had weaker target attainment: 100/174 (57.5%). Piperacillin/tazobactam reached target in 49/92 (53.3%), meropenem in 49/70 (70.0%), ceftazidime/avibactam in 1/7 (14.3%), and meropenem/vaborbactam in 0/3. The empirical target used clinical breakpoints, making this a conservative exposure stress test but not necessarily the patient-specific MIC target.

Results - predictors and clinical outcome

Multivariable predictors of target non-attainment were ARC (OR 12.288, 95% CI 3.314-45.569, P <0.001), male sex (OR 3.792, 95% CI 1.926-7.464, P <0.001), and age <65 years (OR 2.402, 95% CI 1.118-5.161, P = 0.025). Targeted therapy and meropenem were associated with lower odds of non-attainment.

Target attainment was not significantly associated with clinical outcome in univariate analysis: empirical treatment cure was 73.4% with attainment versus 71.7% without attainment (P = 0.847); targeted treatment cure was 67.5% with attainment versus 60.0% without attainment (P = 1.000).

Discussion and limitations

The authors interpret continuous infusion standard dosing as generally adequate for targeted therapy but unreliable for empirical therapy and for some BL/BLI combinations. They highlight ARC, younger male patients, and BLI underexposure as practical reasons to use TDM and possibly intensified dosing.

Stated limitations include heterogeneity, absence of clear clinical-outcome association, breakpoint-based empirical targets, single TDM sample, selection bias from sampling-window exclusions, eGFR-estimated rather than measured renal clearance, total rather than directly free concentrations, and no follow-up assessment of MDR rectal colonization/resistance emergence.

Workflow / algorithm - Dosing, TDM, and target classification

Inputs:
- Adult onco-haematological patient with febrile neutropenia.
- Empirical or targeted beta-lactam selection based on clinical need, colonization/infection history, and susceptibility where available.
- eGFR for renal dose adjustment; suspected infection site and concomitant anti-Gram-positive/antifungal therapy.

Process / decision rules:
1. Give loading dose, then continuous-infusion maintenance regimen adjusted by eGFR.
2. Between days 3 and 7, obtain one peripheral venous blood sample for steady-state concentration.
3. Measure total plasma concentration by validated LC-MS/HPLC method.
4. Estimate free concentration using literature protein-binding assumptions.
5. For targeted therapy, compare fCss to measured isolate MIC; for empirical therapy, compare fCss to EUCAST clinical breakpoint.
6. For BL/BLI combinations, require both beta-lactam and inhibitor target attainment.

Outputs:
- Aggressive PK/PD target attained or not attained.
- Drug-specific target attainment rates.
- Predictors of non-attainment from multivariable regression.

Failure modes:
- Single TDM may miss renal-function changes or time-varying clearance.
- Breakpoint-based empirical targets may over- or under-estimate true MIC-based needs.
- BLI exposure can fail even when beta-lactam exposure appears adequate.
- eGFR-estimated ARC may misclassify renal clearance.

Human review / governance:
- Requires stewardship/ID/pharmacy interpretation, local dosing policy, assay availability, and a process for dose adjustment after TDM.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. Best use is to inform beta-lactam TDM prioritization, continuous-infusion dosing discussions, and stewardship policy in high-risk hematology febrile neutropenia. Do not use it alone to claim clinical outcome benefit or to mandate empiric dosing changes across hospitals.

Implementation value

Rationale: Provides actionable flags for early TDM prioritization and BL/BLI exposure interpretation.

Evidence basis: Identifies ARC, younger male patients, empirical therapy, PTZ, and CAZ/AVI as practical risk areas.

Limiting factor: Does not provide a validated dose-adjustment algorithm or outcome-proven intervention pathway.

Main strengths

  • Prospective data collection in the exact population where empiric broad-spectrum beta-lactam exposure matters.
  • Direct TDM measurement with validated LC-MS/HPLC methods.
  • Separates empirical versus targeted therapy and reports drug-specific attainment.
  • Uses joint BL/BLI targets rather than only beta-lactam component exposure.

Main limitations

  • Observational, monocentric design with high exclusion before analytic cohort.
  • One TDM sample only; dynamic renal clearance not captured.
  • Small n for newer BL/BLI agents and cefiderocol.
  • No demonstrated clinical-outcome benefit, no follow-up MDR colonization/resistance endpoint.
  • Free concentrations estimated rather than measured.
Do not overclaim: The study supports “CI standard dosing may be insufficient for aggressive target attainment in empirical FN, especially with ARC and some BL/BLI regimens.” It does not prove that increasing doses improves survival, reduces resistance, or should replace guideline-directed empiric selection.

Implementation / practice takeaways

For antimicrobial stewardship

Consider making early beta-lactam TDM a priority for onco-haematology FN patients with ARC/high eGFR, younger male patients, prolonged expected neutropenia, MDR colonization, or empirical BL/BLI use.

For empiric therapy

Continuous infusion should not be treated as automatically target-protective. In this cohort, empirical therapy achieved the aggressive breakpoint-based target in only 57.5% of patients.

For piperacillin/tazobactam

PTZ was the most common agent and had frequent non-attainment in both targeted and empirical contexts. The weak link was often tazobactam exposure, not just piperacillin exposure.

For ceftazidime/avibactam

CAZ/AVI had concerning empirical non-attainment in a small sample, driven by avibactam exposure. Use the finding as a TDM/dosing concern, not as definitive comparative effectiveness evidence.

For meropenem

Meropenem had better target attainment than PTZ under study conditions, but broader stewardship concerns remain: carbapenem exposure can select resistance and may affect gut microbiome-related outcomes in transplant patients.

For local policy

The immediately useful output is a risk-triggered TDM workflow: identify ARC/high clearance early, sample within the first week or sooner if feasible, interpret BL/BLI components jointly, and document dose-adjustment decisions.

Bottom line: Use this paper as an exposure-risk and TDM-prioritization reference for hematology febrile neutropenia. It is not an outcome-confirming trial, but it gives a practical reason to distrust “standard dose + continuous infusion” when renal clearance is high or when empirical breakpoint targets are being used.
