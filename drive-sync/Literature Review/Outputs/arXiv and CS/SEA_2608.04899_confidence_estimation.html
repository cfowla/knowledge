Core thesis

The authors argue that verbalized LLM confidence is not merely miscalibrated; it is often sparse, with predictions collapsing onto a small set of preferred numeric values. Sparse scores produce only a few attainable operating points, limiting threshold selection and creating an evaluation hazard when area-under-curve metrics interpolate between points as if intermediate performance were attainable.

The paper recommends stepwise rather than linear interpolation for AUARC when confidence scores are tied or sparse. It then proposes verbalization logprobs: replace a sampled two-digit confidence number with the expected numeric value implied by the token-probability distribution at each digit position. This greatly increases score resolution and approximately matches a four-sample verbalization baseline at much lower token cost.

What changes after a fairer evaluation

With linear AUARC, consistency sampling appears strongest for Qwen3-32B (0.808). With stepwise AUARC, it falls to 0.683 and last place; verbalization sampling becomes strongest among the original baselines at 0.734. Token logprobs remain unchanged because their scores are continuously distributed. The size of the rank reversal supports the paper's central warning that interpolation is not an innocuous implementation detail.

What the paper adds

  • A concrete sparsity diagnostic: concentration in the five most common scores (%t5) and the number of unique confidence values.
  • An operational interpretation of sparse thresholds: on SST-2, moving from 97.9% to at least 98% accuracy with vanilla verbalization requires rejection to jump from 23.6% to 39.7%.
  • A low-call-count alternative to repeated sampling: verbalization logprobs yields 0.736 AUARC at 1.7× token cost versus 0.734 at 6.8× for verbalization sampling.
Main caveat. “Stepwise is correct” is strongest under a deterministic threshold policy that cannot split examples with identical confidence. Linear interpolation can be interpreted as randomized or arbitrary tie breaking. The paper exposes the problem but does not fully frame interpolation as an explicit deployment-policy choice or provide sensitivity analyses across tie policies.

4. Section-by-section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames confidence estimation as necessary for deciding when LLM classifications can be trusted. It reports extreme verbalization sparsity, a large dependence of AUARC rankings on interpolation, and a proposed logprob-weighted verbalization method that improves AUARC without additional inference calls relative to vanilla verbalization.

The abstract's headline results are supported in the main tables: Qwen3-32B uses eight unique scores on SST-2; consistency sampling reverses from best to worst across interpolation methods; and verbalization logprobs gains 2.3 AUARC points over vanilla verbalization.

1. Introduction

The authors locate the problem in selective classification, where confidence thresholds determine which predictions are retained. Prompted LLMs lack the direct class-probability interface of conventional classifiers, so practitioners use self-reported confidence, repeated sampling, or token logprobs.

The introduction separates sparsity from calibration: even a confidence method with acceptable aggregate calibration may be operationally poor if it produces too few distinct thresholds. The paper's three contributions are documenting sparsity, showing evaluation-rank instability, and proposing a continuous logprob-weighted score.

2. Background: confidence estimation for LLM classifiers

The paper groups methods into verbalization-based, sampling-based, and logit-based approaches. Vanilla verbalization directly asks for confidence; top-2 verbalization asks for two guesses and associated probabilities; consistency sampling uses label frequency over four samples; verbalization sampling averages four self-reported confidences; and token logprobs uses the predicted label token probability.

The study excludes deeper white-box methods and semantic-uncertainty approaches designed for open-ended generation. The comparison therefore targets methods that are broadly understandable and usable in classification settings, though actual API support differs.

3. The sparsity limitation

Across SST-2, SST-5, Amazon ESCI, and Yahoo! Answers, verbalization-based estimates cluster heavily: 45–93% of values fall in the five most common scores for Qwen3-32B. The pattern also appears with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, with reasoning enabled, and when the verbalized range is changed from 0–100 to 0–9.

The authors argue that AUARC should use stepwise interpolation because sparse scores permit only discrete threshold outcomes. Linear interpolation fills in unattainable intermediate operating points, disproportionately benefiting sparse methods and changing rankings.

4. Verbalization logprobs

The proposed method computes an expected two-digit confidence from token probabilities at the tens and units positions. The units distribution is evaluated under the generated prefix rather than conditioned on every alternative tens digit, an approximation the authors justify on API and computational grounds.

On Qwen3-32B, the method reduces %t5 from 92 to 1, improves AUROC, ECE, and AUARC relative to vanilla verbalization, and increases unique values by 80–200×. It achieves similar AUARC to four-sample verbalization sampling while requiring one generation rather than four.

5. Conclusion and limitations

The paper concludes that sparsity is a practical and evaluative problem, advocates stepwise AUARC, and positions verbalization logprobs as a simple improvement when token probabilities are available.

The authors acknowledge tokenizer dependence, a fixed four-sample comparison, the need to study other threshold metrics, and the absence of a sparsity remedy for APIs without logprobs. Additional limitations apparent from the study include the small model set, narrow task family, lack of uncertainty intervals, and limited reproducibility reporting.

Appendices A–G

The appendices specify the four datasets, reproduce the interpolation effect on Claude, define token-count cost multipliers, show sparsity persists with reasoning, replicate the phenomenon with a 0–9 range, describe prompts and sampling settings, and document how prior AUARC implementations often approximate linear interpolation.

These appendices materially strengthen the paper because they demonstrate that the core artifact is not limited to one model, one prompt range, or one inference setting. They do not, however, add statistical uncertainty, prompt-sensitivity tests, or broader model families.

Proposed calculation

Let xᵢ be the tens digit token and xᵢ₊₁ the units digit token.

confidence = Σ(d=0..9) 10·d·P(xᵢ=d) + Σ(d=0..9) d·P(xᵢ₊₁=d)

Approximation: the units-position distribution is taken under the generated prefix,
not recomputed conditional on every possible tens digit.

Prompt and evaluation workflow (Table 11 + Appendix F)

1. Present a class-label set and an input example.
2. Baseline/logprobs/consistency prompt: return label only, no explanation.
3. Vanilla prompt: return label plus one numeric confidence in 0–100.
4. Top-2 prompt: return two labels and a probability for each.
5. Sampling variants: repeat four times at temperature 1.0.
6. Convert outputs to confidence scores.
7. Sort / threshold by confidence and compute accuracy-rejection points.
8. Integrate AUARC using stepwise or linear interpolation.
9. Report AUROC, ECE, AUARC, %t5, and relative token count.

Metric implementation issue

For sparse or tied scores:
- Group examples by distinct confidence threshold.
- Do not invent intermediate deterministic operating points inside a tie group.
- Compute the area using an explicitly stated step convention.
- If randomized tie-breaking is operationally allowed, report that policy separately.
- Always report score resolution (unique values and/or concentration), not AUARC alone.

Major strengths

  • Separates calibration from resolution. This is conceptually useful: a confidence system can be numerically plausible yet operationally unusable.
  • Finds a metric implementation failure with practical consequences. The rank reversal is large enough to alter research conclusions.
  • Uses operational examples. The rejection jump makes the threshold limitation concrete.
  • Includes robustness checks. Claude, reasoning mode, and the 0–9 range all preserve the core sparsity phenomenon.
  • Proposes a low-call-count remedy. The new method competes with repeated sampling at a fraction of token usage.

Critical limitations and unresolved questions

  • Tie-policy ambiguity. Stepwise integration matches deterministic thresholding by unique confidence values. Linear interpolation can approximate randomized rejection within tied groups. The paper should define the target policy before declaring one interpolation universally correct.
  • Endpoint convention. Curves are assigned accuracy 1.0 at rejection rate 1.0 by convention. Sensitivity to this endpoint is not reported and may matter for area metrics.
  • No uncertainty quantification. Mean metrics are reported without confidence intervals, resampling distributions, or significance tests.
  • Limited model and task breadth. Two models and four English classification datasets are insufficient to establish general behavior across tokenizers, model scales, languages, or open-ended outputs.
  • Proposed method evaluated incompletely. Verbalization logprobs appears in the main Qwen non-reasoning analysis but not the Claude or reasoning tables.
  • Prompt robustness is untested. The study does not vary wording, output format, demonstrations, temperature for deterministic methods, or confidence elicitation rubrics.
  • Cost is a token proxy. Relative token count does not capture latency, batchability, API pricing, logprob retrieval overhead, or reasoning-token billing.
  • Calibration is not solved. Better AUARC and higher resolution do not imply truthful probabilities or reliable absolute risk estimates.
Confidence in appraisal: moderate-high. The paper is internally coherent and the main numerical claims are directly visible in the supplied tables and figures. Confidence is capped because the source is a preprint without reported statistical uncertainty or a linked reproducibility artifact.

9. Implementation takeaways

Change evaluation code nowCompute AUARC over distinct confidence thresholds, preserve ties, and state the step convention. Do not silently call a generic trapezoidal auc() function on sparse threshold points.
Report score resolutionAdd number of unique values, top-k concentration, tie-group sizes, and attainable coverage levels to every confidence-estimation benchmark.
Declare the abstention policyState whether rejection can split identical-confidence examples, randomize within ties, or only act on global thresholds. Metric interpolation should match that policy.
Use verbalization logprobs selectivelyAdopt only when the runtime exposes digit-token probability distributions and tokenization is stable. Validate parsing and the digit vocabulary as a contract.
Do not equate AUARC with calibrationEvaluate ranking/selection, calibration, and score resolution separately. The paper's method performs differently across AUARC, AUROC, and ECE.
Add robustness testsFor production evaluation, vary prompt wording, score range, model, tokenizer, and tie policy; bootstrap metric differences and publish seed/configuration details.

Minimal implementation checklist

[ ] Parse confidence outputs with strict validation.
[ ] Count unique scores and tie-group sizes before computing AUARC.
[ ] Build operating points only at distinct thresholds.
[ ] Use and document deterministic stepwise integration.
[ ] Optionally report randomized-tie sensitivity as a separate analysis.
[ ] Report AUARC + AUROC + calibration + score-resolution metrics.
[ ] Record prompt, model version, tokenizer, temperature, and sampling count.
[ ] For verbalization logprobs, verify digit-token coverage and fallback behavior.
[ ] Bootstrap differences across examples or datasets.
[ ] Treat confidence as decision support, not an unvalidated probability of correctness.
