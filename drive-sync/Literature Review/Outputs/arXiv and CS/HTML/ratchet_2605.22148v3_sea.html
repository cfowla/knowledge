One-page synthesis

Core thesis. A self-evolving skill library needs explicit lifecycle maintenance, but a threshold-based retirement rule is only as reliable as the scalar reward it reads. The authors propose Ratchet: a fixed-width skill shelf with evidence-gated contribution eviction plus a constrained authoring prior, then derive a directional judge-error boundary beyond which retirement becomes uninformative.

Source-derived The paper defines library drift as the condition where expected held-out pass@1 with the active library falls below the same frozen model’s no-skill control. Ratchet uses five frozen-model roles—Router, Solver, Critic, Synthesizer, Curator—over an append-only log. The Curator reads only a per-skill statistic and trial count, evicts at n(s) ≥ Nmin and ĉ(s) ≤ −τ, and maintains at most C active skills. Defaults are τ=0.10, Nmin=100, and C=50.

Source-derived On a hard 100-task MBPP+ slice, three 100-round runs with Claude Opus 4.7 rise from mean round-0 held-out pass@1 0.258 ± 0.047 to a mean peak of 0.658 ± 0.042; the within-run rolling gain is +0.328 ± 0.018 versus +0.002 ± 0.005 when skill injection is withheld. The strongest negative ablation is aggressive early eviction (Nmin=20, τ=0), which yields −0.019 ± 0.010, below the no-skill control.

Source-derived The judge model is a two-direction binary channel. With eviction pass-rate threshold πτ=(1−τ)/2, the paper’s central boundary is: if true failures are scored as passes at ρF→P ≥ πτ, no skill is asymptotically evictable; if true passes are scored as failures at ρP→F ≥ 1−πτ, the rule becomes indiscriminate. At τ=0.10, those edges are 0.45 and 0.55. An audited strict judge is estimated at roughly (ρF→P,ρP→F)=(0.01,0.95): far from the false-pass cliff but beyond the starvation edge.

Source-derived The corruption sweep supports the qualitative asymmetry: symmetric noise attenuates retirement but does not disable it; false-pass bias can drive genuine contribution-based retirements to zero. End-task score is a poor alarm: the worst held-out excursion on RPT/71 is only −0.065 at the theoretical false-pass boundary and then recovers at still-higher bias as synthesis itself starves.

Appraisal — load-bearing concern
The formal guarantee has an internal estimator/scale problem as written. Equation (2) defines ĉ(s)=2p̂(s)−1, a signed raw success rate, while Equation (3) defines contribution as c(s)=E[p(x|s)−p0(x)]. Those are not the same estimand unless an additional condition ties p0 to 0.5 or a paired no-skill measurement is incorporated. Yet Proposition 1 assumes ĉ is unbiased for Equation (3). Separately, the numerical Hoeffding examples mix the [−1,1] contribution scale and [0,1] pass-rate scale: the printed Equation (4) with τ=.10, ε≈.39, Cδ=.05 implies a 0.54 deficit, not the stated 0.30. These issues materially weaken confidence in the claimed formal floor, while leaving the empirical lifecycle results and the simpler threshold-channel boundary as distinct findings.

Section condensation

1. Introduction

The authors frame self-evolving skill libraries as open-loop systems that author, retrieve, and inject persistent procedural artifacts but rarely remove them based on measured utility. They introduce “library drift” as a measurable failure mode and argue that explicit maintenance—not better authorship alone—is the missing controller.

The paper’s proposed synthesis is that lifecycle governance and reward reliability are inseparable: a bounded controller can provide a floor only if its sensor is sufficiently informative. The introduction previews the false-pass cliff, the empirical MBPP+ gain, and a pre-deployment audit for directional judge error.

2. Library drift: when a skill library becomes harmful, and why it is rarely measured

Library drift is defined against a within-model no-skill control: the shelf is harmful when expected pass@1 with the active library is below expected pass@1 without skill injection. The proposed mechanism is retrieval dilution and persistence of superseded artifacts; adaptive abstention can mask deterioration in aggregate curves.

The literature survey positions prior systems as heavily invested in authorship but sparsely instrumented for contribution-based retirement under bounded width. Table 1 is a feature-presence comparison, not a performance benchmark, and the authors explicitly distinguish their contribution from concurrent score-based maintenance systems.

3. Ratchet: five roles, one log, three levers

One frozen model fills Router, Solver, Critic, Synthesizer, and Curator roles. The durable state is an append-only evidence log. The Router returns at most one skill or declines, preserving single-skill attribution; failures are critiqued and clustered into candidate skill patterns; the Curator makes deterministic lifecycle decisions from per-skill counts/statistics.

The three levers are: (1) evidence-gated eviction at n≥Nmin and ĉ≤−τ; (2) a fixed active width C with weakest-skill eviction on overflow; and (3) a meta-skill authoring prior that constrains new skills toward reusable schemas. Supporting mechanisms are canonicalization, a cover guard, and a rollback guard that fired only twice in 300 round-decisions.

4. Why a closed loop has a floor, and what buys the finiteness

Proposition 1 claims a no-skill-relative lower bound whose finite terms depend on finite eviction margin and shelf width, while Proposition 2 extends the analysis to a two-direction reward channel. The intuitive boundary in Section 4.1 is clean: the fixed eviction threshold can be translated out of the feasible true-pass domain by false-pass bias, whereas symmetric noise primarily contracts resolution.

Appraisal The core theoretical argument is not fully secure as written because the implemented statistic in Equation (2) does not estimate the no-skill-relative contribution defined in Equation (3), yet that equivalence is assumed in Proposition 1. The paper also mixes contribution-scale and pass-rate-scale Hoeffding radii in its numeric examples. These are not cosmetic notation issues because the theorem is the paper’s claimed formal basis for “non-divergence.”

5. Evidence under an honest reward

MBPP+/100 uses 60 train and 40 held-out tasks selected because the frozen base model fails at least one probe seed. The primary model is Claude Opus 4.7, with 100 rounds and seeds 42, 7, and 13. The reported rolling gain is the last-ten minus first-ten held-out pass@1 within each run. The clean reward is unit-test grading.

The default controller gains +0.328 ± 0.018; injection-withheld is essentially flat. Ablations identify injection, model-based routing, the authoring prior, and an evidence floor as load-bearing. Cross-model single-seed runs show positive gaps for Kimi, GLM-5, and DeepSeek, with weak/nonpositive gaps for Qwen3-Coder, Mistral Large 3, and GPT-5.5; the authors attribute these to capability/headroom and statistic effects. SWE-V/150 is presented as preliminary: no-skill mean 0.65, injection peak 0.87, but only 18–19 rounds.

6. The sensor specification, measured

The paper proposes an offline defect-injection audit for reference-free domains, pairing deterministic structural checks with a held-out LLM judge for semantic defects. On the authors’ audited strict judge, the union estimates false-pass error near 0.01 and phantom-failure error near 0.95, giving very low channel resolution κ≈0.04.

A corruption sweep on RPT/71 and three additional slices tests the predicted asymmetry. Genuine contribution-driven retirement is separated from width churn. False-pass bias disables retirement near/past the boundary; symmetric noise attenuates rather than kills it. The authors emphasize that the governable rectangle is necessary, not sufficient: a condition can lie inside it yet still have no currently evictable skills.

7. Scope, limitations, conclusion, broader impact

The authors generalize the threshold-channel result beyond skills to any artifact store retired by a scalar threshold. They explicitly limit the study to exogenous corruption, enumerable audit defect classes, routed-conditional association rather than causal contribution, three seeds for main experiments, single-seed cross-family probes, and no direct governance bake-off.

The operational warning is that lifecycle control can fail before end-task metrics clearly signal it. The paper therefore recommends auditing the sensor before enabling the controller and monitoring the direction of judge error rather than only aggregate accuracy.

Technical core

Operational definitions and rules

Library drift: E[pass@1 | Sₜ] < E[p₀] for some t > 0.
Implemented per-skill statistic: ĉ(s) = (#pass − #fail)/n = 2p̂(s) − 1.
Evict when: n(s) ≥ Nmin AND ĉ(s) ≤ −τ. Defaults: Nmin=100, τ=0.10.
Capacity: |S| ≤ C, default C=50; overflow evicts arg minₛ ĉ(s).
Judge channel: P(scored pass | true fail)=ρF→P; P(scored fail | true pass)=ρP→F; κ=1−ρF→P−ρP→F.
Eviction pass-rate threshold: πτ=(1−τ)/2. At τ=.10, πτ=.45.
Paper's certification edges: false-pass cliff ρF→P ≥ .45; starvation edge ρP→F ≥ .55.

Algorithm 1 — one governed round

ACT
1. For eval then train tasks: Router chooses one ACTIVE skill or declines.
2. Solver attempts the task; append graded capsule to the log.
3. For each train failure: Critic appends attribution + pattern.

GROW
4. Canonicalize and cluster recent failure patterns.
5. For uncovered clusters of size ≥3: Synthesizer writes a skill under the authoring prior.

GOVERN
6. If n(s) ≥ Nmin and ĉ(s) ≤ −τ: mark skill DEPRECATED.
7. While active width exceeds C: evict the skill with minimum ĉ(s).

GUARD
8. If held-out pass@1 is >τrb below the running best for 5 consecutive rounds: restore best shelf.
9. Return shelf and append-only log.
Interpretation boundary. The empirical controller thresholds raw per-skill pass behavior. The theorem, however, is framed in terms of contribution relative to a no-skill counterfactual. A deployment that needs a defensible causal or no-skill-relative “contribution” should consider paired/toggled evaluation or explicitly redefine the estimand rather than assuming Equation (2) supplies Equation (3).

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first for work on self-evolving agents, memory/skill lifecycle governance, evaluator reliability, or artifact-retirement systems. Do not accept the formal non-divergence guarantee at face value without resolving the estimator and scale inconsistencies.
Relevance
9/10

Directly addresses agent reliability, persistent memory governance, LLM-judge failure modes, and operational lifecycle controls.

Evidence basis: explicit maintenance architecture + theory + ablations + corruption tests. Limit: coding/report-generation task families dominate. Raise: broader artifact stores and deployment traces.

Novelty
8/10

The useful novelty is the coupling of artifact retirement with directional reward-channel analysis and an offline pre-deployment audit.

Evidence basis: false-pass cliff, starvation edge, and mechanism-level retirement metrics. Limit: bounded pruning and score-based maintenance exist in adjacent work. Raise: stronger comparative lifecycle bake-off.

Method strength
5/10

Empirical design is thoughtful, but the central formal section contains a load-bearing estimand mismatch and scale arithmetic inconsistency.

Evidence basis: controlled ablations, per-seed reporting, injected error channels, explicit limitations. Limit: Eq.2 vs Eq.3 mismatch; three seeds; single-seed cross-model probe. Raise: corrected theorem/estimator plus independent reproduction.

Evidence strength
6/10

The empirical claim that governance choices and judge-error direction matter is reasonably supported; the theorem-backed floor is not yet equally secure.

Evidence basis: +0.328 default gain, A4 failure, retirement-mechanism sweep. Limit: no external independent validation and formal inconsistency. Raise: corrected analysis, preregistered larger runs, independent benchmark replication.

External validity
5/10

Several model families and a preliminary SWE-bench setting help, but primary evidence comes from narrow slices and frozen-model loops.

Evidence basis: seven model families, RPT slices, SWE-V/150. Limit: single-seed family probes, exogenous noise, enumerable defect audit only. Raise: real long-running libraries with endogenous judge drift and diverse domains.

Implementation value
9/10

The operational lessons—capacity caps, evidence floors, sensor audits, mechanism metrics, append-only provenance—are concrete and transferable.

Evidence basis: explicit roles, thresholds, defaults, prompts/configuration appendix. Limit: “contribution” statistic needs clarification before direct reuse. Raise: paired-control estimator and production monitoring recipe.

Main strengths

  • Defines a testable no-skill control for library drift rather than only naming retrieval dilution.
  • Separates contribution-driven retirement from capacity churn, which is essential for diagnosing a disabled controller.
  • Tests directional reward error rather than collapsing judge quality into one accuracy number.
  • Reports negative ablations, per-seed values, and rollback firings rather than only headline peaks.
  • Provides a pre-deployment audit concept using complementary deterministic and LLM-based defect detection.

Main limitations

  • Formal estimand mismatch: 2p̂−1 is not generally an estimator of p(s)−p0.
  • Scale inconsistency: Hoeffding radii and the stated default downside example mix [−1,1] and [0,1] conventions.
  • Three seeds are insufficient for formal hypothesis testing; authors appropriately avoid some small-difference claims.
  • Cross-model evidence is single-seed and task selection is conditioned on Opus failures.
  • Corruption is exogenous; the harder case where the evolving library learns to exploit a judge’s blind spots is excluded.
  • The defect audit certifies only named/enumerable failure classes.
  • The per-skill statistic is routed-conditional and associational; Router selection creates survivorship/selection confounding.
  • No head-to-head governance bake-off on a shared log.

What the results support

Supported with moderate confidence: lifecycle maintenance can materially change a persistent skill library; overly aggressive early eviction can be harmful; bounded capacity and evidence gating are operationally sensible; false-pass and phantom-failure judge errors should be tracked separately; end-task score can miss controller failure.

What the results do not yet establish

Not established: the exact no-skill-relative floor in Proposition 1/2 for the implemented statistic; that the reported judge-error rectangle is sufficient for robust production governance; that Ratchet outperforms other maintenance systems head-to-head; or that the observed gains generalize to long-running, endogenous, real-world agent ecosystems.

Implementation / research takeaways

1. Keep a real controlPeriodically compare the same model with skill injection disabled. A lifecycle metric needs a reference state, not only an absolute task score.
2. Separate retirement mechanismsLog contribution-based retirement, capacity eviction, synthesis count, and abstention separately; total deprecations can hide a dead sensor-driven curator.
3. Use an evidence floorDo not threshold very young artifacts. A4 is a concrete warning that premature retirement can make governance worse than no governance.
4. Bound active widthA fixed capacity is useful independently of the contribution estimator because it limits retrieval dilution and prevents unbounded active-state growth.
5. Audit error directionFor LLM judges, estimate failure→pass and pass→failure separately against enumerated defect classes. Aggregate agreement can conceal the failure mode that disables retirement.
6. Fix the contribution estimandIf the goal is “contribution relative to no skill,” measure paired/toggled outcomes or otherwise define a baseline-aware estimator. Do not relabel raw pass margin as no-skill-relative contribution.
7. Watch for endogenous driftA one-time judge audit is weakest when artifacts can adapt to the judge. Re-audit along the trajectory or use held-out/rotating evaluators if optimization pressure can exploit blind spots.
8. Treat the paper as build-useful, not theorem-finalThe architecture and failure-mode decomposition are immediately useful; the mathematical guarantee should be re-derived before being used as a safety certificate.
