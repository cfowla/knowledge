One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Open-ended data-analysis agents can reach the right reject/fail-to-reject conclusion while reporting a p-value inconsistent with the canonical analysis. P-Bench is designed to expose that gap, and Fisher-R1 uses executable synthetic training plus outcome-grounded RL to reduce it.

What the paper contributes. P-Bench contains 425 real-data tasks (203 Easy, 222 Hard) spanning economics, biology, and medicine. Each task supplies a scientific question, data description, and CSV but withholds the statistical method. The agent explores data in R, selects and executes a method, and reports a p-value and hypothesis decision. Keys are produced from executed canonical analyses and expert-audited. Fisher-R1 is initialized by SFT on filtered teacher trajectories, then trained with DAPO using a reward dominated by z-space p-value closeness (weight 0.9) plus conclusion correctness (0.1).

Headline evidence. Fisher-R1-14B reaches P-Hard Strict pass@1 33.0±1.7 versus 30.5±1.6 for GPT-5.4 and 26.3±3.0 for DeepSeek V4 Pro. On P-Easy Strict pass@1 it scores 64.2±0.6, narrowly below GPT-5.4 at 64.7±3.3; it leads GPT-5.4 on the other three Strict metrics. The stronger claim is not that Fisher-R1 solves hypothesis testing, but that targeted training materially improves p-value-grounded execution while substantial residual failure remains, especially on P-Hard.

Section condensation

Abstract & 1. Introduction

The paper argues that executable code is not sufficient for trustworthy hypothesis testing: agents can notice assumption violations but still choose methods that yield misleading p-values. Figure 1 uses a tumor-purity example in which GPT-5.4 proceeds with linear regression despite outliers while Fisher-R1 chooses Spearman correlation.

The proposed gap is evaluation of inferential validity rather than mere workflow execution. P-Bench and Fisher-R1 are presented as the benchmark/training pair intended to make method-driven statistical errors machine-checkable and trainable.

2. Problem Formulation

A task input is x=(q,D,s): scientific question, dataset, and data description. The model interacts with a fixed R environment in a multi-turn loop; actions can contain code, intermediate decisions, or a final answer, and observations return diagnostics and statistical outputs. The final trajectory is parsed for p-value and reject/fail-to-reject decision.

Related-work positioning distinguishes P-Bench from data-analysis benchmarks, multiple-choice method selection, claim verification, and autonomous research agents. The targeted object is upstream inferential choice on executable data rather than text entailment or code correctness alone.

3. P-Bench

P-Bench derives tasks from economics replication packages, biology data, and biostatistics teaching materials. A three-stage pipeline reproduces canonical analyses, filters failures, grounds retained claims in execution logs, and packages self-contained tasks. Expert reviewers audit final task/request/key alignment. The paper also applies controlled perturbations to reflect outliers, heteroskedasticity, clustering, missingness, and other practical irregularities.

The 425 tasks split into 203 Easy and 222 Hard. Hard status is deterministic from method family or adversarial perturbation. The benchmark covers 17 method categories; no category exceeds 19%. Domains include economics, biology, and medicine, and designs include both randomized and observational studies.

4. Fisher-R1 Training

The training corpus contains 8,642 executable synthetic tasks generated across six axes: method, domain scenario, sample size, effect size, prompt style, and seed. SFT uses Claude-Sonnet-4.6 teacher trajectories following a five-step analysis workflow; 3,851/4,611 trajectories (≈83.5%) survive automatic quality control.

RL uses DAPO with asymmetric clipping (0.20, 0.28) and dynamic sampling. Reward is format-gated and combines p-value closeness and conclusion correctness with weights 0.9/0.1. P-value closeness is computed in capped two-sided z-space. There is no explicit reward for matching a method label; method adequacy is inferred through agreement of the resulting p-value with the canonical analysis.

5. Experiment

Models are evaluated on P-Easy and P-Hard with three independent rollouts per task. Raw measures conclusion correctness at α=0.05. Strict additionally requires |z(p̂)-z(p*)|<0.5. pass@1 averages per-trial success; pass@3 counts a task solved if any rollout succeeds.

Fisher-R1 strongly improves over Qwen-2.5-Coder backbones and leads the open-source models on all Strict metrics. The 14B model beats GPT-5.4 on three of four Strict metrics. SFT+DAPO is clearly stronger than either stage alone. An embedding-neighbor analysis shows eval-to-train prompt similarity below a high train-to-train band, supporting—but not proving—generalization rather than prompt memorization.

6. Discussion

The authors interpret P-Bench as evidence that frontier agents remain unreliable for open-ended hypothesis testing and that verified outcome-grounded training can improve performance. They emphasize oversight/evaluation use rather than replacement of statisticians.

Explicit limits include one hypothesis test per task and no multiple-comparison pipelines. Future directions are assumption justification, uncertainty over method choice, and recognizing when a single hypothesis test is inadequate. The warning that statistical agents can confer false legitimacy on weak claims is unusually important and should be treated as a deployment boundary, not a side note.

Appendices A-D

Appendix B expands P-Bench provenance and gives economics, biology, and medicine examples. Appendix C explains the choice of R, the five-stage synthetic generator, the 27-method task grid, 60 data-quality perturbations, training hyperparameters, H200 compute, and relaxed-Strict sensitivity results. Appendix D provides the exact multi-turn training prompt.

Reproducibility-relevant details are substantial: SFT 3 epochs at 1e-5; RL 1 epoch at 1e-6; prompt/response maxima 2048/4096 tokens; RL rollout temperature 0.7, top-p 1.0, group size 8; inference temperature 0.3, top-p 0.9; 14B RL ≈36 hours on H200 hardware. The dense Welch-test simulation template is shown as Figure 7.

AI/ML systems appraisal

TaskOpen-ended single-hypothesis statistical testing with tool-executed R analysis.
Data425 expert-audited real-data tasks; synthetic executable training corpus kept separate from P-Bench by construction.
MethodSFT teacher trajectories + DAPO RL with p-value/conclusion reward.
MetricsRaw/Strict pass@1 and pass@3; Strict measures z-space p-value closeness plus decision correctness.
BaselinesGPT-5.4, DeepSeek V4 Pro, GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3 models, DataMind, Qwen backbones.
AblationsBackbone vs DAPO-only vs SFT-only vs SFT+DAPO.
RobustnessHard split, adversarial data issues, relaxed Strict threshold, prompt-level similarity audit.
Deployment evidenceNone: benchmark/training study, not prospective deployment or scientist-in-the-loop validation.
Central validity concern. The answer key operationalizes a canonical analysis, not the set of all defensible analyses. The paper avoids explicit method-label reward, but Strict still rewards p-values close to one reference procedure. That makes the benchmark strong for reproducibility against a chosen analysis and less definitive for cases where multiple methods are scientifically defensible and produce legitimately different p-values.

Evaluation & appraisal

Verdict: Read first. High-value work for anyone building reliable data-analysis/scientific agents because it isolates a consequential failure mode and provides a concrete executable evaluation/training pattern. Treat it as benchmark evidence, not proof of statistical autonomy.
Relevance9.5/10

Direct fit to agent reliability, executable validation, and scientific workflow design.

Evidence basis: benchmark + RL system centered on inferential validity.
Limiter: not clinical deployment.
Raise/lower: higher with real workflow evaluation; lower if only code-generation performance matters.

Novelty8.5/10

The combination of open-ended method selection, executed p-value checking, and verified-reward training is distinctive.

Evidence basis: P-Bench formulation + z-space reward.
Limiter: components build on known agent/RL methods.
Raise/lower: stronger with independently replicated benchmark uniqueness.

Method strength7.5/10

Executable keys, expert audit, strong baselines, ablation, and sensitivity analysis are meaningful strengths.

Evidence basis: 425 tasks; 3 rollouts/task; SFT/DAPO ablation; relaxed threshold.
Limiter: canonical-key dependence, no human-statistician baseline, limited failure taxonomy.
Raise/lower: higher with blinded external task/key construction and multiple defensible-reference analyses.

Evidence strength7.0/10

The data support improvement on P-Bench, but not broad claims of reliable autonomous statistics.

Evidence basis: consistent gains and run SDs across splits/metrics.
Limiter: benchmark-only evidence; no prospective external validation.
Raise/lower: higher with independent replication and real-study error interception outcomes.

External validity5.5/10

Cross-domain source diversity helps, but each task is still one bounded test in a curated R environment.

Evidence basis: economics/biology/medicine; 17 methods.
Limiter: no multi-testing, longitudinal project context, ambiguous hypotheses, or deployment workflow.
Raise/lower: higher with prospective studies and multi-stage analyses.

Implementation value8.0/10

The paper gives unusually concrete reward, prompt, hyperparameter, and data-generation details.

Evidence basis: Appendix C/D and equations.
Limiter: linked repository check found only a README at review time, limiting immediate reproducibility.
Raise/lower: higher when code/data/checkpoints are actually published and versioned.

Main strengths

  • Separates correct conclusion direction from p-value-grounded correctness.
  • Answer keys are tied to executed analyses and expert review rather than prose extraction alone.
  • Training reward is machine-checkable and aligned to inferential outputs.
  • Ablation shows SFT and RL contributions rather than reporting only a final model.
  • Appendix supplies prompt, hyperparameters, compute, generator structure, and sensitivity analysis.

Main limitations

  • Single-test tasks omit multiple comparisons, model-selection multiplicity, sequential exploration, and researcher degrees of freedom.
  • Canonical-analysis agreement is not identical to statistical validity when several analyses are defensible.
  • No human-statistician comparator or prospective test of whether the agent catches real errors before publication.
  • Prompt-embedding similarity is a narrow contamination check, not a full training-data audit.
  • P-Hard Strict pass@1 remains low (~31-33%), which is evidence of residual unreliability rather than near-solved performance.
  • Current linked code repository was not yet implementation-complete at verification time.

Confidence in appraisal: high for what the paper reports; moderate for reproducibility and generalization. The PDF is detailed and quantitative, but external reproduction and deployment validation are absent.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Build-usefulScore executed evidence, not just final prose. For hypothesis-testing agents, separate conclusion correctness from numerical evidence agreement.
Build-usefulUse executable, provenance-linked answer keys and keep task construction separate from model training.
Build-usefulReward design can tolerate multiple methods by scoring outcome-grounded evidence rather than exact method labels, but this does not eliminate reference-analysis bias.
Evaluation-usefulUse harder splits that encode assumption checking and adversarial data quality; report pass@1 and multi-try success separately.
GovernanceRequire explicit assumption checks, uncertainty over method choice, trace logs, and human escalation when evidence is ambiguous.
Do not overclaimBetter P-Bench scores do not imply a model can autonomously validate a scientific manuscript, control multiplicity, or distinguish every legitimate alternative analysis.
