One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Agent-written knowledge needs stricter storage defaults. Quipu puts the policy gate, history, authority, trust composition, verdict evidence, and audit data inside the knowledge store instead of relying on cleanup or external middleware.

The paper's strongest contribution is architectural. It makes six guarantees explicit and then connects each guarantee to a concrete storage mechanism and deterministic benchmark probe. The design evaluates candidate writes against the pending post-state, keeps denied-write verdicts after rollback, intersects authority along delegation chains, prevents composed views from increasing trust, stores governance evidence as queryable facts, and versions both rules and data so prior decisions can be replayed under the rules that existed at the time.

The empirical results are internally coherent. Census rejects all six planted defects that survive in the ungated control, all seven composition probes preserve the lattice, and all 50 satisfied decisions replay correctly as of their historical instant. The external evidence exercise is also useful: a content-aware reader reconstructs all 512 DEMM property judgments with zero overclaim, while container-presence baselines overclaim heavily. The evidence is still mostly author-built and deterministic. The agent experiment is small, the real trace contains five decisions, and the DEMM adapter and reader are study-authored.

Best use. Read this as a design and test pattern for governed agent memory, provenance-aware RAG stores, durable agent skills, policy-bearing data layers, and audit evidence. The reusable part is the contract and benchmark structure more than the specific SQLite implementation.

Section condensation

Abstract and introduction

The paper argues that accept-then-clean, weak temporal history, flat trust, and external governance are poor defaults when software agents produce plausible but wrong graph facts quickly. Quipu reverses those defaults by refusing invalid writes before commit, making data and governance bitemporal, partitioning trust and authority by named graph, and storing policy, traces, and signed verdicts in the same system.

The introduction frames this as a storage-layer problem rather than a prompt or dashboard problem. The author discloses implementation and drafting assistance from Claude and points to an archived release and development repository.

Requirements and design principles

The six principles are the paper's main contract. GS1 evaluates the post-state. GS2 keeps signed verdicts for refusals. GS3 narrows authority through delegation. GS4 prevents trust from increasing through composition and keeps coverage separate from the lattice value. GS5 makes audit decidable from stored evidence. GS6 versions rules as well as facts so historical decisions can be evaluated honestly.

The principles depend on one another. A refusal is useful evidence only if it persists. A signed verdict is trustworthy only if the signing root cannot be minted by the governed store. A trust floor is enforceable only if the partition identity and authority are stable. The result is a coupled design rather than six independent options.

Data model and governance plane

Quipu uses an append-only bitemporal EAVT fact log with named graphs and assert, retract, and tombstone operations. Retracts close validity intervals without erasing history. Tombstones let an overlay hide a fact in a composed view without mutating the underlying layer. Graphs are the unit of authority and trust, and imported packs preserve a stable content identity across stores.

Governance data is ordinary queryable data. Policy claims, shape versions, traces, authority grants, decision requests, and signed verdicts live in the store. The write path stages a candidate post-state in a savepoint, checks authority and policy, commits or rolls back, and then preserves the verdict either way.

Implementation

The implementation is a Rust crate backed by one SQLite file, with custom SPARQL evaluation plus CLI, REST, and MCP interfaces. The paper reports about 67,000 lines of code and more than 1,000 tests. Shapes and ontologies are kept in a bitemporal registry instead of being overwritten.

The performance figures are characterization, not competitive benchmarking. Storage is about 8.3 KB per ingested episode in the reported range. The basic graph-pattern evaluator is a nested loop, and the paper openly describes quadratic growth. A cache improves the constant factor, while an in-memory prototype shows that the read path could be redesigned substantially. The seed-42 gated write medians are 1.3 ms for ungoverned writes and 2.7 ms for compliant governed writes.

Census benchmark

Census is deterministic by design. The gated and control arms run the same scripted lifecycle, with no LLM in the core loop. Six planted defects are mixed with 100 clean writes. Seven probes exercise composition, and a policy amendment makes historical replay observable rather than theoretical.

The benchmark uses logical timestamps and a self-contained random generator. Three consecutive repeats produce byte-identical manifests. A five-decision Yupana trace is added as a small real-world check, but it is not large enough to establish external validity.

RQ1 to RQ5

RQ1 finds that policy claims are skipped for ungoverned target types, but authority checking still creates overhead relative to the ungated control. RQ2 is the clearest causal result: zero of six planted defects remain with gating and all six remain without it. RQ3 shows that the in-store audit reproduces planted violation and incompleteness categories. SARC agrees on verdicts but a faithful export creates 168 coverage discrepancies because the two systems encode abstention differently.

RQ4 reports seven of seven successful composition probes with no false refusal among the four clean compositions. RQ5 replays all 50 satisfied verdicts correctly under historical rules. Latest-only policy would misclassify all 50 after the policy amendment. The six denials only prove that the rule in force justified refusal because the rejected write delta was intentionally discarded.

Agent arm and real trace

Four Claude models receive five recording actions, no view of the policy specification, refusal messages as feedback, and one revision. The protocol runs three trials per model. All label and vocabulary refusals are repaired in one revision across 12 trials. Authority refusals produce different behaviors, including correct rerouting, principled refusal, and invalid attempts to move the fact elsewhere.

One false household-district record lands because the policy specification lacks the relevant district-membership rule. This is an important failure: strict gating limits the system to the rules actually authored. In the five genuine Yupana decisions, the audit reports two violations and six incompleteness findings, including a locally enforced blast-radius rule absent from the stored policy catalog.

DEMM-Bench

The DEMM pass asks whether emitted evidence is sufficient to reconstruct eight governance properties after controlled evidence degradation. Quipu exports 56 decisions across three evidence planes, then applies eight degradation conditions to produce 64 scored cases. A property-level reader reconstructs all 512 cells with PSA 1.0 and zero overclaim or underclaim. Presence-only baselines fail because the containers remain present even when their contents have been damaged.

The result is useful but not fully independent. Quipu is a self-added benchmark regime, and the study authors wrote the adapter, degradations, and property-level reader. The first pass also exposed a verdict-attribution weakness that the implementation fixed before the paper reported its final numbers. That is good engineering evidence, but it is weaker than a frozen-system external replication.

Related work and conclusion

The paper connects Quipu to named graphs, information-flow lattices, ontology tooling, SARC governance work, runtime verification, and agent evidence-sufficiency benchmarks. The claimed contribution is the conjunction of strict storage defaults rather than novelty for every individual mechanism.

The conclusion keeps several boundaries explicit. It makes no comparative query-speed claim, does not equate labels with row-level access control, and treats denial replay as an attestation check. Future work targets more agent tasks and models and a portable governed-store specification that other storage systems could implement and test.

Architecture and methods

candidate write
  -> stage pending post-state
  -> intersect principal authority
  -> evaluate policy claims and SHACL shapes
     -> allow: commit bitemporal facts
     -> refuse: roll back attempted delta
  -> after transaction resolves, persist signed verdict
  -> retain actor, principal chain, policy, target, outcome, hash, and time

historical audit
  -> read facts as of decision transaction
  -> read labels and authority as of decision transaction
  -> read policy and shapes as of decision transaction
  -> distinguish violation from incompleteness
  -> compare stored verdict with reconstructable evidence
Design implication. The most portable idea is that governance state and evidence should share the same historical semantics as the data they govern. A system that versions facts but not policy cannot answer what was required at a past instant.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read soon. The paper is useful for system design and evaluation. Its evidence is strong enough to justify copying several engineering patterns, but too self-contained to support broad claims about production reliability or general database performance.
9/10
Relevance

Directly applicable to durable agent memory, provenance, policy-bearing stores, RAG governance, and auditable AI workflows. Main limit: it is storage-focused rather than a complete agent stack. Higher with demonstrated integration into several real agent systems; lower if the project has no persistent agent-written state.

8/10
Novelty

The pieces are familiar, but the strict conjunction and the benchmark that tests each inversion add value. Main limit: named graphs, lattices, bitemporality, SHACL, and signed audit records are not individually new. Higher if the portable GS contract gains independent implementations.

7/10
Method strength

Deterministic ground truth, matched gated and ungated scripts, replay, repeatability checks, an external checker, a real trace, and DEMM contracts make the evaluation inspectable. Main limit: one seeded core run, self-authored benchmark machinery, and a small agent arm. Higher with preregistered multi-seed independent replication.

7/10
Evidence strength

The core causal result for planted defects is clean, and the paper reports important negative and boundary findings. Main limit: many claims remain engineering demonstrations, and the DEMM pass changed the evaluated system before the reported final run. Higher with frozen-artifact external reproduction.

5/10
External validity

The Yupana trace and DEMM contract help, but the real trace has five decisions and the agent arm uses one model family and one task. Higher with several organizations, stores, model families, workloads, and failure distributions.

9/10
Implementation value

The paper specifies concrete invariants, write ordering, authority semantics, replay behavior, audit categories, evidence fields, and test probes. Main limit: it does not provide a comparative storage-engine study or a completed portable standard. Higher with interoperability fixtures and cross-store conformance tests.

Main strengths

  • Requirements, mechanisms, and benchmark probes line up unusually well.
  • The benchmark separates violations from missing evidence instead of forcing every case into pass or fail.
  • The paper reports failure cases that matter, including the missing household-district policy and the first-pass verdict-attribution gap.
  • Historical replay includes policy and shapes, not only data.

Main limitations

  • The deterministic Census run is synthetic and centered on one seed.
  • The repeated agent arm is one task, four models from one family, and three trials per model.
  • The real trace is only five decisions.
  • The DEMM regime, degradations, and reader are study-authored. This is not independent validation.
  • Single-run write latencies and query prototypes should not be treated as comparative database performance.

Implementation takeaways

Version rules with dataIf a system must explain a past decision, policy and schemas need historical identity too. Latest-only rules make replay misleading.
Persist refusalsA rejected mutation may be the highest-value audit event. Record the policy, target, actor chain, evidence hash, outcome, and time after the attempted write transaction resolves.
Evaluate the post-stateCheck candidate facts in the combined state they would create. Pre-state checks miss defects that exist only after composition.
Make trust non-wideningDerived views should not become more trusted than their least-trusted or least-covered input. Keep coverage explicit instead of hiding missing declarations inside the trust value.
Separate violation from incompletenessA contradiction and a missing fact demand different remediation. Audit output should preserve that distinction.
Test evidence content, not containersA trace, ledger, or schema can exist and still fail to establish a needed governance fact. Score property-level reconstructability.
What not to copy blindly. Do not assume SQLite plus this evaluator is the right performance envelope, do not use labels as authorization, and do not interpret a gate as complete if the policy specification leaves important domain facts unstated.
