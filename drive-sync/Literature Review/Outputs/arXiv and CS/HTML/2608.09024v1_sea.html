One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The strongest contribution is not the headline ESI leaderboard; it is the benchmark architecture for temporally bounded, source-verifiable information acquisition. The paper gives a concrete design for tracking which EHR fact entered a conversation, from which role, at what turn, and from which eligible source event.

Core thesis. Conversational triage should be evaluated as a multi-stage process rather than as acuity classification from a fixed snapshot. EHR2Dial-Triage partitions each encounter into clinician-visible information, patient-reportable information, and hidden future/evaluation fields; generates role-specific dialogue under asymmetric access; verifies accepted disclosures; and stores event-turn lineage. This enables separate evaluation of question selection, information acquisition, ESI prediction, evidence-grounded closing generation, and safety failures.

Load-bearing results. Role realization broadly follows the intended access rules: 95.1% of initial vital-sign facts are first introduced by the clinician, whereas 98.8% of chief-complaint facts, 91.2% of arrival-mode facts, and 91.4% of pain facts are first introduced by the patient. By Round 10, generated dialogues cover 66% of eligible patient-reportable facts and 86% of triage-important facts; the frozen reader’s entropy falls from 1.61 to about 1.04 nats and top-1 ESI accuracy reaches 59% versus a 42% majority baseline. In matched Experiment I, terminal FactCov ranges widely enough to distinguish models after the early rounds; GLM-4.7-Flash reaches 68.0% FactCov@10 and 56.5% HistoryMedCov@10. In Experiment II, Claude Fable 5 leads ESI prediction (Macro-AUC 0.763, Macro-F1 0.321, QWK 0.517), while GPT-5.6 leads dialogue-grounding metrics for the patient-facing closing (Key Recall 0.562; Grounded Precision 0.660) and Distinct-2 (0.185).

Safety signal. Under-triage severity and reported confidence are not reliably aligned. Figure 6 shows that six of nine models with at least ten severe under-triage calls report higher mean confidence on those severe errors than on correct predictions. GLM-4.7-Flash has 15% under-triage by at least three ESI levels. The authors therefore argue for evaluating error severity, confidence reliability, and escalation/human-review pathways rather than relying on aggregate classification performance alone.

Main limitations. Single selected MIMIC-IV-ED cohort; synthetic patient/clinician dialogue rather than prospective triage; recorded ESI used as the target rather than clinician-adjudicated reference; no external multi-site validation; limited uncertainty reporting for model-to-model comparisons; and incomplete detail in the paper about how the 4,041-case acuity-enriched cohort was sampled from the parent database.

Section condensation

Abstract

The paper frames ED triage as interactive information acquisition under incomplete information, not merely final acuity prediction. EHR2Dial-Triage is presented as an agentic framework and benchmark grounded in MIMIC-IV-ED, with explicit role and time boundaries, event-level provenance for accepted disclosures, and evaluation of information elicitation, evidence use, five-level ESI prediction, and patient-facing communication.

1. Introduction

The introduction argues that triage clinicians must decide which missing facts matter, ask focused questions, update urgency assessments, and communicate an immediate plan under time pressure. The key design motivation is that fixed-snapshot prediction benchmarks do not measure this acquisition process, while dialogue resources often lack temporal links between conversational statements and underlying EHR events.

The proposed remedy is to use EHR information both as case context and as a hard factual/temporal boundary: accepted disclosures must map to eligible source events valid at the current point in the encounter. The paper then separates two capabilities—acquiring relevant evidence and using/communicating the completed evidence—because they need not move together.

2. Related Work

Sections 2.1–2.4 position the work across ED acuity prediction, clinical dialogue corpora, EHR-grounded patient simulation, interactive information-seeking benchmarks, and conversational triage. The differentiator is finer-grained provenance and timing: the benchmark records the supporting EHR event and first accepted dialogue turn for each disclosure and separates initially visible, patient-elicitable, and future-hidden information.

Table 1 is the central positioning device. In the authors’ comparison, EHR2Dial-Triage is the only listed resource marked as simultaneously providing a reusable on-demand generator, patient-level EHR grounding, event-turn lineage, and temporal tracing.

3. EHR-to-Dialogue Triage Modeling

3.1 Information partition. Each encounter is split into clinician-visible Ci and patient-reportable Pi information at the generation cutoff. Ci includes selected demographics, triage fields, initial measurements, and eligible prior-record information; Pi includes elicitable history such as symptom details, medications, allergies, and medical history. Recorded ESI and post-triage events are hidden from both roles.

3.2–3.3 Generation and lineage. The clinician conditions on Ci plus public dialogue history; the patient conditions on Pi, a non-clinical persona, and current dialogue. A verifier maps accepted disclosures to normalized facts. The cumulative state Zi,t records facts first accepted at each turn with source, round, and speaker; only Pi-originating facts receive acquisition credit.

4. EHR2Dial Validation

4.1 Role-consistent realization. The observed role that first states a fact generally matches the intended access partition. Initial vital signs are clinician-led; chief complaint, arrival mode, and pain are overwhelmingly patient-led. Home medications are dense in the source record (7.27 facts/encounter on average) but only 39.7% are explicitly verbalized, emphasizing selective rather than exhaustive record reproduction.

4.2–4.3 Interaction and relevance. The corpus has 4,041 dialogues with median eight clinician-patient rounds and 17.3 speaker turns per dialogue on average. Progressive disclosure is informative: by Round 10, coverage reaches 66% of all eligible patient facts and 86% of triage-important facts; fixed-reader entropy falls and ESI accuracy improves to 59% versus a 42% majority baseline. The authors explicitly classify these as internal benchmark checks, not clinical validation.

5.1–5.2 Experiment I: Clinician Information Elicitation

Every clinician model interacts with the same patient simulator under the same fact bank, disclosure policy, verifier, stopping rule, and ten-round budget. FACT COV and HISTORY MED COV are encounter-level coverage metrics macro-averaged across cases; a fixed patient-disjoint multinomial reader measures how acquired facts change the concentration of the ESI distribution.

Early behavior is tightly clustered—FactCov@2 spans 18.9–19.8% and FactCov@6 52.7–53.9%—but terminal behavior diverges. GLM-4.7-Flash reaches 68.0% FactCov@10 and 56.5% HistoryMedCov@10; MedGemma-1.5-4B is strongest early. Symptom-detail questions account for roughly 12–22% of questions across models. Lower Round-10 reader entropy for gpt-oss-20B, GLM-4.7-Flash, and MedGemma-1.5-4B suggests that fact value depends on what is elicited, not only how many facts are collected.

5.3 Experiment II: ESI Prediction and Closing Generation

All models receive the same completed speaker-labeled dialogue and no separate EHR serialization, target ESI, reference closing, provenance annotations, verifier decisions, or post-triage information. Models output a five-class score vector, discrete ESI prediction, and concise closing. ESI metrics include Macro-AUC, Macro-F1, QWK, and under-triage; closing metrics include grounding, reference similarity, and lexical diversity.

Claude Fable 5 leads the displayed ESI metrics (0.763 Macro-AUC, 0.321 Macro-F1, 0.517 QWK). Open-weight leaders differ by metric. GPT-5.6 leads Key Recall (0.562), Grounded Precision (0.660), and Distinct-2 (0.185), whereas reference-similarity leaders differ (MedGemma BERTScore F1 0.263; Gemma ROUGE-L 0.160; GLM BLEU-4 0.053). This reinforces the paper’s claim that matching a reference closing and preserving dialogue support are not the same objective.

5.4 Error Analysis: Under-Triage and Reported Confidence

The paper evaluates cumulative under-triage by one, two, or three ESI levels and separately compares mean confidence on correct predictions with confidence on severe under-triage, defined as assigning ESI 4–5 to a case recorded ESI 1–2. Error-frequency rankings change with severity threshold; GPT-5.6 and Claude Fable 5 show lower multi-level under-triage than several open-weight models.

The more consequential finding is that confidence often does not fall during severe failure. Six of nine models with enough severe cases lie above the equal-confidence line in Figure 6(b), meaning they are, on average, more confident when severely under-triaging than when correct. The source therefore treats escalation and human review as necessary evaluation dimensions.

6. Conclusion

The authors conclude that information acquisition, ESI prediction, evidence grounding, and patient-facing communication capture distinct performance dimensions. They argue that interactive medical AI safety should be evaluated across the full acquisition-to-communication pathway, not only by a final prediction.

Proposed next steps include clinician-adjudicated safety evaluation, multi-institutional validation, and richer patient-facing communication measures for clarity, actionability, and response to evolving or ambiguous reports.

Evaluation and appraisal

9/10

Relevance

Direct fit for clinical NLP, interactive medical agents, benchmark design, provenance, and agent reliability.

Evidence basis: The paper operationalizes information acquisition, evidence use, triage prediction, and grounded communication in one EHR-linked benchmark.

Principal limit: The work is benchmark-centric rather than a clinical outcomes study.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with prospective or clinician-in-the-loop deployment evaluation; would fall if the released benchmark or code cannot reproduce the stated workflow.

9/10

Novelty

Event-turn lineage plus explicit temporal partitioning is a substantive benchmark-design contribution.

Evidence basis: Table 1 positions EHR2Dial-Triage as the only listed resource combining reusable generation, patient-level EHR grounding, event-turn lineage, and temporal tracing.

Principal limit: Novelty is demonstrated by the authors’ resource comparison rather than an exhaustive systematic review.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with broader independent comparison or adoption; would fall if closely equivalent lineage mechanisms are shown in omitted prior work.

7/10

Method strength

The experimental controls are careful: fixed patient simulator in Experiment I, fixed completed dialogue in Experiment II, patient-disjoint frozen reader, and explicit leakage boundaries.

Evidence basis: Methods define role access, event lineage, metrics, and common comparison conditions in detail.

Principal limit: Cohort selection details are limited, model-comparison uncertainty is not reported, and there is little component-level ablation of generation/verifier choices.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with explicit sampling flow, confidence intervals/bootstrap uncertainty, ablations, and independent replication.

6/10

Evidence strength

Results directly support benchmark-behavior claims but do not establish clinical safety or efficacy.

Evidence basis: The paper reports consistent role realization, progressive information gain, model comparisons, and under-triage/confidence analyses on 1,013 evaluation encounters.

Principal limit: Evidence is benchmark-only, uses recorded ESI as the target, lacks clinician-adjudicated outcome validation, and reports mostly point estimates.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with clinician adjudication, external datasets, repeated-seed uncertainty, and prospective validation.

3/10

External validity

Generalizability is intentionally limited by a single, selected, acuity-enriched MIMIC-IV-ED cohort and synthetic role-based conversations.

Evidence basis: The authors explicitly warn that cohort label mix and performance are not estimates of real-world prevalence or deployment performance.

Principal limit: No multi-institutional or prospective validation is reported.

Would raise/lower: Would rise materially with external hospitals, contemporary real-time triage workflows, diverse populations, and human-clinician comparisons.

9/10

Implementation value

The provenance and evaluation architecture is highly reusable for building clinical-agent evaluation harnesses.

Evidence basis: The paper specifies information partitions, verifier behavior, event-turn lineage, metric formulas, source-field boundaries, and states that code is available.

Principal limit: Implementation value is for research/evaluation infrastructure, not direct clinical triage deployment.

Would raise/lower: Would rise with independently verified code reproducibility, benchmark cards, and standardized export schemas; would fall if critical generation/verifier components are not reproducible.

Best use. Benchmark and evaluation-harness design for clinical conversational agents: role-bound information access, temporal leakage control, atomic disclosure verification, event-turn provenance, multi-stage metrics, and safety error decomposition.
Do not use for. Claims that an LLM is ready to triage real patients, estimates of real-world ESI prevalence, or direct comparisons of clinical safety across deployed systems. The paper itself does not establish those endpoints.

Strengths

Explicit information boundaries; auditable source/turn lineage; controlled matched comparisons; separation of acquisition from downstream reasoning; full metric definitions; safety analysis that considers error magnitude and confidence; and a concrete code-availability statement.

Principal methodological concerns

Source-derived: the cohort is selected and acuity enriched; validation is internal; external clinician-adjudicated and multi-institutional validation are future work. Appraisal inference: using recorded ESI as the sole target constrains target validity because it is an operational record label rather than an independently adjudicated reference for this study. Model tables mostly provide point estimates without interval estimates or repeated-run uncertainty. The paper also does not present strong ablations isolating the effect of verifier policy, persona construction, prompt design, or dialogue-generation model on downstream benchmark behavior.

Confidence in appraisal: moderate-high. The methods, main tables, figures, and appendices are sufficiently detailed for benchmark-level appraisal. Confidence is reduced by the absence of external validation and by limited reporting of cohort-sampling flow and model-comparison uncertainty.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Adopt the provenance patternRepresent each accepted clinical disclosure as an atomic fact with source event, first accepted turn, speaker role, and eligibility state.
Separate acquisition from reasoningUse one experiment where models control the questions under a fixed patient simulator, and a second where all models receive the same completed dialogue.
Freeze leakage boundariesDefine clinician-visible, patient-reportable, future-hidden, evaluation-only, and target-only fields before generation; never let the evaluator silently cross those boundaries.
Measure efficiency, not just terminal coverageA bounded interaction budget prevents models from winning simply by asking indefinitely many questions. Add question yield or marginal information value if adapting the framework.
Keep safety severity-awareReport one-, two-, and three-level under-triage separately; aggregate under-triage can hide large clinically concerning misses.
Do not treat confidence as a safety gate by defaultFigure 6 shows that severe errors can remain highly confident. Any confidence-triggered escalation policy would require calibration and independent validation.
For clinical deployment, add human ground truthBefore practice use, replace or supplement recorded ESI with clinician adjudication, external sites, prospective workflows, and patient-safety outcomes.
Reproducibility gap to check nextThe source states code is available. A follow-up verification run should inspect the repository, frozen cohort construction, prompts, verifier implementation, seeds, and reproducibility of Tables 3–4.
