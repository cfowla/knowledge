One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. RTPO is unusually relevant to agent reliability because it links three failure modes—context mismatch, weak turn credit, and asynchronous policy drift—to one training-structure problem and tests corresponding fixes rather than presenting only a new optimizer name.

Core thesis. Flattening multi-turn tool interactions into one trajectory creates structural inconsistency between rollout and training, obscures which turn caused the outcome, and makes long trajectories increasingly stale during asynchronous optimization. RTPO instead treats each turn as a macro-action, trains turns from the end backward, compares sibling continuations from the same boundary state, and regenerates downstream continuations under the current policy.

What the evidence supports. On eight main benchmarks RTPO reports overall 61.66 versus 53.71 for GRPO and 57.68 for SeeUPO, corresponding to reported relative gains of 21.50% and 10.76%. Mechanism-specific experiments show an exact rollout/train log-probability ratio near 1.0 by construction, higher controlled credit-assignment performance, and long-horizon gains from on-policy continuation. Supplementary τ3-Airline evaluation adds a small but useful stateful environment, compute-matched comparisons, failure-mode analysis, and explicit training-cost measurements.

Main caution. The strongest convergence theorem is tabular and assumes fixed downstream policies; the shared neural implementation does not inherit a global non-convex convergence guarantee. RTPO also costs materially more to train: the τ3-Airline supplement reports about 41.3% more GPU-hours than GRPO at G=3, and G=4 raises generation tokens by about 152.8% over G=3.

Section condensation

Abstract / Introduction

The paper frames multi-turn RL instability as more than a sequence-length problem. Each turn changes future context, environment state, and policy inputs, so a flattened trajectory can become internally inconsistent when rollout uses truncated/summarized context while training recomputes probabilities on the full history. The authors identify three coupled problems: rollout–training mismatch, trajectory-only credit assignment, and asynchronous drift.

RTPO is positioned as a unified response: preserve the exact per-turn conditioning context, optimize turns in reverse order, use state-matched sibling rollouts for turn-level advantages, and refresh downstream continuations with the latest policy.

Section 2 — Theoretical analysis

The rollout/training mismatch changes the denominator of the token importance ratio and can produce state aliasing when context compression is non-injective. The trajectory-level reward is decomposed into downstream randomness, true current-turn advantage, and an upstream state effect; this explains why group-relative comparisons across different states can have the wrong sign for a current action.

For asynchronous trajectories, tokenwise clipping cannot reconstruct the correct full-trajectory importance ratio. Exact trajectory correction would have rapidly increasing variance with horizon, motivating a formulation that avoids stale downstream continuation rather than attempting to correct it afterward.

Section 3 — RTPO method

RTPO defines a turn-boundary MDP in which each complete response is a macro-action and the model context c_k is preserved identically between rollout and training. Reverse training starts at the final turn and moves backward; after a turn is optimized, later turns are treated as fixed continuation when earlier turns are trained.

At each boundary, G−1 sibling rollouts branch from the same state. Their terminal returns define a local group baseline and a turn-level advantage applied only to that turn's generated tokens. On-policy continuation refreshes downstream sampling under the current policy, eliminating trajectory-level correction for the sibling continuation. The theory proves recursive-optimality results under strong tabular/stochastic-approximation assumptions and derives finite-group properties for the sibling advantage.

Section 4 — Main experiments

The primary setup uses Qwen3-8B and compares RTPO with GRPO, SeeUPO, ARPO, and TreeGRPO across math and knowledge-reasoning benchmarks. Table 1 gives the headline suite result: RTPO 61.66 overall, with reported relative improvement of 21.50% over GRPO and 10.76% over SeeUPO. RTPO is strongest on every listed benchmark row, though absolute margins vary substantially by task.

The controlled analyses are more informative than the headline average. Figure 3 shows structural rollout/train agreement for RTPO while baseline ratios recover only partially. Table 2 controls context to focus on turn credit and favors RTPO-CA overall. Table 3 compares on-policy and stale/off-policy continuation: gains are positive on GAIA, WebWalkerQA, and XBench but essentially absent on HLE, suggesting the continuation mechanism matters most on longer interactive search.

Appendices — validity, implementation, and extensions

The appendices make the paper substantially stronger. They spell out the assumptions behind Theorems 1–3, provide pseudocode and hyperparameters, report three-run means/standard deviations, explain Qwen3 context filtering, and quantify compute budgets. The stateful τ3-Airline extension is especially useful because tool actions modify later state rather than only retrieving information.

That extension also exposes limitations: training gains can regress late, zero-advantage rates remain very high under sparse binary rewards, and the method costs more. Compute-matched and inference-budget-matched results suggest RTPO can improve Pass@4/completion and inference efficiency even when training budget is constrained, but the held-out stateful test set contains only 20 tasks.

Algorithm 1 — executable workflow

1. Generate trunk trajectories and save turn-boundary states/snapshots.
2. For k = K−1 ... 0:
   a. Synchronize current downstream parameters to inference.
   b. Restore boundary snapshot and sample G−1 on-policy siblings.
   c. Continue each sibling to terminal reward.
   d. Compute same-state group baseline and turn-level advantage.
   e. PPO-update current-turn output tokens only.
3. Continue backward until turn 0.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Directly targets long-horizon agent training stability and tool-use reliability.

Evidence: Unified agentic-RL diagnosis plus controlled stability experiments.

Limit: Not specific to clinical agents or production environments.

Would raise: Replication on deployed agent workloads and domain-specific settings would raise it.

Novelty 8/10

Combines reverse-order updates with state-matched sibling credit and current-policy continuation as one mechanism.

Evidence: Distinct formulation relative to GRPO, SeeUPO, ARPO, and TreeGRPO.

Limit: Reverse-order updating itself is not new.

Would raise: Independent confirmation that the unified mechanism, not implementation details, drives gains would raise it.

Method strength 8/10

Theory is explicit and empirical evaluation includes controlled component analyses and compute-aware supplements.

Evidence: Eight main benchmarks, three-run evaluation, controlled credit and continuation comparisons, stateful τ3-Airline supplement.

Limit: Strict convergence assumptions are stronger than the neural implementation and some supplemental experiments are small.

Would raise: More seeds, larger models, and independent reproductions would raise it.

Evidence strength 7/10

The core stability claims are supported by matched baselines and mechanism-specific experiments.

Evidence: Table 1-3 results, consistency curves, three-run means/std, stateful and compute-matched supplemental evaluations.

Limit: Some headline relative gains are suite summaries; uncertainty is limited and long-horizon stateful evaluation is small.

Would raise: Larger stateful test sets and confidence intervals across training runs would raise it.

External validity 6/10

Results span math, web search, and a stateful airline tool environment.

Evidence: Qwen3 models, multiple benchmark families, held-out τ3-Airline tasks.

Limit: Model families, turn horizons, and deployment environments remain narrow.

Would raise: Cross-family models, longer horizons, production tools, and more stateful environments would raise it.

Implementation value 8/10

Algorithm, hyperparameters, context handling, cost trade-offs, and pseudocode are detailed enough to guide a build.

Evidence: VeRL/vLLM/FSDP stack, Algorithm 1, Table 4, synchronization and sibling settings, cost tables.

Limit: Reverse-stage synchronization and sibling continuations add substantial training overhead.

Would raise: A released executable repository and validated lower-cost synchronization schedule would raise it.

Strengths

  • Theory and algorithm are tightly connected to three explicit failure modes.
  • Controlled mechanism experiments go beyond a single aggregate benchmark table.
  • Appendices expose assumptions, uncertainty, cost, failure dynamics, and a stateful tool environment.
  • Compute-matched and inference-budget-matched analyses make efficiency claims more interpretable.

Limitations

  • Neural training does not inherit the strongest tabular global-convergence guarantee.
  • Training overhead is material, and denser sibling groups become expensive quickly.
  • Stateful held-out evaluation is small; benchmark breadth is larger than deployment realism.
  • Sparse rewards still produce very high zero-advantage rates.
  • Late-stage degradation shows that the method does not eliminate over-optimization or training collapse in every setting.
Do not overclaim: RTPO does not prove stable global optimization for shared neural LLM policies, and benchmark gains do not establish production-safe autonomous tool use.

Implementation and research takeaways

Most reusable design idea
Train against the exact context actually seen at rollout time; do not silently recompute likelihoods on a richer history.
Credit assignment
Same-state sibling branches give a cleaner local baseline than cross-state trajectory grouping when terminal reward is sparse.
Fresh continuation
Regenerating downstream continuation under current parameters avoids high-variance trajectory correction, at the cost of synchronization and sampling.
Engineering constraint
Reverse-stage synchronization plus sibling rollouts can add ~40% training cost at the reported G=3 stateful setting.
Evaluation lesson
Include stateful tool environments, failure-mode trajectories, compute-matched tests, and per-seed stability—not only static benchmark averages.
Best next verification
Replicate on a second model family and longer stateful horizons with fixed wall-clock/GPU budget and confidence intervals across training seeds.
