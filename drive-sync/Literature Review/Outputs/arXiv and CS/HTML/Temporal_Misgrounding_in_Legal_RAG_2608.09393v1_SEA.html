One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The paper is unusually useful for RAG reliability work because it converts an intuitive failure—retrieving the wrong legal version—into a controlled benchmark with deterministic scoring and explicit provenance checks.

Core thesis. Mutable legal corpora are not static document collections. A question’s date is part of the retrieval key. When a system retrieves only the currently consolidated version of a statute, it can cite a genuine article with high confidence yet still ground the answer in an inapplicable legal state. The authors call this temporal misgrounding.

Central evidence. On 209 expert-reviewed R3 questions chosen to be both temporally drifted and all-model-hard, pooled strict accuracy is 3.0% for LLM-only and 2.7% for static current-version RAG. Serving the correct version of the gold article raises strict accuracy to 99.1%; an end-to-end version-aware retriever reaches 98.3%. Static RAG has 0% date-applicable-version provenance, versus 100% for the oracle and 99% for end-to-end retrieval. The remaining 0.8-point oracle-to-production gap is attributed to article recall, not date resolution.

What matters beyond law. The general architecture lesson is to model mutable corpora as entities with version identity and validity intervals, condition retrieval on the query’s temporal state, and score grounding provenance separately from answer correctness. That pattern transfers conceptually to versioned clinical guidance, formularies, policies, software documentation, and other rapidly changing authoritative corpora—but this paper does not test those domains.

Section-by-section condensation

Abstract & Introduction

The paper defines temporal misgrounding as retrieving/citing the currently in-force version when an earlier or future version is applicable. It argues that legal RAG becomes a temporally indexed retrieval problem because article identifiers persist across amendments, correctness is conditional on validity dates, and adjacent versions are semantically near-identical.

The research question is deliberately conditional: on legal questions where the date-applicable answer differs from current law, how much does temporal misgrounding bottleneck modern LLM/RAG accuracy? The benchmark therefore isolates the failure rather than estimating its population prevalence.

2. Related Work

The paper situates FiscalQA Pro among legal benchmarks, temporal QA, enterprise search, and graph-based temporal legal retrieval. It adopts nugget decomposition from KARL but removes LLM-as-judge by restricting nuggets to deterministic identifier and numeric checks.

Concurrent legal temporal-grounding studies in German and broader legal-search settings are presented as convergent evidence for the phenomenon, while this paper distinguishes itself by fine-grained version indexing, deterministic scoring, and an all-model-hard filter across eleven evaluated models.

3. Why Static RAG Fails on Legal QA

The structural argument has three corpus properties: the same article identifier can map to different content over time; the correct version is a function of date; and successive versions are often nearest neighbors in semantic embedding space. Combined with a single-version index and model recency bias, these properties produce current-law substitution, future-law leakage, wrong-amendment resolution, and multi-version confusion.

The scored R3 track tests only the first mode. The broader taxonomy is therefore a framework and hypothesis generator, not fully validated by this experiment.

4. Corpus Construction

The authors extract complete article histories for the CGI, four annexes, and LPF from the official Légifrance API, storing each version’s stable article identity, version identifier, validity dates, state, and full text. The result is 32,436 article-versions across 8,076 stable article identifiers, with 4.02 versions per identifier on average; CGI principal alone has 21,040 versions across 3,696 articles.

An auxiliary jurisprudence resource links tax decisions to the article version valid at decision time. It contains 69,208 links across 32,034 decisions. Precision is high but recall is uneven, making it useful as weak supervision rather than definitive ground truth.

5. Benchmark Design

FiscalQA Pro has four regimes; the paper’s controlled experiment focuses on R3 temporal reasoning. The released R3 corpus has 221 curated questions, with 209 retained for scoring after 12 answerability exclusions. The scored set spans 33 CGI articles, seven tax sub-domains, and 36 years of statutory drift.

Scoring uses two required nuggets per R3 question—article and exact date-applicable numeric value—with deterministic regex/numeric matching. The selection filter rejects any question answered by any evaluated model in any of four closed-book draws, and a divergence filter ensures the current text lacks the gold value for 208/209 scored questions. This creates a powerful diagnostic set but makes the static-RAG low score partly a construction check.

6. Experiments

Four effective conditions are compared: A, closed-book LLM; B, static current-version RAG given the gold article; C_or, gold article with the date-applicable version; and C_prod, end-to-end retrieval of article plus date-applicable version. Eleven models cover closed/frontier and open-weight families.

C_prod uses hybrid dense + BM25 retrieval with reciprocal-rank fusion, sends the top five unique articles through the version layer, and serves the date-applicable version. All retrieval conditions share the same labeled prompt and 8,000-character query-windowed budget, and the authors verify that the gold value is present in every served gold article window.

7. Results

Mean strict accuracy is A 3.0% [1.4, 4.7], B 2.7% [1.3, 4.8], C_or 99.1% [98.4, 99.9], and C_prod 98.3% [95.9, 99.6]. Provenance is 0% for B, 100% for C_or, and 99% for C_prod. Every model individually shows a highly significant B→C improvement; C_prod remains above 95% strict in all model point estimates.

The oracle-to-end-to-end gap is only 0.8 points and is concentrated in first-stage article recall. The one reported provenance miss is article 1417, affecting two questions outside the top five. Query-windowing fixes an earlier truncation artifact; prompting full long articles can instead hurt through long-context distraction.

8. Conclusion

The authors conclude that explicit version indexing and date-conditioned retrieval largely eliminate the measured temporal-misgrounding failure on their diagnostic R3 set. They release the corpus, benchmark, responses, and most pipeline code, while the exact end-to-end C_prod retrieval stack remains proprietary.

9. Limitations and Future Work

Direct external validity is limited to French tax law, 209 scored questions, and the current-law-substitution failure mode. The paper proposes cross-jurisdiction replication and expanded question types for future-law leakage, prior-amendment requests, and two-version comparisons.

Because the benchmark intentionally selects temporal drift and all-model-hard cases, it cannot support a claim that static legal RAG usually performs near 3%. It does support the narrower claim that static indexing is structurally incapable of supplying the correct version when the relevant version is absent from the index.

Impact Statement

The authors frame temporal grounding as a high-stakes reliability issue for tax compliance and legal research, while explicitly stating that outputs should be verified by qualified professionals and not treated as legal advice.

Appendix A — Corpus and benchmark statistics

Table 2 gives the six-code version-depth breakdown; Table 3 shows the R3 distribution across seven fiscal sub-domains. These tables support the claims that the corpus is deeply versioned and the benchmark spans multiple tax topics rather than one article family.

Appendix B — Version-aware jurisprudence linking

The linker uses regex-based citation extraction with context disambiguation and validity-interval resolution. In a 100-link audit, precision is 98–99%. In a separately annotated 50-decision sample, article-level precision is 88.6%, article-level recall 53.4%, and decision-level recall 97%; performance is weakest on noisier aggregated data and procedural LPF citations.

Appendix C — Article 219 case study

The same question and corpus illustrate the mechanism: LLM-only and static current-version RAG answer 25%, while date-conditioned version retrieval serves the 2018 article and yields the correct 33⅓%. The visual is pedagogical rather than part of the scored benchmark.

Appendix D — Reproducibility

Data releases include the versioned corpus, link dataset, benchmark, audit samples, gold standard, and model responses; code includes extraction/linking, evaluation harness, deterministic scorer, and clustered statistics. SHA256 checksums and fixed random seeds are reported.

The principal reproducibility limitation is C_prod: its encoder weights, multi-version index, and inference code are proprietary. The authors provide per-question responses so the published scores can be independently recomputed, but the full end-to-end retrieval result cannot be regenerated from released artifacts alone.

Appendix E — Well-formedness control

On the same questions and prompt, changing only the served version lifts pooled strict accuracy from 2.7% to 99.1%. The current version lacks the gold value for 208/209 questions, and the one non-drifted control is answered correctly by static RAG. These checks strongly support version drift—not ill-posed questions or a broken reader—as the cause of B’s failure.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 9/10

Rationale: Directly addresses RAG reliability, deterministic evaluation, retrieval architecture, and failure analysis.

Evidence basis: Controlled legal-RAG benchmark with versioned retrieval and multiple models.

Principal limiting factor: Domain is legal rather than biomedical/clinical NLP.

What would raise/lower: Replication in a second high-stakes mutable corpus would raise confidence in transfer.

Novelty 8/10

Rationale: Makes legal temporal drift an explicit retrieval-grounding failure and evaluates it with a version-indexed corpus.

Evidence basis: Version-aware benchmark, deterministic nuggets, controlled static-vs-versioned comparison.

Principal limiting factor: Related temporal legal QA studies are concurrent and the conceptual fix is structurally simple.

What would raise/lower: Independent novelty comparison against concurrent German work and broader failure modes would raise the score.

Method strength 8/10

Rationale: Paired conditions, frozen benchmark, deterministic scoring, article-cluster bootstrap, and same-prompt controls support causal interpretation within the selected regime.

Evidence basis: 209 scored questions, 11 models, oracle and end-to-end ablations, cluster-aware inference.

Principal limiting factor: Static-RAG failure is partly enforced by the divergence filter; C_prod is proprietary and selection is all-model-hard.

What would raise/lower: A fully released end-to-end retriever and pre-registered external replication would raise the score.

Evidence strength 8/10

Rationale: The condition gap is very large, consistent across models, and supported by well-formedness and provenance checks.

Evidence basis: B 2.7% vs C_prod 98.3% pooled strict; all per-model B→C gains p<10^-55; C_prod provenance 99%.

Principal limiting factor: Evidence is conditional on a deliberately drifted hard subset rather than an unbiased sample of legal questions.

What would raise/lower: Performance on an unfiltered representative legal-QA sample would strengthen prevalence and practical-effect claims.

External validity 5/10

Rationale: The mechanism is plausible beyond France, but the study directly tests only French tax statutes and one dominant failure mode.

Evidence basis: 33 CGI articles, seven fiscal sub-domains, 1989–2025 drift.

Principal limiting factor: No cross-jurisdiction replication; future-law leakage, wrong-amendment resolution, and multi-version comparison are not evaluated.

What would raise/lower: Replication on Swiss/German/Belgian versioned law or a non-legal mutable corpus would raise the score.

Implementation value 8/10

Rationale: Provides a clear architecture pattern: retain explicit versions, infer/query date, filter by validity interval, and evaluate provenance separately from answer accuracy.

Evidence basis: Public corpus, benchmark, scorer, scripts, and reproducible A/B/C_or conditions.

Principal limiting factor: The exact C_prod encoder/index/inference stack is proprietary.

What would raise/lower: Open end-to-end retriever weights/index plus deployment latency/cost/error analyses would raise the score.

Strengths

  • Clean causal control: B and C_or differ primarily in which version of the same gold article is served.
  • Deterministic scoring avoids temporal bias from an LLM judge.
  • Grounding provenance is measured separately from answer correctness.
  • Cluster-aware inference addresses repeated questions within articles.
  • Paper is unusually explicit about selection artifacts, query-windowing, provider substitutions, contamination checks, and unreleased components.

Limitations that materially constrain interpretation

  • Diagnostic sampling: static RAG is tested on questions deliberately selected for current-version divergence; 2.7% is not an estimate of routine legal-RAG accuracy.
  • All-model-hard conditioning: closed-book results are intentionally near floor by selection.
  • Limited external validation: one jurisdiction, one legal domain, one tested temporal failure mode.
  • Partial end-to-end reproducibility: the C_prod retriever is proprietary.
  • Benchmark scale: 209 scored questions across 33 articles is adequate for the large within-set effect but limited for broad prevalence or heterogeneous error taxonomy.

Do not overclaim: The experiment demonstrates that a static current-only index cannot ground answers in absent historical versions on this selected temporal-drift regime. It does not show that every legal question requires versioned retrieval, that static RAG is generally 2.7% accurate, or that the exact C_prod architecture is uniquely responsible for the improvement.

Implementation / research takeaways

Build

Treat authoritative content as entity + version + validity interval, not as a single mutable document.

Retrieve

Extract or infer the query’s effective date, retrieve candidate entities, then resolve versions deterministically by validity interval.

Evaluate

Score answer correctness and retrieval provenance separately; a citation can be authentic yet temporally wrong.

Benchmark

Use deterministic facts when possible and cluster inference by the real dependency unit rather than treating all questions as IID.

For clinical/biomedical systems

Model inference: the same failure pattern is plausible for versioned guidelines, labels, formularies, local policies, and protocols. This paper does not empirically validate that transfer.

Read next

Prioritize cross-jurisdiction temporal legal QA and studies that test temporally versioned retrieval without selecting only drifted/hard cases.
