One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The paper gives a concrete training and evaluation design for a problem that agent systems often handle poorly: recognizing that a request cannot be answered as stated and choosing a useful next action instead of guessing.

The paper introduces ACA-RL, a reinforcement-learning setup that turns well-posed reasoning problems into missing-premise variants. The model receives a structured reward based on terminal behavior. Asking for the missing premise gets the highest reward, conditioning on an unknown variable comes next, abstention remains positive, and unsupported assumptions or silent hallucination are penalized. The authors also introduce MPB, a 274-instance held-out benchmark with human verification.

The main result is consistent across the three tested model groups. On MPB, ACA-RL scores 51.73 on Qwen3-8B, 57.20 on Qwen3-14B, and 53.56 on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct. The corresponding IDK-RL scores are 48.72, 49.43, and 51.29, while Vanilla PPO is far lower. The useful distinction is not simply that ACA-RL refuses more. Table 5 shows IDK-RL still has higher binary IDK scores, while ACA-RL has higher Behavior Scores because the rubric rewards conditional answers and clarification requests.

Best use. Treat this as a design pattern for uncertainty-aware agent training and evaluation. It is especially useful for separating missing-information detection from the policy that follows detection.
Main caution. Training reward assignment and MPB scoring share the same behavior taxonomy. MPB is held out at the instance level, but the reported margin is still partly a measure of how well training matches the chosen rubric. The paper also relies on synthetic logical perturbations and single-turn outputs.

Section condensation

Abstract and introduction

The authors argue that answer-only RL creates the wrong incentive when a prompt lacks a premise needed for a unique answer. They define a broader target behavior in which a model can ask for the missing premise, express a conditional answer, or abstain. ACA-RL is the proposed training method, and MPB is the evaluation benchmark. The paper reports a 120,000-instance generated training set and a separate 274-instance human-verified benchmark.

Preliminary

The paper defines missing-premise reasoning as a task that resembles a normal reasoning problem but lacks information needed for a unique answer. Evaluation targets terminal response behavior, not probability calibration. The five categories are silent hallucination, explicit assumption, abstention, conditional formulation, and active elicitation.

Missing-premise data construction

The data pipeline first decomposes source problems and builds a reasoning DAG. It then identifies a critical condition on the solution path, modifies that condition, rewrites the problem, and rechecks the result. A quality filter was compared with 556 human-labeled held-out candidates and reported 93.0% accuracy, 94.4% precision, 91.4% recall, and 92.9% F1 for unanswerability detection. MPB comes from a separate held-out pool and receives human review.

ACA-RL reward and objective

The reward hierarchy is explicit: active elicitation 1.0, conditional formulation 0.6, abstention 0.3, explicit assumption -0.3, and silent hallucination -1.0. This makes the model optimize a response policy rather than a calibrated probability. The highest reward reflects the authors' preference for acquiring missing information when possible.

Experimental setup

The main models are Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct. Qwen models receive a supervised cold start before PPO. The comparison includes Vanilla PPO, IDK-RL, and ACA-RL. ACA-RL uses about a 30% missing-premise mixture by default. GPT-5 classifies MPB responses into the five behavior categories, which map to 0, 25, 50, 75, and 100 before averaging.

Main results

ACA-RL raises MPB Behavior Score sharply over answer-only PPO and modestly over IDK-RL across all three model groups. On Qwen3-8B, MPB is 51.73 for ACA-RL, 48.72 for IDK-RL, and 8.66 for Vanilla PPO. Standard reasoning does not remain identical: Qwen3-8B ACA-RL scores 91.50 on GSM8K, 92.60 on MATH-500, and 64.16 on AIME 2024 versus 93.85, 93.40, and 64.58 for Vanilla PPO. Table 2 adds LiveBench 59.0 for both ACA-RL and Vanilla PPO/RLVR, with SciBench 53.7 versus 56.0.

Analysis and ablations

The data-source ablation favors the paper's reasoning-graph synthesis on MPB. Mixture ratio exposes a real trade-off: 50% missing-premise data gives MPB 53.19 but drops GSM8K to 82.10 and AIME 2024 to 52.56, while 30% gives MPB 43.79 with GSM8K 90.14 and AIME 60.83 in the 10K-sample, 100-step run. The training curves suggest that abstention is easier to learn than conditional formulation or elicitation. Larger generated datasets improve later-stage scores in the tested sweep, but the authors correctly stop short of claiming a scaling law.

Related work

The paper connects reinforcement learning for reasoning with unanswerable-question benchmarks, ambiguity and clarification, abstention, verbalized uncertainty, and retrieval-aware missing-information work. Its specific contribution is a learned single-turn policy that ranks asking and conditioning above generic refusal.

Conclusion and limitations

The authors conclude that missing-premise training improves rubric-aligned behavior while retaining much of the tested well-posed reasoning capability. They also state three important limits. Active elicitation is only a text response, not a working multi-turn acquisition policy. Synthetic structural breaks may not resemble organic ambiguity. Training and evaluation share the same behavior taxonomy, so independent scoring and larger human studies remain necessary.

Appendices

Appendix A explains MPB filtering and human verification. Appendix B gives condition-breaking definitions and examples. Appendix C contains a worked reasoning DAG. Appendix E supplies scoring and PPO details, including the 0 to 100 Behavior Score mapping and hyperparameters. Appendix E.4 reports about 98% human agreement with the GPT judge for sampled GPT-5 responses and about 95% for sampled Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct responses, but the inspected text does not give the sample counts. Appendix F summarizes the LLM-agent data-synthesis prompts, and Appendix G discloses LLM use in the method and possible writing assistance.

Evaluation and appraisal

Read first. The paper is useful as an engineering and evaluation reference. Do not treat its benchmark gains as proof that the method handles open-world ambiguity or completes clarification loops in deployed agents.
Relevance
9/10

Directly addresses missing-information handling and constructive uncertainty behavior in reasoning systems.

Evidence basis. A full training pipeline, benchmark, and failure taxonomy are provided.

Main limit. The work is focused on synthetic reasoning tasks rather than deployed agents.

What changes the score. Higher if multi-turn acquisition and tool use were tested.

Novelty
8/10

The main addition is a ranked response policy that rewards asking and conditioning above simple refusal.

Evidence basis. ACA-RL combines controlled missing-premise generation with a five-class terminal reward and MPB.

Main limit. Several adjacent literatures already study abstention, ambiguity, and unanswerable questions.

What changes the score. Higher with clearer evidence that the policy transfers beyond synthetic logical gaps.

Method strength
7/10

The paper has held-out benchmark construction, human checks, multiple model families, baselines, and ablations.

Evidence basis. The filter has human-labeled validation, MPB is held out, and the study includes data-source, ratio, step, initialization, and size analyses.

Main limit. The same behavior taxonomy drives training reward and benchmark scoring, and most results are point estimates.

What changes the score. Higher with independent evaluators, confidence intervals, and more external task families.

Evidence strength
7/10

The main MPB gains are large versus Vanilla PPO and smaller but consistent versus IDK-RL.

Evidence basis. Table 1 repeats the pattern in Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and Llama3.1-8B.

Main limit. The evidence is rubric-aligned, synthetic, and lacks broad statistical uncertainty reporting.

What changes the score. Higher with larger human-scored samples, independent scoring rubrics, and significance or uncertainty estimates.

External validity
5/10

The paper does not show that the policy works on organic user ambiguity or real agent workflows.

Evidence basis. MPB covers math, logic, and real-world word problems and UMWP/SUM add third-party checks.

Main limit. All training data come from DeepScaleR transformations, and active elicitation remains single-turn text.

What changes the score. Higher with natural user queries, domain diversity, multi-turn tasks, retrieval, and tool use.

Implementation value
9/10

The reward taxonomy, data-generation workflow, benchmark scoring, and PPO settings are concrete enough to reuse as a design reference.

Evidence basis. The paper specifies the five scores, 120K training setup, held-out MPB process, and core PPO hyperparameters.

Main limit. The inspected text does not expose a direct repository URL, and the source has a six-versus-seven perturbation-count inconsistency.

What changes the score. Higher with a verified repository, fully reproducible prompts, and independent evaluation code.

Main strengths

  • The response taxonomy distinguishes helpful uncertainty behavior from generic refusal.
  • The benchmark is held out from training and includes human verification.
  • The paper tests three model groups and several ablations instead of presenting one headline comparison.
  • Table 5 makes the difference between refusal rate and broader constructive behavior visible.

Main limitations

  • The training reward and benchmark judge use the same behavior definitions.
  • Synthetic logical gaps may be easier to localize than organic ambiguity.
  • Active elicitation is not tested as a multi-turn process that obtains the missing premise.
  • Most benchmark tables report point estimates without uncertainty intervals or formal significance tests.
  • The reported evaluator-agreement percentages lack sample counts in the inspected text.
  • The six-versus-seven perturbation count is internally unclear.

Implementation takeaways

Separate detection from response choice

A system can detect that information is missing and still choose a poor next action. ACA-RL makes the action policy explicit. Asking, conditioning, abstaining, and guessing are different outcomes.

Do not use IDK as the only safe target

Table 5 shows why a binary refusal metric can hide useful behavior. If a missing variable can be named or requested, reward that separately from generic abstention.

Keep an independent evaluation path

The largest design risk here is rubric coupling. A production evaluation should add independent judges, human review, and test cases that were not generated by the same transformation logic.

Test the full acquisition loop

For agents, the next test is not whether the model writes a clarification question. It is whether the system asks, receives or retrieves the missing fact, updates state, and finishes the task without inventing context.

Build-useful idea. The five-category taxonomy is a good candidate for an agent reliability test harness. Keep its scores separate from task correctness, then test whether the chosen behavior actually resolves the missing information in a subsequent step.
Do not overclaim. This paper does not validate calibrated confidence, clinical or safety-critical decision making, retrieval quality, tool-use reliability, or multi-turn recovery.
