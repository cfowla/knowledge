Scope warning. This paper establishes a reproducible phenomenon in the tested training regimes; it does not demonstrate that every frontier-scale long-context model will lose parametric knowledge, nor does it identify a universal optimal context length.

One-page synthesis

Core thesis. The training context window is not merely a channel for more tokens. When task-relevant information is readily available in context, optimization may favor a context-dependent solution over storing the same information in parameters. The authors call the hypothesis the Information Abundance Paradox and the resulting behavioral fragility context addiction.

The paper triangulates this thesis across several levels. In controlled natural-language pretraining, context windows from 512 to 32K tokens are swept at fixed token and update budgets across 20M–750M Llama-2-style models. Language-modeling loss improves until about 8K and then worsens, while SuperGLUE and closed-book MCQA peak around 2K and then decline. In Qwen3 SFT, the total document budget is fixed at eight while the number of target-domain documents increases from k=0 to 4 to 8. More relevant train-time context improves performance when supporting context is present at test time but reduces no-context robustness and sharply increases vulnerability to conflicting context.

Mechanistic evidence is layered rather than singular. In synthetic tasks, context addiction appears selectively when more demonstrations coincide with lower average training gradient norm, used as a complexity/optimization-path proxy. In natural pretraining and SFT, informative context reduces the FFN-to-self-attention gradient-norm ratio. Module-restricted tuning then provides causal support: self-attention-only updates improve supporting-context performance but worsen conflicting-context performance relative to FFN-only updates. Finally, context-trained models allocate more inference-time attention to context tokens, especially in middle layers. A theoretical monotonicity result shows that with nested inputs, longer context can weakly reduce the minimum task information that must be stored in the weights to achieve a fixed risk threshold.

Best contribution. The paper turns a plausible qualitative concern—“more context can change what gets learned”—into a multi-method training-time hypothesis with controlled relevance manipulations, statistical tests, mechanistic interventions, and an explicit theoretical boundary.
Main constraint. The most controlled natural-language pretraining evidence stops at 750M parameters and uses a selected Gutenberg corpus; the SFT manipulation uses generated supporting/conflicting documents. The result is strong evidence for the phenomenon in these regimes, not a frontier-scale law.

Section-by-section condensation

Abstract & Introduction

The paper challenges a common long-context scaling assumption: if sufficiently good long-context data exist, increasing the training window should only help. The motivating Phi-3 and OLMo 3 comparisons show that longer-context checkpoints often underperform shorter-context counterparts in few- and zero-shot benchmarks, prompting the question of whether the training window itself changes the learned strategy.

The authors distinguish internalization (task information encoded in weights) from contextualization (task information recovered from the current context). Their central hypothesis is that information-rich training contexts can lower the loss through contextualization and thereby reduce pressure to internalize reusable knowledge.

§2 — Information Abundance Paradox

“Information abundance” means task-relevant information available inside a training context, not simply corpus size or entropy. The predicted behavioral signature is context addiction: a model trained with informative context performs well when that context remains helpful but loses robustness when context is removed or made misleading.

§3.1 — Pretraining with varying context length

Four Llama-2-style models (20M, 55M, 259M, 750M) train on 10B tokens from long Project Gutenberg documents. Context windows are 512, 1K, 2K, 4K, 8K, 16K, and 32K; model initialization, corpus, optimizer, approximately 1.05M tokens/step, and 9,537 updates are matched within scale. This isolates window length under a fixed token/update budget, but not a fixed FLOP budget.

Across scales, SuperGLUE and closed-book MCQA peak around 2K and then decline; language-modeling CE loss reaches its best aggregate level around 8K and then rises. Segmented-regression inflections are significant across all 12 model-scale × suite combinations. Per-dataset analysis suggests the preferred train-time window partly tracks evaluation-example length: PTB (mean 28 tokens) is best at 512, LAMBADA (88) at 2K, WikiSPAN (504) at 8K. RoPE, ALiBi, and LongRoPE show the same qualitative trend in the 259M ablation.

§3.2 — SFT with varying context informativeness

Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 14B are fine-tuned on Health, Economics, Law, and Psychology subsets of MMLU-Pro. Every training prompt has eight prepended documents; k=0,4,8 controls how many are target-domain while the remainder come from a paired other domain. Evaluation is held out within domain and uses supporting target-domain context, conflicting target-domain context, or no context. Supporting/conflicting documents are generated with Gemini; token-length distributions are similar, so relevance is the intended manipulation.

Increasing k raises supporting-context performance but lowers no-context accuracy and enlarges the supporting–conflicting gap. From k=0→8, no-context accuracy changes by −5.4, −3.6, −5.3, −1.7, and −6.3 percentage points for Qwen3 0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, and 14B; all but the 8B trend are statistically significant. The supporting–conflicting gap increases by +42.8, +25.6, +23.3, +23.6, and +18.0 points, significant at all five sizes.

§4 — Theoretical account

The authors define a parametric information frontier Ik(ρ): the minimum mutual information I(W;τ) between learned weights and the latent task required to achieve risk ≤ρ with context size k. If shorter inputs are measurable projections of longer inputs, then any k-context predictor can be simulated by an m-context predictor for m>k using the same weight channel.

Therefore Im(ρ)≤Ik(ρ). This is an achievability result: longer context weakly expands the feasible predictor set and can reduce the minimum task information required in weights. It does not show that gradient descent necessarily chooses the minimum-information solution.

§5.1 — Solution complexity

In synthetic pretraining, models solve four deterministic task families with varying numbers of demonstrations. Bitwise and string tasks develop larger supporting–conflicting gaps as context grows; mod10 remains stable and Caesar shows only a limited longest-context deviation. Average pre-clipping gradient norm falls significantly only for bitwise (τ=−0.409, p=.0037) and string (τ=−0.641, p=6.26×10⁻¹⁰), the task families that develop context addiction.

The authors interpret this alignment as evidence that context addiction emerges when demonstrations provide a lower-complexity/easier optimization path than internalizing the rule. Full model-scale results show the association persists and context addiction strengthens with model size in susceptible tasks.

§5.2 — Module-level gradient allocation

Across SFT, k=8 context training lowers the FFN-to-self-attention gradient-norm ratio in all 20 model-domain comparisons; 19 are significant. In pretraining, the ratio declines significantly with each context doubling at every model scale (mean slopes −0.038 to −0.054).

Module-restricted SFT supplies the strongest causal mechanism test. Relative to FFN-only tuning, SA-only tuning improves supporting-context accuracy by +4.5, +9.0, and +2.2 points in 0.6B, 1.7B, and 4B models, but reduces conflicting-context accuracy by −5.9, −10.0, and −12.0 points. This supports the interpretation that attention-directed updates increase context dependence while FFN-directed updates preserve more context-independent robustness.

§5.3 — Token-level attention allocation

Models fine-tuned with k=8 task-relevant documents allocate more inference-time attention mass to context tokens than models fine-tuned without context. In Qwen3-1.7B, cluster-corrected differences are significant across layers 4–16 in Law, Health, Psychology, and Economics; supplementary figures show the same middle-layer pattern at 0.6B, 8B, and 14B.

§6–7 — Related work, discussion, conclusion

The paper situates itself between long-context scaling/extension work, in-context versus parametric learning, and language modeling as compression. Its distinct focus is not whether long contexts can be used, but whether the training context changes where predictive information is stored and which inference pathway the model later relies on.

The authors explicitly limit the natural-language pretraining evidence to ≤750M parameters. Their conclusion is not that long contexts are undesirable, but that context capacity should be evaluated jointly with context-independent competence and robustness to missing or misleading evidence.

Evaluation and appraisal

Verdict: Read first. This is a high-value conceptual and experimental warning for long-context model training. The claim is credible enough to change what should be measured during training and adaptation, but not yet broad enough to be treated as a universal scaling law.

Relevance

9/10

Directly addresses long-context training, robustness, parametric-versus-contextual knowledge, and evaluation design.

Evidence basis: Multiple experiments connect training context to later context reliance.

Principal limit: Relevance is high even though the paper is not clinical.

Would raise/lower: A frontier-scale replication or agent/RAG deployment study would raise practical relevance further.

Novelty

8/10

The core framing unifies non-monotonic context scaling with a parametric/contextual learning tradeoff and supports it across theory and mechanisms.

Evidence basis: Controlled relevance manipulation plus module interventions goes beyond a descriptive long-context benchmark.

Principal limit: Related work already recognizes short-context degradation, representation drift, and contextual versus parametric modes.

Would raise/lower: Independent replications showing the same shift across architectures/data regimes would strengthen novelty as a general principle.

Method strength

7/10

Experiments are unusually controlled for a systems paper: token/update matching, multiple scales/seeds, fixed SFT document budget, domain blocks, statistical testing, ablations, and causal module interventions.

Evidence basis: Figures 2–7 plus Appendices B–H provide converging tests.

Principal limit: Natural pretraining ends at 750M; SFT contexts are synthetic; MMLU-Pro is repartitioned; pretraining is not FLOP-matched.

Would raise/lower: Larger pretrained models, natural retrieval contexts, additional corpora, and pre-registered robustness metrics would raise the score.

Evidence strength

7/10

Data strongly support context-dependence in the tested regimes, and the causal module restriction is a meaningful mechanistic addition.

Evidence basis: All 12 pretraining inflection tests are significant; SFT gap trends significant at all sizes; gradient/attention tests align.

Principal limit: The theoretical result establishes possibility, not optimizer inevitability; gradient norm and FFN/SA role assignments remain proxies/interpretations.

Would raise/lower: Direct measures of retained parametric knowledge, weight-space information, and replicated interventions across architectures would raise confidence.

External validity

5/10

Evidence spans multiple scales and Qwen3 sizes but remains narrow relative to frontier training and real long-context deployments.

Evidence basis: Natural-language pretraining, four SFT domains, synthetic tasks, and external-model motivation provide some breadth.

Principal limit: Gutenberg selection, ≤750M pretraining, generated contexts, and same-benchmark-domain held-out evaluation constrain generalization.

Would raise/lower: Replication on modern trillion-token mixtures, frontier-sized models, natural RAG corpora, and deployed agents would raise the score.

Implementation value

8/10

The paper yields actionable training/evaluation guardrails even without a universal fix.

Evidence basis: It identifies concrete failure tests: no-context, conflicting-context, short-context retention, gradient allocation, and context-attention shifts.

Principal limit: It does not directly compare mitigation strategies such as curricula, context dropout, retrieval corruption training, or mixed-window objectives.

Would raise/lower: Intervention studies showing which recipes preserve both long-context ability and parametric competence would raise implementation value.

Independent strengths

  • Controlled natural-language context-window sweep keeps tokens and updates fixed within model scale and repeats across four scales.
  • SFT fixes total document count while varying relevance, a clean test that separates informative context from token quantity.
  • Supporting, conflicting, and absent-context evaluations operationalize context dependence rather than relying only on in-context performance.
  • Behavioral, optimization, module-intervention, attention, and theoretical analyses converge on the same directional story.
  • Appendices report per-dataset results, compute, hyperparameters, full SFT tables, and explicit statistical tests rather than hiding the evidence behind aggregate plots.

Independent limitations and interpretation risks

  • The most controlled pretraining evidence is small by contemporary standards (20M–750M) and uses a selected long-document Gutenberg corpus; scaling of the inflection is unknown.
  • Token/update matching isolates a useful training variable but does not equalize compute. Table 2 shows substantially higher GPU-hours at long windows, so long-context variants are both more expensive and eventually worse on these evaluations.
  • SFT contexts are generated to be supporting or adversarially conflicting. This is excellent for causal control but may overstate how sharply natural retrieval context induces the same behavior.
  • MMLU-Pro examples are repartitioned from the benchmark test split. Held-out examples are separated from SFT training, but the study does not establish that Qwen3 pretraining was uncontaminated by these benchmark items.
  • The mechanistic mapping “FFN = parametric storage; attention = contextualization” is directionally plausible and intervention-supported, but neither module has an exclusive function. The evidence is about relative update pathways, not a complete localization of knowledge.
  • The attention analysis reports the maximum head within each layer, which is suitable for sparse specialized heads but can emphasize a small subset of heads; it should not be read as a layer-average dependence measure.
  • The theoretical monotonicity proposition is deliberately weak: more context enlarges the feasible predictor set and can lower required parametric information. It does not predict the observed inverted-U curve without optimization assumptions.
  • The Phi-3/OLMo observations are not single-variable experiments because the long checkpoints result from additional training/context-extension stages. They support motivation, not causal attribution.
What not to overclaim. The paper does not show that long-context models necessarily “forget” facts, that attention literally contains all contextual knowledge, or that 2K/8K are generally optimal training windows. It shows a context-dependent learning tradeoff under specific, well-controlled regimes.

Implementation / research takeaways

Measure short/no-context retention

Any long-context adaptation run should retain a closed-book or minimal-context evaluation suite. Long-context gains alone can hide reduced context-independent competence.

Add conflicting-context tests

Evaluate not only retrieval-supported accuracy but behavior when retrieved evidence is wrong, irrelevant, or adversarially plausible. The supporting–conflicting gap is a useful dependence diagnostic.

Vary relevance separately from length

When studying context effects, fix total context budget where possible and manipulate information content/relevance; otherwise length and usefulness remain confounded.

Consider mixed-window curricula

The results motivate testing context dropout, short/long window mixtures, and curricula that explicitly preserve parametric learning pressure. The paper does not establish which mitigation is best.

Track training-path diagnostics

FFN/SA gradient allocation and context-attention shifts may serve as early warning signals for increasing context dependence, but should be treated as diagnostics rather than safety guarantees.

Do not chase one universal optimum

Appendix E suggests the beneficial window depends partly on evaluation-instance length. Optimization should be task/data-specific and judged across both contextual and context-free competence.
