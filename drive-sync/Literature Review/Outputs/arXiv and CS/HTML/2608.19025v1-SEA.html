One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. This paper is unusually useful for an ATOM and SEA pipeline because it does not stop at average extraction accuracy. It shows where the workflow fails, how repeated runs expose instability, and when disagreement should send a case to a person.

The fixed-corpus result is strong but conditional. With an expert prompt and the correct PDFs already supplied, the best model reached 95.7% overall accuracy and 99.5% on numerical affinity fields. Context was harder. Correct troponin-preparation identification reached 80.7%, and 19.2% of fields moved between correct and incorrect across five runs. The practical lesson is not "LLMs extract at 95%." A well-scoped corpus and explicit schema make extraction tractable. Context fields and unstable answers still need review.

The autonomy experiment is the sharper warning. The best Deep Research agent recovered only 53.7% of the expert reference set on average. Two systems fabricated citations, including 27 of 60 unique citations from Qwen3.7-Plus. Cross-model consensus helped on the organoid task. A unanimous five-run vote matched human consensus 93.2% of the time, while a 3-2 split matched it only 55.2%. Five calls spread across different models reached 93.3% agreement with reviewer consensus versus 89.2% for five calls to one model. The clean pipeline rule is to verify citations independently, use several model families when possible, and send split votes to expert review.

Section condensation

Introduction and study design

The authors treat scientific extraction as a provenance and context problem, not a value-copying task. They separate browser products from raw APIs because browser systems may add hidden instructions, retrieval, memory, moderation, and changing compute allocation.

Figure 1 defines four workflows with increasing autonomy: expert prompt plus fixed corpus; self-authored prompt plus fixed corpus; autonomous discovery plus extraction; and a reporting-guideline dataset compared with human reviewers.

Expert prompt and fixed corpus

Seven models processed 18 articles five times each. Scoring covered species, temperature, troponin preparation, measurement method, magnesium, and two affinity values. GPT-5.5 led at 95.7% overall. The three leading models averaged 92.6%, compared with 86.4% for the other four.

Numbers were easier than context. Models found K and Kd correctly in 99.0% and 99.8% of cases, but the troponin preparation in 80.7%. Across models, 76.4% of fields were correct in all five runs, 19.2% varied, and 4.4% were wrong in every run. Model consensus also exposed mistakes in the original reference dataset.

Self-authored prompts

Each model wrote three prompts and aggregated them. Aggregation improved rubric coverage, but rubric rank did not predict extraction rank well. GPT-5.5's master prompt scored 88.2% on the 38-component rubric and achieved 90.3% extraction accuracy.

Every self-authored master prompt underperformed the expert prompt. The measurement-method field fell most, by 27.5 percentage points on average. This supports expert-authored field definitions and conflict rules.

Autonomous literature discovery

Seven Deep Research agents ran five searches each for up to 20 pre-2005 sources. Opus 4.8 had the best mean reference recall at 53.7%. GPT-5.5 reached 38.9%. Qwen3.7-Plus and GLM-5.2 fabricated 45% and 29% of their unique citations. The other five systems produced none in this sample.

End-to-end extraction remained weak because missing sources dominated the result. Opus 4.8 reached 39.9% field accuracy. When a source condition was recovered and a number was supplied, the value was often close. Conditional numeric accuracy cannot repair low corpus recall.

Organoid reporting and human disagreement

Seven models assessed 10 hand-selected brain-organoid papers against 23 criteria. Two PhD reviewers agreed on 79.1% of binary calls. Mean LLM agreement across the two reviewers was 81.1%, so reviewer identity changed the apparent benchmark.

Vote margin carried useful information. Agreement with human consensus fell from 93.2% for 5-0 model votes to 65.0% for 4-1 votes and 55.2% for 3-2 votes. Cross-model allocation improved the five-call result by 4.1 percentage points over same-model repetition.

Methods, availability, and conclusion

The experiments used browser interfaces, recorded named model modes, retained quotations, and manually verified every discovered citation. The repository contains prompts, raw outputs, curated workbooks, and a tutorial. The paper reports generative AI use for analysis scripts and copy-editing.

The proposed split is sensible: experts define the task and evidence standard, models perform repeated extraction, and people resolve disputed or high-impact cases. The exact results are time-sensitive because the paper documents model and interface changes during data collection.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance 10/10

The paper tests the exact failure boundaries that matter for a structured literature pipeline.

Evidence: fixed-corpus extraction, discovery, human review, and ensemble allocation. Limit: narrow scientific domains. A production-task mismatch would lower the score.

Novelty 8/10

The linked progression from prompt design to source discovery and review routing adds real value.

Evidence: four escalating experiments. Limit: consensus and repeated extraction are not new alone. Independent replication would raise the score.

Method strength 7/10

Repeated runs, manual citation checks, corrected ground truth, and released raw data support the main comparisons.

Limit: no preregistration, changing browsers, selected corpora, and limited inferential statistics. Frozen systems and blinded adjudication would raise the score.

Evidence strength 7/10

The results support expert-scoped extraction and human review for discovery failures and disputed cases.

Limit: the experiments do not establish universal accuracy or causal superiority of consensus. External replication and failure-cost analysis would raise the score.

External validity 5/10

The workflow pattern should transfer. The percentages probably will not.

Evidence: two domains and several model families. Limit: browser drift and a hand-selected organoid corpus. Prospective multi-domain testing would raise the score.

Implementation value 9/10

The paper gives clear review gates and releases prompts, raw outputs, curated data, and a tutorial.

Limit: cost, latency, audit logging, and thresholds were not optimized. A production cost-risk study would raise the score.
Main concern. The numbers are tied to browser products in August 2026. The paper itself documents interface refusals, compute changes, and model replacement. Treat the controls as transferable. Treat the leaderboard as perishable.

Best use: designing citation verification, consensus review, and human-escalation rules for ATOM and SEA. Do not use for: claiming automated review is complete, that majority vote proves truth, or that one model's browser result will persist.

Implementation takeaways

Adopt nowRequire primary PDFs, source-type classification, page or quote anchors, stability checks for high-value extraction, and independent citation resolution.
Use selectivelyCross-model calls help most for ambiguous claims, context-heavy fields, and quality-control samples. They do not replace an evidence standard.
Add to ATOMStore run ID, model and interface, prompt version, input hash, vote distribution, and adjudication status. One final value hides the audit trail.
Add to SEASeparate fixed-corpus extraction performance from search recall. Report citation and source-type failures beside claims about autonomous research.
MonitorRe-run golden cases after model or browser updates. Product changes can alter behavior without a protocol change.
Do not overclaimThe study uses two narrow scientific domains and selected corpora. It supports workflow controls, not replacement of expert curation.
