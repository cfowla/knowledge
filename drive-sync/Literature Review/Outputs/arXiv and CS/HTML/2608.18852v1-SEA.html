Scope warning. This is a single-run preprint evaluation of one 9B training setup under a fixed 16-candidate slate. It supports the claim that token-local selector credit improves the tested system. It does not establish how SkillGate scales with model size, candidate slate size, or a full library of thousands of skills.

One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first. The paper is directly useful for agent systems that make small but consequential choices inside long trajectories. The main contribution is specific enough to implement and the paper provides a good audit trail for why outcome-only credit fails.

Core thesis

Outcome-only reinforcement learning broadcasts one trajectory-level advantage across both the few tokens that name a selected skill and the thousands of tokens that execute the task. The paper calls the resulting failure selector credit starvation. SkillGate cuts the read call out of task-outcome credit and adds a separate action-local selector advantage on the skill identity tokens.

What the study did

The authors train from a common Qwen3.5-9B supervised initialization on 491 tasks. Evaluation covers Claw-Eval, SkillsBench, SETA, SWE, and Terminal-Bench 2.0. Each standard slate contains 16 candidates: one oracle, five misleading hard negatives, five relevant bystanders, and five irrelevant bystanders. The main outcome protocol has 385 trials. A 280-trial repeated subset supports detailed read-behavior analysis and a 147-task held-out Claw subset tests generalization.

Scaling read

The paper says model scale alone does not solve selection, but its evidence here is limited. Frontier models are capability references, not controlled baselines. SkillGate itself is trained only at 9B, and K stays fixed at 16 despite a source library of 2,045 skills. The paper therefore shows a selection problem that persists in larger reference models, not a controlled scaling law for SkillGate.

Section condensation

Abstract and Introduction

The paper frames skill selection as a policy decision made during an agent episode, after the model sees names and short descriptions but before it opens a skill body. Existing skill libraries can contain thousands of entries, so progressive disclosure shifts a large share of the problem into routing. The authors argue that ordinary outcome-rewarded RL does not give this selector decision a useful training signal.

The contribution is a mechanistic diagnosis and an objective change. The diagnosis is selector credit starvation. The objective partitions execution credit and selector credit so the few identity tokens are no longer trained by the final task outcome.

Problem Setup and SkillGate

The mixed slate is designed to make routing nontrivial: the oracle competes with misleading near-matches and bystanders. SkillGate locates read-action spans and removes them from the task mask. It then applies selector advantage only to the skill identity span. A clean single-oracle read has utility 1. Other read patterns have utility 0, and the utility is centered within the prompt group.

The two channels are separately normalized so each has fixed total mass when selector tokens exist. The selector coefficient is 0.20. This choice directly attacks length dilution. It also encodes a strong preference for a single correct read, which later matters for the method’s limitations.

Experiments and Results

The controlled training comparison uses the same 9B initialization, training steps, and core RL budget. SkillGate improves main trial success to 53.2%, raises oracle exposure to 83.9%, and drops misleading exposure to 21.8%. Credit-placement ablations show that prompt-group or trajectory-level bonuses do not improve the anchor, while action-local credit changes selection behavior and SkillGate’s single-read condition improves selection purity on the main repeated protocol.

The external-router experiment shows that a separate selector does not reproduce SkillGate’s task result for the frozen SFT executor. The offline audit gives the paper a stronger mechanism story: identity tokens have tiny loss share, that share falls with horizon, wrong-sign credit rises with horizon, and oracle reads are associated with materially better outcomes. The held-out Claw analysis preserves the selection advantage. Bootstrap uncertainty supports the pooled trial-success difference but not pooled pass@4.

Related Work and Conclusion

The paper positions SkillGate among skill retrieval, tool selection, and finer-grained credit-assignment methods. Its distinction is that one policy learns its own mid-episode read decision from on-policy trajectories without a separate selector architecture.

The conclusion states three practical limits. Each configuration is a single run. Correct skill identity must be known for training tasks. The action-local scheme cannot credit abstention. Those are meaningful constraints, not footnotes.

Figures and tables

Figure 1. Selector credit starvation

Purpose. Motivate the failure mechanism before training SkillGate.

Content. Three panels show selector-token loss share, wrong-sign credit, and the task value of reading the oracle. Identity tokens have median 0.14% loss-weight share, the share falls about sevenfold with trajectory length, wrong-sign oracle credit rises from 28.9% to 61.7%, and matched prompt groups show +11.2 percentage points task success with an oracle read.

Interpretation. The paper isolates a plausible credit-assignment defect that worsens with horizon.

Limits. The +11.2-point association is behavior-conditioned, not randomized. Loss-weight share is not gradient norm.

Figure 2. SkillGate overview

Purpose. Show where each training signal lands.

Content. Task outcome credit reaches execution tokens after the complete read call is removed. Selector credit reaches only the skill identity tokens. Clean single-oracle reads receive positive selector utility after within-group centering.

Interpretation. The intervention changes credit placement rather than the rollout environment or optimizer family.

Limits. The selector utility assumes that one correct skill is known during training and favors a single read.

Figure 5. Offline audit of outcome-only training

Purpose. Test whether selector credit starvation appears in the completed training artifacts.

Content. The audit covers 12,800 on-policy trajectories. Identity-token loss-weight share falls with trajectory length, within-group signal-to-noise falls, and the share of oracle reads receiving negative task advantage crosses 50% in the longest bin. Interpretation. This is the paper’s strongest mechanistic evidence because it audits the exact run used as the outcome-only baseline.

Limits. The audit is post hoc and descriptive. It supports the mechanism but does not substitute for multiple independent training replications.

Evaluation and appraisal

Relevance
9.5 / 10

Rationale. Direct fit to long-horizon agents, skill routing, credit assignment, and progressive disclosure.

Evidence basis. Controlled skill-selection experiments plus a training-artifact audit.

Limiting factor. No production registry or clinical workflow evaluation.

Would raise confidence. Replication in deployed skill libraries with live routing logs.

Novelty
8.0 / 10

Rationale. The useful idea is a narrow token-support partition for a mid-trajectory selector action, backed by a concrete failure diagnosis.

Evidence basis. Mechanism, token-local objective, ablation, and audit are tied together.

Limiting factor. Adjacent work already studies step, turn, segment, and tool-specific credit.

Would raise confidence. Independent comparisons against other action-local credit methods under the same setup.

Method strength
7.5 / 10

Rationale. The matched-budget 9B comparison, ablations, held-out Claw set, raw denominators, and clustered bootstrap are well designed for a preprint.

Evidence basis. Five benchmarks, fixed task/slate controls, 491 training tasks, 385-trial main evaluation, and 12,800-trajectory audit.

Limiting factor. Each configuration is one training run.

Would raise confidence. Multiple seeds, preregistered evaluation, and a controlled slate-size sweep.

Evidence strength
7.0 / 10

Rationale. The central trial-success gain has an interval excluding zero and the behavioral mechanism is consistent across tables and audit results.

Evidence basis. +7.9-point pooled trial-success difference, 95% interval +2.1 to +14.3, plus large oracle and misleading-exposure shifts.

Limiting factor. Pass@4 interval crosses zero and per-benchmark intervals are wide. Training-seed variance is unknown.

Would raise confidence. Replicated checkpoints and external reproduction.

External validity
5.5 / 10

Rationale. Five benchmarks help, but the selector is tested in one fixed slate design and one trained model scale.

Evidence basis. Cross-benchmark evaluation and 147 held-out Claw tasks.

Limiting factor. K is fixed at 16, training needs a known oracle, and the public library contains 2,045 skills without an end-to-end library-size sweep. Frontier models are not controlled scaling evidence.

Would raise confidence. Controlled K and library-size scaling, more model sizes, multilingual or domain-specific registries, and abstention-heavy tasks.

Implementation value
9.0 / 10

Rationale. The credit split maps cleanly to instrumented agent training when read actions and identity spans are observable.

Evidence basis. Explicit masks, utility, normalization, hyperparameters, prompt interface, code link, and model link.

Limiting factor. Requires reliable action attribution and oracle labels. It does not solve abstention credit.

Would raise confidence. Reference implementation tests showing robustness to malformed reads, multiple reads, partial matches, and noisy oracle labels.

Strengths

  • The paper connects a concrete training failure to an observable token-level mechanism.
  • The matched-budget comparison and credit-placement ablations isolate the intended design choice better than a broad model leaderboard would.
  • Behavioral metrics are reported alongside task success, so a method cannot look better merely by reading more skills.
  • Raw counts, held-out Claw results, and task-clustered bootstrap intervals make the main result easier to audit.

Main limitations

  • Single training run per configuration leaves training-seed variance unresolved.
  • The training signal requires an oracle skill label, which is expensive or ambiguous in open-ended registries.
  • The fixed 16-candidate slate does not test selector behavior as the visible candidate set or underlying library grows.
  • The model-scale argument is observational. Frontier rows use different models and compatible interfaces and are explicitly not controlled.
  • Abstention is not rewarded by the selector channel, even though the prompt allows no-read behavior when no skill applies.
Do not overclaim. SkillGate is not evidence that a 9B selector beats frontier agents in general, that selection failure worsens according to a known scaling law, or that a 16-candidate result transfers unchanged to 2,000-plus-skill registries. The controlled claim is narrower and useful: under the tested setup, token-local selector credit improves both selection purity and task success versus the same-budget outcome-only baseline.

Implementation takeaways

Instrument decision spans.

If an agent makes a discrete choice inside a long rollout, log the exact tokens or action span that expresses the choice. Final reward alone may barely train it.

Separate selector and executor metrics.

Track oracle or appropriate choice, misleading choice, abstention, read count, and downstream task outcome. A selector can improve while execution stays weak, or vice versa.

Keep credit channels explicit.

Do not let execution failure automatically punish a correct routing decision when the two can be attributed separately.

Test noisy supervision.

Production registries rarely have a perfect oracle. The next implementation test should vary label quality and allow multiple acceptable skills.

Test K, not only model size.

Run controlled slate sizes and library sizes. K=16 is useful evidence, but it is not the scale regime implied by thousands of installed skills.

Add abstention credit.

A production selector needs a positive training path for choosing no skill when none applies. SkillGate’s current action-local utility does not supply one.
