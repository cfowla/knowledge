One-page synthesis

Verdict: Read first for benchmark/evaluation design; do not use as clinical-practice evidence. The paper is useful for designing a local medical-report extraction harness, especially if the goal is to compare direct image-to-JSON extraction against OCR-assisted LLM pipelines. It is not sufficient to justify patient-facing automated interpretation without local validation, clinical review, safety gating, and de-identification governance.

Core thesis

MedRepBench targets a narrow but practical problem: extracting structured fields from real-world medical report images and generating patient-facing, report-grounded explanations. The authors argue that existing document and medical VQA benchmarks do not adequately test this structured, noisy, layout-sensitive report-interpretation task.

What the benchmark measures

The objective protocol measures field-level recall over five attributes: item name, value, unit, reference range, and abnormality flag. The subjective protocol uses an LLM judge to score factuality, reasoning validity, and patient-facing clarity on a 0–2 scale.

Main empirical finding

OCR-assisted pipelines remain stronger than direct no-OCR VLM extraction, especially for values and reference ranges. The best no-OCR VLM baseline in Table 1 averages about 78.60% recall, while the best OCR-assisted VLM averages about 90.31%. OCR-assisted text-only LLMs reach about 91.5% average recall in the strongest rows.

Most useful idea

The Top-K field-level recall design is a clean extraction benchmark pattern: output is truncated to the number of ground-truth items, so hallucinated items displace correct ones and reduce recall even without an explicit precision term.

Most important limitation

Ground truth is bootstrapped from proprietary OCR plus DeepSeek-R1 parsing and then audited. This is practical, but it means the benchmark’s validity depends on OCR quality, schema normalization, and the depth of human review.

Bottom line

Use this paper as a benchmark-design reference and a source of evaluation ideas. Do not treat it as proof that VLMs can safely interpret patient reports in production, especially outside Chinese report formats or without independent clinical validation.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract frames the gap as insufficient benchmarking of structured understanding for medical report images. MedRepBench contains 1,925 de-identified Chinese medical report images across departments, demographics, and acquisition formats, and it deliberately focuses on report-grounded extraction and explanation rather than diagnosis, treatment, or patient-history integration.

The evaluation has two protocols: objective field-level recall for structured report items and automated subjective scoring of patient-facing explanations by an LLM judge. The authors also report a GRPO-based alignment baseline for an InternVL3-8B model with up to a 6% absolute field-recall gain, and they argue that OCR+LLM pipelines are accurate but fragile because layout and OCR errors can propagate.

1 Introduction

The introduction distinguishes report interpretation from report generation. Interpretation means extracting structured clinical findings and generating lay explanations grounded strictly in report content; generation means producing narrative clinical reports. The paper positions the task as relevant to at-home monitoring, physician-patient communication, referral workflows, and electronic information exchange.

The claimed benchmark gap is driven by noisy real-world images, mixed acquisition methods, variable report layouts, and the need to compare end-to-end VLMs with OCR-assisted pipelines. The authors explicitly argue that OCR-assisted systems are often practical and accurate, but that end-to-end visual understanding remains important for layout-aware and text-token-free VLM progress.

2.1 Dataset construction

The authors sample 8,000 reports from a private real-world online medical service source, then apply de-identification, quality filtering, deduplication, and content validation to obtain 1,925 high-quality images. Report sources include photos, PDFs/e-documents, and mobile screenshots from laboratory and examination report types.

Structured labels are produced by proprietary medical OCR followed by DeepSeek-R1 JSON parsing under a constrained schema. Quality control includes heuristic filters, manual inspection of 500 samples, perceptual-image-hash plus OCR-text deduplication with 12.3% near-duplicates discarded, schema validation, rule-based normalization, and a 200-report human audit with reported field-level agreement above 97%.

2.2 Structured interpretation evaluation

The objective metric is field-level recall over five attributes: name, value, unit, reference range, and abnormality flag. Average recall is the mean across these fields, and each predicted item is matched against a reference item by normalized item name.

The most important design choice is Top-K output truncation, where K equals the number of ground-truth items in the report. This fixed-budget evaluation means spurious items occupy limited slots and can lower recall by displacing correct extractions, which partly offsets the lack of an explicit precision metric.

2.3 Automated subjective evaluation via LLM

The subjective protocol scores patient-facing explanations with DeepSeek-R1 using OCR-derived reference content. Dimensions include factuality, interpretability, and reasoning quality, where factuality means strict support from report content and interpretability means lay-readable organization without unsupported diagnosis or treatment recommendations.

The authors acknowledge that DeepSeek-R1 is used both for annotation and judging, but they state that main performance conclusions rely on objective field recall rather than LLM-judge scores. The judge is positioned as a ranking and qualitative diagnostic signal, with a later 60-case human-agreement pilot used as a sanity check.

2.4 Reinforcement learning optimization

The GRPO section uses benchmark recall as a non-differentiable reward for structured item extraction. The policy outputs a JSON list of item tuples, and reward is the average field-level recall over the five target fields after one-to-one matching by normalized item names.

The baseline starts from an InternVL3-8B model fine-tuned with supervised learning, freezes the vision encoder and image-to-text projector, inserts LoRA rank-128 modules into all LLM attention projections, and trains only LoRA weights for 2 epochs on 800 structured report samples. The authors present this as a practical baseline, not an algorithmic contribution.

3 Evaluation

The model suite includes open-source VLMs such as InternVL2.5, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, and LLaMA-4, plus text-only LLMs including Qwen3 variants and DeepSeek models under OCR-assisted input. Proprietary frontier APIs are excluded for reproducibility and privacy-compliance reasons.

The objective protocol applies only to laboratory-style reports because they have stable itemized fields. Examination reports are evaluated subjectively because their narrative structure would make item-level labels unreliable. For the abnormality flag, the model must derive abnormality from value-range comparison or discrete outcomes rather than simply copying text.

4 Results

Objective results show meaningful no-OCR VLM performance but consistent improvement with OCR assistance. Among Table 1 VLM baselines, LLaMA-4-Maverick has the highest calculated average recall in both no-OCR and OCR-assisted settings, while Table 2 text-only LLMs under OCR achieve the highest overall averages with DeepSeek-V3 and Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507.

Subjective results favor larger VLMs, especially Qwen2.5-VL-32B for acceptability and excellence. The paper reports no-OCR failure modes: fine-print numeric/unit recognition mistakes, nested-table or multicolumn layout misalignment, and missed abnormality flags when visual cues are subtle or spatially separated.

4.2 Subjective evaluation reliability

The LLM-judge reliability pilot samples 20 cases from each score category, giving 60 cases total. Three experienced annotators independently rate each case, and majority vote determines the human score; unresolved cases are replaced from the same category.

The reported confusion matrix gives 88.3% agreement and Cohen’s κ = 0.82. Most disagreements are adjacent-category errors, which supports the judge as a scalable diagnostic metric but does not make it a substitute for expert clinical assessment.

4.3 Practical alignment baseline

Ours-GRPO improves the SFT baseline from 73.31% to 79.45% average recall in the no-OCR setting and from 82.37% to 87.41% in the OCR-assisted setting. In the no-OCR comparison, Ours-GRPO slightly exceeds the listed LLaMA-4-Maverick and Qwen2.5-VL-32B baselines despite being an 8B model.

Subjectively, Ours-GRPO is not top-ranked: Qwen2.5-VL-32B has higher acceptability and excellence, while Ours-GRPO is near LLaMA-4-Maverick. The more durable contribution is showing that the benchmark’s recall metric can serve as a reward signal for targeted structured-extraction alignment.

5 Related work

The paper contrasts MedRepBench with medical report generation and medical VQA benchmarks. It argues that prior benchmarks generally emphasize narrative report generation, radiology/pathology VQA, clean OCR, synthetic templates, or broad document tasks rather than noisy real-world report-image structured interpretation.

The related-work section also situates LLMs as both OCR-assisted structured extractors and evaluators. The authors emphasize that their subjective evaluation is medical-factuality oriented and diagnostic, not an independent clinical assessment.

6 Conclusion and 7 Limitations

The conclusion restates that robust end-to-end image-to-structure extraction remains difficult and that benchmark-driven RL alignment can improve a mid-scale VLM. The strongest practical conclusion is that OCR-assisted methods remain powerful but are not the whole problem because layout, visual grounding, and OCR failure propagation matter.

The limitations are material: only open-source models are benchmarked; proprietary GPT/Gemini/Claude-style systems are excluded; the dataset is Chinese and not cross-lingually or cross-country validated; the baseline suite emphasizes general-domain VLMs rather than medical-specialized VLMs; and the paper does not claim deployment readiness.

Key equations and protocols

Field-level recall:
Recall_f = (# correctly extracted field f) / (# ground-truth field f)

Average recall:
AverageRecall = (1 / |F|) × Σ Recall_f, where F = {name, value, unit, reference_range, is_abnormal}

Top-K fixed-budget rule:
K = number of ground-truth items in the report. Model output is truncated to K items, so hallucinated items compete with correct extractions.

GRPO reward:
R = mean field-level recall across the five target fields after one-to-one matching by normalized item names.

AI/ML systems appraisal

Task

Vision-grounded structured understanding of medical report images, plus patient-facing explanations grounded only in report content.

Data

1,925 de-identified Chinese medical report images after curation from 8,000 reports; heterogeneous acquisition modes and departments; labels derived via proprietary OCR plus DeepSeek-R1 parsing with human audit.

Splits/leakage

The PDF describes evaluation and training subsets for GRPO, but does not provide enough detail in the paper text to fully audit train/test split construction, contamination checks, or overlap with pretraining data.

Baselines

Open-source VLMs and LLMs only; no proprietary frontier API comparison. Baselines are adequate for reproducibility framing but incomplete for “best available model” claims.

Metrics

Field-level recall and average recall are clear and useful. Missing or secondary: explicit precision/F1, uncertainty intervals per row, calibration, latency/cost, subgroup robustness, and safety-critical false-normal rates.

Human evaluation

Human audit of 200 reports for labels and 60-case judge validation are useful sanity checks. They are too small to settle clinical validity or broad inter-rater reliability.

Failure analysis

The paper identifies character-level value/unit mistakes, nested-table layout errors, and missed abnormality flags. This is practically useful for parser and VLM evaluation design.

Reproducibility

The dataset and evaluation resources are claimed public, but proprietary OCR and incomplete training details limit full end-to-end reproducibility from the paper alone.

Deployment relevance

High for evaluation harness design; low-to-moderate for direct deployment because there is no prospective workflow, clinician oversight model, local validation, or patient-safety evaluation.

Clinical-practice interpretation: This is not a clinical outcomes study. It should not be used to justify automated patient counseling, diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or report release without clinician review.

Implementation and workflow takeaways

Use the schema

Start with five fields: name, value, unit, reference_range, and is_abnormal. Add local fields only after the base schema is stable.

Evaluate with Top-K

Set K to the number of ground-truth items. This prevents models from improving recall by dumping long lists of plausible fields.

Keep OCR and no-OCR lanes separate

Run image-only VLM, OCR+VLM, and OCR+LLM as separate evaluation tracks. Do not mix results without labeling the input mode.

Measure abnormality errors directly

Track false-normal and false-abnormal classifications separately. A missed abnormal flag can matter more than a minor unit formatting error.

Audit labels independently

Do not rely only on LLM-generated labels. Use clinician or trained reviewer audit, especially for nested tables, handwritten annotations, abnormal flags, and reference ranges.

Validate locally

Before using any model on clinical reports, create a local test set from your institution’s templates and scanner/photo conditions. Report performance by document type and acquisition mode.

Separate extraction from counseling

Accurate field extraction does not prove safe patient-facing explanation. Evaluate hallucinated diagnoses, treatment advice, severity framing, and escalation wording separately.

Build failure dashboards

Track character errors, unit errors, range parsing errors, table alignment errors, missed items, duplicate items, and abnormality-flag errors by report template.

Governance requirements

Use de-identification checks, access controls, audit trails, model/version pinning, reviewer escalation, and periodic drift testing before any operational use.

Suggested local adaptation

1. Define a local structured schema for lab/report fields.
2. Create a 100–300 report local gold set with human-reviewed labels.
3. Compare OCR+LLM, OCR+VLM, and no-OCR VLM under the same Top-K field-level recall metric.
4. Add precision/F1 and safety-weighted abnormality metrics.
5. Stratify by document template, image quality, department, scanner/photo/screenshot source, and language.
6. Permit patient-facing explanation only after separate factuality, readability, and unsafe-advice review.
