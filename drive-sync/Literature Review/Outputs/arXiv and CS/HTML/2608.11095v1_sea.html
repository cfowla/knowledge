One-page synthesis

Core thesis. Agentic coding context files can ratchet upward because adding a defensive instruction is cheap, while deleting an old instruction safely becomes expensive once the reason it was added has been lost. The paper calls this catastrophic remembering. It proposes prompt comments—maintainer-facing rationale hidden from the executor—as a provenance channel that can make pruning safer and reduce extraneous instructions.

The paper triangulates the claim across three layers. First, a formal model defines a hidden constraint set, a minimum prompt cover D*, and recoverability of an instruction's latent rationale. If recoverability decays with age, the deletion hazard falls and equilibrium prompt size diverges even when the task itself is stationary. Second, a longitudinal corpus of 1,867 public GitHub repositories yields 247,694 instruction lifetimes: mean instruction count rises 226% over file lifetime, non-rewrite commits add 4.9 net instructions on average, wholesale rewrites account for most deaths, and deletion hazard falls with instruction age (log-hazard −0.032/commit; 95% interval −0.047 to −0.019). A rewrite resets size but not the growth process: files fall to 59.5% of the pre-rewrite level and recover to 91.5% within 10 commits, with post-rewrite growth slightly faster than pre-rewrite growth.

Third, the paper constructs a controlled testbed by inverting IFEval so the optimal prompt is known. At 15 maintenance steps across 552 histories, informative comments end at −5.8% excess size versus +60.4% without comments at similar constraint satisfaction. At 51 steps, the gap is +1.4% versus +211.3%, reported as removing 99.3% of excess. A WildIFEval adaptation then shows that 16 distractor instructions depress constraint satisfaction by 24.1 percentage points, while informative comments recover 11.6 points over three rounds (50.4% → 62.0%; 23.1% relative). A second LLM judge reproduces the direction, though neither judge is human ground truth.

Best contribution: the paper makes “prompt bloat” measurable as a lifecycle and maintenance problem rather than a static file-size problem. Its strongest practical idea is not deletion itself but capturing rationale at write time in a non-executable channel.
Central caution: the intervention can over-prune. Informative comments leave 12.1% of Inverse-IFEval worlds with an empty prompt; on those hard worlds, satisfaction is worse. The authors explicitly recommend a human deletion path and excluding safety-relevant instructions until tested.

Section condensation

Abstract & Introduction

The abstract frames catastrophic remembering as the dual of catastrophic forgetting: a maintainer preserves instructions that should be removed because the evidence licensing deletion has disappeared. The paper reports three headline empirical claims—real prompt growth, declining deletion hazard, and the comment intervention—and positions prompt comments as the analog of software comments that preserve “why.”

The introduction distinguishes three candidate mechanisms for older instructions surviving: staleness would predict rising deletion hazard with age; content fragility can generate falling marginal hazard through selection; imperfect recall predicts falling hazard and a stronger decline when more humans edit the file. This yields testable signatures rather than treating file growth as self-explanatory.

2 · Forgetting Requires Remembering Why

The formal setup models a task as a stated objective plus hidden constraints. A prompt is a set of instructions, and the target is a smallest instruction set that maximizes expected constraint satisfaction. Correctness and excess size are evaluated separately, preventing a smaller prompt from “winning” by simply forgetting requirements.

The key mechanism is recoverability of latent rationale. Without knowing why an instruction was added, exhaustive subset counterfactuals can be exponential in prompt size; with rationale, a relevant check can be constant-time. The paper factorizes deletion hazard into rationale recoverability and probability of excess, then derives divergence of equilibrium prompt size as recoverability tends toward zero. This is a conceptual model, not an identified causal law from repository data.

3 · Prompts Ratchet: Growth, Rewrite, Regrowth

The field corpus includes 1,867 repositories, 1,801 multi-version files, 299,440 transitions, and 247,694 instruction lifetimes. At the last tracked version, the median file has 39 instructions (P90 131). Among 1,576 multi-version repositories, 64.3% grow and 26.6% shrink, with median net +7; excluding mass rewrites, 19,267 commits average +4.9 net instructions. Lifetime-normalized instruction count rises 226%, versus 10% growth in mean instruction length and 140% growth in total prompt size.

Most disappearance is wholesale: rewrite/migration censoring accounts for 77.3% of observed deaths. After the first rewrite, instruction count drops to 59.5% of the pre-rewrite level and returns to 91.5% within 10 commits; growth is 4.1% per commit before and 4.9% after. The deletion hazard falls with age, and gamma-frailty models reduce but do not remove the slope. An exploratory multi-author×age interaction is negative (−0.0211, z=−11.7), consistent with the authors’ imperfect-recall story but not a randomized causal test.

4 · Comments Halt the Ratchet and Buy Back Instruction-Following

Inverse-IFEval turns each benchmark item into a maintenance world whose hidden constraints remain verifiable while the original directives become the known minimum cover. A fresh maintainer repeatedly sees only a general objective, censored failure signals, the current prompt, and whatever its arm is allowed to preserve. Informative comments carry failure rationale and outcomes to the next maintainer but are removed before executor inference.

At T=15, informative comments reduce final excess from +60.4% to −5.8% at similar satisfaction; at T=51, from +211.3% to +1.4%. Comment-shaped noise does not reproduce the effect. Ablations indicate outcomes are load-bearing, and stronger maintainers ratchet harder under no comments. The protocol can over-prune, particularly in three-instruction-cover worlds and in the 12.1% of worlds it empties.

4.3 · WildIFEval: noisy instructions and instruction-following

The WildIFEval adaptation seeds a prompt with true directives plus distractors. With K=1 and G=16 across 64 worlds, interference lowers satisfaction on true instructions from 65.6% to 41.5% (−24.1 points; 95% interval −33.4 to −14.9). Informative comments then raise satisfaction over three rounds from 50.4% to 62.0% (+11.6 points; 95% interval 5.1 to 18.3), a reported 23.1% relative gain. Comment-shaped noise differs from control by only 2.7 points with an interval spanning zero.

Because WildIFEval lacks programmatic verifiers, an arm-blind LLM judge scores one constraint-response pair at a time. Re-scoring 6,336 verdicts with a second judge produces a +7.8-point treatment effect versus +11.6 with the first; the between-judge effect difference is +3.8 points with an interval crossing zero. Raw agreement is 82.1%, κ=0.639. This checks robustness to one alternative judge, not judge accuracy.

5 · Related Work

The paper connects agent-context-file evolution, agent memory eviction, organizational rule lifecycles, software comments/decision records, continual learning, and instruction-following benchmarks. The most useful conceptual move is to distinguish removal based on an observable staleness proxy from removal based on recoverable provenance: an old instruction is not automatically obsolete.

Prior work is used as framing rather than as data generated by this study. The paper’s empirical novelty is instruction-level lifecycle tracking and controlled prompt-maintenance worlds with a known minimum cover.

6 · Discussion

The authors’ main interpretation is that instructions outlive the reasoning that justified them, and that storing this rationale in a maintainer-only channel can make prompt maintenance less append-only. They argue that software engineering has long recognized analogous “why” documentation problems and that agentic prompt maintenance should adopt a similar separation between executable directives and rationale.

The paper explicitly calls the implementation a prototype. Proposed next steps include native comment syntax for coding agents, maintenance best practices, alternative continual-learning-style interventions, representative constraint counts, and non-mechanically-verifiable constraints.

Limitations, LLM use & ethics

Important corpus limitations are the fixed 50% rewrite threshold, a matcher validated on only 50 transitions, an unvaried segmentation grammar, public-GitHub sampling, unmeasured language/domain composition, and no evidence for non-English instructions, system prompts, or skill files. Inverse-IFEval uses covers of only 2–3 instructions, short horizons, one canonical model in both roles, and mechanically verifiable English constraints. WildIFEval seeds bloat instead of observing natural growth and depends on LLM judges without human ground truth.

The authors state that LLMs were used broadly for technical and writing support, while a programmatic generator produced every reported number. Authorship metadata are reduced to distinct-editor counts, and released artifacts omit identities. The key safety caveat is operational: writing rationale is low-risk; acting automatically on rationale to delete directives is not.

Appendices A–E

Appendix A supplies the corpus tracker, segmentation, matching, competing-risk definitions, growth decomposition, Nelson–Aalen hazard procedure, gamma-frailty robustness, and exploratory authorship interaction. Appendix B makes the controlled maintenance loop and feedback censorship reproducible. Appendix C gives cover-size stratification, capability sweep, comment ablations, seed replication, per-world tail behavior, and a test showing that exposing comments to the executor increases bloat rather than helping correctness.

Appendix D exposes the full WildIFEval design grid and the second-judge analysis. Appendix E documents 49,680 model calls, 37.9M tokens, 45 model-hours, CPU-only corpus processing, no model training, dependency pinning, verifier reimplementation, licensing/redistribution boundaries, corpus coverage limits, and privacy reduction of author metadata. These appendices materially strengthen reproducibility and make the paper more informative than its main-text headline alone.

Evaluation & appraisal

Verdict: Read first. For agentic coding, prompt maintenance, long-lived instruction stores, and memory/provenance design, this is unusually build-useful. Treat the comment intervention as a strong prototype hypothesis, not a deployment-ready deletion policy.

Evidence basis: theory + real repository histories + controlled maintenance worlds.

Limiting factor: no production deployment study.

Would move score: native agent-harness implementation with longitudinal operational outcomes.

Evidence basis: instruction-lifetime tracking, minimum-cover inversion, maintainer-only comment channel.

Limiting factor: rationale comments borrow a familiar software-engineering primitive.

8.5/10Implementation value

Rationale: the intervention is simple and the information boundary is explicit: preserve “why” for maintainers, strip it from executor context.

Evidence basis: Figures 7–8, comment-content ablations, comment-strip ablation, safety discussion.

Limiting factor: deletion action remains unsafe without policy controls.

Would move score: validated deletion floors, safety-relevant exclusions, audit trails, and rollback/monitoring in a real coding-agent harness.

Strengths

The strongest aspect is triangulation: the paper does not rely on repository correlations alone or on a toy benchmark alone. It also exposes design failures, negative/near-null placebo results, over-pruning tails, judge disagreement, and the limits of the Sonnet capability row rather than hiding them. Appendix-level transparency materially improves interpretability.

Principal limitations

The causal chain is not fully closed. The field study cannot assign rationale loss; the multi-author interaction is exploratory and proxies turnover. The causal intervention establishes that preserved rationale can prevent bloat in small known-optimum worlds, but those worlds do not match the median 39-instruction context file. WildIFEval validates interference/recovery with more distractors but seeds the excess and uses LLM scoring. The leap from “comments help a controlled maintainer prune” to “this is why real CLAUDE.md files grow” remains a triangulated inference, not one experiment.

What not to overclaim

Do not read the paper as evidence that old instructions should be automatically deleted, that comments make safety-critical prompt pruning safe, that every growing context file is caused by imperfect recall, or that the effect is established for system prompts, skills, non-English instructions, or arbitrary production agents.

Implementation / practice takeaways

1 · Separate directive from rationaleStore executable instruction and maintainer-facing rationale as different typed fields. Do not rely on comments embedded in the executor prompt.
2 · Capture outcome history, not prose aloneThe ablation suggests a useful rationale record includes what failed, what was tried, and what happened. A narrative of attempts without outcomes can be worse than no provenance.
3 · Treat deletion as a governed actionUse human review, deletion floors, protected/safety-critical directives, audit logs, and rollback. Comment capture can be automated more safely than instruction deletion.
4 · Track lifecycle metricsUseful monitoring: instruction births/deaths, age-specific deletion hazard, rewrite frequency, post-rewrite regrowth, excess/duplicate directives, comment coverage, and deletion reversals.
5 · Keep rationale out of executor context by defaultThe comment-strip ablation suggests executor-visible rationale can add prompt bulk without improving correctness. Maintain a strict projection boundary.
6 · Validate at realistic cover sizesBefore using the protocol operationally, test dozens of interacting constraints, long horizons, multiple maintainer/executor models, real code tasks, and safety-sensitive instructions.
Build-useful design pattern. Represent each persistent instruction as something like {directive, rationale, trigger/failure, outcome, recurrence_count, provenance, last_reviewed}; present only directive to the executor, while the maintainer receives the richer record. This is an implementation inference from the paper’s information boundary, not a schema reported by the source.
