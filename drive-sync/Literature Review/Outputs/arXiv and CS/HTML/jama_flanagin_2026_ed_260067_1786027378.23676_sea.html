One-page synthesis

Core thesis. JAMA Network permits selected AI assistance when transparently disclosed and verified by authors, while prohibiting uses that substitute AI for human scholarly judgment, compromise confidentiality, misrepresent research provenance, or create/manipulate clinical media outside disclosed formal research.

The most consequential updates are operational. Authors are advised not to use AI/LLMs to generate, format, or manage references because of accuracy and integrity risks; AI drafting of Opinion manuscripts, Letters to the Editor, and online Comments is not permitted; AI-generated or AI-manipulated clinical images, illustrations, video, and audio are generally prohibited unless the AI use is part of formal research design or methods and is fully disclosed. Grammar/spelling checking is permitted without disclosure; translation and manuscript assistance are permitted with specified disclosure and author responsibility.

The policy centers accountability on human authors. Authors must verify AI-assisted content, remain responsible for accuracy and integrity, disclose content-creation/revision/formatting assistance in acknowledgments, and provide tool/version/manufacturer/date/use details. Failure to disclose may lead to rejection or postpublication corrective action. For research involving AI, the article points authors to prior JAMA Network reporting guidance. The article does not test whether these rules reduce hallucinations, bias, low-value submissions, or publication errors.

Section condensation

Rationale and policy context

The editorial frames AI as potentially useful for reducing author workload and improving efficiency, while emphasizing concerns about undisclosed use, hallucinated or fabricated content, formulaic submissions, and uncertainty about what journals require authors to disclose. It argues that more uniform disclosure expectations may improve transparency but that disclosure alone does not resolve all integrity problems.

The editors describe direct JAMA Network experience with inaccurate or nonexistent references and with growth in formulaic Opinion/Letter submissions, using those observations together with cited literature to justify more explicit restrictions. These observations are not presented with denominators, rates, sampling methods, or uncertainty estimates.

References, manuscript preparation, and human authorship

The updated policy advises against using AI/LLMs to generate, format, or manage bibliographic references and instead recommends standard reference managers plus author verification. AI may assist with manuscript preparation, literature searching, translation, grammar/spelling, and certain research tasks under defined disclosure and responsibility conditions.

Human-authorship boundaries are explicit: AI may not draft Opinion manuscripts, Letters to the Editor, online Comments, or author responses to reviewers/editors. Grammar/spelling assistance does not require disclosure; translation is permitted only after initial human-written text, with disclosure and accuracy responsibility.

Images, multimedia, confidentiality, and accountability

AI-created or AI-manipulated clinical images and illustrations are prohibited unless part of formal research design/methods and fully disclosed; the same restriction applies to clinical video and audio. Limited uniform image adjustments may be allowed when they preserve underlying clinical/scientific integrity, are disclosed, and original files remain available for editorial review.

Peer-review use of external AI/LLM tools is prohibited because entering manuscripts or reviewer summaries would violate confidentiality. Across all permitted uses, authors remain responsible for submitted content, must verify AI outputs, and may face rejection or postpublication correction for incomplete disclosure.

Implementation / practice takeaways

Build into authoring workflowsMaintain an AI-use log capturing tool, version, manufacturer, dates, affected manuscript sections, and author verification.
Reference workflowKeep AI out of reference generation/formatting/management; use conventional reference managers and perform an explicit integrity check.
Human-only content classesFlag Opinion, Letters, online Comments, reviewer responses, and peer-review content as non-AI drafting zones under JAMA Network policy.
Clinical media controlsRequire provenance, original files, and a formal research-method exception before accepting any AI-created/manipulated clinical image, illustration, video, or audio.
Governance boundaryTreat this as JAMA Network policy. Re-check the target journal's current Instructions for Authors rather than assuming universal adoption.
What not to overclaimThe editorial does not establish that disclosure policies reduce hallucinations, bias, or low-value submissions; it defines governance rules and rationale.
