One-page synthesis

Core thesis: psychiatric medication information systems should not merge regulatory adverse-event reports and patient narratives into one undifferentiated answer stream. The paper proposes a provenance-aware knowledge graph and multi-agent retrieval architecture that keeps FDA/FAERS, WebMD, and Reddit evidence roles separate while using LLMs for extraction, routing, summarization, comparison, and validation.

The study builds a large psychiatric medication information corpus from 466,525 information-rich Reddit posts, 60,782 WebMD reviews, and FAERS records from 2005 Q2 through 2025 Q1, mapped against an ATC-N drug dictionary and controlled clinical vocabularies. The authors benchmark nine LLMs for named-entity recognition over physician-annotated data, select GPT-4.1-mini as the pipeline default because it balances accuracy, throughput, and cost, and construct a Neo4j graph with Medication, Condition, SideEffect, and Post nodes linked by treatment, adverse-event, withdrawal, comorbidity, and provenance edges.

The strongest evidence is architectural and descriptive: community platforms, especially Reddit and WebMD, are more similar to each other than to FDA/FAERS for several antidepressant adverse-event profiles, and sertraline shows community mentions appearing before FDA dates for many events. The study is not yet a clinical product evaluation. It is best read as a build-useful pattern for provenance-aware clinical RAG and patient education systems, not as proof that a chatbot can safely counsel patients about antidepressants.

Section-by-section condensation

Abstract

The abstract identifies a real safety communication problem: psychiatric medication knowledge is split between formal regulatory adverse-event records and patient-generated narratives. The authors argue that integrating these sources without confusing evidence with anecdote is important because poor contextualization can increase fear, nocebo effects, and non-adherence.

The paper reports a provenance-aware multi-agent framework over 466,525 Reddit posts, 60,782 WebMD reviews, and 20 years of FAERS data for nine antidepressants. Key claims include high name-level NER performance for medications and conditions, high community-source concordance, community lead-time for some sertraline adverse events, and a Neo4j graph that preserves source traceability while keeping regulatory facts distinct from patient experience.

Introduction

The introduction frames psychiatric medication education as a high-uncertainty communication problem. Patients often need information about sleep, appetite, concentration, affect, weight, motivation, discontinuation symptoms, and side effects after short clinical encounters. Online forums provide experience-near language but also carry accuracy, representativeness, nocebo, stigma, and adherence risks.

The authors position LLMs, knowledge graphs, and multi-agent workflows as a way to separate retrieval, evidence role, synthesis, and validation. Regulatory sources can anchor formal claims, while community narratives can add lived-experience context. The intended system is educational, source-grounded, and safety-constrained, not autonomous clinical decision support.

Methods: drug list construction and data collection

The drug dictionary comes from the WHO ATC system, filtered to the nervous-system branch, yielding 626 generic drug names. The authors used a constrained LLM prompt to expand generic names into brand synonyms, then used the resulting ATC-N dictionary for keyword generation, data collection, and medication normalization.

Reddit data were collected from Academic Torrents from June 2005 through April 2025, filtered by ATC-N keywords, deduplicated, language-filtered, and further filtered for information richness, producing 466,525 posts from 1,138,331 cleaned candidates. WebMD reviews were scraped and filtered similarly, leaving 60,782 reviews. FAERS quarterly files from 2005 Q2 through 2025 Q1 were parsed with faers-toolkit, filtered to ATC-N drugs, and aligned by FDA receipt date.

Methods: NER, mapping, and graph construction

The LLM NER prompt extracts medications, dosages, forms, duration, continuation, primary condition, comorbid conditions, side effects, and relations such as TREATS, CAUSES, CAUSES_BY_WITHDRAW, and COMORBID_WITH. Nine LLMs were benchmarked against a physician-annotated gold-standard NER dataset; GPT-4.1-mini was chosen for the main extraction pass because it balanced accuracy, cost, and throughput.

Extracted medications map to ATC-N identifiers, conditions to ICD-10 terms, and side effects to MedDRA Preferred Terms using embedding-based nearest-neighbor matching with entity-specific thresholds. Neo4j stores canonical Medication, Condition, SideEffect, and Post nodes. Full post text is kept in a SQLite sidecar database rather than the graph, while MENTIONS edges and supporting post ID lists preserve provenance for evidence-traced retrieval.

Methods: multi-source comparison and chatbot architecture

The comparative analysis aligns FDA, WebMD, and Reddit at the drug–adverse-event level for nine antidepressants. The authors analyze overlap in adverse-event sets, source balance, relative prominence, and first-mention timing across sources. This explicitly treats sources as complementary rather than interchangeable.

The chatbot architecture uses parallel NER and intent agents, then a KG query generation agent, source-specific graph queries, a summarization agent, a comparison agent, and a validation agent. Source selection is intent-driven: demographic or epidemiologic questions route more toward structured FAERS/WebMD data, while experiential questions route preferentially toward Reddit.

Results: NER performance

Name-level extraction was strong, but attribute-level extraction was weaker. GPT-4.1-mini had the highest medication-name F1 at 0.969; Claude-Sonnet-4 had the highest primary-condition F1 at 0.973; Deepseek-V3 had the highest side-effect-name F1 at 0.912. Dosage, duration, and frequency were notably harder than entity names.

The key methodological implication is that the graph can be relatively reliable for entity presence, but less reliable for precise attributes such as adverse-event duration, frequency, and dose. This matters for patient-facing use because timing, severity, recurrence, and dosage context are often exactly what users need for safe interpretation.

Results: knowledge graph and source similarity

The Reddit-derived KG integrates ontology-grounded psychiatric pharmacotherapy entities from 466,525 posts. The graph uses canonical medication, condition, and side-effect nodes, typed clinical relation edges, and direct post-level provenance. Sertraline is used as a subgraph example linking medication, ICD-10 conditions, MedDRA side effects, and source posts.

Across nine antidepressants, WebMD and Reddit usually showed the highest adverse-event overlap. Examples include WebMD–Reddit Jaccard similarity of 0.905 for desvenlafaxine, 0.875 for duloxetine, 0.833 for fluoxetine, 0.805 for paroxetine, and 0.786 for sertraline. FDA remained related but systematically distinct, supporting the authors’ view that community sources add patient-centered salience and timing rather than simply duplicating regulatory data.

Results: sertraline case study

Sertraline had higher overlap between WebMD and Reddit (Jaccard 0.786) than between FDA and WebMD (0.577) or FDA and Reddit (0.558). Frequency-based scatterplots showed the same pattern: Reddit–WebMD correlation was high (r = 0.847), while FDA–Reddit and FDA–WebMD were moderate (r = 0.593 and 0.588).

Volcano plots showed that some experiential symptoms, including dry mouth, sexual dysfunction, and panic, appeared more prominent in community comparisons, whereas medically framed events were more enriched in FDA. Lead-time analysis for sertraline was skewed negative, meaning many events appeared earlier in Reddit or WebMD than in FDA, but the authors appropriately avoid claiming causal early detection.

Discussion and limitations

The discussion argues that community and regulatory data should be treated as complementary pharmacovigilance views. Reddit contributes broad experience narratives, WebMD adds semi-structured review context, and FAERS adds formal postmarketing safety data. A provenance-aware graph can keep these roles visible during retrieval and synthesis.

Limitations are substantial: weak attribute-level extraction, English-only corpora, residual mapping error, focus on nine antidepressants, and no demonstration of clinical utility, patient usability, response safety, adherence benefit, or decision-making impact. The authors call for broader medication classes, multilingual corpora, and prospective clinician/patient evaluation before deployment claims.

Appendix extraction

Appendix A — Brand synonym expansion prompt

The brand-expansion prompt asks an LLM to return structured XML containing a generic name and zero or more brand-name tags. The prompt includes a nervous-system domain constraint and a “None” sentinel to distinguish no-brand outputs from malformed or off-topic outputs.

Appraisal: this is practical for recall, but brand-name expansion by LLM should be audited against trusted drug databases. Brand synonyms are high-impact in clinical retrieval because false positives can pull in wrong medications, and false negatives can miss relevant narratives.

Appendix B — Information-rich post classifier

The authors manually labeled 3,500 Reddit posts using criteria requiring a nervous-system drug, a side effect/adverse event, and enough specific detail about symptoms, timing, dosage, sequence, daily-life impact, or medical attention. About 23.3% of posts were labeled information-rich.

A BERT-base-uncased classifier was fine-tuned for three epochs and achieved weighted accuracy 0.866, precision 0.875, recall 0.866, and F1 0.869 on the test set. The information-rich class had lower precision/F1 than the not-information-rich class, so retained data are improved but not clean enough to treat as clinician-validated adverse-event evidence.

Appendix C — Medical-doctor annotation interface

The interface shows a full Reddit post with highlighted model spans, a structured JSON preview, and controls for medical doctors to confirm or flag extraction errors. It also lets annotators flag incorrect subfields, which is important because many model errors occur at the attribute level rather than the entity-name level.

Appraisal: the interface supports adjudication, but the paper does not provide enough detail to assess inter-annotator agreement, annotation volume by entity type, disagreement resolution rates, or whether the physician gold standard captures ambiguous patient language reliably.

Appendix D — NER prompt

The NER prompt is a single-pass structured extraction prompt that returns medications, conditions, comorbidities, side effects, relations, sentiment, and effectiveness as JSON. It instructs the model to extract only explicit content, use null for missing fields, and avoid paraphrase or inferred data.

Appraisal: the null-handling rule is a strong design choice for clinical extraction. However, the prompt still infers dosage form as oral unless another form is explicitly stated, which may be practical but creates a subtle exception to the otherwise conservative extraction policy.

Appendix E — Embedding mapping thresholds

Free-text entities are embedded with text-embedding-3-small, compared by nearest-neighbor retrieval against reference terms, and accepted when the top candidate exceeds a type-specific threshold calibrated on physician-adjudicated labels. Final thresholds are side effects to MedDRA PT τ = 0.68, conditions to ICD-10 τ = 0.56, and comorbid conditions to ICD-10 τ = 0.54.

Appraisal: separate thresholds by entity type are appropriate because score distributions differ. The risk is residual semantic over-mapping or under-mapping, especially for patient-described side effects that are colloquial, overlapping, or symptom clusters rather than clean MedDRA terms.

Appendix G — KG implementation details

The Neo4j ingest process reads JSON/JSONL records containing post identifiers, original text, auxiliary model outputs, structured entities, and relations. Domain entities are canonicalized before insertion and deduplicated using deterministic lowercased whitespace-normalized UIDs.

Relations aggregate evidence by storing supporting post_ids for each unique entity-pair/relation triple. The system uses batched Python ingestion with Neo4j MERGE semantics and a SQLite sidecar for raw text, sentiment/effectiveness outputs, and per-post evidence tables. This is a reasonable architecture for keeping graph queries compact while still permitting raw-evidence inspection.

Main strengths

  • Correct safety premise: the paper does not treat patient narratives as interchangeable with regulatory evidence.
  • Provenance-aware architecture: post-level MENTIONS edges and source-specific summarization preserve inspectability.
  • Practical schema: medications, conditions, side effects, relations, and source anchors map well to downstream RAG retrieval.
  • Empirical extraction benchmark: nine LLMs are compared against physician annotations, and the paper exposes attribute-level weakness rather than hiding it.
  • Good cost-awareness: model selection considers throughput and operational spend, not only top-line F1.
  • Useful source comparison: Jaccard, correlation, enrichment, and lead-time analyses give a multi-angle view of source divergence.

Main limitations

  • No prospective user testing: the chatbot is not shown to improve comprehension, reduce anxiety, support adherence, or avoid unsafe conclusions.
  • Attribute extraction is weak: side-effect duration, frequency, and dosing attributes remain unreliable, which limits clinical usefulness.
  • No incidence denominator: Reddit, WebMD, and FAERS cannot estimate adverse-event prevalence or patient-specific risk.
  • Selection and language bias: English-only public posts/reviews underrepresent many patients and care settings.
  • Mapping error risk: colloquial symptoms may be over-merged or under-mapped when forced into MedDRA/ICD-10 terms.
  • Deployment details incomplete: validation-agent safety rules, failure handling, monitoring, evaluation rubric, and clinician escalation are not sufficiently specified for production use.
  • Possible model-era instability: model costs, latencies, and F1 rankings are time-sensitive and may change with API/model revisions.
Clinical caution: community reports should be used as salience and language evidence, not as causal evidence, population incidence, or individualized treatment risk. A patient-facing version would require clinician-reviewed answer policies, source labeling, adverse-event escalation logic, crisis/suicidality guardrails, and prospective safety testing.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Build pattern to reuse
Source-role separation

Tag every retrieved claim as regulatory, clinical-reference, patient narrative, review-platform, or model-derived. Do not let synthesis flatten these roles.

Graph design
Entity graph + sidecar text

Keep the KG compact with canonical entities and relation edges, while storing raw text and evidence snippets in a searchable sidecar.

Validation target
Evidence path, not answer fluency

Require final answers to cite source-specific retrieval artifacts and preserve disagreement between sources.

Clinical safety
Educational-only boundary

Explicitly prevent diagnosis, drug initiation/discontinuation advice, or individualized risk prediction without clinician review.
