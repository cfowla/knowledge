# Processing report - arXiv:2608.18591v1

## Disposition

**Retained and fully processed.**

The paper survived the High-skim promotion gate. Its strongest result is not general-purpose model routing; it is pre-flight, within-model reasoning-budget allocation for document workloads. DRB-guided allocation reduces adjusted cost in 13 of 15 model-dataset settings and matches or improves F1 in 9 of 15, while explicitly charging estimator compute overhead. The most important implementation boundary is latency: on inexpensive/fast Gemini settings, the pre-flight scan can add substantial delay, and the exploratory cross-model case study loses enough F1 that it should not be treated as a mature router.

## Focused triage findings

### Cost and latency

DRB is a roughly 1B-parameter multimodal estimator that predicts a seven-class performance label for a `(document, prompt, model, reasoning budget)` configuration. On 100 held-out base documents (2,500 configuration pairs), weighted estimator F1 is 0.753; Class 6 recall is 0.932. In the primary downstream experiment, adjusted cost falls in 13/15 configurations, ranging from 5.2% to 99.0%. The largest savings occur on expensive GPT maximum-effort baselines. Latency is not uniformly favorable: some Gemini settings add roughly 20-147% latency, so estimator economics depend on the downstream model's cost/latency profile.

### Routing calibration

The model-selection experiment is explicitly exploratory. Within Gemini, F1 is 2.5-9.9% below the oracle while adjusted cost falls 9.6-75.9%. Within GPT and across providers, F1 losses are roughly 6-11% despite very large cost savings. This is evidence that the estimator captures coarse cost-avoidance signal but does not transfer cleanly into cross-provider routing without dedicated calibration/supervision.
