## Triage outcome

The full paper supports promotion beyond the original "watch" briefing for inference-serving work. The strongest result is the end-to-end A100 prefill benchmark. At 4k tokens, SpTrans75 reports 31.80 K tokens/s versus 14.33 for FlashAttention and 29.80 for the matched all-INT8 One path. The calculated SpTrans75 to FlashAttention ratio is 2.219-fold.

The promotion is narrow. The implementation is evaluated on A100 only, the routing policies are static templates, and the large 16k factual-recall gains under SpTrans remain an unexplained layout-task interaction.

## SEA output

Verdict: **Skim deeply**

The paper is useful as a mixed-precision dense-attention kernel reference. It is not strong enough to generalize its A100 speedups to newer accelerators or to treat the 16k SpTrans factual-recall jump as a broad model-quality gain.

### Appraisal scores

- Relevance: 7/10. Directly relevant to inference-kernel work, but narrow for broader agent or clinical AI workflows.
- Novelty: 8/10. The paper makes precision a spatial routing decision inside a fused dense-attention loop rather than using one precision path for the full call.
- Method strength: 7/10. The benchmark protocol is reasonably controlled and includes quantization and routing overhead.
- Evidence strength: 6/10. The results support an A100 prefill tradeoff, but they do not establish broad model-quality gains or hardware generality.
- External validity: 4/10. The tested scope is constrained to A100 FP16/INT8 prefill and a small set of 3B to 7B model families.
- Implementation value: 7/10. The paper gives enough kernel, quantization, metadata, and memory detail to guide an implementation experiment.
