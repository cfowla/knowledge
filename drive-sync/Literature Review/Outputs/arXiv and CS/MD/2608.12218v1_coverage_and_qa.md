## SEA appraisal constraints / extraction limitations

1. The natural-language pretraining study is limited to 20M–750M models and a long-document Project Gutenberg subset; frontier-scale behavior is not directly tested.
2. Natural-language pretraining comparisons are token- and update-matched, not FLOP-matched; longer windows have materially higher measured GPU-hour costs.
3. SFT supporting/conflicting contexts are deliberately generated with Gemini rather than retrieved from natural corpora; this strengthens experimental control but narrows ecological validity.
4. MMLU-Pro domain examples are split 80/20 from the benchmark test split for fine-tuning/evaluation. The held-out split is internally separated, but the study does not establish freedom from model pretraining contamination of MMLU-Pro.
5. The theory proves an achievability/feasible-set monotonicity result, not that gradient-based training must minimize parametric information in practice.
6. FFN-versus-attention functional interpretation is supported by module-restricted interventions but remains a coarse mechanistic decomposition; attention mass is summarized by the maximum head within each layer.
7. The motivating Phi-3/OLMo comparisons involve checkpoints produced by different context-extension/training stages and are not a controlled context-length-only experiment; the paper appropriately uses them as motivation rather than primary causal evidence.
8. No dedicated conflict-of-interest statement was identified in the primary PDF; none was inferred.
