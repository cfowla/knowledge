# 2608.18852v1 processing report

## Disposition

Retained. The paper gives a useful mechanism-level result for long-horizon agents: outcome-only training can give a small mid-trajectory selector action very little credit and can punish a correct selection when later execution fails. SkillGate directly changes where the credit lands.

## Selector findings

- SkillGate reached 53.2% overall trial success on the 385-trial protocol, compared with 47.0% for matched-budget outcome-only SkillRL and 40.8% for the SFT RL initialization.
- Oracle exposure was 83.9% with SkillGate versus 54.3% with outcome-only SkillRL.
- Misleading-skill exposure was 21.8% versus 69.6%.
- In the outcome-only audit, skill identity tokens carried a median 0.14% of trajectory loss weight and that share diluted about sevenfold with trajectory length.
- Wrong-sign credit on oracle reads rose from 28.9% in the shortest trajectory bin to 61.7% in the longest.
- The pooled SkillGate minus outcome-only trial-success difference was +7.9 percentage points with a 95% task-clustered bootstrap interval of +2.1 to +14.3.

## Scaling limits

The paper does not establish a controlled scaling law for SkillGate. SkillGate is trained at one model size, 9B, and the candidate slate remains fixed at 16. Larger frontier models are presented as capability references and are explicitly not controlled comparisons. The source library contains 2,045 skills, but the paper does not sweep candidate-slate size or full library size. The strongest defensible conclusion is that larger reference models still show unreliable skill selection in this setup, not that scale cannot solve selection under all conditions.
