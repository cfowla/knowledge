One-page synthesis

Core thesis. CPAgents reframes cardiac imaging PheWAS feature construction as an agentic, constrained, auditable feature-engineering loop: an Analyst profiles statistical pathologies, a Proposer generates medically/statistically motivated composite phenotype formulas, and a Verifier filters and validates candidates before downstream disease classification.

The paper argues that expert-defined single imaging traits undercapture nonlinear effects, ratios, group-adjusted structure, and cross-phenotype interactions. It uses a constrained abstract syntax tree (AST) expression space to generate composite phenotypes such as indexed, sex-standardized left ventricular mass, then validates candidates using marginal AUC, ElasticNet stability, deduplication, SHAP ranking, and cross-model acceptance.

The empirical claim is moderate but not practice-ready: on UK Biobank cardiac imaging data with nine disease categories, the CPAgents variants reportedly take the top rank in 56 of 72 classifier–disease–metric combinations, versus 18 for expert/MESHAgents baselines. The most useful contribution is the harness pattern—especially the AST safety layer and verifier feedback—not direct clinical adoption of the discovered phenotypes.

Main caution. The work is a v1 preprint with internal validation on one population-scale cohort, limited external-validity evidence, sparse reporting of discovered formulas, no external replication, no calibration/fairness analysis, and incomplete transparency around disease label construction, missing data handling, and code/prompt reproducibility.

Section condensation

Abstract

The abstract positions robust cardiac imaging phenotype–disease associations as central to cardiovascular population research and risk stratification, but argues that standard PheWAS pipelines rely on predefined single-variable phenotypes or expert-crafted features. CPAgents is presented as an iterative framework that automatically constructs interpretable composite phenotypes including polynomial, ratio, and interaction forms from base imaging features.

The proposed system uses three agents: an Analyst for statistical pathologies and transformation nominations, a Proposer for numerically safe constrained expressions, and a Verifier for multi-stage validation and evidence trails. Across 72 classifier–disease–metric combinations, the authors report top rank in 56 cases for CPAgents variants versus 18 for baselines, with gains across all nine disease categories.

1. Introduction

The introduction frames cardiac imaging-derived traits from CMR and echocardiography as intermediate phenotypes linking biology, environment, and clinical outcomes. It emphasizes that population imaging cohorts now supply large, standardized feature sets, creating a bottleneck in deciding which combinations to test and how to capture nonlinear or interaction effects while maintaining interpretability and robustness.

The authors identify two limitations in prior work: fixed expert feature sets restrict nonlinear discovery, while unconstrained automated feature expansion increases multiple-testing burden and instability. Their stated contributions are an iterative agentic discovery loop, statistics-informed composition and validation, and compact reproducible formulas with evidence summaries for downstream association analysis and external replication.

2. Method — problem setup and phenotype space

The supervised discovery problem uses a cohort D = (xᵢ, yᵢ), where each subject has cardiac imaging phenotypes plus confounders, and each target is a disease label. The output is a compact set of interpretable closed-form composite phenotypes fₖ that operate on the input vector and aim to improve downstream disease classification.

The expression space is formalized as an AST with leaves as raw scalar features or numeric constants and internal nodes as allowable operators. The permitted operators include element-wise transformations such as log1p, sqrt, clip, power, and where; arithmetic operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division; and group-aware reductions such as group means, standard deviations, and rank transforms. Division receives an epsilon denominator guardrail of 10⁻⁹.

2. Method — clinical example and iterative search

The clinical example is a sex-specific z-score of allometrically indexed left ventricular mass, with left ventricular mass divided by height in meters raised to 2.7, then standardized within sex. The authors emphasize that constants such as 2.7 can be elicited from LLM medical priors instead of discovered by random search, though the source and correctness of such priors still require audit.

The iterative procedure has four phases. The Analyst profiles nonlinearity with ΔAIC between linear and spline logistic models, distributional shape with skewness/kurtosis/outlier rates, pairwise interactions using mutual information and cross-validated logistic confirmation, group effects using ICC and Cramér’s V, and redundancy by absolute-correlation clustering. The Proposer generates candidates using a 2:2:1 split across medical priors, statistical transformations, and exploratory mathematics. The Verifier screens with marginal ΔAUC, ElasticNet stability, correlation and textual deduplication, LightGBM/SHAP importance, and global acceptance if the composite score improves in at least two of four classifiers.

3. Experiments and Results — dataset and implementation

The evaluation uses 26,893 UK Biobank participants and nine disease categories: hypertension, high cholesterol, cardiac disease, peripheral vascular disease, stroke, asthma, COPD, diabetes, and depression. Cardiac imaging phenotypes are extracted from a standardized CMR pipeline, and confounders include sex, age, weight, height, alcohol intake, log10 alcohol intake, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure.

The split is 6:2:2 for training, validation, and test, with discovery and tuning restricted to training/validation and final evaluation conducted on the test set. The LLM backend for Analyst and Proposer is DeepSeek-V3.2 through LangChain; spline profiling uses B-splines with df = 4 and degree = 3; stability selection uses 50 ElasticNet bootstrap runs with l1-ratio 0.5 and C = 0.5; metrics are averaged over five random seeds. Baselines are expert-defined features and MESHAgents-derived features, with comparisons across SVM, AdaBoost, MLP, and LDA.

3. Results, robustness, and cluster separability

Table 1 reports AUC and Recall for nine diseases, four classifiers, expert features, MESHAgents features, and CPAgents variants. The narrative result is that CPAgents variants occupy the top rank in 56 of 72 classifier–disease–metric combinations and appear in the top two in 51 settings, compared with 33 and 31 top-two settings for expert and MESHAgents features.

Table 2 reports CPAgents average AUC 0.686 ± 0.076 and Recall 0.643 ± 0.069. Ablation suggests the Verifier is the most important module: removing it lowers AUC to 0.590 ± 0.006 and Recall to 0.544 ± 0.012. Composite phenotypes also have the highest silhouette×10 score at 0.413, above raw features at 0.293, expert features at 0.276, MESHAgent features at 0.113, and constituent features at 0.094.

4. Conclusion

The conclusion restates that CPAgents discovers clinically interpretable composite cardiac imaging phenotypes and strengthens phenotype–disease association signals compared with expert-defined single traits. The authors argue that the combination of specialized agents, statistical robustness checks, compact formulas, and audit trails can produce phenotype libraries better aligned with pathophysiology.

The stated next step is extension to multimodal data and evaluation of whether composite phenotypes can support clinical decision-making and trial design. The paper does not yet provide enough external validation, prospective evaluation, or operational governance detail to justify clinical deployment.

Workflow / algorithm — CPAgents closed-loop candidate generation

WORKFLOW / ALGORITHM — Analyst–Proposer–Verifier phenotype discovery loop
Inputs: Base cardiac imaging phenotypes; confounders; disease labels; clinical feature definitions; current feature set; forbidden target labels; allowed AST operators.
Process / decision rules: Analyst profiles nonlinearity, distribution shape, interactions, group effects, and redundancy. Proposer generates candidates from medical priors, statistical recommendations, and exploratory mathematics in a 2:2:1 allocation. Interpreter validates AST expressions and repairs failed candidates with up to two LLM retry attempts. Verifier applies marginal ΔAUC, ElasticNet stability, deduplication, SHAP ranking, and cross-model validation.
Outputs: Accepted composite phenotype formulas; evidence trails; rejected candidate feedback; updated feature set.
Failure modes: LLM-generated clinically plausible but empirically brittle constants; leakage through adaptive validation; unreported missing-data effects; confounder-control ambiguity; weak disease labels; spurious group operations; formulas that are interpretable syntactically but not clinically meaningful.
Human review: Not clearly specified as a required gate. Human clinical review should be added before treating any generated formula as biologically or operationally meaningful.
Governance: Strong starting elements include constrained operators, numerical safety, repair/deduplication, evidence trails, and rejection feedback. Missing elements include versioned prompts, formula provenance, external replication, subgroup/fairness checks, calibration, monitoring triggers, and code/data release.

Implementation and practice takeaways

Worth extractingUse a hard AST/interpreter layer between LLM proposals and executable features. Treat LLMs as candidate generators, not as trusted feature-selection authorities.
Verifier patternCombine marginal utility, stability selection, deduplication, global importance ranking, and cross-model acceptance before admitting features to the library.
Evidence trailLog every candidate formula, source prompt, statistical rationale, validation metric, rejection reason, repair attempt, and final accepted formula.
Clinical governance gapAdd domain expert review, provenance for medical constants, external validation, subgroup/fairness checks, and calibration before clinical use.
RAG/parser relevanceThe architecture maps well to deterministic scientific curation: Analyst = source diagnostics, Proposer = structured candidate extraction, Verifier = schema/rule/statistical gate.
Do not overclaimThe paper does not establish causal disease mechanisms, patient benefit, diagnostic readiness, or transportability across hospitals, scanners, or populations.
Best use. Add to an agent-harness/RAG design folder as a compact example of constrained generation plus verification for scientific feature discovery.
Do not use for. Clinical decision support, direct patient risk stratification, or formula adoption without independent validation and governance.
